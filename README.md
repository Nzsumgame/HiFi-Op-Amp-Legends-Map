# HiFi Op-Amp Legends Map

A sourced map of the audio op-amp ICs that Hi-Fi and DIY-audio enthusiasts rate most highly. For each part it records the part numbers and makers, why people like it, and every datasheet document and revision we could trace. It also keeps a register of cases where the silicon changed but the part number did not.

> **Why revisions matter.** A part number does not guarantee a particular die. Texas Instruments' current NE5532 (datasheet SLOS075**K**, December 2025) is new silicon. Typical slew rate is down from 9 to 5 V/µs, the absolute-maximum supply is down from ±22 V to ±18 V, and several load-drive specs have been deleted. TI reportedly now builds the NE5532, LM833, MC33078 and RC4580 on one shared die. TI's OPA134/OPA2134/OPA4134 moved to a new fab in 2024 (SBOS058**B**). On the new die the offset-trim pins are NC and the headroom and channel-separation specs are lower. Parts sold under these numbers today are not always the parts that earned the reputation. Other cases include second-source dies (Signetics vs TI vs JRC 5532s, TI LM833 vs LM833-N, TI LF353 vs LF353-N) and process moves such as TL07x → TL07xH. Every recorded case, with how to tell old from new, is in **[docs/REVISION-HAZARDS.md](docs/REVISION-HAZARDS.md)**.

## The map

Each family links to its own page. A family page lists part numbers and grades, lineage, key specs, reputation with sources, silicon changes, the datasheet list and the datasheet revision history. ⚠ marks a family with a documented same-part-number silicon or second-source change. The small letter after each name is its tier: **A** legend or revision hazard, **B** widely praised, **C** niche, or included as a baseline.

## Datasheets

[docs/DATASHEETS.md](docs/DATASHEETS.md) and `data/datasheets.csv` list every datasheet document recorded per family, with vendor, document number, revision letter, date, URL, and what kind of copy the URL points to. Copies on distributor and third-party sites often preserve an older revision that the vendor no longer serves.

To download the PDFs, including every historical revision the Internet Archive holds, use the fetcher:

```bash
pip install pypdf                      # optional: labels each PDF with its doc number / revision / date
python tools/fetch_datasheets.py --dry-run            # show every URL and archive query, fetch nothing
python tools/fetch_datasheets.py --family NE5532 OPAx134
python tools/fetch_datasheets.py                      # everything (slow: be polite to archive.org)
```

The fetcher combines three sources:

1. **direct**: every URL recorded in `data/opamps.json`.
2. **ti**: for each TI literature number (e.g. `SLOS075`) it probes `ti.com/lit/ds/<lit><rev>/<lit><rev>.pdf` for revisions `''` and `a` to `z`.
3. **wayback**: Internet Archive CDX prefix queries over the current and legacy datasheet locations of every maker:
   - TI's `lit/ds/symlink`, `focus.ti.com` and `www-s.ti.com/sc/ds`
   - `national.com/ds/…`
   - `analog.com/static/imported-files/…`
   - `cds.linear.com/docs/…/1028fd.pdf`
   - `njr.co.jp`/`njr.com`/`nisshinbo-microdevices.co.jp`
   - Philips `acrobat/datasheets/NE5532_n.pdf` and NXP
   - onsemi/Motorola `pub/Collateral`
   - ST and Fairchild

   The fetcher also queries each family's own `archive_seed_urls`. Every distinct capture digest is downloaded once.

PDFs are de-duplicated by SHA-256 and saved as `datasheets/<family>/<family>__<date>__<vendor>__<doc_rev>__<sha8>.pdf`. They are also listed in `datasheets/manifest.json` and in `datasheets/INDEX.md`, which gives one row per distinct PDF in date order, so old and new revisions sit side by side. Re-runs resume where they stopped.

You can also run it on GitHub: **Actions → Fetch datasheets → Run workflow**. The PDFs are uploaded as a workflow artifact, and are optionally committed to the branch.

Datasheets are the copyright of their publishers. This repository stores links and metadata, and the fetcher downloads copies for personal reference.

## Method and caveats

The data was gathered by a multi-agent web research pass: several discovery lenses, then per-family research followed by an independent adversarial fact-check. The lenses covered English, Japanese, Chinese and Korean enthusiast communities, engineering references, and revision hazards. Details and limits are in [docs/METHOD.md](docs/METHOD.md). In short:

- **Sound descriptors are folklore, not measurements.** They are recorded because they explain a part's reputation. Measured data (noise, THD, slew) comes from datasheets and measurement sites.
- **Every silicon-change claim carries a confidence level and sources.** "High" means a vendor document (datasheet revision history, PCN) or several independent solid reports; "low" means forum lore.
- **Datasheet URLs** were collected from search results. Vendor URLs change, so the fetcher also tries the Internet Archive. `verification.unverified_urls` lists links the fact-checker could not confirm.

Corrections and additions are welcome: edit `data/opamps.json`, regenerate the docs, and open a PR with sources.
