# AD847 / AD827

**High-speed bipolar video op-amp, single/dual** · Analog Devices (c. 1990–present) · Tier C (niche / baseline)

*ADI's 50 MHz, 300 V/µs video op-amp: a 1990s CD-player and DAC upgrade favourite for dynamic sound and strong bass.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | Supply range | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 nV/√Hz | 50 MHz unity-gain bandwidth | 300 V/µs | ±5 V to ±15 V specified (9–36 V total) | AD847 4.8 mA, AD827 5.25 mA per amp |

## Why it's rated

A 1990s cult swap: video amps fitted into CD-player outputs, DAC I/V stages and headphone amps. The rec.audio FAQ called them 'the better op amps for audio'. Soomal pairs the AD847 with the AD797 for big dynamics and bass. Others hear the AD827 as warm, sweet, sharp in attack, sometimes thin in bass. No measurements found.

**Described as:** warm, a bit too sweet, sharp attack, huge dynamics (动态大), powerful bass (低频气势十足), both ends lifted (两头翘), forward vocals, clean highs, slightly coarse

**Used in:** CD-player analog output stage upgrades, DAC I/V conversion and output filters, Headphone amplifiers and preamps, Op-amp rolling, e.g. Fosi ZA3, Video and line drivers, ADC buffers, cable driving

## Watch out

- Bipolar input with about 3 µA bias: DC offset rises with high or unmatched source impedances.
- A 50 MHz part in sockets needs tight decoupling and short leads.
- About 5 mA per amp at ±5 V to ±15 V, more than NE5532-class parts.
- SMD AD827 is 16-lead SO (R-16); only the DIP-8 drops into standard dual sockets.
- Some find it too sweet or loose in bass; one Japanese test reported reduced gain below 200 Hz.
- **Counterfeits:** No specific reports. 'Fever Dual Op Amp' marketplace listings and old non-Z JN stock are likely remark targets; buy authorized. Genuine SMD AD827 is 16-lead SO only, so SOIC-8 'AD827' is a remark. 'Replace AD827' modules aren't AD827s.
