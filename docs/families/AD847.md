# AD847 / AD827 (single / dual high-speed bipolar video op amp)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*ADI's 50 MHz, 300 V/us video op amp. In the 1990s it became a favourite CD-player and DAC upgrade, praised for fast, dynamic sound with strong bass.*

**Technology:** A bipolar, voltage-feedback, high-speed op amp (50 MHz unity-gain bandwidth, 300 V/us). It is internally compensated to be stable at unity gain and is rated to drive unlimited capacitive load. ADI's SPICE macro-model uses an NPN differential input pair. According to ADI's electronics textbook, the AD848 (stable at G>=5) and AD849 (stable at G>=25) are basically the same design with different internal compensation. The AD827 is the dual version of the AD847. The fab process name (complementary bipolar or otherwise) was not verified this run.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche-cult op amp for 1990s-era swaps. The AD847/AD827 were ADI video amplifiers that audiophiles fitted into CD-player output stages, DAC I/V and filter stages and headphone amps. The rec.audio FAQ listed the AD847/AD827 among 'the better op amps for audio'. Chinese reviewers (Soomal) group the AD847 with the AD797 as famous for big dynamics, a 'grand' sound and powerful bass. Western and Japanese listeners describe the AD827 as warm and slightly sweet, with a sharp attack, forward vocals and clean highs; some find it a little coarse or thin in the bass. It is still named as a rolling option: Fosi Audio lists the AD827 as compatible with the ZA3. No Audio Science Review or Samuel Groner measurements were found this run.

**How people describe the sound:** warm, a bit too sweet (AD827, Audio Asylum), sharp attack, huge dynamics / grand sound (Soomal: 动态大，声音大气), powerful bass (低频气势十足), ADI 'both ends lifted' tonal tilt (两头翘, Soomal), vocals forward, clean highs, slightly coarse (Japanese reviews), fast, clean square-wave response without overshoot

**Typical uses:** CD-player analog output stage upgrades, DAC I/V conversion and output filter stages, headphone amplifiers and preamps, op-amp rolling in consumer gear (e.g. Fosi ZA3), original design use: video and line drivers, cable driving (unlimited capacitive load)

**Caveats:**

- Bipolar input with microamp-level bias current (about 3 uA per the ATE-25 table): DC offset rises with high-impedance or unmatched source resistances. Check DC-coupled stages.
- A 50 MHz part in sockets or adapters needs tight supply decoupling and short leads. This is general high-speed practice, not a documented AD827-specific failure.
- Needs at least about 9 V total supply and draws about 5 mA per amp, more than audio parts such as the NE5532-class alternatives.
- The AD827 SOIC appears to be a 16-lead package (AD827JRZ-16), so only the DIP-8 version is a drop-in for standard dual sockets.
- Some listeners find it too sweet, or light and loose in the bass. One Japanese headphone-amp test reported reduced gain below about 200 Hz and a 'sparse' impression (anecdotal).

## Counterfeits

No AD847- or AD827-specific counterfeit reports were found this run. The risk profile matches other discontinued-looking audio favourites. Marketplace listings such as '2pieces AD827 AD827JN AD827JNZ DIP-8 Fever Dual Op Amp' on Amazon, eBay and AliExpress come from unknown sellers, and older non-Z 'JN' stock is a typical remarking target. Buy from ADI or authorised distributors (Mouser, Digi-Key). ADI's AD827 surface-mount part appears to be a 16-lead SOIC (AD827JRZ-16), so an 'AD827' in SOIC-8 should be treated as suspect until confirmed against the datasheet. Discrete modules marketed as 'replace AD827' are not AD827s. Low confidence: this advice is inferred and not taken from documented fake cases.

## Related parts and alternatives

AD826 (ADI dual, 50 MHz / 350 V/us, unlimited capacitive load, 5-36 V; the closest sibling and preferred over the AD827 in an Audio Asylum shootout), AD817 (ADI single equivalent of the AD826), AD848 / AD849 (same design as the AD847 with lighter compensation; stable only at G>=5 or G>=25, so not drop-ins for unity-gain audio stages), LM4562 / LME49720 (modern low-distortion bipolar audio dual), OPA1612 / OPA1602 (modern low-noise bipolar audio dual), AD8066 (ADI high-speed FET-input dual popular with rollers)

## Open questions

- No datasheet revision letters or dates were observed for either part, and the ADI revision histories were not read. The session hit its WebSearch limit (200/200) before any query ran. Next step: search analog.com for 'AD847 Rev.' and 'AD827 Rev.', and check datasheet mirrors (alldatasheet, datasheetarchive) for old revisions.
- Lifecycle is unresolved: the discovery hint asked whether the AD827 is obsolete. Mouser pages exist for AD827JNZ and AD827JRZ-16 (scrape date unknown), but ADI's Production/NRND/Obsolete status was not verified.
- No PCN was found for a fab transfer, die change or process change. None is known, but that has not been confirmed.
- The archive_seed_urls are inferred from ADI URL patterns: the uppercase AD847.pdf and AD827.pdf under /media/en/, and the /static/imported-files/ and /UploadedFiles/ legacy paths. Only the lowercase ad847.pdf datasheet URL and ad847.cir were actually seen. The /static/imported-files/ pattern was seen for other ADI parts, such as the OP270.
- The column mapping of the ADI 'Ask the Applications Engineer-25' table was inferred (e_n, i_n, Vos, Ib, supply, Iq per amp). Check it against the datasheet, and get the THD, output current and noise test frequency.
- The AD827 SOIC being 16-lead only (AD827JR / AD827JRZ-16) comes from orderable suffixes and needs checking against the datasheet package section.
- Grade letters: the SPICE models confirm J, A and S grades. The package codes for AD827AQ and any AD827 LCC or /883 parts are not verified. The rec.audio FAQ mentions an 'AD827LD', which may be illustrative rather than a real orderable.
- The process name (junction-isolated complementary bipolar?) is not verified.
