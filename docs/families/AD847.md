# AD847 / AD827 (single / dual high-speed bipolar video op amp)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*ADI's 50 MHz, 300 V/us video op amp. In the 1990s it became a favourite CD-player and DAC upgrade, praised for fast, dynamic sound with strong bass.*

**Technology:** A bipolar, voltage-feedback, high-speed op amp (50 MHz unity-gain bandwidth, 300 V/us). It is internally compensated to be stable at unity gain and is rated to drive unlimited capacitive load. ADI's SPICE macro-model uses an NPN differential input pair. According to ADI's electronics textbook, the AD848 (stable at G>=5) and AD849 (stable at G>=25) are basically the same design with different internal compensation. ADI's AD827 data sheet calls it the dual version of the 'industry-standard AD847'. The fab process name (complementary bipolar or otherwise) was not verified this run.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche-cult op amp for 1990s-era swaps. The AD847/AD827 were ADI video amplifiers that audiophiles fitted into CD-player output stages, DAC I/V and filter stages and headphone amps. The rec.audio FAQ listed the AD847/AD827 among 'the better op amps for audio'. Chinese reviewers (Soomal) group the AD847 with the AD797 as famous for big dynamics, a 'grand' sound and powerful bass. Western and Japanese listeners describe the AD827 as warm and slightly sweet, with a sharp attack, forward vocals and clean highs; some find it a little coarse or thin in the bass. It is still named as a rolling option: Fosi Audio lists the AD827 as compatible with the ZA3. All of these are subjective, anecdotal listening reports. No Audio Science Review or Samuel Groner measurements were found.

**How people describe the sound:** warm, a bit too sweet (AD827, Audio Asylum), sharp attack, huge dynamics / grand sound (Soomal: 动态大，声音大气), powerful bass (低频气势十足), ADI 'both ends lifted' tonal tilt (两头翘, Soomal), vocals forward, clean highs, slightly coarse (Japanese reviews), fast, clean square-wave response without overshoot

**Typical uses:** CD-player analog output stage upgrades, DAC I/V conversion and output filter stages, headphone amplifiers and preamps, op-amp rolling in consumer gear (e.g. Fosi ZA3), original design use: video and line drivers, flash-ADC input buffers, cable driving (unlimited capacitive load)

**Caveats:**

- Bipolar input with microamp-level bias current (about 3 uA per the ATE-25 table): DC offset rises with high-impedance or unmatched source resistances. Check DC-coupled stages. The AD827's offset is specified at 2 mV max.
- A 50 MHz part in sockets or adapters needs tight supply decoupling and short leads. This is general high-speed practice, not a documented AD827-specific failure.
- It is specified from +/-5 V to +/-15 V and draws about 5 mA per amp, more than audio parts such as the NE5532-class alternatives.
- The AD827 surface-mount version is a 16-lead SO (R-16: AD827JR-16 / AD827JRZ-16), so only the DIP-8 version is a drop-in for standard dual sockets. The non-Z AD827JR-16 is obsolete at Digi-Key.
- Some listeners find it too sweet, or light and loose in the bass. One Japanese headphone-amp test reported reduced gain below about 200 Hz and a 'sparse' impression (anecdotal).

## Counterfeits

No AD847- or AD827-specific counterfeit reports were found. Marketplace listings such as '2pieces AD827 AD827JN AD827JNZ DIP-8 Fever Dual Op Amp' on Amazon, eBay and AliExpress come from unknown sellers, and older non-Z 'JN' stock is a typical remarking target. Buy from ADI or authorised distributors (Digi-Key, Mouser, Arrow still listed AD827JNZ in 2026). The AD827 data sheet ordering guide confirms that the only surface-mount AD827 is a 16-lead plastic SO (R-16: AD827JR-16, now obsolete at Digi-Key, and AD827JRZ-16). Any 'AD827' in SOIC-8 is therefore not a genuine ADI package and should be treated as a remark. Discrete modules marketed as 'replace AD827' are not AD827s. Beyond the package fact, this advice is inferred (low confidence).
