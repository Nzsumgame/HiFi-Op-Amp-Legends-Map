# LME49713

**Bipolar current-feedback audio op-amp, single** · National Semiconductor (2007–2011) → Texas Instruments (2011–2017) · Tier B (widely praised)

*Current-feedback single: 1.9 nV/√Hz, 0.00036% THD+N, 1900 V/µs, ±100 mA. A quiet, muscular headphone and buffer stage.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | THD+N | Supply range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.9 nV/√Hz | 132 MHz bandwidth (CFB: set by feedback resistor) | ±1900 V/µs | 0.00036% | ±5 V to ±18 V |

## Why it's rated

A niche enthusiast favourite. Head-Fi titled a thread 'LME49713, could be a killer opamp'. A diyAudio poster who worked on the chip called it the best-sounding op-amp on National's LME process, preferred to voltage-feedback parts in listening tests. A Head-Fi user found it 'dead silent and with real drive' into 150 Ω. Fast and tricky to stabilise.

**Described as:** dead silent, real drive, effortless load driving

**Used in:** Headphone amp or output buffer (drives 150 Ω), DAC output and I/V stages on SOIC-to-DIP adapters, Make-up gain for passive summing mixers (RMS Folcrom)

## Watch out

- Single, SOIC-8 or TO-99 only: needs a DIP-8 adapter; not a drop-in for dual NE5532/LME49720.
- Current feedback: feedback resistor sets bandwidth and stability; no capacitor across it. VFB circuits may oscillate.
- diyAudio: ran hot or oscillated in a Sony CDP-557ESD's NE5534 sockets, where an AD844 worked.
- About 1.8 µA input bias current can cause large offsets with high-value bias resistors.
- Obsolete since the 2017 last-time buy; only old stock and brokers remain.
- **Counterfeits:** No LME49713-specific fakes reported, but sibling LME49720/LM4562 fakes are well documented on diyAudio. Obsolete since 2017, so marketplace SOIC/TO-99 units carry remark risk. Check marking, date code and can finish; bench-test bias current, slew and distortion.

## Silicon changes

None documented. Compare datasheet revisions before assuming old and new parts are identical.
