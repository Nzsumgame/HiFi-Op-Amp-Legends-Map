# OP285 (ADI dual 9 MHz precision 'Butler' bipolar/JFET audio op-amp)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** Bipolar classics

*The laser-trimmed precision sibling of the OP275 (same Butler bipolar/JFET input, 9 MHz, 22 V/us). CN upgrade guides and Head-Fi users praise its sweet, resolving midrange.*

**Technology:** Dual voltage-feedback op-amp with ADI's 'Butler Amplifier' input stage, which pairs bipolar transistors (for accuracy and low noise) with JFETs (for speed). Its headline specs (9 MHz GBW, 22 V/us, 6 nV/rtHz, 0.0006% THD, 5 mA) match the OP275's. What sets it apart is a laser-trimmed input offset guaranteed below 250 uV. It is not faster than the OP275. Whether the two share a die is unconfirmed.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded: Unclassified).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A long-standing Chinese upgrade favourite that is almost always mentioned with the OP275. Reprinted CN op-amp guides (CSDN, ZOL, PCPOP, 21ic) credit OP275/OP285 with a soft, 'tube-like' midrange, intimate vocals and high resolution. One CN comparison says the OP285G sounds like the OP275G but with more colour saturation and a wider soundstage, and slightly less bass. Car-audio shops (sounting.com) list it as a 'fever-grade' op-amp. Head-Fi's 'I like the OP285' thread reports good resolution, a refined but resolving treble, and a sound that is 'right' overall, but posters say it needs care in the circuit. diyAudio's best-sounding-dual thread suggests it as a budget alternative to try alongside the OPA627/OPA637. All of this is enthusiast opinion, not measurement.

**How people describe the sound:** soft, 'tube-like' midrange (CN guides), intimate vocals (CN guides), high resolution / clear outlines, refined but resolving treble (Head-Fi), more colour saturation and wider soundstage than OP275 (one CN comparison), slightly less bass than OP275 (one CN comparison)

**Typical uses:** op-amp rolling in DIY and Chinese DACs, preamps and headphone amps, car-audio head-unit / DSP op-amp upgrades, I/V and output stages as a precision alternative to the OP275

**Caveats:**

- The praise mostly comes from reprinted CN guides and forum anecdotes, not measured comparisons.
- Head-Fi posters say it needs careful circuit conditions (decoupling, loading) to sound its best.
- The DIP-8 OP285GP was reportedly obsolete by about 2002. Socketed rolling today usually means SOIC OP285GSZ on an adapter, old stock or remarked parts.
- Its headline specs are the same as the OP275's. Claims that it is 'faster' than the OP275 are wrong.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Unclassified: Analog Devices, 2017 (PDN 17_0038 issued as Rev. -, later revised to Rev.

Product discontinuance of the non-RoHS OP285GS variants, with OP285GSZ named as the pin-compatible replacement. The sources found document no die change. This is a part-number/lead-finish transition, not a case of the same part number with different silicon.

- **When:** 2017 (PDN 17_0038 issued as Rev. -, later revised to Rev. B and Rev. C)
- **Affected:** OP285GS, OP285GS-REEL, OP285GS-REEL7
- **How to tell old from new:** Non-Z SOIC part numbers are discontinued. The replacement carries the Z suffix: OP285GSZ (and its -REEL/-REEL7 versions).
- **Audio impact:** None expected. No electrical or die change is documented.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - pin-compatible Z-suffix replacement named in the PDN
- **Confidence:** medium

## Counterfeits

No ADI counterfeit advisory was found. Marketplace listings (e.g. Amazon '5PCS OP285G OP285 OP285GS OP285GSZ OP285GSZ-REEL SOP8') lump several part numbers into one product, which is a common sign of remarked or mixed stock. The DIP-8 OP285GP is reportedly obsolete since about 2002, so fresh-looking DIP 'OP285GP' parts sold for op-amp rolling are likely old stock or remarked. Buy OP285GSZ through authorized distributors such as Arrow.

## Verification notes

Status: **verified-with-corrections**

**Refuted or corrected during verification:**

- The draft called the OP285 the 'faster sibling of the OP275 (about 9 MHz GBW)'. This is refuted: the OP285's 9 MHz / 22 V/us match the OP275's (the OP275 is also listed as 9 MHz, e.g. https://uk.rs-online.com/web/p/op-amps/1832628). The OP285's distinction is its trimmed 250 uV max offset.

**Corrections applied:**

- Rewrote the technology text: the OP285 is the precision (laser-trimmed offset) sibling of the OP275, not a faster one. The 'separate die, not a grade' claim is unconfirmed and was moved to open_questions.
- Set channels to 2 for all part numbers and filled in statuses. Added OP285GS-REEL/-REEL7 and OP285GSZ/-REEL/-REEL7.
- Marked OP285GS and its reel versions as discontinued under ADI PDN 17_0038 (replacement OP285GSZ). Marked OP285GP (PDIP) obsolete, per a broker page.
- Added the current ADI datasheet (Rev. C), the product page, a RadioLocman Rev. C mirror and other third-party copies whose revisions are unknown.
- Added key specs from the datasheet: 9 MHz, 22 V/us, 6 nV/rtHz, 0.0006% THD, 250 uV max offset, 2 nA offset current, 5 mA supply current.
- Added a PDN 17_0038 entry as a part-number/lead-finish transition, with no documented die change.
- Manufacturer simplified to 'Analog Devices'. The PMI lineage is kept but marked unconfirmed.
