*ADI's 1995 JFET-input, rail-to-rail-output dual that runs from 3 V. A CMoy/portable-amp favourite; Tangent rated it punchier in the bass and more detailed than OPA132/134.*

| Slew rate | Supply range |
|---|---|
| 22 V/µs typ, 14 min at 5 V; 20 typ, 13 min at 3.3 V; 25 typ, 17 min at ±15 V | 3 V to 36 V single, ±1.5 V to ±18 V dual; absolute max 36 V |

## Why it's rated

A 2000s DIY headphone-amp favourite for battery-powered CMoy-style portables. Tangent (tangentsoft; also in Soomal's Chinese translation) rated it a bit punchier in the bass and more detailed than the OPA134/132. He considered it ideal for battery amps because it runs on 3 V with a rail-to-rail output, but warned it can be too aggressive with already forward headphones and that its roughly 15 mA output is low. Soomal says the AD823 'is also considered' warm with good dynamics; that is general reputation, not a Soomal listening test. Soomal reports that earlier Graham Slee SOLO versions used it, and it appears in the Colorfly C4 Pro and Hidizs AP100 DAPs (its role in the AP100 is unclear). No independent audio measurements (ASR, Samuel Groner) were checked.

**Described as:** more bass impact than OPA134/OPA132 (Tangent), more detailed than OPA134/OPA132 (Tangent), aggressive / forward (Tangent; Chinese translation: 冲), warm (偏暖; Soomal, citing general reputation), good dynamics (Soomal, citing general reputation), 'emotional', good with acoustic music (Head-Fi, per discovery notes; thread not verified)

**Used in:** Battery-powered CMoy / portable headphone amps (3 V single supply, rail-to-rail output), Graham Slee SOLO headphone amp, earlier versions per Soomal, DAP analog stage: Colorfly C4 Pro (Soomal teardown); Hidizs AP100 (Soomal teardown; role unconfirmed, either output filter or headphone driver), Op-amp rolling in DIY and commercial DAC/amps, ADI's own 3 V stereo headphone-driver application circuit

## Watch out

- Low output current: 15-17 mA linear, rated 0.5 V from the rails. Weak for low-impedance headphones without a buffer (Tangent).
- Offset is much higher on ±15 V (3.5 mV max vs 0.8 mV at 5 V).
- Input overvoltage: if an input can exceed +VS by more than 300 mV, or signal is applied with the supplies off, use a series resistor (1 kΩ withstands 10 V continuous).
- About 20° phase margin driving 470 pF as a follower; add a series output resistor for capacitive loads.
- PDIP exists only as the original AD823ANZ. The AD823A redesign is SOIC/MSOP only.
- New stock after PCN 26_0029 (2026) comes from a different wafer fab (Camas, WA). ADI states no fit/form/function change, but old and new stock are not guaranteed to be identical silicon.
- Can sound too aggressive with already forward headphones (Tangent).
- Reputation evidence is mostly subjective (Tangent, Chinese reviews, Head-Fi).
- **Counterfeits:** No AD823-specific counterfeit reports were found; forums were not searched for this. Soomal notes that the Graham Slee SOLO SRG II had its op-amp marking sanded off, which is not an AD823 counterfeit report. The following are inferences. (1) Leaded AD823AN appears to have been discontinued (PDN 10_0081, c. 2010) and is absent from the Rev. E ordering guide, so 'AD823AN' DIPs with recent date codes deserve scrutiny. (2) The AD823A exists only in SOIC/MSOP, so an 'AD823A' DIP is either a loosely described AD823AN/ANZ or a fake. (3) Listings confuse the original AD823AR/ARZ (A grade) with the 2012 AD823AARZ die; check the full order code and the datasheet number (D00901 vs D09439). (4) Genuine stock made after PCN 26_0029 (2026) comes from a different fab, so small differences between old and new genuine parts do not by themselves indicate fakes. Buy from authorized distributors.

## Silicon changes

### Fab / process transfer: Analog Devices, 2026 (PCN 26_0029 Rev. -; exact issue and implementation dates not confirmed)

ADI PCN 26_0029, 'Qualification of ADI Camas Wafer Fab CB Processes', moves Complementary Bipolar production to 'the existing qualified process at the Analog Devices Camas, WA Fab to ensure a reliable and continuous supply', with 'no expected impact to fit, form, function or reliability'. A web-search summary lists AD823ANZ and AD823ARZ among the affected parts. The part number stays the same while the fab changes. No die-layout change is stated.

- **Affected:** AD823ANZ, AD823ARZ
- **How to tell:** Not confirmed, because the PCN's identification section was not read. ADI normally separates old and new stock by date code or lot after the implementation date. The part number, marking scheme and data sheet (Rev. E) are unchanged.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. ADI claims no change to fit, form or function, and the data-sheet limits are unchanged. No listening or measurement comparisons of stock made before and after the transfer exist. Anyone who prizes older AD823ANZ stock should record date codes.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - ADI states no fit/form/function impact and the data sheet is unchanged. The risk matters mainly for matched pairs or comparisons of old and new stock.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Wafer fab | previous ADI fab (not named in the sources seen) | ADI Camas, WA fab (existing qualified CB process) |

- **Renumbering / successor** · Analog Devices, 2012 (AD823A data sheet Rev. A 5/12, Rev. B 6/12): ADI created a materially different die under a near-identical name, and it sits alongside the original rather than replacing it. The AD823A uses the XFCB dielectrically isolated complementary-bipolar process with a two-stage folded-cascode design. The original AD823 uses the CB process with a nested integrator. Revisions 0 to E of the original AD823 data sheet show only editorial, abs-max, figure and ordering changes. Pinout and supply range match, but specs, output drive and packages differ.
