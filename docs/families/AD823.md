# AD823 (dual 16 MHz JFET-input, rail-to-rail output) + AD823A (2012 XFCB redesign)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** JFET-input

*ADI's 1995 JFET-input, rail-to-rail-output dual that runs from 3 V. A CMoy/portable-amp favourite, rated for more bass impact and detail than OPA132/134 (Tangent).*

**Technology:** Original AD823: N-channel JFET input stage on ADI's complementary bipolar (CB) process, where PNP and NPN fT is 600-800 MHz. Uses a nested-integrator topology with Miller compensation (C1, C5) and a complementary common-emitter rail-to-rail output stage. Offset and drift are set by thin-film trimming. The input range extends 0.2 V below -Vs, and there is no phase inversion with inputs at the supply rails. Supply: 3-36 V single or ±1.5 to ±18 V. AD823A (2012) is a different die with the same dual pinout. It is built on the XFCB process (dielectrically isolated complementary bipolar with JFETs and thin-film resistors), uses a two-stage design with a folded-cascode first stage, has laser-trimmed thin-film resistors and a rail-to-rail output.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A 2000s DIY headphone-amp favourite for battery-powered CMoy-style portables. Tangent (tangentsoft; confirmed through Soomal's Chinese translation) rated it slightly more impactful in the bass and more detailed than the OPA134/132, and ideal for battery amps because it runs on 3 V with rail-to-rail output. He also warned it can be too aggressive with already forward headphones, and that its roughly 15 mA output current is low against the roughly 40 mA typical of other op-amps. Soomal says the AD823 'is also considered' warm with good dynamics as a headphone amp; this reports general reputation, not a Soomal listening test. Soomal reports that earlier Graham Slee SOLO versions used it, and that it is fitted in the Colorfly C4 Pro and Hidizs AP100 DAPs (its role in the AP100 is unclear). No ASR, Samuel Groner or other independent measurements were checked in this pass.

**How people describe the sound:** more bass impact than OPA134/OPA132 (Tangent), more detailed than OPA134/OPA132 (Tangent), aggressive / forward (Tangent; Chinese translation: 冲), warm (偏暖; Soomal, citing general reputation), good dynamics (Soomal, citing general reputation), 'emotional', good with acoustic music (Head-Fi, per discovery notes; thread not verified)

**Typical uses:** Battery-powered CMoy / portable headphone amps (3 V single supply, rail-to-rail output), Graham Slee SOLO headphone amp, earlier versions per Soomal (the SRG II unit examined had its op-amp marking sanded off), DAP analog stage: Colorfly C4 Pro (Soomal teardown: 'the op-amp is the AD823'); Hidizs AP100 (Soomal teardown; Hidizs officially described the AD823 as the filter op-amp with OPA2613 driving the headphones, while Soomal's photo caption labels the AD823 as the headphone output, so its role is unconfirmed), Op-amp rolling in DIY and commercial DAC/amps (e.g. suggested as an alternative in spbkaizo's CMOY design), ADI's own 3 V stereo headphone-driver application circuit

**Caveats:**

- Low output current: 15-17 mA linear, rated 0.5 V from the rails. Tangent notes most op-amps are rated around 40 mA, so it is weak for low-impedance headphones without a buffer.
- Offset is much higher on ±15 V (3.5 mV max vs 0.8 mV at 5 V), which matters in DC-coupled stages.
- Input overvoltage: if an input can exceed +VS by more than 300 mV, or signal is applied with the supplies off, use a series resistor (1 kΩ withstands 10 V of continuous overvoltage). ADI says the part is damaged if left in that state for more than 10 s.
- About 20° phase margin driving 470 pF as a follower; add a series output resistor for capacitive loads or cables.
- PDIP is available only as the original AD823ANZ. The AD823A redesign is SOIC/MSOP only.
- Can sound too aggressive with already forward headphones or systems (Tangent).
- AD823A Rev. B warns to use caution at a 30 V supply because excessive output current may overheat the part.
- Reputation evidence is mostly subjective (Tangent, Chinese reviews, Head-Fi); no controlled measurements of audio performance were found.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Analog Devices: 2012 (AD823A datasheet Rev.

The original AD823's datasheet revisions show no die, process or fab change: Rev. 0 to E changes are format, unit typos, abs-max, figure, ordering and editorial only. Tables 1-3 values are numerically identical between Rev. A and Rev. E, and no PCN was found. ADI did create a materially different die under a near-identical name. The AD823A uses the XFCB dielectrically isolated complementary-bipolar process with a two-stage design (folded-cascode first stage). The original uses the CB process with a nested-integrator design. Pinout and supply range match, but specs, drive and package options differ.

- **When:** 2012 (AD823A datasheet Rev. A 5/12, Rev. B 6/12)
- **Affected:** AD823AARZ, AD823AARZ-RL, AD823AARZ-R7, AD823AARMZ, AD823AARMZ-R7
- **How to tell old from new:** Separate part 'AD823A'. Order codes carry a double A (AD823AARZ vs the original AD823ARZ). Packages are SOIC_N and MSOP only, with MSOP branding H34. Datasheet doc D09439 is titled 'Wide Supply Dual, 17 MHz...'; the original is D00901 'Dual, 16 MHz...'. Hazard: the original AD823 datasheet (Rev. A) labels its A-grade spec column 'AD823A', and original SOIC codes are AD823AR/ARZ, so a listing that just says 'AD823A' is ambiguous. SOIC top-marking differences are unverified.
- **Audio impact:** No listening or measurement reports for the AD823A were found. On paper it fixes the AD823's main headphone weakness with about 2.5x the linear output current. It also has lower noise, higher slew and closed-loop output impedance below 0.01 Ω (versus below 0.2 Ω). Do not assume the praised AD823 sound carries over: the community reputation comes from the original CB-process part, mostly the PDIP AD823AN/ANZ.
- **Drop-in risk:** Medium. Same dual SOIC pinout and 3-36 V supply, but there is no DIP version, so DIP rollers need an adapter. Compensation and cap-load behaviour differ (the front page quotes 470 pF at 25% overshoot), input common-mode abs max is ±VS ± 0.7 V, and Rev. B warns of overheating from output current at a 30 V supply.
- **Confidence:** high

## Counterfeits

No AD823-specific counterfeit reports were found; web search was unavailable, so forums were not checked. Soomal notes that the Graham Slee SOLO SRG II had its op-amp marking sanded off, possibly because the SOLO was often cloned; this is not an AD823 counterfeit report. The following are inferences, not documented reports. (1) Leaded AD823AN is absent from the Rev. E (11/11) ordering guide, so 'AD823AN' DIPs with recent-looking date codes deserve scrutiny. (2) The AD823A exists only in SOIC/MSOP, so any 'AD823A' DIP is either an original AD823AN/ANZ described loosely or a fake. (3) Marketplace listings often confuse the original AD823AR/ARZ (A grade) with the 2012 AD823AARZ die; check the full order code and the datasheet doc number (D00901 vs D09439). Buy from authorized distributors.

- What role does the AD823 play in the Hidizs AP100: output low-pass filter (Hidizs' claim, per Soomal) or headphone driver (Soomal's photo caption)?
- The contents and measurements of the Head-Fi op-amp round-up thread (870832), the Head-Fi AD823/OPA2132/AD8066 review (242125) and the diyAudio AD823-vs-OPA2132 thread were not verified.
- The AD823 datasheet's Theory of Operation quotes a '1 mV maximum' offset, while Table 1 gives 0.8 mV max at 5 V: a minor internal inconsistency present in both Rev. A and Rev. E.
- All http://www.analog.com/static/imported-files/..., /UploadedFiles/... and /en/products/... URLs in archive_seed_urls are inferred. The static/imported-files/data_sheets/<PART>.pdf pattern is observed for AD8235.
