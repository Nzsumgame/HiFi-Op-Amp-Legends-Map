# AD823 (dual 16 MHz JFET-input, rail-to-rail output) + AD823A (2012 XFCB redesign)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** JFET-input

*ADI's 1995 JFET-input, rail-to-rail-output dual that runs from 3 V. A CMoy/portable-amp favourite; Tangent rated it punchier in the bass and more detailed than OPA132/134.*

**Technology:** Original AD823: N-channel JFET input stage on ADI's complementary bipolar (CB) process (PNP/NPN fT 600-800 MHz). Nested-integrator topology with Miller compensation and a complementary common-emitter rail-to-rail output stage. Offset and drift are thin-film trimmed. Input range extends 0.2 V below -Vs, with no phase inversion at the rails. Supply 3-36 V single or ±1.5 to ±18 V. ADI PCN 26_0029 (2026) moves the listed AD823 order codes to ADI's Camas, WA wafer fab, with no datasheet change. AD823A (2012) is a different die with the same dual pinout: XFCB process (dielectrically isolated complementary bipolar with JFETs and thin-film resistors), two-stage design with a folded-cascode first stage, laser-trimmed, rail-to-rail output.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (2 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A 2000s DIY headphone-amp favourite for battery-powered CMoy-style portables. Tangent (tangentsoft; also in Soomal's Chinese translation) rated it a bit punchier in the bass and more detailed than the OPA134/132. He considered it ideal for battery amps because it runs on 3 V with a rail-to-rail output, but warned it can be too aggressive with already forward headphones and that its roughly 15 mA output is low. Soomal says the AD823 'is also considered' warm with good dynamics; that is general reputation, not a Soomal listening test. Soomal reports that earlier Graham Slee SOLO versions used it, and it appears in the Colorfly C4 Pro and Hidizs AP100 DAPs (its role in the AP100 is unclear). No independent audio measurements (ASR, Samuel Groner) were checked.

**How people describe the sound:** more bass impact than OPA134/OPA132 (Tangent), more detailed than OPA134/OPA132 (Tangent), aggressive / forward (Tangent; Chinese translation: 冲), warm (偏暖; Soomal, citing general reputation), good dynamics (Soomal, citing general reputation), 'emotional', good with acoustic music (Head-Fi, per discovery notes; thread not verified)

**Typical uses:** Battery-powered CMoy / portable headphone amps (3 V single supply, rail-to-rail output), Graham Slee SOLO headphone amp, earlier versions per Soomal, DAP analog stage: Colorfly C4 Pro (Soomal teardown); Hidizs AP100 (Soomal teardown; role unconfirmed, either output filter or headphone driver), Op-amp rolling in DIY and commercial DAC/amps, ADI's own 3 V stereo headphone-driver application circuit

**Caveats:**

- Low output current: 15-17 mA linear, rated 0.5 V from the rails. Weak for low-impedance headphones without a buffer (Tangent).
- Offset is much higher on ±15 V (3.5 mV max vs 0.8 mV at 5 V).
- Input overvoltage: if an input can exceed +VS by more than 300 mV, or signal is applied with the supplies off, use a series resistor (1 kΩ withstands 10 V continuous).
- About 20° phase margin driving 470 pF as a follower; add a series output resistor for capacitive loads.
- PDIP exists only as the original AD823ANZ. The AD823A redesign is SOIC/MSOP only.
- New stock after PCN 26_0029 (2026) comes from a different wafer fab (Camas, WA). ADI states no fit/form/function change, but old and new stock are not guaranteed to be identical silicon.
- Can sound too aggressive with already forward headphones (Tangent).
- Reputation evidence is mostly subjective (Tangent, Chinese reviews, Head-Fi).

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Analog Devices: 2026 (PCN 26_0029 Rev. -; exact issue and implementation…

ADI PCN 26_0029 moves production to 'the existing qualified process at the Analog Devices Camas, WA Fab to ensure a reliable and continuous supply', with 'no expected impact to fit, form, function or reliability'. A web-search summary lists AD823ANZ and AD823ARZ among the affected parts. This is a same-part-number, different-fab hazard. Whether the die layout itself changes was not visible.

- **When:** 2026 (PCN 26_0029 Rev. -; exact issue and implementation dates not confirmed)
- **Affected:** AD823ANZ, AD823ARZ
- **How to tell old from new:** Not confirmed. The PCN's identification and date-code section was not read. ADI PCNs usually separate old and new stock by date code or lot after the implementation date. Part number, marking scheme and datasheet (Rev. E) stay the same.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. ADI claims no fit, form or function change and the datasheet limits are unchanged. There are no listening or measurement comparisons of pre- and post-transfer stock. Rollers who prize older AD823ANZ stock should record date codes.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - ADI states no fit/form/function impact and the datasheet is unchanged. The risk is mainly for matched-pair or old-vs-new comparisons.
- **Confidence:** medium

### 2. Analog Devices: 2012 (AD823A datasheet Rev.

The original AD823 datasheet revisions (0 to E) show only format, unit-typo, abs-max, figure, ordering and editorial changes; its only known production change is the 2026 fab transfer (see the separate entry). ADI separately created a materially different die under a near-identical name. The AD823A uses the XFCB dielectrically isolated complementary-bipolar process with a two-stage folded-cascode design, while the original uses the CB process with a nested integrator. Pinout and supply range match; specs, drive and packages differ.

- **When:** 2012 (AD823A datasheet Rev. A 5/12, Rev. B 6/12)
- **Affected:** AD823AARZ, AD823AARZ-RL, AD823AARZ-R7, AD823AARMZ, AD823AARMZ-R7
- **How to tell old from new:** Separate part 'AD823A' with double-A order codes (AD823AARZ vs the original AD823ARZ). SOIC_N and MSOP only; MSOP branding H34. Datasheet D09439 'Wide Supply Dual, 17 MHz...' vs the original D00901 'Dual, 16 MHz...'. Hazard: the original AD823 datasheet labels its A-grade column 'AD823A' and original SOIC codes are AD823AR/ARZ, so a listing that just says 'AD823A' is ambiguous. SOIC top-marking differences are unverified.
- **Audio impact:** No listening or measurement reports for the AD823A were found. On paper it fixes the AD823's main headphone weakness with about 2.5x the linear output current, and it adds lower noise, higher slew and lower closed-loop Zout. The praised AD823 sound comes from the original CB part (mostly PDIP AD823AN/ANZ) and should not be assumed to carry over.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium - same dual SOIC pinout and 3-36 V supply, but there is no DIP (DIP rollers need an adapter). Compensation and cap-load behaviour differ, input CM abs max is ±VS ± 0.7 V, and Rev. B warns of overheating at a 30 V supply.
- **Confidence:** high

## Counterfeits

No AD823-specific counterfeit reports were found; forums were not searched for this. Soomal notes that the Graham Slee SOLO SRG II had its op-amp marking sanded off, which is not an AD823 counterfeit report. The following are inferences. (1) Leaded AD823AN appears to have been discontinued (PDN 10_0081, c. 2010) and is absent from the Rev. E ordering guide, so 'AD823AN' DIPs with recent date codes deserve scrutiny. (2) The AD823A exists only in SOIC/MSOP, so an 'AD823A' DIP is either a loosely described AD823AN/ANZ or a fake. (3) Listings confuse the original AD823AR/ARZ (A grade) with the 2012 AD823AARZ die; check the full order code and the datasheet number (D00901 vs D09439). (4) Genuine stock made after PCN 26_0029 (2026) comes from a different fab, so small differences between old and new genuine parts do not by themselves indicate fakes. Buy from authorized distributors.

## Related parts and alternatives

AD823A (ADI, 2012 XFCB redesign: same dual pinout, 3-36 V, 40 mA output; SOIC/MSOP only), AD8512 (ADI JFET dual; Tangent: sounds almost the same as the AD823, with more output current and lower supply current), AD8620 / AD8610 (ADI precision JFET; Tangent: like the AD823 without some of its aggressiveness), AD8066 / AD8065 (ADI FET-input; frequent AD823 comparison on Head-Fi), OPA2132 / OPA2134 (TI/Burr-Brown FET-input audio duals; the usual reference comparison)

## Open questions

- PCN 26_0029: issue date, implementation date, date-code or lot identification, the previous fab, whether the reel variants (-RL/-R7) and AD823A parts are included, and whether the die layout changes. Only search-result summaries were seen.
- PDN 10_0081 Rev. B and PDN 16_0062: exact discontinued order codes, replacements and last-time-buy dates were read from search summaries only. The inferred mapping is AD823AN to AD823ANZ, and AD823AR/-REEL/-REEL7 to AD823ARZ/-RL/-R7.
- No copies were confirmed of AD823 Rev. 0 (5/95), B (2/07), C (5/10) or D (6/10), or of AD823A Rev. A (5/12). The datasheetq '2007' entry and the datasheet4u/datasheetcatalog copies are candidates that have not been checked.
- Lifecycle status of AD823AARZ/AARMZ is unverified; AD823ANZ and AD823ARZ are presumed active because they appear in a 2026 PCN.
- Does ADI officially position the AD823A as the AD823's replacement?
- Top-marking differences between AD823 SOIC and AD823A SOIC are unknown.
- The Hidizs AP100's use of the AD823 (filter or headphone driver) is unresolved.
- The Head-Fi threads (242125, 870832) and the diyAudio AD823-vs-OPA2132 thread were not read.
- The AD823 Theory of Operation quotes a '1 mV maximum' offset, while Table 1 gives 0.8 mV max at 5 V (a minor inconsistency in Rev. A and Rev. E).
- The static/imported-files and UploadedFiles archive seeds are inferred patterns; the plain UploadedFiles/Data_Sheets/AD823.pdf seed is probably wrong.
