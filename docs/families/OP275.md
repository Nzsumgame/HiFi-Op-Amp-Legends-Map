# OP275 (dual 'Butler' bipolar/JFET audio op-amp, Analog Devices)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** Bipolar classics

*ADI's audio dual with its 'Butler' bipolar+JFET input. China's classic 'warm, tube-like' op-amp and Musiland's house part; ADI says music fans rate it.*

**Technology:** Dual voltage-feedback op-amp with ADI's 'Butler Amplifier' input stage, which combines bipolar and JFET transistors. It is unity-gain stable. ADI's claim, repeated on a 1999 German product page and in EEVblog #738, is that it was the first op-amp with a Butler front-end. The design came from the PMI (Precision Monolithics) group: the SPICE macro-models are credited 'JCB / PMI' and carry an Analog Devices copyright of 1992 and 1995. Analog Devices is the only maker; there are no second sources.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

OP275 was ADI's audio-targeted dual. ADI's AN-1429 (Rev. 0, by David Guo) says ADI op-amps 'are reputable with music fans, like the AD797, OP275, AD8620, and ADA4627-1'. In the Chinese community (Soomal) it is the textbook 'warm, tube-like' op-amp. It was Musiland's signature part, confirmed in Soomal teardowns and reviews: Long March II (2007), MD10 (2007), Monitor 01 US and 02 US (2009), 01 PS (2010, with TPA6120), MD11 (2010), HP11 (2010/11, ahead of LME49600 buffers), MD30 (2011) and MD12 (2014, marked OP275G). In 2009 it cost nearly 20 RMB, about 20x an NE5532. Soomal's RMAA plots of OP275 products show a spray of low-level harmonics that do not affect the score. Soomal links these to the warm sound but says it has no direct proof, and it notes that some OP275 cards sounded neutral. Other confirmed OEM uses: Denon DVD-A1 (with NE5532 and OP37) and DVD-2900, the Yamaha DME32, the Apogee Mini-DAC, the HiFiMAN HM-602 filter stage, and the TempoTec DAII Plus headphone stage (with TPA6120A). Japanese hobbyist rankings (nabe, Zigsow) reportedly praise its balanced, 'straight' sound, but this was not verified this run.

**How people describe the sound:** warm (偏暖 / 暖色调), tube-like, valve-amp style (胆味 / 胆机风格), rich and mellow (醇厚柔和), musical (很有音乐味道), warmth without losing treble (Soomal, Monitor 01 US), 'straight' or balanced (Japanese reviews; unverified this run)

**Typical uses:** DAC post-filter and output stage in Chinese USB DACs and sound cards (Musiland Monitor and MD series), headphone-amp gain stage in front of a current buffer (Musiland HP11 with LME49600; Monitor 01 PS and TempoTec DAII Plus with TPA6120), analog stages of DVD-Audio/SACD players (Denon DVD-A1, DVD-2900), pro-audio DSP and converters (Yamaha DME32, Apogee Mini-DAC), DAP analog filter (HiFiMAN HM-602), op-amp rolling in socketed sound cards and motherboards (Soomal tried it on the Gigabyte G1.Sniper A88X), guitar pedals (a forum trace of MXR MC402 Rev E, via third-party notes)

**Caveats:**

- Needs at least ±4.5 V (9 V total) supplies, so it does not suit low-voltage single-cell circuits.
- Weak as a headphone driver on its own: Soomal says one OP275 cannot drive a 300 Ω HD580 with authority, so products pair it with buffers.
- Measurements show a series of low-level harmonics (Soomal RMAA), so it is not a lowest-distortion choice by modern standards.
- The sound depends on the implementation: Soomal found some OP275 cards neutral.
- Expensive relative to NE5532/4580. Musiland used CLC2059 in the cheaper 01 Mini (2009) and MC33079 in 03 US and 03 PS (2011), but still used OP275G in the MD12 (2014).
- 'Warm/tube-like' is subjective community reputation; Soomal says it has no direct proof that the harmonics cause it.
- Counterfeits are rumoured, but no specific report was confirmed.

## Counterfeits

No concrete counterfeit report was found. A full-text search of all 29 Soomal posts that mention OP275 found no fake or remarked-part report; the only 'sanded chips' note concerned other ICs in the Apogee Mini-DAC. OP275 was sold in China as a premium upgrade op-amp (nearly 20 RMB each in 2009, about 20x an NE5532), which is the usual profile for remarked parts. Genuine parts are marked 'OP275G' on top (Soomal MD12 photo; EEVblog #738 reads '275G'). Buy OP275GPZ (DIP) or OP275GSZ (SOIC) from authorized distributors. A heuristic, not from a vendor: a relabelled NE5532/4558-class die would measure differently, so compare supply current (about 2 to 2.5 mA per amplifier), slew rate (22 V/µs) and GBW (9 MHz) against the datasheet. The GP to GPZ and GS to GSZ suffix change is the RoHS package transition; no evidence of a die change was found.
