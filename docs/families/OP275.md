# OP275 (dual 'Butler' bipolar/JFET audio op-amp, Analog Devices)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** Bipolar classics

*ADI's audio dual with its 'Butler' bipolar+JFET input. China's classic 'warm, tube-like' op-amp and Musiland's house part; ADI says music fans rate it.*

**Technology:** Dual voltage-feedback op-amp with ADI's 'Butler Amplifier' input stage, which combines bipolar and JFET transistors. It is unity-gain stable. The datasheet says the design combines 'the accuracy and low noise performance of bipolar transistors, and the speed and sound quality of JFETs', and calls its 22 V/µs slew rate the fastest of any standard audio amplifier. ADI's claim that it was the first op-amp with a Butler front-end is repeated on a 1999 German product page and in EEVblog #738. The design came from the PMI (Precision Monolithics) group: the SPICE macro-models are credited 'JCB / PMI' and carry an Analog Devices copyright of 1992 and 1995. Analog Devices is the only maker; there are no second sources.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

OP275 was ADI's audio-targeted dual; its own datasheet pitches JFET 'sound quality'. ADI's AN-1429 (Rev. 0, David Guo) says ADI op-amps 'are reputable with music fans, like the AD797, OP275, AD8620, and ADA4627-1'. In the Chinese community (Soomal) it is the textbook 'warm, tube-like' op-amp and Musiland's signature part: Long March II (2007), MD10 (2007), Monitor 01 US and 02 US (2009), 01 PS (2010, with TPA6120), MD11 (2010), HP11 (2010/11, ahead of LME49600 buffers), MD30 (2011) and MD12 (2014, marked OP275G). In 2009 it cost nearly 20 RMB, about 20x an NE5532. Soomal's RMAA plots show a spray of low-level harmonics; Soomal links them to the warm sound but says it has no direct proof, and notes some OP275 cards sounded neutral. Other OEM uses: Denon DVD-A1 and DVD-2900, Yamaha DME32, Apogee Mini-DAC, HiFiMAN HM-602 filter stage, TempoTec DAII Plus. Japanese hobbyist rankings (nabe, Zigsow) reportedly praise a balanced, 'straight' sound; not verified.

**How people describe the sound:** warm (偏暖 / 暖色调), tube-like, valve-amp style (胆味 / 胆机风格), rich and mellow (醇厚柔和), musical (很有音乐味道), warmth without losing treble (Soomal, Monitor 01 US), 'straight' or balanced (Japanese reviews; unverified)

**Typical uses:** DAC post-filter and output stage in Chinese USB DACs and sound cards (Musiland Monitor and MD series), headphone-amp gain stage in front of a current buffer (Musiland HP11 with LME49600; Monitor 01 PS and TempoTec DAII Plus with TPA6120), analog stages of DVD-Audio/SACD players (Denon DVD-A1, DVD-2900), pro-audio DSP and converters (Yamaha DME32, Apogee Mini-DAC), DAP analog filter (HiFiMAN HM-602), op-amp rolling in socketed sound cards and motherboards, guitar pedals (a forum trace of MXR MC402 Rev E, via third-party notes)

**Caveats:**

- Needs at least ±4.5 V (9 V total) supplies, so it does not suit low-voltage single-cell circuits.
- Weak as a headphone driver on its own: Soomal says one OP275 cannot drive a 300 Ω HD580 with authority, so products pair it with buffers.
- Measurements show a series of low-level harmonics (Soomal RMAA), so it is not a lowest-distortion choice by modern standards.
- The sound depends on the implementation: Soomal found some OP275 cards neutral.
- Expensive relative to NE5532/4580. Musiland used CLC2059 in the 01 Mini (2009) and MC33079 in 03 US and 03 PS (2011), but still used OP275G in the MD12 (2014).
- 'Warm/tube-like' is subjective community reputation; Soomal says it has no direct proof that the harmonics cause it.
- Counterfeits are rumoured, but no specific report was confirmed.

## Other change notes (lifecycle, packaging, successors, lore)

### 1. Package / assembly: Analog Devices, 2007 onward (PCN 07_0024; the Rev.

An assembly-material change. Sumitomo discontinued mold compounds 6300H, 6650RL, 6710S, 6730B and 7050B, so ADI changed the mold compound, and in some cases the die-attach material, for PDIP, SOIC and other packages, together with a polyimide implementation. Two earlier searches linked OP275 to this PCN, but the parts list was not read.

- **When:** 2007 onward (PCN 07_0024; the Rev. E reissue date was not seen)
- **Affected:** OP275 PDIP and SOIC codes (exact list not read)
- **How to tell old from new:** Not visible in the part number. Compare the date code or lot with the PCN effective date in the PCN 07_0024 Rev. E parts list.
- **Audio impact:** None expected. Only package and die-coat materials changed, and no electrical spec change was reported.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - die design, pinout and data sheet are unchanged; only materials changed.
- **Confidence:** low
- **Verification:** A combined search on '07_0024' with OP275 returned the Rev. E form and parts list but did not show an OP275 row, so the listing is still unconfirmed. Package kind, so no further searches were spent.

### 2. Package / assembly: Analog Devices, RoHS transition (date not confirmed)

Lead-free ordering codes replaced the leaded GP and GS codes. Distributors list OP275GS as obsolete and OP275GPZ/OP275GSZ as active. No die change was reported.

- **When:** RoHS transition (date not confirmed)
- **Affected:** OP275GP → OP275GPZ, OP275GS → OP275GSZ
- **How to tell old from new:** RoHS parts carry a Z suffix in the ordering code. The top mark on recent parts reads 'OP275G'.
- **Audio impact:** None reported.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - the ordering code differs, but the pinout and data sheet are the same.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Verification:** Not re-searched (lead-finish entry backed by distributor listings).

### Revision chain status

I used all 6 of 6 WebSearch calls.  Analog Devices OP275 is the only vendor document; there are no second sources.  **Revision chain known now (oldest to newest):** 1. **Rev. 0 / original PMI-era edition (about 1992–93):** still missing. No copy or date was found. The SPICE model Rev. B dated 10/92 suggests the part existed by late 1992. 2. **REV. A:** undated and pre-RoHS (lists OP275GP). Held by the Futurlec OP275GP.pdf and pken.net copies. 3. **REV. B:** NEW. Held by the Reichelt copy at https://cdn-reichelt.de/documents/datenblatt/A200/DATASHEET_OP275-AD-EN.pdf. Its page-1 footer 'REV. B Information furnished by Analog Devices...' shows in the search result title. Date not seen. 4. **Rev. C:** current. Held by analog.com/media/.../OP275.pdf and RadioLocman di=121467. Date not seen.  **Still missing:** - The dates of Rev. A, B and C. The old-layout ADI sheets carry no revision-history table, and no footer date showed in any snippet. - Any Rev. 0 or PMI-branded original. - Whether Rev. C (or an unlabelled later reprint) added the lead-free Z part numbers. This matters for the RoHS-era change.  **Where to look next:** Several third-party copies may hold Rev. A or B, but their revision was not shown: datasheetcatalog OP275 and OP275GS, alldatasheet IDs 49047/49049/49052 (12 pages, 189 KB), DatasheetBank 71480, DatasheetQ, datasheet4u/datasheetspdf 475420, and Octopart 9742324. Opening them would identify the editions.  No silicon change under the same part number was documented. A diyAudio thread ('OP275 dual OPA, might be many fake source originated in China', https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/op275-dual-opa-might-be-many-fake-source-originated-in-china.309442/) covers counterfeits, not a real die revision.

## Counterfeits

No concrete counterfeit report was found. The discovery hint that counterfeits 'have been reported' was not confirmed. A full-text search of 29 Soomal posts found no fake or remarked-part report. OP275 was sold in China as a premium upgrade op-amp (nearly 20 RMB in 2009, about 20x an NE5532), which is the usual profile for remarked parts. Genuine current parts are marked 'OP275G' on top. Buy OP275GPZ (DIP) or OP275GSZ (SOIC) from authorized distributors. A heuristic, not from a vendor: a relabelled NE5532/4558-class die would measure differently, so check supply current (about 2 to 2.5 mA per amplifier), slew rate (22 V/µs) and GBW (9 MHz). The GP-to-GPZ and GS-to-GSZ change is the RoHS transition, with no evidence of a die change. ADI PCN 07_0024 (mold compound) may also apply to OP275.
