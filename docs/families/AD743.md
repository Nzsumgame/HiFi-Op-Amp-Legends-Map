# AD743 / AD745 (ultralow-noise BiFET single op amps; AD745 = decompensated high-speed version)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*ADI BiFET single with bipolar-class 2.9 nV/rtHz voltage noise and only ~7 fA/rtHz current noise, prized for high-impedance sources such as MM phono and hydrophones.*

**Technology:** BiFET: JFET input differential pair (p-channel JFETs in ADI's macro-models) with bipolar gain and output stages. Single op amp. AD743 is unity-gain stable. AD745 uses the same noise and input design but is decompensated for higher bandwidth. In ADI's models its dominant pole is 4.8x higher at the same 132 dB open-loop gain.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

More of an instrumentation legend than a mainstream op-amp-rolling part. It combines 2.9 nV/rtHz white voltage noise with only about 6.9 fA/rtHz current noise, so it stays quiet with both low and very high source impedances. That makes it a reference choice for hydrophone, piezo and charge-amplifier front ends and for low-noise measurement. For example, Rubiola picked the AD743 over the OPA627 for AM-noise detector amplifiers because of its lower voltage noise. In audio it has a niche following in MM phono/RIAA stages and high-impedance mic or piezo preamps, where the cartridge's inductive impedance makes current noise matter. Community sound descriptors and ASR-style measurements could not be retrieved in this run because the web search budget was already used up.

**Typical uses:** MM phono / RIAA preamp input stage (niche following; per discovery notes, not web-verified), Hydrophone and piezo-film charge amplifiers (e.g. AD745 charge amplifier for PVDF film in a Taiwanese thesis), Low-noise measurement front ends (AM/phase-noise detector amplifiers, Rubiola arXiv:physics/0512082), Cryogenic bolometer readout / precision bias circuits (AD743 in arXiv physics/9902042 circuit), High-impedance sensor and accelerometer preamps

**Caveats:**

- Single op amp only: not a drop-in for the dual (5532/TL072-style) DIP-8 sockets used in most op-amp rolling. Two parts per stereo pair plus an adapter are needed.
- The surface-mount part is a 16-lead wide SOIC (R-16), not SOIC-8, so an adapter or custom footprint is needed.
- AD745 is decompensated (dominant pole 4.8x higher than AD743 in ADI's models) and must run at a noise gain of about 5 or more (exact minimum gain to verify in the datasheet). It is not a unity-gain buffer.
- Large JFET input capacitance (20 pF in the model). With high-value feedback resistors this adds a feedback pole and raises noise gain at high frequency (e_n x C), so a feedback capacitor may be needed.
- About 8 mA quiescent current per amplifier, high for a JFET op amp, so it runs warm in DIP.
- Minimum supply is about +/-4.8 V (9.6 V total); not a 5 V single-supply part.
- Low-volume, relatively expensive ADI part; which grades/packages remain in production was not verified.

## Counterfeits

No AD743/AD745-specific counterfeit reports were found; the web search budget was already used up, so forums were not checked. Generic risk: expensive single JFET op amps in DIP-8 are common targets for marketplace remarking. Checks: (1) supply current should be about 8 mA per amp at +/-15 V; a remarked TL071-class part draws far less and is much noisier. (2) The documented surface-mount package is the 16-lead SOIC (R-16), so treat an 'AD743/AD745' in SOIC-8 with suspicion and compare it with the ADI ordering guide. (3) Check the ADI logo and date-code format against known-good parts. (4) Buy from ADI or authorised distributors.
