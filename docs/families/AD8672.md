# AD8671 / AD8672 / AD8674 (ADI very-high-precision low-noise single/dual/quad)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*ADI's 2.8 nV/√Hz, 10 MHz precision op-amp. Head-Fi op-amp rollers found the AD8672 cleaner and more detailed than the NJM2068 and AD8620, though less relaxed to listen to.*

**Technology:** Precision, very-low-noise, low-input-bias-current op-amp (bipolar input per draft, consistent with 12 nA max Ib): 2.8 nV/√Hz, 75 µV max Vos, 10 MHz GBW, 4 V/µs, ±5 V to ±15 V supplies, unity-gain stable, no phase reversal

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A tier-C audio reputation that rests mainly on one Head-Fi thread, 'AD8672 - a nice audio opamp?'. One listener found it cleaner than the NJM2068, more detailed than both the NJM2068 and the AD8620, and harsher than the mellow AD8620 ('not to say piercing'). They also found it less relaxing, which they put down to the extra detail. ADI designed and markets it as a precision part, not an audio part.

**How people describe the sound:** clean, detailed, forward / somewhat harsh (one listener), not relaxing

**Typical uses:** Op-amp rolling in DIY headphone amps and DAC output stages (Head-Fi), Precision low-noise signal conditioning (ADI's intended use)

**Caveats:**

- The praise is thin: mostly one Head-Fi thread, and subjective
- Needs at least ±5 V (10 V total), so it is unsuitable for low-voltage or USB-powered gear
- SOIC/MSOP/TSSOP only, so rolling needs an adapter
- Bipolar input with up to 12 nA bias current: keep source impedances low

### Revision chain status

Analog Devices is the only vendor. It has one data sheet (AD8671_8672_8674, no literature number), and no second sources or acquirer re-issues are known. Known chain, oldest to newest: Preliminary Technical Data Rev 0.2 (2/21/02; copies on sisoog, already listed, and dzsc) -> Rev 0 (initial release, date not found, probably 2003) -> Rev A (date not found) -> Rev B (4/04; A to B changes not captured) -> Rev C (6/05; changes to Figure 6 and updated outline dimensions; a copy identified as Rev C is on datasheetbank.com) -> Rev D (12/09; Figure 3 deleted, thermal and absolute-maximum-ratings changes, Power Dissipation section added) -> Rev E (6/10; change list still not captured) -> Rev F (3/13, current; on analog.com, radiolocman/rlocman, DigiKey). Still missing: the dates and change lists for Rev 0 and Rev A; the Rev A to Rev B and Rev D to Rev E change details; and copies that hold Rev 0, A, B, D or E. The already-listed Octopart cache files (10011492, 8460108, 68369075), alldatasheet 455850 and omo-ic may hold older revisions, but their footers were not shown. The legacy ADI path (analog.com/static/imported-files/data_sheets/...) never came up in search results, so it is not listed as a seed. Wayback captures of it, if it exists, would be the most likely source of Rev C/D/E, but the URL is unverified. The '09/25/2015' date that a search summary gave for Rev F probably comes from product-page or file metadata. It is not a data sheet revision date and was not recorded.

## Counterfeits

No part-specific counterfeit reports found. As with other popular SMD precision op-amps, buy from authorized distributors. Leaded non-Z codes (AD8672ARM, AD8674ARU) are old stock, so treat loose market lots with caution.

## Related parts and alternatives

AD8675 / AD8676 (ADI 36 V, 2.8 nV/√Hz, rail-to-rail output), AD8597 / AD8599 (ADI ultralow-distortion, ultralow-noise), AD8620 (ADI JFET dual, the 'mellow' reference in Head-Fi comparisons), OP27 / OP270 (ADI classic precision low-noise bipolar)
