# NJM8901 / NJM8901E (JRC entry-line JFET-input dual)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** Japanese audio series (New JRC / Nisshinbo)

*Entry-tier New JRC JFET-input audio dual said to use MUSES-derived sound tuning; one Japanese blog review calls it easy to listen to, with light bass.*

**Technology:** BiFET (JFET-input) dual op-amp. A stereo.jp article on a visit to JRC (seen only as a search summary) places it in the entry tier of New JRC's high-quality audio op-amp line: entry NJM8801 / NJM8901 / NJM4582, then mass-production MUSES8820 / MUSES8920 / MUSES8832, then flagship MUSES01 / 02 / 03. The entry tier applies sound-quality techniques developed for MUSES to more mass-producible technology. Process, compensation and lead-frame details are not confirmed from any datasheet. No part of this description was re-checked in the verification pass, because no search could run.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche Japanese-market part with very little coverage. It is not a recognised 'legend'. One Japanese blog listening comparison (guiblo, 2023) describes NJM8901E as easy to listen to with light bass, and says MUSES8820D gives noticeably deeper, weightier bass. A Japanese YouTube op-amp listening comparison reportedly includes it, but its content was not re-verified. No bench measurements were found (Audio Science Review, Samuel Groner, Japanese measurement blogs).

**How people describe the sound:** light / lean bass (低音があっさり目), from guiblo only, easy to listen to (聴きやすい), from guiblo only, less bass weight than MUSES8820D, from guiblo only

**Typical uses:** op-amp rolling in DACs / headphone amplifiers, the guiblo comparison context (an SOP8-class package needs a DIP adapter), budget alternative to generic NJM4580 / NJM4558-class duals (inferred from its entry-tier positioning, not from a user report)

**Caveats:**

- Evidence is very thin: one subjective, unblinded blog comparison and one YouTube shoot-out whose content was not re-checked. No measurements were found.
- The sound descriptors come from a single listener and cannot be generalised to 'Japanese listeners'.
- NJM8901E is probably surface-mount (EMP8/SOP8-class, inferred from JRC suffix convention), so a DIP8 socket needs an adapter board.
- No datasheet was retrieved, so stability, output drive, supply range, quiescent current, noise and distortion are all unverified.
- The verification pass could not run any web search, so no claim in this record has been independently re-checked.
