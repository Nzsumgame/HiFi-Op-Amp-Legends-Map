# NE5532 / NE5532A / SA5532 / SE5532 (dual low-noise bipolar; incl. NJM5532, KA5532, BA15532, RC5532)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** Bipolar classics

*The 1979 Signetics dual that became the pro-audio benchmark: 5 nV/rtHz, 600 ohm drive, cheap. Douglas Self's reference part; Soomal calls it the 'king of op-amps'.*

**Technology:** Bipolar, internally compensated (unity-gain stable) dual; the dual version of the NE5534 core. Legacy parts (Signetics/Philips, onsemi, TI up to SLOS075J) use an NPN input pair with anti-parallel input-protection diodes and a class-AB output specified into 600 ohm. TI's current die (documented from SLOS075K, Dec 2025) is a redesign on a newer process after the move from SFAB Sherman (150 mm) to RFAB Richardson. A TI E2E thread (Jun 2026) says NE5532, LM833, RC4580 and MC33078 now share one die, and summaries describe it as RC4580-derived with no input diodes. Input-stage polarity is disputed: Gearspace says PNP, the E2E-thread summary says NPN.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (3 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

The reference pro-audio op-amp since 1979 and the stock part that op-amp rolls are judged against. Douglas Self uses it as the benchmark in Small Signal Audio Design and argues bad reports come from misuse. NwAvGuy's 2011 measurements put it alongside far pricier parts. Soomal (China) calls it '号称运放之皇' ('reputed king of op-amps') and uses it as a benchmark tier ('4558 < 5532/4580'). Japanese reviewer nabe treats it as the baseline 'normal audio op-amp' (rank B). Vintage Signetics and Philips stock has a following in Japan and China, and junker-life preferred Signetics/Philips by ear over TI and JRC. These are subjective reports.

**How people describe the sound:** neutral / clean, transparent at line level when used correctly (Self), warm (Soomal: '5532那种温暖特点'), soft, warm mid-bass with slower transients and less treble resolution than modern parts (Soomal RNHP review), retro / vintage flavour ('复古的味道', Soomal), lightly coloured (nabe, Japan), flat or plain next to boutique parts (op-amp rollers)

**Typical uses:** mixing consoles and pro line stages driving 600 ohm, CD player and DAC I/V and output stages, preamps, tone controls, active crossovers, powered speakers (e.g. Soomal's 鹂之声 启明星), headphone amps (with or without buffers), MM phono and RIAA stages

**Caveats:**

- TI parts on the new die (documented from SLOS075K, Dec 2025, though forums say shipments began earlier) are rated 5 V/us slew and ±18 V absolute maximum, and the 600 ohm drive specs are gone. Designs on ±18 to ±22 V rails can be overstressed. Production boards failed QA after the change (Scott Dorsey, via Headphonesty).
- The new TI die reportedly drops the input-protection diodes and halves HBM ESD (2 kV to 1 kV).
- Needs split rails of at least about ±5 V. The output is not rail-to-rail (common-mode range ±12 V on ±15 V).
- Quiescent current is several mA per package, so it runs warm in dense or small builds.
- Bipolar input bias current (hundreds of nA) causes DC offset with high source impedance. It is not a drop-in for FET-input sockets that rely on low bias current.
- Legacy parts have anti-parallel input-protection diodes, so they are unsuitable as comparators or with large differential inputs.
- Brands differ. Douglas Self reportedly found TI parts noisier with higher HF THD than Fairchild/onsemi (forum report, not primary-source verified). JRC NJM5532 is described as looser.
- Counterfeit and remarked 'NOS Signetics/Philips' parts are common on marketplaces.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Texas Instruments: Process/fab PCN reportedly dated Nov 15, 2023 (diyAudio);…

TI replaced the NE5532/NE5532A/SA5532/SA5532A silicon with a redesign on a newer process in RFAB, without changing the part numbers. A TI E2E thread says NE5532, LM833, RC4580 and MC33078 are now the same die, and summaries describe it as RC4580-derived. Hackaday reports that the process-change notice gave impact 'None'. The datasheet was updated only in Dec 2025 (Rev K), and the datasheet PCN followed in Apr 2026. Hackaday calls the result incompatible even with the original Signetics NE5532.

- **When:** Process/fab PCN reportedly dated Nov 15, 2023 (diyAudio); production moved from SFAB Sherman (150 mm) to RFAB Richardson around 2024 (Gearspace: 'K version – 2024>'); datasheet Rev K Dec 2025; datasheet PCN 20260429001 Apr 30, 2026 (first ship Jul 29, 2026)
- **Affected:** NE5532, NE5532A, SA5532, SA5532A, NE5532P, NE5532D, NE5532DR, NE5532AP, NE5532APSR, SA5532P, SA5532DR, SA5532ADR, SA5532ADRG4
- **How to tell old from new:** A SLOS075K or later datasheet means the new die; SLOS075J or earlier means the legacy die. Orderable part numbers are unchanged, and no date-code cutover has been published. Legacy parts have back-to-back diodes across the inputs and the new die reportedly has none, so an unpowered diode check between IN+ and IN- can separate them (E2E-thread summary). A bench test also works: slew about 5 V/us on the new die versus 9 V/us on the legacy die. TI E2E thread 1294367 (late 2023) asks for the PCN on an NE5532DR 'symbolization format' (marking) change, which may be a visual clue (unconfirmed). The PCN number for the process change has not been identified. Candidates are the Nov 15, 2023 PCN (number not captured), 20240529002, 20240806004, 20241217004, 20250129000 and 20250730004; none is confirmed to list NE5532.
- **Audio impact:** Full-power bandwidth at 10 V peak falls from about 143 kHz to about 80 kHz (computed from the slew rate), which leaves less HF THD margin at high output level. Drive into 600 ohm and other heavy loads is no longer guaranteed. Consoles and powered speakers on ±18 to ±22 V (often unregulated) rails can exceed the new absolute maximum. Without the input diodes, circuits that relied on them for differential-input clamping lose that protection. The higher GBW and different input stage can change stability and HF behaviour in legacy layouts. Production boards failed QA after the TI parts were swapped in (Scott Dorsey, via Headphonesty).
- **Drop-in risk:** high – same part number but lower voltage rating, slower slew, halved ESD, no input diodes and deleted load-drive guarantees; not a drop-in for legacy ±18 to ±22 V or 600 ohm designs
- **Confidence:** high

### 2. Signetics / Philips: about 2000 (pad-printed to laser-marked transition;

Japanese blogs (radiokits.jp / takinx) report that later laser-marked Philips parts behave differently at the input-protection diodes, and that many did not work properly when two were connected directly. They also claim the Signetics masks were lost in the 2000 Albuquerque fire and the layout was redrawn. No vendor document supports either claim.

- **When:** about 2000 (pad-printed to laser-marked transition; Philips Albuquerque fab fire, March 2000)
- **Affected:** NE5532N, NE5532AN, NE5532, NE5534
- **How to tell old from new:** Pad-printed (tampo) Signetics marking before about 2000 versus laser-marked Philips parts after.
- **Audio impact:** Claimed subtle sonic difference and different input-diode behaviour. Anecdotal only.
- **Drop-in risk:** low – no documented spec change; folklore
- **Confidence:** low

### 3. Multiple second sources (TI legacy,…: 1980s–present

Each vendor built the same generic number on its own die. Forum reports: Douglas Self found TI noisier with higher HF THD than Fairchild/onsemi; original Signetics reportedly up to about 10 dB quieter with about 20 dB lower distortion than modern TI; JRC NJM5532 called 'looser'; Japanese opinion puts TI closest to Signetics. Soomal (2009) says the 鹂之声 启明星 speaker's noise floor fell when it switched from JRC5532 to TI5532, but the crossover was retuned in the same revision, so the op-amp's share is unclear.

- **When:** 1980s–present
- **Affected:** NE5532, NJM5532, KA5532, BA15532, RC5532
- **How to tell old from new:** Manufacturer logo and prefix (NJM, KA, BA, RC) and the vendor datasheet.
- **Audio impact:** Possible differences in noise, HF THD and bias current between brands. Evidence is mostly forum measurements and listening reports.
- **Drop-in risk:** low – pin- and function-compatible; parameter spread only
- **Confidence:** low

### Revision chain status

I used all 14 WebSearch calls. A few results were near misses, and some gaps remain.  TI SLOS075 (oldest to newest): original Nov 1979, A (Sep 1990), B (missing), C (Jun 2000), D, E and F (all missing), G (Jun 2002), H (May 2004), I (Apr 2009), J (Jan 2015), K (Dec 2025). I copy at televideo.ws ('1FEATURES' layout). The Octopart SA5532DR copy, alldatasheet 27245, 27248 and 785133, and datasheetcatalog show no revision. alldatasheet 27248 (NE5532P, 10 pages, upper-case 1990 to 2004 style title) is the most likely place to find one of the missing B, D, E or F. Fetch it to check.  Philips/Signetics: 1997 Sep 29 Product specification (radiolux.com.ua, probably also alldatasheet 17968), then 2001 Aug 03 Product data, which 'Supersedes data of 1997 Sep 29' (ben.cz). Missing: any pre-1997 Signetics or Philips edition (the 1979 original and any 1980s or 1994 data-book editions), any NXP re-issue after 2006, and the Philips semiconductors.philips.com/acrobat file names. Fairchild: KA5532 Rev. 1.0.1 (©2002, Farnell) is new. NE5532 Rev. 1.0.2 (©2009) was already known. ## Counterfeits

Remarked fakes are common on marketplaces, especially 'NOS Signetics/Philips' parts (vintage stock sells for over 100 RMB in China, which motivates relabelling). Elektroda shows a fake NE5532 decapped next to a genuine one (https://www.elektroda.com/rtvforum/topic3386559.html), and TI E2E has an 'NE5532: Genuine or fake?' thread (https://e2e.ti.com/support/audio-group/audio/f/audio-forum/1474706/ne5532-genuine-or-fake). Discovery notes also mention relabelled LM741-class dies (diyAudio fake-AD797/LM741 thread). To spot fakes: a genuine 5532 draws several mA per package, far above LM358/741-class parts. It should drive 600 ohm cleanly and slew about 9 V/us (legacy) or 5 V/us (new TI). Compare the marking font and logo against known date codes, and buy from authorized distributors. A genuine new TI part now slews at only 5 V/us and has no input diodes, so neither test alone proves a fake.

## Related parts and alternatives

onsemi NE5532 / SA5532 (NE5532/D still specifies the legacy 9 V/us, 10 MHz and 600 ohm drive), NE5534 / NE5534A (single, externally compensated sibling), JRC/Nisshinbo NJM5532, or NJM2114 (JRC's improved 5532), LM4562 / LME49720 (National/TI low-distortion bipolar dual), OPA1612 / OPA1602 (TI SoundPlus bipolar), NJM2068 (lower noise and power; O2 gain stage), MUSES8820 (bipolar MUSES), OPA1688 (called 'a 21st-century NE5532' on diyAudio), Note: an E2E thread says TI's LM833, MC33078 and RC4580 now share the new TI NE5532 die, so none of them is a legacy-behaviour substitute from TI

## Open questions

- TI SLOS075 revisions B, D, E and F: dates not found.
- No SLOS075L was found in September 2026 searches. K (Dec 2025, packaging addendum 18-Jun-2026) appears current. One GitHub ML dataset cites 'TI-NE5532-SLOS075L' (unverified).
- Which PCN announced the fab/die change? diyAudio cites one dated Nov 15, 2023 (number not captured). RFAB 'Process Technology, Die Revision' PCNs 20240529002, 20240806004, 20241217004, 20250129000 and 20250730004 surfaced in searches, but none is confirmed to list NE5532.
- Does the NE5532DR 'symbolization format change' (TI E2E 1294367, late 2023) coincide with the die change and give a visual identifier?
- New-die input polarity: Gearspace says PNP; the E2E-thread summary says it is RC4580-derived with NPN inputs. There is no TI statement.
- Are the input-protection diodes gone? Headphonesty, Mithat and the E2E thread say yes; the SLOS075K description text reportedly still lists them.
- No published date-code or lot-trace cutover exists between the legacy and new TI dies.
- Revisions not captured: Philips/NXP, Signetics, the current onsemi NE5532/D (after Rev. 2, Sep 2013), ST, JRC NJM5532, Rohm BA15532 and Raytheon RC5532.
