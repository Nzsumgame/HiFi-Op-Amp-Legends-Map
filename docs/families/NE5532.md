# NE5532 / NE5532A / SA5532 / SE5532

**Bipolar dual, low-noise, unity-gain stable** · Signetics (1979–early 1990s) → Philips/NXP (1990s–2006 (Philips); NXP after 2006) · second sources: Texas Instruments, onsemi, Fairchild / Samsung, Raytheon, JRC/Nisshinbo, STMicroelectronics, Rohm · Tier A (legend / revision hazard)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** TI moved NE5532/A and SA5532/A to an RFAB die (around 2024) shared with LM833, RC4580, MC33078. (+1 more) [Details](#silicon-changes)

*The 1979 Signetics dual that became the pro-audio benchmark: 5 nV/√Hz, 600 Ω drive, cheap. Douglas Self's reference.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | Supply range | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 nV/√Hz typ (TI product page headline: 6 nV/√Hz) | 10 MHz (≤ rev J, onsemi) → 12 MHz (rev K) | 9 V/µs (≤ rev J, onsemi) → 5 V/µs (rev K) | ±5 V to ±15 V; abs max ±22 V (J) → ±18 V (K) | 8 mA typ (J) → 6 mA typ, 16 mA max (K) |

## Why it's rated

The reference pro-audio op-amp since 1979 and the stock part rolls are judged against. Douglas Self uses it as his benchmark and blames bad reports on misuse; NwAvGuy's 2011 measurements put it beside far pricier parts. Soomal calls it '号称运放之皇' (reputed king of op-amps); Japan's nabe rates it the baseline audio op-amp. Vintage Signetics/Philips stock has a following.

**Described as:** neutral, clean (Self), warm (温暖), soft warm mid-bass, slower transients, retro flavour (复古的味道), lightly coloured, plain beside boutique parts

**Used in:** Mixing consoles and pro line stages into 600 Ω, CD player and DAC I/V and output stages, Preamps, tone controls, active crossovers, powered speakers, Headphone amps, with or without buffers, MM phono and RIAA stages

## Watch out

- New TI die: 5 V/µs, ±18 V max, no 600 Ω specs; ±18–22 V boards have failed QA.
- New TI die reportedly drops input diodes and halves HBM ESD (2 kV to 1 kV).
- Needs at least about ±5 V; not rail-to-rail. Several mA per package, so it runs warm.
- Bipolar bias current causes offset with high source impedance; legacy input diodes rule out comparator use.
- Brands differ: Self reportedly found TI noisier than Fairchild/onsemi. Fake 'NOS Signetics/Philips' parts are common.
- **Counterfeits:** Remarked fakes are common, especially 'NOS Signetics/Philips' (vintage sells for over 100 RMB in China). Genuine parts draw several mA and drive 600 Ω. New TI parts slew 5 V/µs without input diodes, so neither test proves a fake.

## Silicon changes

### Die redesign: Texas Instruments, 2023-11 · PCN 20231114002.1

TI moved NE5532/A and SA5532/A to an RFAB die (around 2024) shared with LM833, RC4580, MC33078. Rich Cabot: 'they just relabeled a 4580'. The 2023 PCN claimed no impact; SLOS075K followed in Dec 2025 and datasheet PCN 20260429001.1 (first ship 29 Jul 2026). Hackaday (3 Jun 2026) calls it incompatible.

- **Affected:** NE5532, NE5532A, SA5532, SA5532A, NE5532P, NE5532D, NE5532DR, NE5532AP, NE5532APSR, SA5532P, SA5532DR, SA5532ADR, SA5532ADRG4
- **How to tell:** Part numbers unchanged, no date-code cut-over; 2024+ lots may be new silicon. Legacy parts show anti-parallel diodes between IN+ and IN−; new die reportedly none. Slew 5 vs 9 V/µs.
- **Audio impact:** Full-power bandwidth at 10 V peak falls from about 143 to 80 kHz; 600 Ω drive unguaranteed; ±18–22 V rails overstressed; input clamping gone.
- **Drop-in risk:** high: same part number, lower voltage rating, slower slew, halved ESD, no input diodes.
- **Confidence:** high

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Unity-gain bandwidth (typ) | 10 MHz (Rev J) | 12 MHz (Rev K) |
| Slew rate (typ) | 9 V/µs | 5 V/µs |
| Supply voltage absolute max | ±22 V | ±18 V |
| HBM ESD | 2 kV | 1 kV |
| Supply current (typ) | 8 mA | 6 mA |
| Input-protection diodes | anti-parallel diodes across inputs | removed (Headphonesty, Mithat, E2E thread); the Rev K description text reportedly still mentions them |
| Vopp into 600 ohm (typ) | 26 V | spec removed |
| Max output-swing bandwidth (±10 V, 600 ohm) | 140 kHz | spec removed |
| Output impedance | 0.3 ohm | spec removed |
| Crosstalk attenuation, small-signal gain, overshoot | specified | specs removed |
| Input bias current sign | positive only (NPN input) | ± (input stage changed; polarity disputed) |
| Equivalent input noise, CMRR, DC AVD | 5 nV/√Hz @1 kHz, 100 dB, 100 V/mV | reported unchanged (EEVblog) |

### Second-source difference: Multiple second sources (TI legacy, onsemi/Fairchild, JRC NJM5532, Signetics/Philips, Raytheon, Rohm, Samsung), 1980s–present

Each vendor built its own die. Forum reports: Self found TI noisier, with higher HF THD, than Fairchild/onsemi; original Signetics reportedly up to 10 dB quieter and 20 dB lower distortion than modern TI; JRC 'looser'. A Soomal noise gain (JRC5532 to TI5532) is confounded by a crossover retune.

- **Affected:** NE5532, NJM5532, KA5532, BA15532, RC5532
- **How to tell:** Manufacturer logo and prefix (NJM, KA, BA, RC), plus the vendor datasheet.
- **Audio impact:** Possible brand differences in noise, HF THD and bias current; evidence mostly forum reports.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: pin- and function-compatible across legacy vendors (post-2024 TI excepted).
- **Confidence:** low
