# NE5532 / NE5532A / SA5532 / SE5532 (dual low-noise bipolar; incl. NJM5532, KA5532, BA15532, RC5532)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** Bipolar classics

*The 1979 Signetics dual that became the pro-audio benchmark: 5 nV/rtHz, drives 600 ohm loads, cheap. Douglas Self's reference part and Soomal's '运放之皇' (king of op-amps).*

**Technology:** Bipolar, internally compensated (unity-gain stable) dual: the dual version of the NE5534 core. Original and pre-2024 TI parts use an NPN input pair and input-protection diodes, with a class-AB output able to drive 600 ohm loads. TI's 2024+ die (datasheet SLOS075K) is a new design on a different process and fab (SFAB to RFAB reported); forums report a PNP input stage.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (3 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

The reference pro-audio op-amp since 1979, and the stock part that op-amp rolls are judged against. Douglas Self uses it as his benchmark in Small Signal Audio Design and argues bad reports come from misuse. NwAvGuy measured it level with the 'elite' parts. Soomal (China) calls it '号称运放之皇' (king of op-amps) and uses it as a benchmark tier ('4558 < 5532/4580'). Japan treats it as the baseline 'normal audio op-amp' (nabe rank B). Vintage Signetics and Philips stock has a cult following in Japan and China: early parts sell for over 100 RMB, and junker-life heard Signetics/Philips as better than TI/JRC.

**How people describe the sound:** neutral / clean, transparent at line level when used correctly, warm (Soomal: '5532那种温暖特点'), soft, warm mid-bass with slower transients and less treble resolution than modern parts (Soomal RNHP review), retro / vintage flavour ('复古的味道', Soomal), lightly coloured (nabe, Japan), flat or plain next to boutique parts (op-amp rollers)

**Typical uses:** mixing consoles and pro line stages driving 600 ohm, CD player and DAC I/V and output stages, preamps, tone controls, active crossovers, powered speakers (e.g. Soomal's 鹂之声 启明星), headphone amps (with or without buffers), MM phono and RIAA stages

**Caveats:**

- TI parts made from 2024/25 (datasheet SLOS075K) have a different die: 5 V/us slew rate and ±18 V absolute maximum. Designs on ±18 to ±22 V rails can be overstressed, and production boards failed QA after the change (Scott Dorsey, via Headphonesty).
- Needs split rails of at least about ±5 V. The output is not rail-to-rail (common-mode range ±12 V on ±15 V).
- Quiescent current is about 6 to 8 mA per package, so it runs warm in dense or small builds.
- Bipolar input bias current (hundreds of nA) gives DC offset with high source impedance. It is not a drop-in for FET-input sockets that rely on low bias current.
- Legacy parts have anti-parallel input-protection diodes, so they are not suitable as comparators or with large differential inputs.
- Brands differ. Douglas Self reportedly found TI parts noisier with higher HF THD than Fairchild/onsemi (forum report). JRC NJM5532 is described as looser.
- Counterfeit and remarked 'NOS Signetics/Philips' parts are common on marketplaces.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Texas Instruments: Fab/die change about 2024 (SFAB Sherman to RFAB Richardson…

TI replaced the NE5532/NE5532A/SA5532 silicon with a new design on a newer process in its RFAB 300 mm fab, reportedly one die shared with LM833, MC33078 and RC4580, without changing the part numbers. Forums report a PNP input and heavy dominant-pole compensation. The process-change notice said impact 'None'. The datasheet was only updated in Dec 2025 (Rev K), and the formal datasheet PCN came in Apr 2026.

- **When:** Fab/die change about 2024 (SFAB Sherman to RFAB Richardson reported); datasheet Rev K Dec 2025; datasheet PCN Apr 30, 2026
- **Affected:** NE5532, NE5532A, SA5532, SA5532A, NE5532P, NE5532DR, NE5532AP, NE5532APSR, SA5532P, SA5532DR, SA5532ADR, SA5532ADRG4
- **How to tell old from new:** Datasheet SLOS075K or later means the new die; SLOS075J or earlier means the legacy die. TI PCN 20260429001 (datasheet change) plus an earlier RFAB process/die PCN whose number is not confirmed (candidates 20241217004 / 20250129000 / 20250730004). Orderable part numbers and markings unchanged; a date-code cutover is not published. Per TI E2E (Jun 2026) the same die now ships as NE5532, LM833, RC4580 and MC33078. Practical test: slew rate about 5 V/us, not 9 V/us.
- **Audio impact:** Full-power bandwidth at 10 V peak falls from about 143 kHz to about 80 kHz (computed from SR), which leaves less margin for HF THD at high output level. Drive into 600 ohm and heavy loads is no longer guaranteed. Consoles and powered speakers on ±18 to ±22 V (often unregulated) rails can exceed the new absolute maximum and fail. The higher GBW and different input stage can change stability and HF behaviour in legacy layouts. Production boards failed QA after TI parts were swapped in (Scott Dorsey via Headphonesty).
- **Drop-in risk:** high – same part number but lower voltage rating, slower slew and deleted load-drive guarantees; not a drop-in for legacy ±18 to ±22 V or 600 ohm designs
- **Confidence:** high

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|

| Slew rate (typ) | 9 V/us | 5 V/us |
| Supply voltage absolute max | ±22 V | ±18 V |
| HBM ESD | 2 kV | 1 kV (forum-reported) |

### 2. Signetics / Philips: about 2000 (pad-printed to laser-marked transition;

Japanese blogs (radiokits.jp / takinx) report that later laser-marked Philips parts behave differently at the input-protection diodes and that many would not work properly when two were connected directly. They also claim the Signetics masks were lost in the 2000 Albuquerque fire and the layout was redrawn. No vendor document supports either claim.

- **When:** about 2000 (pad-printed to laser-marked transition; Philips Albuquerque fab fire, March 2000)
- **Affected:** NE5532N, NE5532AN, NE5532, NE5534
- **How to tell old from new:** Pad-printed (tampo) Signetics marking before about 2000 versus laser-marked Philips parts after.
- **Audio impact:** Claimed subtle sonic difference and different input-diode behaviour. Anecdotal only.
- **Drop-in risk:** low – no documented spec change; folklore
- **Confidence:** low

### 3. Multiple second sources (TI legacy,…: 1980s–present

The same generic number was built on independent dies by each vendor. Forum reports: Douglas Self found TI noisier with higher HF THD than Fairchild/onsemi; original Signetics reportedly up to about 10 dB quieter and about 20 dB lower distortion than modern TI; JRC NJM5532 called 'looser'; Japanese opinion puts TI closest to Signetics. Soomal (2009): the 鹂之声 启明星 speaker switched from JRC5532 to TI5532 and its noise floor fell. The same revision also retuned the electronic crossover, so the op-amp's share is confounded.

- **When:** 1980s–present
- **Affected:** NE5532, NJM5532, KA5532, BA15532, RC5532
- **How to tell old from new:** Manufacturer logo and prefix (NJM, KA, BA, RC) and the vendor datasheet.
- **Audio impact:** Possible differences in noise, HF THD and bias current between brands. Mostly forum measurements and listening reports.
- **Drop-in risk:** low – pin- and function-compatible; parameter spread only
- **Confidence:** low

## Counterfeits

Remarked fakes are common on marketplaces, especially 'NOS Signetics/Philips' parts (vintage stock sells for over 100 RMB in China, which motivates relabelling). Elektroda documents a fake NE5532 decapped next to a genuine one (https://www.elektroda.com/rtvforum/topic3386559.html), and TI E2E has an 'NE5532: Genuine or fake?' thread (https://e2e.ti.com/support/audio-group/audio/f/audio-forum/1474706/ne5532-genuine-or-fake). Discovery notes also mention relabelled LM741-class dies (diyAudio fake-AD797/LM741 thread). How to spot: a genuine 5532 draws several mA per package (TI Rev K: 6 mA typ total), far above LM358/741-class parts. Check that it drives 600 ohm cleanly and that slew is about 9 V/us (legacy) or 5 V/us (new TI); a much slower part is suspect. Compare marking font and logo against known date codes. Buy from authorized distributors. Note that a genuine new TI part now also slews at only 5 V/us, so slew rate alone does not prove a fake.

## Related parts and alternatives

onsemi NE5532 / SA5532 (still specifies the legacy 9 V/us, 600 ohm drive and higher supply range), NE5534 / NE5534A (single, externally compensated sibling), JRC/Nisshinbo NJM5532 or NJM2114 (JRC's improved 5532), LM4562 / LME49720 (National/TI low-distortion bipolar dual), OPA1612 / OPA1602 (TI SoundPlus bipolar), NJM2068 (lower noise and power; O2 gain stage), MUSES8820 (bipolar MUSES), OPA1688 (called 'a 21st-century NE5532' on diyAudio), Note: TI LM833 / MC33078 / RC4580 reportedly share the new TI NE5532 die, so they are not legacy-behaviour substitutes from TI
