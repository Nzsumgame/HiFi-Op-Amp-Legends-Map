# OPA1611 / OPA1612 (TI SoundPlus bipolar-input, 1.1 nV/√Hz single/dual)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*TI's 1.1 nV/√Hz bipolar audio op-amp with −136 dB THD+N at 1 kHz. It is widely used for I/V conversion and buffering in modern DACs, DAPs and Hi-Fi phones.*

**Technology:** Bipolar-input, voltage-feedback op-amp, unity-gain stable. The output is rail-to-rail (within 0.6 V at 2 kΩ, 0.2 V at 10 kΩ). The inputs have back-to-back differential clamp diodes. Copper leadframe (SBOS450C §7.3.1). Rev A (Aug 2009) and Rev B (Jul 2011) front pages carry 'Burr-Brown Audio' branding and the title 'High-Performance Bipolar-Input AUDIO OPERATIONAL AMPLIFIERS'; web search shows this in the mirror titles. Rev C (Aug 2014) is titled 'OPA161x SoundPlus™'. TI's current parametric listing still tags the part 'Burr-Brown™ Audio'. The OPA1611 comes in SOIC-8 (D) only. The OPA1612 comes in SOIC-8 (D) or SON-8 3×3 mm (DRG), which TI's parametric table calls WSON. No DIP package appears in the datasheet or the orderable addendum.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (3 recorded: Unclassified).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Its measured noise and distortion are exceptional (1.1 nV/√Hz, −136 dB THD+N), and measurement-oriented users treat it as essentially transparent (discovery note). In audioXpress 'Modern Op-Amp Distortion Tests, Part 1' (author not confirmed here), the OPA1612 showed less distortion than the NE5532 with shunt feedback and much less as a voltage follower, but about the same with series feedback. Soomal teardowns show it spreading through Chinese Hi-Fi phones and DAPs from 2014: FiiO X5, Meizu MX4 Pro, vivo X5 Max, Xiaomi Note and Smartisan T2. In 2022 Soomal said a very large share of recent products use the OPA1612 for I/V conversion. Japanese hobbyists call it a neutral 'reference sound' all-rounder (discovery note, not verified). The subjective camp is split. Soomal found it 'hard, not pleasing' (偏硬，不够讨喜) in a 2020 AE-9 op-amp swap, though with better dynamics and resolution than the NE5534. In 2016 Soomal argued that bipolar parts like the OPA1612 sound worse on average than DiFET types, and that passage mislabels the OPA1612 as 'Bipolar FET'.

**How people describe the sound:** neutral / 'reference sound' (JP, maimai-audio; discovery note, unverified), flat, not genre-dependent (JP owners; discovery note, unverified), transparent (measurement community; discovery note), musical, delicate and warm (OPA1611; Audiogon thread per discovery note; low confidence), hard / 偏硬, not pleasing (Soomal 2020, AE-9 op-amp swap, where it is written 'OP1611'), better dynamics and resolution than NE5534 (same Soomal 2020 passage), average sound worse than DiFET types (Soomal 2016, 901 player / MINIBOX review)

**Typical uses:** DAC I/V conversion: Sabaj A20d (2022), with three OPA1612 for I/V and separate op-amps marked '2149U' doing the LPF, and Topping DX7 Pro (both per Soomal 2022), Output LPF in DAPs (SoundAware MR1 with dual CS4398, per Soomal 2017), Post-I/V amplifier or buffer in Hi-Fi phones and DAPs: Meizu MX4 Pro (2014), vivo X5 Max (2014), Xiaomi Note (2015, after ADA4896 I/V), Smartisan T2 (2016, two OPA1612 replacing one OPA2604), FiiO X5 (2014, with an LMH6643 buffer), all per Soomal, Input or error amplifier in VFA-CFA composite headphone amps with TPA6120 or THS6012 (e.g. the PWieland/SCAM project), Professional audio, microphone preamps, mixing consoles, broadcast gear, audio test equipment and A/V receivers (TI's listed applications), Op-amp rolling via DIP-8 adapter boards

**Caveats:**

- Current noise is fairly high (1.7 pA/√Hz @1 kHz). With source or feedback networks of 10 kΩ or more it loses its noise advantage over FET-input parts, as a JP hobbyist's 2026 design notes (indare/pcb_work) also found.
- The input common-mode range stops 2 V from each rail, so G = +1 buffers clip early on low supplies.
- The back-to-back input diodes can conduct on fast edges in low-gain or G = +1 circuits. TI says to limit input current to 10 mA.
- At low gain, capacitive loads reduce phase margin and can cause peaking or oscillation; TI suggests about 50 Ω series isolation (§8.4). Layout and decoupling on DIP adapters matter.
- Iq is 3.6 mA per channel and θJA of SOIC-8 is 150 °C/W. TI says soldering rather than socketing reduces temperature rise.
- It comes only in SOIC or SON, so every 'DIP' part is an adapter board. Relabelled TL082 fakes are documented (diyAudio 332536).
- The audioXpress advantage over the NE5532 depends on topology: it is clear for shunt feedback and followers, and about equal with series feedback.
- The subjective descriptors conflict with each other, and no controlled listening test was verified.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Unclassified: Texas Instruments, PCN# 20260223007.1, dated about 24 Feb 2026 per the search…

A TI change notification with a Group 1 qualification report lists OPA1612AID among roughly 40 unrelated devices: op-amps such as the OPA2187, OPA2210 and OPA4140, INA18x/INA8xx, DACx0004, TLV417x and TPS65910 PMICs, in SOIC, TSSOP and QFN packages. The change type (fab, assembly, material or die) was not visible. The broad, mixed device list suggests a site- or process-level change rather than an OPA1612-specific redesign, but that is inference.

- **When:** PCN# 20260223007.1, dated about 24 Feb 2026 per the search summary
- **Affected:** OPA1612AID
- **How to tell old from new:** Not determinable from search snippets. The change type, effective date and any new fab or assembly-site code or date-code cutover must be read from the PCN PDF or TI's PCN dashboard. The datasheet is unchanged (SBOS450C).
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. No datasheet revision followed as of Sep 2026.
- **Drop-in risk:** low — the nature of the change is unconfirmed, and no datasheet, pinout or spec change has been seen
- **Confidence:** low

### 2. Unclassified: Texas Instruments, PCN 20260223007.1 dated 24 Feb 2026; estimated sample…

This refines the existing OPA1611 known-hazard entry. A Mouser-hosted copy of PCN# 20260223007.1 is titled 'Add Cu as Alternative Wire Base Metal for Selected Device(s)'. It qualifies a new assembly material set that adds Cu as an additional bond-wire option. It is not a fab, process or die revision, so this PCN is not a silicon change for OPA1612.

- **When:** PCN 20260223007.1 dated 24 Feb 2026; estimated sample availability 25 Apr 2026; proposed first ship 25 May 2026
- **Affected:** OPA1612AID (as listed in the existing known-hazard record)
- **How to tell old from new:** The part number and die are unchanged. This is an assembly change that adds a copper bond-wire option. There is no external marking cue.
- **Audio impact:** Negligible. A bond-wire metal change does not affect the op-amp's electrical design. Claims of an audible difference would be anecdotal.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: assembly-material change only, with no die or datasheet change.
- **Confidence:** high

| Bond wire | existing wire metal (presumably Au) | Cu added as an alternative bond wire |

### 3. Unclassified: Texas Instruments, PCN 20260223007.1 (2026)

PCN 20260223007.1 adds Cu as an additional bond-wire option for the listed devices 'to align with world technology trends and use wiring with enhanced mechanical properties'. It has 60-day acknowledgment and sample-request windows. Searches found no other OPA1611/OPA1612 PCN (fab, die or datasheet) and no E2E new-die reports. OPA1611AID/AIDR was TI's named replacement for the discontinued LME49710 (PDN 20170721000C).

- **When:** PCN 20260223007.1 (2026)
- **Affected:** OPA1611 / OPA1612 (per orchestrator lead; product-affected list not seen in search snippet)
- **How to tell old from new:** Assembly/BOM change: Cu bond wire added as an alternative option. The devices stay in the current assembly facility, with piece-part changes.
- **Audio impact:** None expected. Bond-wire material has no meaningful effect on op-amp audio performance.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - packaging material option only; same die and datasheet.
- **Confidence:** medium

|---|---|---|

## Counterfeits

Confirmed by web search in diyAudio thread 332536 ('Fake OPA1612 from China'): a buyer bought 10 'OPA1612' from a Chinese eBay seller for €6.80 in total. Under the microscope they turned out to be relabelled TL082 JFET dies, identified by the cross-shaped 'butterfly' FET input devices, not bipolar OPA1612 dies. A TI E2E thread, 'OPA1612 - Fake chips?' (532783), exists; its content was not seen. diyAudio thread 399994 ('OPA1612 from Aliexpress, no counterfeit but why so cheap?') reports cheap AliExpress parts that decapped as genuine OPA1612 dies, so cheap does not always mean fake. The roughly 150 nA input bias reported there (discovery note) is inside the ±250 nA max. Screening tips from the datasheet: (1) TI lists only SOIC-8 (D) and, for the OPA1612, SON-8 (DRG), so a DIP-8 body marked OPA161x is not a TI package. (2) The genuine SOIC top mark is 'OPA' over '1611A' or '1612A', and the DRG mark is 'OVII'. (3) Iq should be about 3.6 mA per channel, 4.5 mA max. (4) Input bias should be tens of nA; pA-level bias points to a JFET relabel. (5) Noise should be about 1.1 nV/√Hz, so a relabelled TL08x is roughly ten times noisier. Buy from TI or authorised distributors.
