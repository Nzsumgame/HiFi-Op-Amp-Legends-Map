# OPA1611 / OPA1612 (TI SoundPlus bipolar-input, 1.1 nV/√Hz single/dual)

Texas Instruments (Burr-Brown Audio line) (2009–present) → Texas Instruments (2015–present) → Third-party board makers (e.g. NFJ in Japan, AliExpress sellers) (2010s–present) · Tier A (legend / revision hazard)

*TI's 1.1 nV/√Hz bipolar audio op-amp with −136 dB THD+N at 1 kHz. It is widely used for I/V conversion and buffering in modern DACs, DAPs and Hi-Fi phones.*

| THD+N | Supply range |
|---|---|
| 0.000015% (−136 dB) | ±2.25 V to ±18 V (4.5–36 V); absolute max 40 V |

## Why it's rated

Its measured noise and distortion are exceptional (1.1 nV/√Hz, −136 dB THD+N), and measurement-oriented users treat it as essentially transparent (discovery note). In audioXpress 'Modern Op-Amp Distortion Tests, Part 1' (author not confirmed here), the OPA1612 showed less distortion than the NE5532 with shunt feedback and much less as a voltage follower, but about the same with series feedback. Soomal teardowns show it spreading through Chinese Hi-Fi phones and DAPs from 2014: FiiO X5, Meizu MX4 Pro, vivo X5 Max, Xiaomi Note and Smartisan T2. In 2022 Soomal said a very large share of recent products use the OPA1612 for I/V conversion. Japanese hobbyists call it a neutral 'reference sound' all-rounder (discovery note, not verified). The subjective camp is split. Soomal found it 'hard, not pleasing' (偏硬，不够讨喜) in a 2020 AE-9 op-amp swap, though with better dynamics and resolution than the NE5534. In 2016 Soomal argued that bipolar parts like the OPA1612 sound worse on average than DiFET types, and that passage mislabels the OPA1612 as 'Bipolar FET'.

**Described as:** neutral / 'reference sound' (JP, maimai-audio; discovery note, unverified), flat, not genre-dependent (JP owners; discovery note, unverified), transparent (measurement community; discovery note), musical, delicate and warm (OPA1611; Audiogon thread per discovery note; low confidence), hard / 偏硬, not pleasing (Soomal 2020, AE-9 op-amp swap, where it is written 'OP1611'), better dynamics and resolution than NE5534 (same Soomal 2020 passage), average sound worse than DiFET types (Soomal 2016, 901 player / MINIBOX review)

**Used in:** DAC I/V conversion: Sabaj A20d (2022), with three OPA1612 for I/V and separate op-amps marked '2149U' doing the LPF, and Topping DX7 Pro (both per Soomal 2022), Output LPF in DAPs (SoundAware MR1 with dual CS4398, per Soomal 2017), Post-I/V amplifier or buffer in Hi-Fi phones and DAPs: Meizu MX4 Pro (2014), vivo X5 Max (2014), Xiaomi Note (2015, after ADA4896 I/V), Smartisan T2 (2016, two OPA1612 replacing one OPA2604), FiiO X5 (2014, with an LMH6643 buffer), all per Soomal, Input or error amplifier in VFA-CFA composite headphone amps with TPA6120 or THS6012 (e.g. the PWieland/SCAM project), Professional audio, microphone preamps, mixing consoles, broadcast gear, audio test equipment and A/V receivers (TI's listed applications), Op-amp rolling via DIP-8 adapter boards

## Watch out

- Current noise is fairly high (1.7 pA/√Hz @1 kHz). With source or feedback networks of 10 kΩ or more it loses its noise advantage over FET-input parts, as a JP hobbyist's 2026 design notes (indare/pcb_work) also found.
- The input common-mode range stops 2 V from each rail, so G = +1 buffers clip early on low supplies.
- The back-to-back input diodes can conduct on fast edges in low-gain or G = +1 circuits. TI says to limit input current to 10 mA.
- At low gain, capacitive loads reduce phase margin and can cause peaking or oscillation; TI suggests about 50 Ω series isolation (§8.4). Layout and decoupling on DIP adapters matter.
- Iq is 3.6 mA per channel and θJA of SOIC-8 is 150 °C/W. TI says soldering rather than socketing reduces temperature rise.
- It comes only in SOIC or SON, so every 'DIP' part is an adapter board. Relabelled TL082 fakes are documented (diyAudio 332536).
- The audioXpress advantage over the NE5532 depends on topology: it is clear for shunt feedback and followers, and about equal with series feedback.
- The subjective descriptors conflict with each other, and no controlled listening test was verified.
- **Counterfeits:** Confirmed by web search in diyAudio thread 332536 ('Fake OPA1612 from China'): a buyer bought 10 'OPA1612' from a Chinese eBay seller for €6.80 in total. Under the microscope they turned out to be relabelled TL082 JFET dies, identified by the cross-shaped 'butterfly' FET input devices, not bipolar OPA1612 dies. A TI E2E thread, 'OPA1612 - Fake chips?' (532783), exists; its content was not seen. diyAudio thread 399994 ('OPA1612 from Aliexpress, no counterfeit but why so cheap?') reports cheap AliExpress parts that decapped as genuine OPA1612 dies, so cheap does not always mean fake. The roughly 150 nA input bias reported there (discovery note) is inside the ±250 nA max. Screening tips from the datasheet: (1) TI lists only SOIC-8 (D) and, for the OPA1612, SON-8 (DRG), so a DIP-8 body marked OPA161x is not a TI package. (2) The genuine SOIC top mark is 'OPA' over '1611A' or '1612A', and the DRG mark is 'OVII'. (3) Iq should be about 3.6 mA per channel, 4.5 mA max. (4) Input bias should be tens of nA; pA-level bias points to a JFET relabel. (5) Noise should be about 1.1 nV/√Hz, so a relabelled TL08x is roughly ten times noisier. Buy from TI or authorised distributors.
