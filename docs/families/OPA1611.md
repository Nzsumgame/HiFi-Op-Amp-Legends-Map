# OPA1611 / OPA1612 (TI SoundPlus bipolar-input, 1.1 nV/√Hz single/dual)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*TI's 1.1 nV/√Hz bipolar audio op-amp with −136 dB THD+N at 1 kHz. It is widely used for I/V conversion and buffering in modern DACs, DAPs and Hi-Fi phones.*

**Technology:** Bipolar-input, voltage-feedback op-amp, unity-gain stable. The output is rail-to-rail (within 0.6 V at 2 kΩ, 0.2 V at 10 kΩ). The inputs have back-to-back differential clamp diodes. Copper leadframe (SBOS450C §7.3.1). The Rev C datasheet is titled 'SoundPlus'. Unverified mirror titles suggest that 2009–2011 copies carried 'Burr-Brown Audio' branding, and TI's current parametric listing tags the part 'Burr-Brown™ Audio' under Features. The OPA1611 comes in SOIC-8 (D) only. The OPA1612 comes in SOIC-8 (D) or SON-8 3×3 mm (DRG), which TI's parametric table calls WSON. No DIP package appears in the datasheet or the orderable addendum, and no DIP orderable is known.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Its measured noise and distortion are exceptional (1.1 nV/√Hz, −136 dB THD+N), and measurement-oriented users treat it as essentially transparent (discovery note). Soomal teardowns show it spreading through Chinese Hi-Fi phones and DAPs from 2014: FiiO X5, Meizu MX4 Pro, vivo X5 Max, Xiaomi Note and Smartisan T2. In 2022 Soomal said a very large share of recent products use the OPA1612 for I/V conversion. Japanese hobbyists call it a neutral 'reference sound' all-rounder (discovery note, not re-verified). The subjective camp is split. Soomal found it 'hard, not pleasing' (偏硬，不够讨喜) in a 2020 AE-9 op-amp swap, though with better dynamics and resolution than the NE5534. In 2016 Soomal argued that bipolar parts like the OPA1612 average worse sonically than DiFET types such as the OPA2134 and OPA2604; that passage wrongly calls the OPA1612 a 'Bipolar FET'. Discovery notes credit an audioXpress BJT-input distortion test, attributed to Douglas Self, with favouring it over the NE5532. Neither the finding nor the authorship was verified.

**How people describe the sound:** neutral / 'reference sound' (JP, maimai-audio; discovery note, unverified), flat, not genre-dependent (JP owners; discovery note, unverified), transparent (measurement community; discovery note), musical, delicate and warm (OPA1611; Audiogon thread per discovery note; low confidence), hard / 偏硬, not pleasing (Soomal 2020, AE-9 op-amp swap, where it is written 'OP1611'), better dynamics and resolution than NE5534 (same Soomal 2020 passage), average sound worse than DiFET types (Soomal 2016, 901 player / MINIBOX review)

**Typical uses:** DAC I/V conversion: Sabaj A20d (2022), with three OPA1612 for I/V and separate op-amps marked '2149U' doing the LPF, and Topping DX7 Pro (both per Soomal 2022), Output LPF in DAPs (SoundAware MR1 with dual CS4398, per Soomal 2017), Post-I/V amplifier or buffer in Hi-Fi phones and DAPs: Meizu MX4 Pro (2014), vivo X5 Max (2014), Xiaomi Note (2015, after ADA4896 I/V), Smartisan T2 (2016, two OPA1612 replacing one OPA2604), FiiO X5 (2014, with an LMH6643 buffer), all per Soomal, Input or error amplifier in VFA-CFA composite headphone amps with TPA6120 or THS6012 (e.g. the PWieland/SCAM project), Professional audio, microphone preamps, mixing consoles, broadcast gear, audio test equipment and A/V receivers (TI's listed applications), Op-amp rolling via DIP-8 adapter boards

**Caveats:**

- Current noise is fairly high (1.7 pA/√Hz @1 kHz). With source or feedback networks of 10 kΩ or more it loses its noise advantage over FET-input parts, as a JP hobbyist's 2026 design notes (indare/pcb_work) also found.
- The input common-mode range stops 2 V from each rail, so G = +1 buffers clip early on low supplies.
- The back-to-back input diodes can conduct on fast edges in low-gain or G = +1 circuits. TI says to limit input current to 10 mA.
- At low gain, capacitive loads reduce phase margin and can cause peaking or oscillation; TI suggests about 50 Ω series isolation (§8.4). Layout and decoupling on DIP adapters matter.
- Iq is 3.6 mA per channel and θJA of SOIC-8 is 150 °C/W. TI says soldering rather than socketing reduces temperature rise.
- It comes only in SOIC or SON, so every 'DIP' part is an adapter board of variable quality and chip provenance. Counterfeit reports exist but were not re-verified in this pass.
- The subjective descriptors conflict with each other, and no controlled listening test was verified. Soomal's DiFET-vs-bipolar generalisation is its own opinion and mislabels the OPA1612 as 'Bipolar FET'.

## Counterfeits

None of the counterfeit reports were re-read in this pass, so confidence is low to medium. Discovery notes cite a diyAudio thread ('Fake OPA1612 from China', 332536) and a TI E2E thread ('OPA1612 - fake chips', 532783) reporting relabelled TL082-class JFET parts sold on AliExpress and Taobao, often on DIP-8 adapter boards. Another diyAudio thread (399994) reports cheap AliExpress OPA1612s that seemed genuine but measured about 150 nA input bias. That is above the 60 nA typical but inside the ±250 nA max, so it is not proof of a fake. Screening tips from datasheet facts: (1) TI lists only SOIC-8 (D) and, for the OPA1612, SON-8 (DRG), so a DIP-8 body marked OPA161x is not a TI package. (2) The genuine SOIC top mark is 'OPA' over '1611A' or '1612A', and the DRG mark is 'OVII'. (3) Iq should be about 3.6 mA per channel, 4.5 mA max. (4) Input bias should be tens of nA; pA-level bias points to a JFET relabel. (5) Noise should be about 1.1 nV/√Hz, so a relabelled TL08x is roughly ten times noisier. Buy from TI or authorised distributors.

## Open questions

- Research limit: in this verification pass the session-wide WebSearch budget was already used up (200/200), so none of the 15+ requested web queries could run; all three attempts were refused. As a substitute, this pass used GitHub code search (about 20 queries) and GitHub-hosted files: two TI-served Rev C PDFs, TI PSpice model headers, a TI parametric CSV snapshot, KiCad and EDA libraries, and the Soomal mirror. Non-GitHub URLs could not be re-checked and are listed as unverified.
- No PCN search was possible. Fab, assembly-site or die-change PCNs for OPA1611/OPA1612 are still outstanding. No silicon change is known, and none is implied by the datasheet history.
- SBOS450 (original, Jul 2009) and SBOS450A (Aug 2009): no content or URL found. Their change logs are not in Rev C.
- Which pre-2014 mirror holds Rev A and which holds Rev B is unconfirmed. There are two 'Burr-Brown Audio' front-page layouts (Mouser/Qobuz/Octopart-21221010 vs Reichelt/ic114), consistent with Rev B's 'Updated front-page figure'. The headers need checking. No older revision was found on GitHub: code searches for SBOS450A, SBOS450B and 'REVISED JULY 2011' returned nothing, and the GitHub PDF copies found are all Rev C.
- Inferred, not seen: the TI URLs sbos450b/sbos450b.pdf, sbos450a/sbos450a.pdf, sbos450/sbos450.pdf, slos931a/slos931a.pdf, slos931/slos931.pdf, and the focus.ti.com symlinks. They appear only as archive seeds.
- OPA1612-Q1 (SLOS931): rev A is confirmed as of Aug 2022, but whether a later revision exists is unknown. The −40 to +125 °C and AEC-Q100 details, the document number of the Chinese translation and the lifecycle of OPA1612AQDRQ1 were not verified.
- The Rev C datasheet contradicts itself on output drive: ±30 mA on the front page against ±40 mA in §7.1. TI's parametric table gives 55 mA 'Iout typ', which matches ISC +55 mA.
- DRG (SON-8) has an IB max of ±300 nA against ±250 nA for SOIC. It is presumably the same die with package-related guard-banding, which TI does not state.
- TI's PSpice model for OPA161x is found in a folder named 'sbom396g', suggesting the model package is TI literature SBOM396 revision G. This is inferred from a folder name only.
- Branding: TI's current parametric listing tags the part 'Burr-Brown™ Audio' while the Rev C datasheet title says 'SoundPlus'. The pre-2014 'Burr-Brown Audio' front pages rest only on the previous run's mirror titles.
- The TL082-relabel counterfeit reports (diyAudio 332536, TI E2E 532783), the 150 nA AliExpress report (diyAudio 399994), the audioXpress findings and their attribution to Douglas Self, the maimai-audio 'reference sound' description and the Audiogon descriptors were taken from discovery notes and not re-read.
- No quad OPA161x was found; this was not exhaustively verified.
