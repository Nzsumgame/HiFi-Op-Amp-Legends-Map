# AD811 (Analog Devices single high-speed current-feedback op-amp, used as a DAC I/V stage)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*A very fast (about 2500 V/us) current-feedback video amp that diyAudio builders use as the I/V converter for current-output DACs such as the PCM61 and PCM1702.*

**Technology:** Single bipolar current-feedback video op-amp: about 2500 V/us slew, about 25 ns settling, high output current (from the draft and diyAudio posts; not re-checked against the datasheet this round)

## Why enthusiasts rate it

According to diyAudio posters (the dedicated thread 'AD811 as I/V stage for current DACs...', the build thread 'AD811 IV completed and tested', and the long 'Best opamp for I/V conversion?' thread), the AD811 sounds much better than other op-amps in a classic I/V stage, and better than an OPA861 alternative. Posters credit its 2500 V/us slew and 25 ns settling, against about 550 ns for the OPA627. They usually pair it with PCM61, PCM1702 and similar current-output DACs, and they warn that it needs a small heatsink at about +/-14 V. These are forum impressions, not measured comparisons.

**How people describe the sound:** better than other op-amps in a classic I/V stage (diyAudio posters)

**Typical uses:** I/V conversion for current-output DACs (PCM61, PCM1702 and similar), Video / high-speed line driver (vendor's intended use)

**Caveats:**

- Runs hot: posters advise a small heatsink at about +/-14 V
- It is current-feedback, so the feedback resistor value sets bandwidth and stability. A capacitor directly across the feedback resistor can make it oscillate, so it is not a drop-in replacement for voltage-feedback op-amps
- Single channel: a dual-op-amp socket needs an adapter
- Praise comes from forum listening impressions only
