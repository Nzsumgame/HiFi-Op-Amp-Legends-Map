# AD844

**Current-feedback op-amp, single, with TZ node** · Analog Devices (c. 1989–present) · Tier B (widely praised)

*Its ~50 Ω current-feedback input and exposed TZ node make it a DIY favourite for open-loop DAC I/V stages.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | Supply range | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 nV/√Hz | 60 MHz (G = −1), 33 MHz (G = −10) | 2000 V/µs | ±4.5 V to ±18 V | 6.5 mA |

## Why it's rated

A DAC DIY favourite for I/V: DAC current enters the low-impedance inverting input and the TZ node feeds a buffer, usually without global feedback. The long diyAudio thread 'Using the AD844 as an I/V' calls it the 'best opamp based I/V so far by a long way' and rates it above the OPA627. These are forum opinions, not measurements.

**Used in:** Open-loop I/V for current-output DACs, buffered by OPA627, 2–3 paralleled per channel for lower I/V impedance, High-speed video and pulse amplification, Current-conveyor (CCII+) building block

## Watch out

- Feedback resistor sets bandwidth; a capacitor directly in the feedback path invites oscillation.
- Open-loop TZ use leaves gain, linearity and DC offset uncorrected by feedback.
- 6.5 mA per part adds up when several are paralleled.
- Only SMD package is SOIC-16W; no 8-lead SOIC for drop-in swaps.
- Praise comes from subjective forum listening.
- **Counterfeits:** No AD844-specific counterfeit reports, but remarked eBay/AliExpress op-amps are well documented (Hackaday, Super-Freq). Buy from authorized distributors; be wary of loose 'AD844AN/JN' DIPs sold as new, since current production is AD844ANZ.

## Silicon changes

None documented. Compare datasheet revisions before assuming old and new parts are identical.
