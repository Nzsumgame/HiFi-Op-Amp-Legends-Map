# AD711 / AD712 / AD713 (ADI precision BiFET single / dual / quad)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*ADI's 1986 laser-trimmed BiFET family (0.0003% THD, 20 V/us, pA bias), sold as an LF412/TL082 upgrade. The AD712 was a classic 1990s TL072 swap in CD players and DIY preamps.*

**Technology:** BiFET: JFET-input op amp with bipolar gain and output stages. Offset and drift are set by laser wafer trimming, per the ADI data sheet. Internally compensated and unity-gain stable. Common-mode range is asymmetric (+14.5 V / -11.5 V at ±15 V supplies).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

The AD712 was a mainstream 'better' audio dual in the late 1980s and 1990s. The rec.audio FAQ (v1.5, June 1993) lists it among the better op amps for audio, next to NE5532, LM833, AD827 and OPA2604. ADI marketed it as an 'enhanced replacement for LF412 and TL082', so DIYers used it as a drop-in upgrade for TL072/TL082 sockets in CD players, DACs and preamps. Its strengths are 0.0003% THD, 120 dB crosstalk, 20 V/µs slew and pA bias, which suit high-impedance line stages and filters. It is now a tier-C legacy part, eclipsed by lower-noise JFET parts. No modern measurements (Audio Science Review, Samuel Groner) were found this session, and no verified sonic descriptors were collected.

**Typical uses:** Drop-in TL072/TL082/LF412 upgrade in CD-player and DAC output/filter stages, High-impedance line buffers and active filters (the data sheet includes second-order and 9-pole Chebyshev filter examples), DAC/ADC buffers and I/V stages that need fast settling (the data sheet shows AD565A/AD7545 settling examples), Photodiode and electrometer preamps (pA bias, with guarding)

**Caveats:**

- Voltage noise of 18 nV/√Hz at 1 kHz is high next to bipolar parts (NE5532, OP27) and modern JFETs (OPA1642, OPA827). It is a poor choice for low-impedance or MM-phono inputs.
- Output phase reversal if either input goes more negative than about -14.1 V at ±15 V supplies. The CM range reaches only -11.5 V, so avoid large negative input swings (data sheet footnote).
- Needs at least ±4.5 V supplies and is not rail-to-rail, so it does not suit low-voltage portable or USB-powered gear.
- Output is about 25 mA. It is not a headphone driver.
- Loads above about 1500 pF need a 100 Ω isolation resistor (data sheet Figure 46).
- JFET bias current doubles every 10 °C. Hot chassis raise bias and offset errors in DC-coupled designs.
- Pinout is the standard dual (AD712) / single (AD711); check the single-channel pinout before rolling.

## Counterfeits

No documented counterfeit AD711/712/713 parts were found. The discovery hint that 'AD711-class dies are found in remarked fake OPA627s' is only partly supported. The widely cited ZeptoBars decap (2014, also covered by Hackaday) found an AD744 die under an OPA627 marking. The AD711 attribution comes from forum summaries and is unconfirmed (low confidence). Electrical clues: an AD711-class die would slew about 20 V/µs, far below the OPA627's roughly 55 V/µs. Its supply current would also be well below the ~7 mA per channel that the OPA627 counterfeit checks use; AD712 Rev. I gives 5.0 mA typ for two amplifiers. Data sheets before Rev. D (9/2001) carried a metallization photograph, which is useful for comparing decapped dies. Buy AD712 from authorised distributors. Genuine current production uses Z (RoHS) suffixes for PDIP/SOIC.

## Related parts and alternatives

ADI ADA4627-1 / ADA4625-1/-2 / ADA4610 (modern ADI JFET-input precision parts; LTspice models added 2018), ADI AD8610 / AD8620 (lower-noise ADI JFET single/dual), TI OPA1641 / OPA1642 / OPA1644 (low-distortion audio JFET single/dual/quad), TI OPA2134 / OPA134 / OPA4134 (audio FET-input drop-in; see the TI 2024 die-change caveat), TI OPA827 / OPA828 (premium low-noise JFET singles), ADI AD825 (fast JFET; named by some DIYers as the better AD712 replacement; unverified), ADI AD823 (JFET-input dual, rail-to-rail output; common portable-audio swap), TL072 / LF412 / ADTL082 (the parts the AD712 was designed to replace; lower cost, higher offset)
