# AD711 / AD712 / AD713 (ADI precision BiFET single / dual / quad)

Analog Devices (1986 to present) · Tier C (niche / baseline)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** ADI PCN 25_0091 qualifies the Camas, WA wafer fab for the ADI bipolar process 'to ensure reliable and continuous supply'. [Details](#silicon-changes)

*ADI's laser-trimmed BiFET family (AD712 ©1986; 0.0003% THD, 20 V/µs, pA bias), sold as an LF411/412 and TL081/082 upgrade and a common 1990s DIY TL072 swap.*

| Slew rate | Supply range |
|---|---|
| 20 V/µs typ; 16 V/µs min (J/A/S), 18 V/µs min (K) | ±4.5 V to ±18 V operating; rated at ±15 V; absolute max ±18 V |

## Why it's rated

The AD712 was a mainstream 'better' audio dual in the late 1980s and 1990s. The rec.audio FAQ (v1.5, June 1993, from an archived text file) lists it among the better op amps for audio, next to NE5532, LM833, AD827 and OPA2604. ADI marketed the AD711/AD712 as enhanced replacements for the LF411/TL081 and LF412/TL082, so DIYers used the AD712 as a drop-in upgrade in TL072/TL082 sockets. Its strengths are 0.0003% THD, 120 dB crosstalk, 20 V/µs slew and pA bias, which suit high-impedance line stages and filters. It is now a tier-C legacy part, overtaken by lower-noise JFET parts. The discovery claims about CD-player use and Walt Jung's ADI-era writing were not web-verified. No modern measurements and no verified sonic descriptors were collected.

**Used in:** Drop-in TL072/TL082/LF412 upgrade in CD-player and DAC output/filter stages (DIY practice; attributed to forum sources), High-impedance line buffers and active filters (the data sheet includes second-order and 9-pole Chebyshev filter examples), DAC/ADC buffers and I/V stages that need fast settling (the data sheet shows AD565A/AD7545 settling examples), Photodiode and electrometer preamps (pA bias, with guarding)

## Watch out

- Voltage noise of 18 nV/√Hz at 1 kHz is high next to bipolar parts (NE5532, OP27) and modern JFETs (OPA1642, OPA827). It is a poor choice for low-impedance or MM-phono inputs.
- Output phase reversal if either input goes more negative than about -14.1 V at ±15 V supplies. The CM range reaches only -11.5 V (data sheet footnote).
- Needs at least ±4.5 V supplies and is not rail-to-rail.
- Output is about 25 mA. It is not a headphone driver.
- Loads above about 1500 pF need a 100 Ω isolation resistor (data sheet Figure 46).
- JFET bias current doubles every 10 °C. Hot chassis raise bias and offset errors in DC-coupled designs.
- Pinout is the standard single (AD711) / dual (AD712) / quad (AD713). Check the pinout before swapping parts.
- **Counterfeits:** No counterfeit AD711/712/713 parts have been documented. The risk runs the other way: fake OPA627s. The ZeptoBars decap (2014, covered by Hackaday) found an AD744 die under an OPA627 marking. Users in the Head-Fi thread 'fake opa627's in the wild' also report remarked AD711-type parts sold as OPA627, with about 18 kΩ measured between pins as a check. That report is forum-level only (low confidence). Electrical clues: an AD711-class die slews about 20 V/µs, against about 55 V/µs for the OPA627. It also draws far less supply current (AD712 Rev. I: 5.0 mA typ for two amplifiers). Data sheets before Rev. D (9/2001) carried a metallization photograph, which helps when comparing decapped dies. Buy from authorised distributors; current PDIP/SOIC production carries the Z (RoHS) suffix.

## Silicon changes

### Fab / process transfer: Analog Devices, PCN 25_0091 Rev. -, published 6 Jun 2025, effective 8 Sep 2025. A Rev. A form and a Rev. A qualification report followed; their dates were not captured.

ADI PCN 25_0091 qualifies the Camas, WA wafer fab for the ADI bipolar process 'to ensure reliable and continuous supply'. It lists multiple AD711, AD712 and AD713 part numbers. A companion report, 'Qualification of ADI Camas Wafer Fab Bipolar Process', mentions AD712 alongside AD624 and AD8221. The part numbers stay the same while the wafer fab changes. This is separate from PCN 26_0029 (Camas, Complementary Bipolar process), which covers AD797 and AD823.

- **Affected:** AD711 (variants not itemised in search summary), AD712 (multiple variants), AD713 (multiple variants)
- **How to tell:** None given. ADI says it keeps standard lot traceability. The summaries mention no marking change or new date-code scheme, and do not name the source fab.
- **Audio impact:** None documented. ADI states no expected impact to fit, form, function or reliability. A fab transfer of the same design can shift typical, unspecified parameters within data-sheet limits, but no listening or measurement comparison was found.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - same part numbers and data sheet, and a drop-in by ADI's statement. The main effect is lot provenance (Camas wafers versus the previous fab).
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Wafer fab site | previous ADI bipolar fab (not named in the sources seen) | ADI Camas, WA qualified |
