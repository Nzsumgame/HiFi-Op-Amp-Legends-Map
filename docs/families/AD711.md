# AD711 / AD712 / AD713 (ADI precision BiFET single / dual / quad)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*ADI's laser-trimmed BiFET family (AD712 ©1986; 0.0003% THD, 20 V/µs, pA bias), sold as an LF411/412 and TL081/082 upgrade and a common 1990s DIY TL072 swap.*

**Technology:** BiFET: JFET-input op amp with bipolar gain and output stages. Offset and drift are set by laser wafer trimming. Internally compensated and unity-gain stable. The AD713 is four AD711-type amplifiers on one monolithic die (AD713 Rev. F). Common-mode range is asymmetric (+14.5 V / -11.5 V at ±15 V supplies, AD712 Rev. I).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

The AD712 was a mainstream 'better' audio dual in the late 1980s and 1990s. The rec.audio FAQ (v1.5, June 1993, from an archived text file) lists it among the better op amps for audio, next to NE5532, LM833, AD827 and OPA2604. ADI marketed the AD711/AD712 as enhanced replacements for the LF411/TL081 and LF412/TL082, so DIYers used the AD712 as a drop-in upgrade in TL072/TL082 sockets. Its strengths are 0.0003% THD, 120 dB crosstalk, 20 V/µs slew and pA bias, which suit high-impedance line stages and filters. It is now a tier-C legacy part, overtaken by lower-noise JFET parts. The discovery claims about CD-player use and Walt Jung's ADI-era writing were not web-verified. No modern measurements and no verified sonic descriptors were collected.

**Typical uses:** Drop-in TL072/TL082/LF412 upgrade in CD-player and DAC output/filter stages (DIY practice; attributed to forum sources), High-impedance line buffers and active filters (the data sheet includes second-order and 9-pole Chebyshev filter examples), DAC/ADC buffers and I/V stages that need fast settling (the data sheet shows AD565A/AD7545 settling examples), Photodiode and electrometer preamps (pA bias, with guarding)

**Caveats:**

- Voltage noise of 18 nV/√Hz at 1 kHz is high next to bipolar parts (NE5532, OP27) and modern JFETs (OPA1642, OPA827). It is a poor choice for low-impedance or MM-phono inputs.
- Output phase reversal if either input goes more negative than about -14.1 V at ±15 V supplies. The CM range reaches only -11.5 V (data sheet footnote).
- Needs at least ±4.5 V supplies and is not rail-to-rail.
- Output is about 25 mA. It is not a headphone driver.
- Loads above about 1500 pF need a 100 Ω isolation resistor (data sheet Figure 46).
- JFET bias current doubles every 10 °C. Hot chassis raise bias and offset errors in DC-coupled designs.
- Pinout is the standard single (AD711) / dual (AD712) / quad (AD713). Check the pinout before swapping parts.

## Counterfeits

No counterfeit AD711/712/713 parts have been documented. The risk runs the other way: fake OPA627s. The ZeptoBars decap (2014, covered by Hackaday) found an AD744 die under an OPA627 marking. Users in the Head-Fi thread 'fake opa627's in the wild' also report remarked AD711-type parts sold as OPA627, with about 18 kΩ measured between pins as a check. That report is forum-level only (low confidence). Electrical clues: an AD711-class die slews about 20 V/µs, against about 55 V/µs for the OPA627. It also draws far less supply current (AD712 Rev. I: 5.0 mA typ for two amplifiers). Data sheets before Rev. D (9/2001) carried a metallization photograph, which helps when comparing decapped dies. Buy from authorised distributors; current PDIP/SOIC production carries the Z (RoHS) suffix.

## Open questions

- Web checking was limited to 5 searches. ADI's PCN database was not searched, so no die change, fab transfer or PCN was found for AD711/712/713. That means none was found, not that none exists. The RoHS 'Z' suffix transition (c. 2005–2006) was a lead-finish change, not a silicon change.
- ADI PDN 10_0081 Rev. B reportedly lists AD711JNZ and AD713JNZ (search summary only; not read). Were these PDIP options discontinued c. 2010, and which options remain in production?
- AD711 Rev. E: print date and revision history not captured. When were the AD711 B/C/S/T grades dropped, and is the TO-99 can obsolete?
- AD713 Rev. F date and change log, and the dates of Rev. C through E, are unknown.
- AD712-EP: orderable part number, grade and Rev. 0 date not captured.
- Revisions of the alldatasheet AD711 (12 pp) and AD712 (15 pp) copies and the Farnell AD713 copy were not seen. The Octopart copy's Rev. G identification rests on a search summary.
- The AD712 original (1986), Rev. A, B and C dates are unknown; the Rev. I change log starts at Rev. C→D (9/2001).
- Rev. F (6/2006) lists 'Changes to Specifications Section' without detail. A Rev. E (2002) copy is needed to check whether any limits changed.
- The report of AD711 dies inside fake OPA627s comes only from Head-Fi forum posts. The only documented decap is the AD744 case.
- The discovery claims about Walt Jung's ADI-era writing and CD-player/DAC use were not verified. No modern measurements or community sound descriptors were collected.
- The inferred URLs remain unverified: uppercase AD713.pdf, the /UploadedFiles/Data_Sheets/ pattern and the static/imported-files legacy paths.
