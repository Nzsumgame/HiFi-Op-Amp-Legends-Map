# LM6171 / LM6172 (National high-speed low-distortion voltage-feedback op-amp, single/dual)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*National's 100 MHz bipolar VFB video op-amp (LM6172 3000 V/us, LM6171 3600 V/us). Rollers call it neutral and detailed, but opinion is split and it is layout-sensitive.*

**Technology:** Bipolar high-speed voltage-feedback (VFB) amplifier, unity-gain stable, built for video and high-speed use. The LM6171 is the single, with A and B offset grades in the National models; the LM6172 is the dual. Vendor figures: 100 MHz unity-gain bandwidth; slew rate 3600 V/us for the LM6171 and 3000 V/us for the LM6172; supply current 2.5 mA for the LM6171 and 2.3 mA per channel for the LM6172.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A cult op-amp-rolling pick rather than a mainstream audio part, and opinion is split. JP: nabe's Chu-Moy comparison ranked the LM6172 'straightest' (least coloured), ahead of the OP275, and found the OPA627AP muddied detail. A Zigsow reviewer calls the LM6172IN/NOPB 'simply a high-speed stone'. Other JP listeners are negative: a goo blog post is titled 'LM6172 無駄買いですよ' (a wasted purchase), and other reports call it light in the bass and treble-leaning, or less resolving than the LM4562/LME49720 as a buffer. diyAudio posters call the LM6171/6172 'very detailed and neutral'. Head-Fi has dedicated threads (Mind the LM6171/2; OPA637 vs LM6171 mini shoot-out; LM6172's sound signature; LM6171 vs OPA604 vs OPA627) with mixed impressions, from neutral and clean to 'too thick/sombre' or 'technical, slightly cold'. The Chinese DiyEden/Soomal notes (2005) are a translation of an English first-person CMoy op-amp test that cites headwize threads; they are not independent CN opinion. Those notes say it has more powerful bass than the OPA132/134, a subtle but real difference from the OPA134PA at 15 V, and is better than the OPA132/134 at low supply. They also say it turns soft and round rather than harsh when starved of supply, and is not for beginners. Commercial use: per a Soomal teardown, the Shanling H1.1 DAC (2013) uses LM6171s in its headphone module. A search summary reports a CMoy test that ranked the LM6172 lowest in noise floor against the LM4562, NE5532, OPA2277, OPA2134 and OPA2132; it apparently comes from a UIUC Physics 406 student report, but that attribution is unconfirmed. No ASR or Groner measurement was found.

**How people describe the sound:** very detailed and neutral (diyAudio), straightest / least coloured (nabe), 'simply a high-speed stone' (Zigsow), more powerful bass than OPA132/134 (DiyEden translation), soft and very round when supply-starved, not harsh (DiyEden translation), light / weak bass, treble-leaning (some JP listeners), too thick / 'sombre' (some Head-Fi posters), technical, slightly cold (Head-Fi)

**Typical uses:** Op-amp rolling in Chu-Moy/CMoy headphone amps, Headphone output stage (Shanling H1.1 DAC uses the LM6171), DAC I/V or output stages in DIY builds, Video and high-speed signal paths, ADC drivers (original target market)

**Caveats:**

- Layout- and stability-sensitive ('cranky' per AMB forum and Head-Fi). Needs tight decoupling and a suitable circuit; risky in sockets and adapters.
- Not beginner-friendly; the result depends heavily on the surrounding circuit (DiyEden translation).
- Opinion is split: praise (nabe, diyAudio) sits beside clearly negative JP reviews (goo blog 'wasted purchase', light bass) and mixed Head-Fi impressions.
- Bass descriptions conflict: JP reports light bass, the translated CMoy notes report powerful bass.
- The DiyEden/Soomal translation wrongly calls it a 'high-speed FET-input' op-amp. The LM6171/6172 is bipolar.
- All praise is subjective listening; there are no vendor audio-THD claims aimed at audio users.

## Counterfeits

No specific counterfeit or remarking reports for the LM6171 or LM6172 were found in the available evidence. This pass had no fresh web search, so this is not a confirmed negative. The DIP-8 LM6172IN and LM6171BIN are the parts rollers want. Marketplace DIP listings exist (for example the Amazon.co.jp 'Phoncoo' LM6172IN DIP8). Genuine TI-era LM6172IN/NOPB DIP stock has been sold by JP retailers (Bispa, eleshop.jp). Before trusting cheap 'National' DIP stock, check the logo (National vs TI), date code, laser marking and /NOPB-era labelling.
