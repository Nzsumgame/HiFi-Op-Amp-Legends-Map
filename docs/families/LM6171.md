# LM6171 / LM6172

**High-speed bipolar voltage-feedback op-amp, 1/2 channels** · National Semiconductor (by 1996–2011) → Texas Instruments (2011–present) · Tier B (widely praised)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** TI qualified FFAB (Freising) with BICOMHD (FR-BIP-1, 200 mm) for the LM6172 beside National's DL-LIN VIP3 (150 mm), with a changed die and added TI Mexico assembly. [Details](#silicon-changes)

*National's 100 MHz video op-amp (3600 / 3000 V/µs). Rollers call it neutral and detailed, but opinion is split.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 nV/√Hz (LM6172, unconfirmed) | 100 MHz unity-gain bandwidth | 3600 V/µs (LM6171); 3000 V/µs (LM6172) | 2.5 mA (LM6171); 2.3 mA per channel (LM6172) |

## Why it's rated

A cult rolling pick with split opinion. nabe ranked the LM6172 'straightest', ahead of the OP275; diyAudio posters call it very detailed and neutral. Some JP listeners call it a wasted purchase with light bass; Head-Fi is mixed. The Shanling H1.1 DAC uses LM6171s, per Soomal. No ASR measurements. All reports predate the 2024 LM6172 die change.

**Described as:** very detailed, neutral, straightest, least coloured, 'simply a high-speed stone', stronger bass than OPA132/134, soft when supply-starved, light, treble-leaning bass, too thick, 'sombre', technical, slightly cold

**Used in:** Op-amp rolling in Chu-Moy/CMoy headphone amps, Headphone output stage (Shanling H1.1 uses LM6171), DAC I/V or output stages in DIY builds, Video, high-speed paths, ADC drivers (original target)

## Watch out

- Layout- and stability-sensitive ('cranky' per AMB and Head-Fi); needs tight decoupling; risky in sockets.
- Not beginner-friendly; results depend heavily on the surrounding circuit.
- Opinion split, bass reports conflict; the DiyEden/Soomal CMoy translation wrongly calls it FET-input.
- From 2025 LM6172 stock may carry the new BICOMHD die; reviews describe VIP3 silicon.
- No vendor audio-THD figures; a CMoy noise-floor ranking (UIUC P406 report) is unconfirmed.
- **Counterfeits:** No LM6171/LM6172 fakes reported. Rollers want DIP LM6172IN and LM6171BIN; Bispa and eleshop.jp retailed genuine stock. Check logo, date code and /NOPB marking. Since 2025, NiPdAu leads or a different pin-1 mark may mean genuine new-process parts.

## Silicon changes

### Die redesign: Texas Instruments, 2024-12-18 · PCN 20241217001.1

TI qualified FFAB (Freising) with BICOMHD (FR-BIP-1, 200 mm) for the LM6172 beside National's DL-LIN VIP3 (150 mm), with a changed die and added TI Mexico assembly. Part of TI's 150 mm exit. First ship 2025-03-18. One part number now covers two silicon builds.

- **Affected:** LM6172 family ('select devices'; orderables not listed here)
- **How to tell:** Datasheet SNOS792E. Mexico vs Melaka assembly differs in lead finish (matte Sn vs NiPdAu), compounds, pin-1 notch vs dimple; shows site, not fab. Risk: 2025+ date codes.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. A new process can change noise, distortion, slew and phase margin. All listening reports predate 2025 (VIP3 silicon).
- **Drop-in risk:** medium: redesigned die on a different process; SNOS792E changes unknown; socketed stability margins may differ.
- **Confidence:** high

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Fab / process | DL-LIN VIP3, 150 mm wafers | FR-BIP-1 (FFAB) BICOMHD, 200 mm wafers (added) |
| Die | Original National VIP3 die | Changed die (per the PCN summary) |
| Assembly site | TI Melaka | TI Melaka or TI Mexico (added), with different lead finish, mold/mount compound and pin-1 mark |
| Datasheet | SNOS792D (Mar 2013) | SNOS792E (Dec 2024); electrical-table changes not captured |
