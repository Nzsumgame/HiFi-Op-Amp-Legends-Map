# LM6171 / LM6172 (National high-speed low-distortion voltage-feedback op-amp, single/dual)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*National's 100 MHz bipolar VFB video op-amp (LM6171 3600 V/us, LM6172 3000 V/us). Rollers call it neutral and detailed, but opinion is split and it is layout-sensitive.*

**Technology:** Bipolar high-speed voltage-feedback (VFB) amplifier, unity-gain stable, built for video and high-speed use. It was made on National's VIP3 complementary bipolar process: TI PCN 20241217001.1 names 'DL-LIN VIP3, 150 mm' as the LM6172's current process. The LM6171 is the single, with A and B offset grades; the LM6172 is the dual. Vendor figures: 100 MHz unity-gain bandwidth; slew rate 3600 V/us (LM6171) and 3000 V/us (LM6172); supply current 2.5 mA (LM6171) and 2.3 mA per channel (LM6172). Since the Dec 2024 PCN, the LM6172 is also built on TI's BICOMHD process at FFAB on 200 mm wafers, with a changed die.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded: Die redesign).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A cult op-amp-rolling pick, not a mainstream audio part, and opinion is split. JP: nabe's Chu-Moy comparison ranked the LM6172 'straightest' (least coloured), ahead of the OP275. A Zigsow reviewer calls the LM6172IN/NOPB 'simply a high-speed stone'. Other JP listeners are negative: a goo blog post is titled 'LM6172 無駄買いですよ' (a wasted purchase), and others report light bass or say it resolves less than the LM4562 as a buffer. diyAudio posters call the LM6171/6172 'very detailed and neutral'. Head-Fi threads give mixed impressions, from neutral and clean to 'too thick/sombre' or 'technical, slightly cold'. The Chinese DiyEden/Soomal notes (2005) translate an English CMoy test; they are not independent CN opinion. They report stronger bass than the OPA132/134 and say it goes soft rather than harsh when supply-starved. Commercial use: per a Soomal teardown, the Shanling H1.1 DAC (2013) uses LM6171s in its headphone module. A CMoy noise-floor ranking that puts the LM6172 lowest is attributed, unconfirmed, to a UIUC Physics 406 student report. No ASR or Groner measurement was found. All these impressions predate the Dec 2024 LM6172 die change (PCN 20241217001.1).

**How people describe the sound:** very detailed and neutral (diyAudio), straightest / least coloured (nabe), 'simply a high-speed stone' (Zigsow), more powerful bass than OPA132/134 (DiyEden translation), soft and round when supply-starved, not harsh (DiyEden translation), light bass, treble-leaning (some JP listeners), too thick / 'sombre' (some Head-Fi posters), technical, slightly cold (Head-Fi)

**Typical uses:** Op-amp rolling in Chu-Moy/CMoy headphone amps, Headphone output stage (the Shanling H1.1 DAC uses the LM6171), DAC I/V or output stages in DIY builds, Video and high-speed signal paths, ADC drivers (original target market)

**Caveats:**

- Layout- and stability-sensitive ('cranky' per AMB forum and Head-Fi). Needs tight decoupling; risky in sockets and adapters.
- Not beginner-friendly; the result depends heavily on the surrounding circuit (DiyEden translation).
- Opinion is split: praise (nabe, diyAudio) sits beside negative JP reviews and mixed Head-Fi impressions.
- Bass descriptions conflict between the JP reports and the translated CMoy notes.
- The DiyEden/Soomal translation wrongly calls it FET-input. The LM6171/6172 is bipolar.
- From 2025, LM6172 stock may carry the new BICOMHD die (PCN 20241217001.1). Listening reports refer to the older VIP3 silicon.
- All praise is subjective listening; there are no vendor audio-THD claims.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Die redesign: Texas Instruments, PCN 20241217001.1 issued 2024-12-18; proposed first ship…

TI qualified FFAB (Freising) with the BICOMHD process (FR-BIP-1, 200 mm) for the LM6172, alongside the original National DL-LIN VIP3 process (150 mm). The die changed as a result. This is part of TI's exit from 150 mm fabs. The same part number now covers two different silicon builds on different bipolar processes.

- **When:** PCN 20241217001.1 issued 2024-12-18; proposed first ship 2025-03-18
- **Affected:** LM6172 family (the PCN says 'select devices'; specific orderables were not captured)
- **How to tell old from new:** New material goes with datasheet SNOS792E (the previous revision was D). Per the PCN, TI Melaka and TI Mexico assembly differ in lead finish (matte Sn vs NiPdAu), mount and mold compound, and pin-1 designator (notch vs dimple). The site-to-feature mapping should be read from the PCN. These features show only the assembly site, not the fab. Because FFAB is added rather than replacing DL-LIN, old (VIP3) and new (BICOMHD) die can ship under the same orderable. Parts with date codes from 2025 onward are the ones at risk. Whether the LM6171 single is covered by this PCN was not confirmed.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. A new bipolar process and die can change noise, distortion, slew and phase margin. All LM6172 listening impressions predate 2025 and refer to VIP3 silicon. Rollers should not assume new stock sounds or behaves the same, especially in the layout-sensitive, socketed builds this part is known for.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium: the die was redesigned on a different bipolar process, and the SNOS792E electrical changes were not captured. Stability margins in high-speed, socketed circuits could differ.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Verification:** This entry was not from the sweep and was kept as adversarially checked by an earlier pass. Two extra WebSearches did not surface the PCN 20241217001 text or show whether LM6171 is included. Search snippets of the current LM6172 datasheet still describe the part as VIP III, so check the SNOS792E revision history for the BICOMHD note.

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Fab / process | DL-LIN VIP3, 150 mm wafers | FR-BIP-1 (FFAB) BICOMHD, 200 mm wafers (added) |
| Die | Original National VIP3 die | Changed die (per the PCN summary) |
| Assembly site | TI Melaka | TI Melaka or TI Mexico (added), with different lead finish, mold/mount compound and pin-1 mark |
| Datasheet | SNOS792D (Mar 2013) | SNOS792E (Dec 2024); electrical-table changes not captured |

## Other change notes (lifecycle, packaging, successors, lore)

### 1. Lifecycle: Texas Instruments, PDN 20230906005 (rev .3), 2023.

TI PDN 20230906005.3, 'Discontinuance of Select Devices', includes LM6171BIM, with LM6171AIMX/NOPB recommended as the pin-to-pin replacement. DigiKey lists LM6171BIM/NOPB as obsolete and no longer manufactured. The LM6171 family itself remains Active in the A grade. The Nov 2023 datasheet revision (SNOS745D) coincides with the PDN. Its change notes were not seen, so no spec delta is claimed.

- **When:** PDN 20230906005 (rev .3), 2023. The LM6171 datasheet was revised to SNOS745D in November 2023.
- **Affected:** LM6171BIM/NOPB, LM6171BIM, LM6171BIN (listed obsolete by distributors; not confirmed in the PDN text)
- **How to tell old from new:** Discontinuance of the B-grade LM6171 orderables, not a silicon change. The A grade (LM6171AIM/NOPB, LM6171AIMX/NOPB) remains Active, and the PDN names LM6171AIMX/NOPB as the pin-to-pin replacement. Any B-grade part sold now is old stock.
- **Audio impact:** None to the silicon. The B and A grades are selections of the same die. Only B-grade availability is affected.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: grade discontinuance only. The recommended A-grade replacement has the same pinout and is the higher-grade selection.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Verification:** New in this curation pass. Two WebSearches found PDN 20230906005.3 listing LM6171BIM with LM6171AIMX/NOPB as the replacement, and DigiKey showing LM6171BIM/NOPB obsolete. This comes from search summaries only; the PDN PDF was not read, and LM6171BIN's presence in it is unconfirmed.

### Revision chain status

LM6171 (single). National DS012336 is newly identified from the datasheetcatalog filename. Known chain: National DS012336 edition(s), undated in the search results; the TI header gives an original date of May 1998; the 1996 SPICE models suggest an earlier first release. Then TI SNOS745B, implied and unconfirmed. Then SNOS745C (March 2013, header seen; new copy at danomsk.ru). Then SNOS745D (November 2023). Missing: National DS012336 edition dates and revisions, and SNOS745 A/B dates. Several National-era copies were found with revision not shown: pdf.datasheetcatalog DS012336.PDF, DigiKey media, Octopart LM6171BIN, alldatasheet 9003/NSC, ChipFind, datasheet4u, manualmachine, PE2BZ, waynestegall, yumpu. LM6172 (dual). The National DS number is still not found in these searches. Known chain: National edition(s) (TI gives an original date of May 1999; the LM6172 SPICE model was added on 16 April 1996). Alldatasheet has two distinct National uploads: a 16-page, 551 KB early edition (IDs 9004/9005/9008) and a 25-page, 1 MB NOPB-era edition (ID 578684), dates not shown. Then SNOS792C, which is odd: its indexed header reads 'MAY 2004 – REVISED SEPTEMBER 2004'. Then SNOS792D (March 2013) and SNOS792E (December 2024, BICOMHD die change per PCN 20241217001.1). Missing: the National DS number, National edition dates, SNOS792A/B, and change lists for all TI revisions. No second sources were found.

## Counterfeits

No specific counterfeit or remarking reports for the LM6171 or LM6172 were found. The DIP-8 LM6172IN and LM6171BIN are the parts rollers want. Marketplace DIP listings exist (e.g. Amazon.co.jp 'Phoncoo' LM6172IN DIP8). Genuine TI-era LM6172IN/NOPB DIP has been retailed by Bispa and eleshop.jp. Check the logo (National vs TI), date code, laser marking and /NOPB labelling. Separately, since PCN 20241217001.1 (first ship Mar 2025), genuine LM6172 can carry a new BICOMHD die. A 2025+ date code, NiPdAu leads or a different pin-1 mark do not mean a fake; they may be new-process parts.
