# LT1363 / LT1364 / LT1365 (single/dual/quad 70 MHz, 1000 V/us C-Load bipolar)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*Linear's 1994 70 MHz, 1000 V/us C-Load bipolar dual. A cheap Japanese favourite called fast and transparent; English forums are split between 'magic' and 'coloured'.*

**Technology:** Complementary bipolar process (Linear Technology's own). Voltage-feedback amplifier that slews like a current-feedback one: each input is a complementary NPN/PNP emitter follower driving a ~500 ohm resistor (R1), the currents are mirrored into a high-impedance gain node, and the output stage is a pair of complementary followers. The NPN and PNP base currents cancel to first order, so input bias current can be either polarity. C-Load compensation places an RC network across the output stage; it is bootstrapped under light loads and adds compensation under capacitive or low-impedance loads, so the part is stable with any capacitive load. Slew rate scales with input step size, so it is highest at low gain (tested at AV = -2).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Reputation is mixed. It rests on forum and blog listening reports, re-checked here only through search-result summaries captured earlier in this session; no independent measurements (ASR, Samuel Groner) were checked. English-language posts call it the 'magic' op-amp: dynamic and involving (though tonally 'rounded'), with 'much greater transparency and less grain'. They rank it with the AD8610 as an OPA627 alternative with a different flavour. A diyAudio 'best sounding opamps' thread describes it as heavily coloured and euphonic, simplifying the music and able to 'kill nuances' in high-quality systems. The same thread says it suits delta-sigma DAC filters and low-impedance headphones. Some listeners prefer the lower-bias LT1358 (same circuit). In Japan it is a popular value pick: Akizuki sells the LT1364CN8#PBF for under 1,000 yen. Bloggers call it fast and uncoloured, with precise imaging, a 'tight, lean-muscled' sound and clean treble without harshness. One blogger found it lighter in bass than the MUSE8820. A Zigsow review is titled 'cool sound, hot chip'. Zigsow opinions go both ways: one reviewer prefers its transparency to the OPA627AU, while another review puts the OPA627AP in a different league. Mineden says it covers the OPA2604's thin midrange. A 2023 pooq.biz post tries to recreate it with discrete parts.

**How people describe the sound:** transparent, less grain, dynamic, involving, tonally rounded (some), fast, uncoloured (Japanese reviews), pinpoint imaging, tight, lean-muscled, clean treble without harshness, lighter bass than MUSE8820 (one blog), heavily coloured / euphonic (diyAudio critics), cool sound (while the chip runs hot)

**Typical uses:** DAC I/V, output filter and buffer stages (delta-sigma DACs), op-amp rolling in DACs, headphone amps and sound cards (Xonar/Prodigy buffers; listed among swap options in the Maverick TubeMagic D1 FAQ), line buffers and cable drivers (datasheet application), video/RF amplification and fast data-acquisition buffers (datasheet)

**Caveats:**

- Mixed reputation. English forum views range from 'magic' and transparent to 'heavily coloured', and it can 'kill nuances' in high-end systems. Japanese reviews are mostly positive but still mixed against the OPA627.
- Runs hot: 6.3 mA typ (7.5 max) per amplifier. A DIP-8 dual at +/-15 V dissipates about 0.38 W, a rise of about 50 C at 130 C/W, which matches the 'hot chip' remark.
- 70 MHz GBW makes it sensitive to layout, sockets and decoupling. The datasheet asks for 0.01-0.1 uF RF-quality bypass close to the pins, plus CF > RG*CIN/RF when feedback resistors exceed 5 kohm. One Japanese DIY builder's GitHub design notes (indare/pcb_work) rank it the most layout-sensitive part in their stock. They predict, by analysis rather than measurement, ringing or oscillation with 47k/10k feedback and only 0.1 uF decoupling in a DIP socket.
- High input bias current (0.6 uA typ, 2 uA max, either polarity) produces large DC offsets with high-value bias or feedback resistors (about 200 mV worst case with 100 kohm). Use balanced source impedances or AC coupling.
- Voltage noise of 9 nV/rtHz is higher than the NE5532 or LM4562, with a 1/f corner of roughly 600-700 Hz per graph G05. It is a poor choice for low-impedance, high-gain or phono stages.
- Do not use as a comparator or with sustained differential input: supply current rises to tens of mA and the part may be damaged.
- C-grade parts are only guaranteed to meet specifications from 0 to 70 C.
- Slew rate depends on input level and is highest at low closed-loop gain.

## Counterfeits

No LT1363/4/5-specific counterfeit reports were found. The 'genuine Linear parts carry a distinctive white print' hint traces to a web-search summary of new_western_elec's October 2019 post 'Are fake op-amps everywhere?' (偽物のOPAMPが溢れている？). That post is about fakes of the OPA627, OPA637, OPA2604 and MUSES01. It lists silk-print appearance among its identification cues, and the summary mentions white silk print on genuine Linear parts. It is not an LT1364-specific statement, so treat it as low confidence. The same post calls slew-rate measurement the most effective fake test. Practical checks: the SO-8 marking should read '1364' per the datasheet. A genuine dual draws about 12.6 mA quiescent at +/-15 V (6.3 mA per amplifier typ, 15 mA max). A square-wave test at AV = -2 should show roughly 1000 V/us (750 min); a remarked slow bipolar dual would fail at once. Buy from ADI or authorised distributors (DigiKey, Mouser) or established Japanese retailers such as Akizuki. Lead-free #PBF/#TRPBF suffixes are ordering variants, not a sign of a fake.

## Related parts and alternatives

LT1361 / LT1362 (LT1360 single): same C-Load topology at 4 mA per amp, 50 MHz, 800 V/us, VOS 1 mV; runs cooler, LT1358 / LT1359 (LT1357 single): same topology at 2 mA per amp, 25 MHz, 600 V/us; some listeners prefer it, LT1813: dual 100 MHz, 700 V/us, 3 mA per amp; the LT1364 datasheet calls it a 'Low Voltage, Low Power LT1364/LT1365'. Check its supply rating before using it on +/-15 V rails (not verified here)., AD8610/AD8620 and OPA627/OPA2627: the JFET parts listeners compare it with, LME49720/LM4562, OPA1612, OPA1656: modern audio duals with much lower noise and distortion
