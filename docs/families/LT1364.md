# LT1363 / LT1364 / LT1365 (single/dual/quad 70 MHz, 1000 V/us C-Load bipolar)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*Linear's 1994 70 MHz, 1000 V/us C-Load bipolar dual. A cheap Japanese favourite called fast and transparent; English forums are split between 'magic' and 'coloured'.*

**Technology:** Complementary bipolar process (Linear Technology's own). Voltage-feedback amplifier that slews like a current-feedback one: each input is a complementary NPN/PNP emitter follower driving a ~500 ohm resistor (R1), the currents are mirrored into a high-impedance gain node, and the output stage is a pair of complementary followers. The NPN and PNP base currents cancel to first order, so input bias current can be either polarity. C-Load compensation places an RC network across the output stage; it is bootstrapped under light loads and adds compensation under capacitive or low-impedance loads, so the part is stable with any capacitive load. Slew rate scales with input step size, so it is highest at low gain (tested at AV = -2).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Reputation is mixed and rests on forum and blog listening reports; no independent measurements (ASR, Samuel Groner) were found. On Head-Fi, one user found it as quiet as the OPA2134 but with 'much greater transparency and less grain'; another says OPA6x7 parts lose pace and rhythm while the LT1364 is fast and detailed; a third rates it and the OPA627 as equally good with different flavours (an AD8610 grouping from an earlier summary was not re-confirmed). A diyAudio 'best sounding opamps' thread says it suits delta-sigma DAC filters and low-impedance headphones thanks to its slew rate and low distortion at low gain, but critics there call it heavily coloured and euphonic, able to 'kill nuances' in high-quality systems. Some listeners prefer the lower-bias LT1358 (same circuit). In Japan it is a popular value pick sold by Akizuki (LT1364CN8#PBF); bloggers call it fast and uncoloured with precise imaging, a 'tight, lean-muscled' sound and clean treble. One blogger found it lighter in bass than the MUSE8820. Zigsow reviews go both ways against the OPA627 (one prefers the LT1364's transparency to the OPA627AU, another puts the OPA627AP in a different league). Mineden says it covers the OPA2604's thin midrange. A 2023 pooq.biz post tries to recreate it with discrete parts.

**How people describe the sound:** transparent, less grain (vs OPA2134, one Head-Fi user), fast / detailed, dynamic, involving, tonally rounded (some), uncoloured (Japanese reviews), pinpoint imaging, tight, lean-muscled, clean treble without harshness, lighter bass than MUSE8820 (one blog), heavily coloured / euphonic (diyAudio critics), cool sound (while the chip runs hot)

**Typical uses:** DAC I/V, output filter and buffer stages (delta-sigma DACs), op-amp rolling in DACs, headphone amps and sound cards (Xonar/Prodigy buffers; listed among swap options in the Maverick TubeMagic D1 FAQ), low-impedance headphone drive (diyAudio suggestion), line buffers and cable drivers (datasheet application), video/RF amplification and fast data-acquisition buffers (datasheet)

**Caveats:**

- Mixed reputation: English forum views range from transparent and fast to 'heavily coloured', and it can 'kill nuances' in high-end systems. Japanese reviews are mostly positive but mixed against the OPA627.
- Runs hot: 6.3 mA typ (7.5 max) per amplifier. A DIP-8 dual at +/-15 V dissipates about 0.38 W, a rise of about 50 C at 130 C/W, matching the 'hot chip' remark.
- 70 MHz GBW makes it sensitive to layout, sockets and decoupling. The datasheet asks for 0.01-0.1 uF RF-quality bypass close to the pins, plus CF > RG*CIN/RF when feedback resistors exceed 5 kohm. One Japanese DIY builder's GitHub notes (indare/pcb_work) rank it the most layout-sensitive part in their stock, by analysis rather than measurement.
- High input bias current (0.6 uA typ, 2 uA max, either polarity) produces large DC offsets with high-value bias or feedback resistors (about 200 mV worst case with 100 kohm). Use balanced source impedances or AC coupling.
- Voltage noise of 9 nV/rtHz is higher than the NE5532 or LM4562, with a 1/f corner of roughly 600-700 Hz per graph G05. Poor choice for low-impedance, high-gain or phono stages.
- Do not use as a comparator or with sustained differential input: supply current rises to tens of mA and the part may be damaged.
- C-grade parts are only guaranteed to meet specifications from 0 to 70 C.
- Slew rate depends on input level and is highest at low closed-loop gain.

### 1. Package / assembly: Analog Devices (Linear Technology), PCN 21_0060, 11 Nov 2021 (Rev -, Rev A and Rev B exist)

Marking only, not a die change. On legacy Linear 8-SOICN products the top mark changed from ink to laser. Both marking styles are genuine. This resolves part of the completeness-sweep watch item.

- **When:** PCN 21_0060, 11 Nov 2021 (Rev -, Rev A and Rev B exist)
- **Affected:** LT1363CS8 (incl. #PBF / #TRPBF), LT1364CS8 (incl. #PBF / #TRPBF)
- **How to tell old from new:** Older parts have an ink top mark and newer parts have a laser top mark (8-SOICN). The top-mark text is still '1363' or '1364'.
- **Audio impact:** None expected (marking only).
- **Drop-in risk:** low - marking change only; same die and package.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Verification:** Search summary of PCN 21_0060 Rev B lists LT1363CS8#TRPBF and LT1364CS8. The ink-to-laser description comes from the LT1361-family pass for the same PCN. The PDF itself was not read.

### 2. Package / assembly: Analog Devices (Linear Technology), PCN 22_0241 (Rev - and Rev A;

Assembly material change. The epoxy formulation changed from Henkel 8290 to Henkel 8290A (Loctite Ablestik 8290 is a die-attach paste). ADI states no impact to fit, form or function, with improved reliability, and the material declaration is updated. The die and wafer process are unchanged.

- **When:** PCN 22_0241 (Rev - and Rev A; Rev A dated 18 Dec 2023 per search summary). Effective from date code 2242 (2022 week 42).
- **Affected:** LT1364 (variants not itemised; per PCN 22_0241 affected-parts summary)
- **How to tell old from new:** By date code: parts coded 2242 or later use the new epoxy. There is no visible marking difference.
- **Audio impact:** None expected (assembly material only; no electrical specification change).
- **Drop-in risk:** low - assembly material change qualified to industry standards; same die.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Verification:** WebSearch summaries of PCN 22_0241 give the Henkel 8290 to 8290A epoxy change, the 2242 date-code cut-in and 'no impact to fit, form, function'. A separate summary lists LT1361, LT1362 and LT1364 among the affected LT13xx parts. The PDF itself was not read.

## Counterfeits

No LT1363/4/5-specific counterfeit, PCN or die-change reports were found by web search (2026-09). The 'genuine Linear parts carry a distinctive white print' hint traces to new_western_elec's October 2019 post on fake OPA627/OPA637/OPA2604/MUSES01 parts; it is not an LT1364-specific statement (low confidence). That post calls slew-rate measurement the most effective fake test. Practical checks: SO-8 marking should read '1364' per the datasheet; a genuine dual draws about 12.6 mA quiescent at +/-15 V (6.3 mA per amplifier typ, 15 mA max); a square-wave test at AV = -2 should show roughly 1000 V/us (750 min), which a remarked slow bipolar dual would fail. Buy from ADI or authorised distributors (DigiKey, Mouser, Arrow) or established Japanese retailers such as Akizuki. #PBF/#TRPBF suffixes are ordering variants, not a sign of a fake.

## Related parts and alternatives
