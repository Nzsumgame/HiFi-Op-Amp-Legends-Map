# LME49723 / LME49723MA / LME49723NA

**Bipolar-input dual audio op-amp, voltage feedback** · National Semiconductor (2008–2011) → Texas Instruments (2011–present) · Tier C (niche / baseline)

*Obscure National/TI LME dual; one seasoned diyAudio listener ranks it among the best, with fuller bass than LM4562.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | THD+N | Supply range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.6 nV/√Hz typ | 20 MHz (unconfirmed) | ±20 V/µs typ | 0.0002% typ | ±2.5 V to ±17 V |

## Why it's rated

A niche cult part. On diyAudio one experienced listener puts it among the 'absolute best sounding' VFB op-amps (with LT1028, OPA132UA, LME49720HA) and calls it his favourite DIP op-amp: fuller bass and more detail than the LM4562, but sometimes thin on high violin notes. AudioKarma rollers preferred OPA2604. No measurement review exists.

**Described as:** fuller bass and body, detailed, sometimes too clear, thin high violin notes

**Used in:** DIP-8 op-amp rolling in CD players, DACs, preamps, Upgrades in headphone amps and line stages, SOIC-8 DIY audio stages, e.g. Rezzonics NuTube designs

## Watch out

- TI lists it Active but points to OPA1662 as a newer drop-in.
- The DIP version is reportedly scarce; most stock is SOIC-8, needing an adapter for socket rolling.
- Praise comes from a handful of listeners. A Head-Fi comment (unconfirmed) says it sounds unlike LM4562/LME49720/LME49860.
- AudioKarma advises careful decoupling against oscillation on sister LME49720; no LME49723-specific report.
- Split-supply part (±2.5 V minimum); one hobby project swapped it for an MCP6001 for low voltage.
- **Counterfeits:** No LME49723-specific fakes reported, but sister LME parts are faked (remarked LME49710, tinytransistors.net 2022; fake LME49720NA on diyAudio). With the DIP scarce, distrust cheap marketplace 'LME49723NA' parts; buy LME49723MA/NOPB from TI, Mouser or DigiKey.

## Silicon changes

None documented. Compare datasheet revisions before assuming old and new parts are identical.
