# LME49723 (National / TI dual bipolar audio op-amp)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*Little-known National/TI LME-series dual bipolar audio op-amp. A few diyAudio veterans rank it among the best-sounding VFB parts, with fuller bass than the LM4562.*

**Technology:** Dual voltage-feedback op-amp with a bipolar input, from National Semiconductor's LME49xxx 'high-fidelity audio' series. The sources found here do not describe the process or topology.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche cult part. In the long diyAudio thread 'The best sounding audio integrated opamps', one experienced listener names the LME49723 among the 'absolute best sounding' VFB op-amps, together with the LT1028, OPA132UA and LME49720HA. It is also his favourite plastic-DIP op-amp. In his A/B tests it gave more convincing bass dynamics and fullness, and more detail, than the LM4562/LME49720. On some pieces, though, he found too much clarity and not enough body on high violin notes. One listener also reports that the LM4562, LME49720 and LME49860 all sound different, and that the LME49723 differs from those three more than they differ from each other. AudioKarma members included it in long CD-player op-amp rolls, where the OPA2604 and a Sparkos discrete were preferred. No objective measurement review (Audio Science Review, Samuel Groner) was found.

**How people describe the sound:** fuller, more convincing bass dynamics and body, more detail than the LM4562/LME49720, on some recordings too much clarity, with thin body on high violin notes (one listener), sounds more different from the LM4562/LME49720/LME49860 than they do from each other

**Typical uses:** DIP-8 op-amp rolling in CD players, DACs and preamps, Op-amp upgrades in headphone amps and line stages, SOIC-8 audio stages in DIY/boutique designs, e.g. Rezzonics NuTube Screamer overdrive and NuTubeAmp (LME49723MA/NOPB in their BOMs)

**Caveats:**

- The DIP version (LME49723NA) is reportedly scarce. Most supply is SOIC-8, which needs an SOIC-to-DIP adapter for socket rolling.
- The praise comes from a handful of listeners. No published measurements or objective comparisons were found.
- AudioKarma advises careful supply decoupling to avoid oscillation for its sister part, the LME49720. No LME49723-specific oscillation report was found.
- Hobbyist projects have swapped it out where a single-supply or rail-to-rail part was needed (e.g. ADE-GlobalHealth oae_hardware replaced it with an MCP6001 RtR). It is a split-supply audio part, not a low-voltage rail-to-rail op-amp.

## Counterfeits

No counterfeit report specific to the LME49723 was found. Its sister LME parts are widely faked: a remarked 'LME49710' was documented by tinytransistors.net (2022), and a fake 'LME49720NA' was discussed on diyAudio. Genuine DIP LME49723NA stock is reportedly scarce, so treat cheap DIP 'LME49723NA' from marketplace sellers with suspicion. Prefer the SOIC LME49723MA/NOPB from authorised distributors (TI, Mouser 926-LME49723MA/NOPB, Digi-Key). This is general caution, not a documented LME49723 case (confidence low).
