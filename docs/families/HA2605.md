# HA-2600 / HA-2602 / HA-2605 and HA-2620 / HA-2622 / HA-2625 (Harris high-input-impedance bipolar singles)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*Harris 500 Mohm bipolar singles: HA-2600/2605 (12 MHz, 7 V/us) and uncompensated HA-2620/2625 (100 MHz GBW, 35 V/us, gain >5). Jung-era audio praise unverified.*

**Technology:** Bipolar single op-amps with very high input impedance (500 Mohm, HA-2600/HA-2620) per Intersil/Renesas datasheets. HA-2600/2602/2605 are internally compensated (12 MHz unity-gain bandwidth, 7 V/us, 150 kV/V). HA-2620/2622/2625 are the 'very wideband, uncompensated' versions (100 MHz GBW, stable for closed-loop gain >5, 35 V/us, 150 kV/V). Dielectric-isolation process and input-stage topology were not confirmed from a datasheet this run.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

No Hi-Fi community praise was verified. No search budget was spent on reputation this run. Discovery notes say these Harris parts were cited in late-1970s/1980s Walt Jung-era audio literature as high-slew, high-impedance options, and that modern enthusiast praise is minimal. Treat this as a historical tier-C entry until the Jung citations are confirmed.

**Typical uses:** High-slew line-level and instrumentation stages in the slew-induced-distortion era (per discovery notes, unverified), High-gain amplification of fast wideband signals from high-impedance sources (HA-2620/2625 at gain >5, per the datasheet description)

**Caveats:**

- HA-2620/2622/2625 are uncompensated and stable only at closed-loop gains greater than 5 (datasheet). Do not use them as unity-gain followers or in low-gain audio stages.
- HA3-2625-5 is obsolete per distributors. Other codes probably come only from surplus, new-old stock or Rochester Electronics.
- Grade-specific values here come from EWB/MicroSim simulator libraries. Only headline typicals were confirmed from datasheets.
- Noise (e_n, i_n), THD, quiescent current and supply range were not verified.

## Counterfeits

No counterfeit or remarking reports were found. This run used no search on it, so this is a gap, not a clean bill. HA3-2625-5 is obsolete per distributors. Current supply is likely surplus, new-old stock or Rochester Electronics, which has a datasheet copy filed under HA2-2625-5 on Octopart. For surplus parts, check that logos and date codes match the Harris or Intersil era. Measure the slew rate (about 7 vs about 35 V/us) and unity-gain stability to tell a 2600-series die from an uncompensated 2620-series die: a 2620/2625 will oscillate as a follower.
