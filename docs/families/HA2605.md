# HA-2600 / HA-2602 / HA-2605 and HA-2620 / HA-2622 / HA-2625

**Bipolar high-input-impedance singles, compensated or not** · Harris Semiconductor (by 1982–1999) → Renesas (2017–present) · second sources: Intersil · Tier C (niche / baseline)

*Harris 500 MΩ bipolar singles: HA-2600/2605 (12 MHz, 7 V/µs) and uncompensated HA-2620/2625 (100 MHz, 35 V/µs, gain >5).*

| GBW | Slew rate |
|---|---|
| 12 MHz (HA-2600/2605); 100 MHz (HA-2620/2625) | 7 V/µs (HA-2600/2605); 35 V/µs (HA-2620/2625) |

## Why it's rated

No hi-fi praise has been verified. These Harris parts were reportedly cited in late-1970s/1980s Walt Jung-era audio literature as high-slew, high-impedance options; modern enthusiast praise is minimal. Treat this as a historical entry until the Jung citations are confirmed.

**Used in:** High-slew line and instrumentation stages (reportedly, SID era), Fast high-gain stages from high-impedance sources (HA-2620/2625, gain >5)

## Watch out

- HA-2620/2622/2625 are uncompensated, stable only at gain >5: never use as followers or low-gain stages.
- HA3-2625-5 is obsolete; other codes likely only from surplus, new-old stock or Rochester Electronics.
- Grade values come from EWB/MicroSim models; a measured HA2-2600-8 sample: 12.4 MHz, +7.5/−6.4 V/µs, 0.85 mV, CMRR 107 dB.
- Noise, THD, supply current and supply range are unverified.
- **Counterfeits:** No counterfeit reports found (none searched). Supply is surplus, new-old stock or Rochester Electronics. Check Harris/Intersil-era logos and date codes. Slew (~7 vs ~35 V/µs) and follower stability tell the dies apart: a 2620/2625 oscillates as a follower.

## Silicon changes

None documented. Compare datasheet revisions before assuming old and new parts are identical.
