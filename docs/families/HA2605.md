# HA-2600 / HA-2602 / HA-2605 and HA-2620 / HA-2622 / HA-2625 (Harris dielectrically isolated bipolar)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*Harris single bipolar op-amps: HA-2600 series at about 12 MHz and 7 V/us, decompensated HA-2620 series at about 35 V/us. Now obsolete. Jung-era audio praise is not verified.*

**Technology:** Bipolar single op-amp on the Harris dielectrically isolated process (per discovery notes, not re-verified). The HA-2600/2602/2605 are the compensated general-purpose wideband parts. The HA-2620/2622/2625 are the wideband, decompensated/uncompensated versions: Harris ships a separate macromodel for them, and simulator libraries give them 35 V/us against 7 V/us. Libraries show high input resistance (300-500 Mohm) and nA-level input bias. The input-stage topology was not confirmed from a datasheet.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

No Hi-Fi community praise could be verified this run because web search was unavailable. The discovery notes say only that these Harris parts were cited in late-1970s and 1980s Walt Jung-era audio literature as high-slew options, and that modern enthusiast praise is minimal. Treat this as a historical, tier-C entry until the Jung citations are confirmed.

**Typical uses:** High-slew line-level and instrumentation stages in the slew-induced-distortion era (per discovery notes, unverified), Wideband high-impedance amplifier stages; HA-2620/2625 in gain stages above unity (inferred from decompensated design)

**Caveats:**

- HA-2620/2622/2625 are the decompensated/uncompensated wideband versions. Do not use them as unity-gain followers. Confirm the minimum stable gain in the datasheet (not retrieved this run).
- Obsolete per discovery notes, so supply is surplus or new-old stock only.
- The spec values listed here come from EWB and MicroSim simulator libraries, not from a Harris/Intersil datasheet.
- Noise (e_n, i_n), THD, quiescent current and supply range were not verified.

## Counterfeits

No counterfeit or remarking reports were found for this family. Web search was unavailable this run, so this is a gap, not a clean bill. As with any obsolete Harris/Intersil part sold as surplus, check that date codes and markings match the Harris or Intersil logo era, and measure the slew rate (about 7 vs about 35 V/us) to tell a 2600-series die from a 2620-series die.
