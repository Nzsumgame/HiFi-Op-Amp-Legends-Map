# OP249 (PMI / Analog Devices dual precision high-speed JFET)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*PMI/ADI's fast (22 V/µs) precision JFET dual. The 1993 rec.audio FAQ rated it 'highly recommended', and Fosi still lists it as a DIP-8 rolling option.*

**Technology:** Dual precision high-speed op amp with a JFET input pair. ADI calls it a 'high speed, precision dual JFET op amp'. The PMI SPICE macromodel (JX = PJF) shows the pair is P-channel. Designed by Precision Monolithics (PMI) and continued by Analog Devices. Around 2010 the wafers moved to ADI's Wilmington fab (ADWIL), as described in PCN 10_0062.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (2 recorded: Die redesign, Fab / process transfer).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Evidence is thin and mostly historical. Bob Neidorff's rec.audio Audio FAQ (part 2, v1.5, last modified 1993-06-04) lists OP249 among its 'highly recommended' audio duals, alongside OPA2604, AD797, LT1115 and HA5112. The same FAQ says FET op amps suit line-level, high-source-impedance signals. Fosi Audio's ZA3 marketing lists OP249 among compatible DIP-8 duals for op-amp rolling. A synth-DIY CV/audio mixer (SOWA) chose it for 'low offset and good dynamic parameters', and Bordodynov's LTspice collection files it under 'AudioOpamp/AD'. No measurement-based audio reviews and no community sound descriptors were found.

**Typical uses:** Line-level audio stages and buffers (the FET-input use case in the 1993 rec.audio FAQ), Op-amp rolling in DIP-8 sockets (for example the Fosi Audio ZA3), DC-coupled CV/audio mixers in synth DIY (SOWA mixer)

**Caveats:**

- The rec.audio FAQ warns that a faster op amp swapped into existing gear may oscillate, depending on design and layout. This is a general warning, not specific to OP249.
- Supply current is about 5.6 mA typ (OP249G) and voltage noise is 17 nV/√Hz at 1 kHz, noisier than bipolar audio duals. Output-drive capability was not verified, so check the ADI data sheet before using it for headphone drive or low-noise duty.
- Reputation rests on a 1990s FAQ and vendor marketing lists. There is no modern measured evidence of audio superiority.
- Parts made before and after the fab transfer and mask change in PCN 10_0062 (about 2010) may differ slightly in offset behaviour.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Fab / process transfer: Analog Devices, before 2010 (date of transfer not captured; described in…

PCN 10_0062 states that the OP249 was transferred to ADI's ADWIL (Wilmington) wafer fab, and that after the transfer the product showed more Vos variation. That finding led to the mask and passivation fixes recorded in the separate die-redesign entry. The same part number was built at a different fab, with a documented shift in offset behaviour.

- **When:** before 2010 (date of transfer not captured; described in PCN 10_0062)
- **Affected:** OP249 (all grades)
- **How to tell old from new:** Not documented. Only lot or date code could separate pre-transfer from post-transfer wafers.
- **Audio impact:** Probably negligible for audio. The documented effect is on DC offset variation, and no AC change is reported.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - same design and pinout; the offset spread was larger on post-transfer lots made before the fix
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Verification:** A search on PCN 10_0062 confirmed the background: 'The transfer of the OP249 to the ADWIL wafer fab identified the product to have more Vos variation'. Split out as its own fab-transfer event.

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Wafer fab | earlier fab (not named in the sources seen) | ADI Wilmington (ADWIL) |
| Vos variation (observed) | baseline | increased (per PCN 10_0062 background) |

### 2. Die redesign: Analog Devices, About 2010 (inferred from the PCN number 10_0062; exact…

PCN 10_0062, 'OP249 Data sheet and Die Changes'. ADI made a minor mask change to improve a diode's performance and stability, and moved ceramic-DIP products to a lower-stress passivation to improve Vos in the ceramic package. Both changes had already been qualified on the military version. The PCN includes Group C qualification data and a comparison of old and new data-sheet specifications.

- **When:** About 2010 (inferred from the PCN number 10_0062; exact issue and effective dates not captured)
- **Affected:** OP249 (all grades), OP249AZ, OP249FZ, OP249GPZ, OP249GSZ
- **How to tell old from new:** No marking change is documented. A date code relative to the PCN effective date is the only likely discriminator. The passivation change applies to ceramic-DIP (CERDIP) parts such as OP249FZ and probably OP249AZ.
- **Audio impact:** Probably negligible for audio. The change targets offset stability and variation, and AC performance (slew, GBW, noise) is not reported as changed.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - same pinout and function; only the offset/TCVos specs were revised, which matters only in DC-precision circuits
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Verification:** A search confirmed the PCN 10_0062 title ('OP249 Data sheet and Die Changes') and its content: a minor mask change for the diode, lower-stress CERDIP passivation, and prior qualification on the military version. It also surfaced the Group C qualification attachment. The effective date was not captured.

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Die mask | original diode layout | minor mask change to improve diode performance and stability |
| CERDIP passivation | previous passivation | lower-stress passivation |
| Offset voltage and Vos temperature coefficient limits (data sheet Table 4) | earlier limits (values not captured) | changed (the Rev. I history lists 'Changes to Offset Voltage Parameter and Offset Voltage Temperature Coefficient Parameter, Table 4'); linking this to the PCN is an inference |
| OP249F columns (data sheet Table 3) | present | deleted (per the revision history; timing relative to the PCN not verified) |

## Counterfeits

No OP249-specific counterfeit or remarking reports were found; this was not searched in depth. OP249 is a legacy PMI/ADI precision part that appears on broker and marketplace sites (for example Worldway Electronics and IC-listing sites). Leaded (non-Z) GP/GS parts and old ceramic parts are mostly broker stock. Compare the logo, top marking and date-code format with genuine ADI parts, and measure supply current (about 5.6 mA typ), offset and slew rate (22 V/µs typ) against the ADI data sheet. Be suspicious of any 'OP249' in unusual packages or at very low prices. Genuine parts made before and after about 2010 can differ in die and passivation (PCN 10_0062).
