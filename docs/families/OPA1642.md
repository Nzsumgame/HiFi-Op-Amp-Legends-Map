# OPA1641 / OPA1642 / OPA1644 (TI SoundPlus JFET-input single/dual/quad)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** JFET-input

*TI's 2009 SoundPlus JFET-input op-amp (5.1 nV/rtHz, -126 dB THD+N, input capacitance that stays flat) and the default DAC low-pass/buffer part in many DAPs and DACs.*

**Technology:** True JFET input stage with a rail-to-rail output stage. Internally compensated and unity-gain stable. Rated 4.5-36 V (±2.25 to ±18 V). Internal phase-reversal protection. Input capacitance stays flat with common-mode voltage. Copper leadframe. TI SoundPlus audio line (Burr-Brown heritage branding). Surface-mount only: SOIC-8, VSSOP-8, SOIC-14, TSSOP-14.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

TI's JFET-input SoundPlus family, pitched at OPA2134-class uses but with far lower distortion. Soomal describes a 'typical OPA1642 style': the whole sound is refined and balanced, with mild, delicate mids and relaxed bass. Paired with the OPA1662 it keeps the 'BB' character: relaxed, grand, full dynamics, warm overall. It is the stock DAC low-pass-filter and line buffer in many Chinese, Korean and Western portable and desktop products: FiiO X3K/X3 II/X5 III/M11/E17K/K13 R2R, Lotoo PAW Gold, Astell&Kern AK380, HiBy RS6, Apogee Element 24/Groove, EarMen CH-Amp. In DIY it is the core of the 'OPA Alice' phantom-powered electret mic (Hackaday 2020), where its low quiescent current is the selling point. DIY DAC builders also pick it for its voltage-independent input capacitance in common-mode audio stages. No Audio Science Review or Samuel Groner measurement of the bare op-amp was checked in this run.

**How people describe the sound:** refined / delicate (细腻), balanced (均衡), mild, fine mids (中频温和而细腻), relaxed bass (低频比较松弛), relaxed, grand, full dynamics, warm overall (松弛大气, 动态饱满, 偏暖; FiiO E17K with OPA1662), Burr-Brown house sound (Soomal)

**Typical uses:** DAC post-filter (LPF) and I/V stages in DAPs and USB DACs, line-out buffers, phantom-powered electret/condenser mic preamps (OPA Alice mic), phono (RIAA) preamps, high-impedance piezo/guitar buffers, active filters, tone controls, mixing consoles (TI target applications)

**Caveats:**

- Surface-mount only (SOIC, VSSOP, TSSOP). Op-amp rolling into DIP-8 sockets needs an adapter board (e.g. the Audiophonics OPA1642 DIP-8 adapter).
- The common-mode input range stops 3.5 V below V+. Below ±3.5 V supplies it does not include midsupply, which limits low-voltage single-supply unity-gain buffers.
- Not a headphone or 600 Ω driver. TI warns that 600 Ω at full supply can trip the current limit (ISC +36/-30 mA) and advises keeping output current under about 13 mA (2.8 kΩ minimum at 36 V single supply).
- Capacitive-load overshoot: the datasheet recommends 24-51 Ω output isolation (Figs 19-20).
- Offset is 1 mV typ / 3.5 mV max, higher than precision parts. DC-coupled outputs may need trimming or a servo.
- VSSOP-8 θJA is 180 °C/W. A shorted output at ±18 V dissipates over 600 mW.
- Soomal's sound descriptions are of whole devices, not the op-amp in isolation.

## Counterfeits

No OPA164x-specific counterfeit reports were found. The search was limited because the web budget was exhausted. Two facts from the datasheet help screen parts. First, TI never offered the family in DIP, only SOIC-8 (D), VSSOP-8 (DGK), SOIC-14 and TSSOP-14, so any one-piece DIP-8 body marked OPA1642 is not a genuine TI package. Legitimate DIP-8 versions are SOIC parts on adapter boards, such as the one Audiophonics sells. Second, the genuine top markings per the 2026 TI addendum are O1641A, O1642A and O1644A on SOIC and TSSOP, and 1641 or 1642 on VSSOP, plus the TI logo and lot code. Because this is a cheap, commodity SMD part, the realistic risks are remarked generic JFET duals sold on DIP adapters. Buy from authorized distributors.

## Open questions

- No web searches ran: the session's WebSearch budget (200/200) was already used up. Research was done through GitHub code search and GitHub-hosted mirrors only, so vendor, distributor, ASR, diyAudio and Head-Fi pages were not queried. Re-run with WebSearch to fill the gaps.
- The URLs opa1644.pdf symlink, focus.ti.com symlinks, sbos484/sbos484a/b/c/d revision-specific paths, opa1642-q1.pdf and sbos791a are inferred from TI URL patterns and were not seen. They appear only in archive_seed_urls and need a Wayback check.
- No copies of the Original (Dec 2009), A (Apr 2010), B (Aug 2010) or C (Dec 2015) revisions were located. They are needed to quantify the Rev D edits to noise (10 Hz/100 Hz e_n and 20 Hz-20 kHz noise), input bias current, VO test conditions and temperature range.
- No PCN was found. The TI PCN database was not accessible. Check whether OPA164x was part of TI's 2024-2026 process migrations that hit NE5532, OPAx134 and TL07x. Note that those die changes reached the datasheet only as a new revision letter. As of the 17-Jun-2026 addendum, SBOS484 is still Rev D.
- The meaning of the '.B' orderable duplicates and the mixed VSSOP lead finishes in the 2026 addendum is undocumented (alternate assembly/test site?). It is not known whether they indicate any die or fab change.
- The OPA164x-Q1 datasheet SBOS791 (Rev A, Nov 2017) was not located. It is unverified whether the -Q1 uses the same die and whether it has a later revision.
- The suspected close relationship between OPA164x and OPA140/OPA2140/OPA4140, which have similar headline specs, is unverified.
- Datasheet mirrors on Mouser, LCSC, alldatasheet and datasheetarchive were not examined, so their frozen revisions are unknown.
- No independent bench measurements of the bare op-amp (ASR, Samuel Groner, Japanese blogs) were checked, and no forum reports of socket or adapter oscillation were found.
