# LF351 / LF353 / LF347 (single/dual/quad BiFET, JFET-input)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** General-purpose baselines

*National's 1977 BI-FET II JFET dual: a cheap TL072-class op-amp found in vintage audio and synth gear, valued mostly for period-correct restorations.*

**Technology:** BiFET: matched high-voltage JFET input pair plus bipolar output stage on one monolithic chip, with an internally trimmed input offset. National called it 'BI-FET II' and advertised it as laser-trimmed. Internally compensated, 0-70 °C commercial grade. National's application hints say each amplifier is biased by its own zener reference (DS005649).

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (6 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

NOT VERIFIED: no web search was available, and forum and measurement sites were not reached. The discovery notes describe the LF353 as a vintage BiFET dual in 1980s-90s consumer and synth gear, called smooth or warm by some listeners and ordinary by measurement-minded reviewers. The datasheets make it a TL072-class part: 16-18 nV/rtHz and 3-4 MHz. TI lists 'Pro Audio Mixers' among its applications, and National specified THD <0.02%. Its appeal is originality in restorations, not performance.

**How people describe the sound:** smooth (discovery notes, unverified folklore), warm (discovery notes, unverified folklore)

**Typical uses:** pro audio mixers (TI SLOS012C application list), preamp, tone-control and filter stages in vintage gear (discovery notes, unverified), synthesizer and modular CV/VCO/VCF circuits (e.g. open4Umodular subosc BOM 'LF353 or TL072'), active filters, integrators, sample-and-hold, D/A converters (TI and National datasheets), budget Chinese DACs (a Xiang Sheng DAC-01A review lists OPA2604 x1 and LF353N x2; low confidence)

**Caveats:**

- Noisier than bipolar audio duals: 16-18 nV/rtHz vs about 5 nV/rtHz for an NE5532. It matters with low-impedance sources.
- Offset up to 10 mV (13 mV over temperature), so DC-coupled stages need trimming, a servo or a coupling capacitor.
- National documents phase reversal: taking either input below the negative common-mode limit forces the output high (no latch). Keep inputs at least 3-4 V above V-. TI SLOS012C does not mention it.
- TI recommends +/-3.5 V minimum, while National says zener biasing gives normal operation from +/-6 V. Commercial 0-70 °C only.
- Output specified only into 2 k/10 k loads, so not for 600-ohm or headphone loads.
- Specs differ by vendor (TI 3 MHz/18 nV; National 4 MHz/16 nV; ST 4 MHz/15 nV). An 'LF353' from another maker is not the same part.
- Never let an input go below the negative supply: National warns this can destroy the unit.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Texas Instruments / National…: TI has published its own LF353 since March 1987.

One generic number with two separately documented specs. National rates its LF353 at 4 MHz and 16 nV/rtHz (RS = 100 ohm) with THD <0.02%. TI rates its LF353 at 3 MHz and 18 nV/rtHz, with no THD figure, the same numbers as its TL07x. DC specs (VOS, IB, ICC, SR) are identical. No vendor states whether TI's 1987 part is its own design or a copy of National's. Two dies is likely but inferred.

- **When:** TI has published its own LF353 since March 1987. Since the 2011 acquisition TI has also sold the National LF353 as LF353-N.
- **Affected:** LF353, LF353P, LF353DR, LF353PE4, LF353N/NOPB, LF353M/NOPB, LF353MX/NOPB
- **How to tell old from new:** TI lineage: orderables LF353P, LF353DR and LF353PE4 (marked LF353P or LF353), datasheet SLOS012 (lf353.pdf). National lineage: orderables LF353N, LF353M and LF353MX (/NOPB under TI), National DS005649 or TI LF353-N (lf353-n.pdf). Pre-2011 National parts carry the NS logo. Top-mark details unverified.
- **Audio impact:** Probably small. National's version is specified slightly quieter and wider-band, with a THD guarantee. Both are TL072-class.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - same pinout, function and DC specs; only minor AC-spec differences
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| GBW typ (min) | 4 MHz (2.7 MHz min), National DS005649 | 3 MHz typ, no min, TI SLOS012C |
| e_n @1 kHz | 16 nV/rtHz, RS = 100 ohm (National) | 18 nV/rtHz, RS = 20 ohm (TI Rev C; RS = 100 ohm in 1988) |
| THD | <0.02% (AV = 10, 20 Vp-p, 10 k; National) | not specified (TI) |
| Minimum supply | normal operation on +/-6 V (National application hints) | +/-3.5 V recommended minimum (TI) |
| ESD HBM | 1700 V (National 2000) | +/-2000 V (TI Rev C) |

### 5. Texas Instruments (TI's own LF353, not…: PCN 20220615003.1 (June 2022, Sherman SFAB to Richardson…

TI moved its own LF353 from SFAB (Sherman) to RFAB (Richardson) on a new die. On TI E2E (thread 1512001), TI says PCNs 20220615003.1 and 20230627002.1 'introduced an entirely new die' with an entirely new design. The guaranteed datasheet limits did not change, but circuits may depend on behaviour beyond those limits. In E2E thread 1442987 ('LF353: new part parameter change'), TI says the bandwidth of the new part rose to about 4.5 MHz because of the fab change. Users report behaviour and stability differences between old and new parts.

- **When:** PCN 20220615003.1 (June 2022, Sherman SFAB to Richardson RFAB) and PCN 20230627002.1 (June 2023). TI E2E threads 1442987 and 1512001 discuss parts shipped after these PCNs.
- **Affected:** LF353DR, LF353DRE4, LF353 (other TI LF353 orderables such as LF353P/LF353PE4 are likely also affected, but no retrieved source lists them)
- **How to tell old from new:** The part number and marking are unchanged. The PCN 20230627002.1 search summary shows the die-revision field changing from C/E/F/H to A. In practice, the date code or lot relative to the PCN first-ship dates is the only external clue. TI says a new fab site produces an 'entirely new' chip. The National-design LF353-N (LF353N/NOPB, LF353M/NOPB, LF353MX/NOPB) is a separate product and is not covered by these PCNs.
- **Audio impact:** A wider real bandwidth on the new die changes phase margin and HF behaviour in active filters, tone stacks, buffers driving cable or capacitive loads, and unity-gain followers. E2E reports mention stability problems in existing designs. Swapping a new-die LF353 into a vintage design can change how it sounds or cause oscillation, even though the paper specs are identical. Noise and THD differences were not quantified in any retrieved source.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium: the part is pin-compatible with unchanged guaranteed limits, but TI confirms an entirely new die with about 50% more bandwidth, and users report stability and behaviour differences in existing circuits.
- **Confidence:** high

| Die revision (PCN field) | C, E, F, H | A (PCN 20230627002.1 search summary) |

## Counterfeits

No LF353-specific counterfeit reports were verified (no web search available). It is a very cheap commodity part, so remarking pays little, but recycled or remarked DIP pulls from marketplace sellers are common for all classic op-amps. Buy TI, ST or onsemi stock from authorised distributors. Current TI parts are marked LF353P (PDIP) or LF353 (SOIC) per TI's addendum. 'LF353AN/BN' or TO-99 'LF353H' parts are genuine National grades from before 2000; offers of them as new production are suspect. When TI stopped using the NS logo on ex-National parts was not verified, so date-code rules for National-marked parts are unconfirmed.

## Related parts and alternatives

TL072 / TL072H (TI; TL072H is TI's redesigned TL07x), OPA1642 (TI JFET-input audio dual, low noise), OPA2134 (JFET-input audio dual), MUSES8920 (JFET-input audio dual), AD712 (precision BiFET dual), NE5532 / LM4562 (bipolar, much lower voltage noise for low-impedance sources)
