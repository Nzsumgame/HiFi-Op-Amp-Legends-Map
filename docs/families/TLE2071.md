# TLE2071 / TLE2072 / TLE2074 (TI Excalibur low-noise high-speed JFET-input)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*A fast TI Excalibur JFET op-amp (10 MHz, about 40 V/us) that Head-Fi CMoy builders called the best cheap chip for 9 V battery headphone amps.*

**Technology:** BiFET. The input is a P-channel JFET differential pair: both TI PSpice macromodels (level 1 /301, level 2 /302) use PJF devices J1/J2. The TI macromodel catalogue calls it 'high speed, biFET', and the MicroSim/TI symbol library calls it 'Excalibur Low-Noise High-Speed JFET-Input Operational Amplifier'. It belongs to TI's 'Excalibur' TLE20xx generation. A third-party parts database says Vos is trimmed on-chip. Single (TLE2071), dual (TLE2072) and quad (TLE2074), each also in a tighter A grade. The March 2000 datasheet also covered a TLE207xY chip (die) form. TI's own macromodels for TLE2071/2072/2074 are line-for-line identical to those for the sister TLE2081/2082/2084, as is the LISO library entry.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche budget favourite, not a legend. Head-Fi CMoy/portable-amp threads (c. 2007-2009) rate the TLE2072 highly: one user tried about 20 op-amps and voted it the best cheap op-amp for a 9 V CMoy. Users rated it above the OPA2132, OPA2134 and OPA2227 at low supply voltage and said it drew less battery current. Listeners describe a forward presentation with a tipped-up, lively midrange, bass with weight and texture, and treble that 'pulls off cymbals with conviction'. Forum lore says the TLE2072 has the better DC specs and the sister TLE2082 the better AC specs, although TI's macromodels for the two are identical. It is also used in open-hardware audio: the ohdsp PCM4202 ADC input filters and ADAU1966 DAC differential-to-single-ended buffers (2016) use the TLE2071/TLE2072. Their designer lists them as examples of 'good quality fast audio grade devices', interchangeable with the NE5532, OPA1652 and OPA1642. No Audio Science Review, Samuel Groner or other distortion measurement was found; the search budget was exhausted.

**How people describe the sound:** forward presentation, tipped-up midrange, lively, bass with weight and texture, treble that pulls off cymbals with conviction, detailed

**Typical uses:** 9 V battery CMoy headphone amplifiers (op-amp rolling), Audio ADC input filter/driver (ohdsp ADC-PCM4202 and ADC-PCM4202-SE boards), DAC differential-to-single-ended output buffers (ohdsp BUF-DiffToSE-ADAU1966), Low-noise JFET charge/transimpedance preamps on 9 V batteries, e.g. the CERN DIY particle detector (TLE2072CP/ACP), Upgrade for TL071/TL072/TL081 sockets needing more bandwidth and slew

**Caveats:**

- The praise rests on a handful of Head-Fi CMoy posts. The sound descriptors are forum folklore and no published measurements were found.
- JFET voltage noise of about 11.6 nV/rtHz typ (17 max) is well above bipolar audio parts. This matters in low-impedance, high-gain stages.
- Output current is modest (about 25 mA per LISO; the macromodel limits near 45 mA). Low-impedance headphones need a buffer.
- The common-mode range reaches V+ but stops several volts above V- (model: -11.3 V at +/-15 V). Keep inputs away from the negative rail.
- Offset-null pins are bonded out only on the ceramic JG package (per partinfo). Plastic DIP/SOIC pins 1, 5 and 8 are NC, which is harmless in TL071-style sockets.
- It is a 10 MHz, ~40 V/us part, so use short decoupling and watch capacitive loads when dropping it into circuits designed for slower TL07x parts. This is general engineering caution, not a documented report.

## Counterfeits

No TLE207x-specific counterfeit or remarking reports were found, but the web-search budget was exhausted before a dedicated search. It is an inexpensive, low-volume TI part and an unlikely main counterfeit target. Treat marketplace 'TLE2072' DIPs as unverified and prefer authorized distributors (Digi-Key 296-10426-5-ND, Mouser 595-TLE2072CP / 595-TLE2072IDR, Farnell). A quick bench check: slew rate should be about 40 V/us and input bias current in the tens of pA at room temperature. A remarked TL072/TL082 slews only about 13 V/us.

## Related parts and alternatives

TLE2081 / TLE2082 / TLE2084 (sister TI Excalibur JFET family with identical TI macromodels; Head-Fi calls the TLE2082 lively and clean), OPA1641 / OPA1642 / OPA1644 (modern TI JFET-input audio op-amps; named by the ohdsp designer as drop-in alternatives), OPA1652 / OPA1656 (modern TI CMOS audio op-amps), NE5532 (bipolar; named by the ohdsp designer as an alternative), OPA2134 / OPA2132 (the JFET audio parts it was compared with in CMoy threads), TL071 / TL072 / TL074 (pin-compatible but slower legacy BiFETs)

## Open questions

- The session's WebSearch budget was exhausted (200/200). Both searches attempted for this family were refused, so none of the 15 required queries ran. This record is built from cached discovery-phase Head-Fi search summaries and offline GitHub sources: TI PSpice libraries, the LISO op-amp library, the partinfo database, bibliographies, KiCad projects and BOMs. Re-run with fresh searches.
- The TI SLOS literature number and the full revision chain of the TLE207x datasheet are unverified. SLOS181 is an unverified recollection: a sibling agent associated the same number with the TLE208x, and it was not added to ti_literature_numbers. To fix, read the footer of ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tle2071.pdf ('SLOSxxx? - <orig date> - REVISED <date>') and its Revision History.
- Archive seed URLs are INFERRED from standard TI URL patterns and were not seen, except these five, which were seen in third-party files: ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tle2071.pdf, ti.com/lit/gpn/tle2071, ti.com/lit/gpn/tle2072, the Aalborg komponenten.es.aau.dk copy and the LCSC copy.
- The December 2009 and March 2000 dates come from third-party bibliographies. The 'Marts 2000' entry may be an original-issue or a revised date; confirm against the PDFs.
- TI's TLE207x and TLE208x macromodels (/301, /302) are byte-identical in parameters, and LISO gives identical values. Is the TLE207x a different die, or a low-noise-tested/trimmed selection of the TLE208x die? The datasheets are needed to compare noise, slew and Vos specs, and to check forum claims that the TLE2072 has better DC specs and the TLE2082 better AC specs.
- No PCN, fab move, die change or NRND/EOL notice was checked. A silicon change under the same part number is neither confirmed nor refuted.
- The lifecycle status of each orderable is not verified from TI: TLE2071/A, TLE2072/A, TLE2074/A, C/I temperature grades, and any M-grade/SMD 5962 or -EP/-Q1 variants.
- Output current, THD+N, exact Iq, supply range and slew rate are not verified from a TI document. Slew is 40 V/us in models and 'about 35 V/us' in partinfo.
- It is unknown whether any independent distortion or noise measurement exists (ASR, Samuel Groner, diyAudio). The Japanese and Chinese community reputation was not searched.
