# TLE2071 / TLE2072 / TLE2074 (TI Excalibur low-noise high-speed JFET-input)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*A fast TI Excalibur JFET op-amp (10 MHz, 40 V/us). One Head-Fi CMoy builder tried about 20 chips and rated it the best cheap choice for 9 V headphone amps.*

**Technology:** BiFET with a P-channel JFET input pair (per the TI PSpice macromodels) and on-chip Vos trimming (per a datasheet feature bullet). TI datasheet SLOS181 title: 'Excalibur Low-Noise High-Speed JFET-Input Operational Amplifiers'. TI's product page describes it as 38 V, 10 MHz, 40 V/us, with the input range including V+ ('In to V+'). It comes as a single (TLE2071), dual (TLE2072) and quad (TLE2074), each also in a tighter A grade. Other variants are an automotive -Q1 family (SGLS226) and a TLE2074AM. The March 2000 edition (SLOS181A) also covered a TLE207xY chip (die) form. TI's macromodels and the LISO entries for the TLE207x are identical to those for the sister TLE208x.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche budget favourite, not a legend. In a Head-Fi CMoy thread (c. 2007-2009), one user who tried about 20 op-amps rated the TLE2072 the best cheap op-amp for a 9 V CMoy, ahead of the OPA2132 and OPA2134. Other posts rate it above the OPA2132, OPA2134 and OPA2227 at low supply voltage, and say it draws less battery current. Sound descriptions (forward, lively midrange, textured bass, strong cymbals) are individual forum impressions. Forum lore says the TLE2072 has the better DC specs and the TLE2082 the better AC specs, although TI's macromodels for the two are identical. The open-hardware ohdsp ADC/DAC boards (2016) use the TLE2071/2072 and name the NE5532, OPA1652 and OPA1642 as alternatives. No ASR, Samuel Groner or other distortion measurement was found.

**How people describe the sound:** forward presentation, tipped-up midrange, lively, bass with weight and texture, treble that pulls off cymbals with conviction, detailed

**Typical uses:** 9 V battery CMoy headphone amplifiers (op-amp rolling), Audio ADC input filter/driver (ohdsp ADC-PCM4202 boards), DAC differential-to-single-ended output buffers (ohdsp BUF-DiffToSE-ADAU1966), Low-noise JFET charge preamps on 9 V batteries (CERN DIY particle detector, TLE2072CP/ACP), Upgrade for TL071/TL072/TL081 sockets (TI markets it as a direct upgrade to TL05x/TL07x/TL08x)

**Caveats:**

- The praise rests on a handful of Head-Fi CMoy posts. The sound descriptors are forum folklore and no published measurements were found.
- JFET voltage noise of about 11.6 nV/rtHz typ (17 max) is well above bipolar audio parts. This matters in low-impedance, high-gain stages.
- Output current is modest (about 25 mA per LISO), so low-impedance headphones need a buffer.
- The input range includes V+ but stops several volts above V-. Keep inputs away from the negative rail.
- Per partinfo (unconfirmed), the offset-null pins are bonded out only on the ceramic JG package; on plastic DIP/SOIC, pins 1, 5 and 8 are NC.
- At 10 MHz and 40-45 V/us it is faster than the TL07x it replaces. Decouple well and watch capacitive loads (general engineering caution).

## Counterfeits

No TLE207x-specific counterfeit or remarking reports are known, but no dedicated search was run. It is an inexpensive, low-volume TI part and an unlikely main target. Treat marketplace 'TLE2072' DIPs as unverified and prefer authorized distributors (Digi-Key 296-10426-5-ND, Mouser 595-TLE2072CP / 595-TLE2072IDR). Bench check: slew rate should be about 40-45 V/us and input bias current in the tens of pA at room temperature. A remarked TL072/TL082 slews only about 13 V/us.

## Related parts and alternatives

TLE2081 / TLE2082 / TLE2084 (sister TI Excalibur JFET family with identical TI macromodels), OPA1641 / OPA1642 / OPA1644 (modern TI JFET-input audio op-amps; named by the ohdsp designer), OPA1652 / OPA1656 (modern TI CMOS audio op-amps), NE5532 (bipolar; named by the ohdsp designer), OPA2134 / OPA2132 (the JFET audio parts it was compared with in CMoy threads), TL071 / TL072 / TL074 (pin-compatible, slower legacy BiFETs; TI positions the TLE207x as a direct upgrade)
