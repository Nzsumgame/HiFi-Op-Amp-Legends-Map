# LME49990 (National/TI ultra-low-noise bipolar single)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*National's c.2010-11 flagship audio single, with about 0.9 nV/rtHz noise and very low THD. Hobbyists roll it as an AD797/LT1028 rival in phono and DAC output stages.*

**Technology:** Bipolar-input, voltage-feedback single op-amp from National's LME audio line (datasheet cover: 'ultra-low distortion, ultra-low noise operational amplifier'). SOIC-8 is the only package reported. The datasheet front page gives 0.9 nV/rtHz typ and 1.3 nV/rtHz max. The 110 MHz GBW and 0.00001% THD figures come from community summaries and were not checked against the datasheet.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A cult favourite single among op-amp rollers and low-noise designers. In the diyAudio thread 'LME49990 audio op amp - better than AD797?', a long-time AD797 fan reportedly called it the 'winner by a mile' and another poster 'the best sounding of any opamp out there' (from the discovery notes; not re-checked this run). In Soomal's 2020 Creative AE-9 test, the reviewer called it the highest-end single op-amp of the series. It made the hybrid fast roll-off filter mode sound finer and more refined, 'a bit like a mid/high-end desktop DAC', but the reviewer judged the LME49710 better value. On Zigsow it is listed with the AD797, THS4631 and LT1115 as a famous single, and reviewers praise its resolution: harmonies and reverb tails are fully revealed. Aurorasound frames it as National's rival to the LT1028A/OPA627A. Engineers are cooler. In EEVblog #541 the 'absolutely incredible ... outperforming the 797' line came from a viewer's letter that Dave Jones read out. Jones himself called the datasheet an 'audiophile wank alert', said never to believe banner specs, and found the noise curves 'not hugely better' than the AD797's, though he thought it probably the best choice for an HP 35670A DSA front-end upgrade. Douglas Self's 'The Design of Active Crossovers' (2nd ed.) has sections 'The LME49990 Opamp' and 'Common-Mode Distortion in the LME49990'. His measured results were not retrieved.

**How people describe the sound:** very extended bass (diyAudio, per discovery notes), clear midrange, sweet top end, finer, more refined texture (质感更加细腻, Soomal), high resolution: each note of harmonies and the full reverb tail audible (Zigsow), harsh and thin when under-bypassed (per Japanese users)

**Typical uses:** MC/MM phono preamplifiers (DIY), DAC and sound-card output/filter stages, e.g. Creative Sound Blaster AE-9 op-amp swaps, low-impedance, low-noise preamps and measurement front-ends (e.g. suggested for an HP 35670A DSA front-end upgrade in EEVblog #541), op-amp rolling in DIP-8 sockets via SOIC-to-DIP adapter boards

**Caveats:**

- SOIC-8 only, so DIP sockets need an adapter board. Adapter quality and extra lead length matter for a part with roughly 100 MHz-class GBW (general high-speed layout concern, not a specific report).
- Japanese users say it needs 10 uF or more of local supply bypassing. With only 0.1 uF it reportedly sounds harsh and thin, a common cause of bad reviews. This is forum folklore, not a datasheet statement.
- Reportedly discontinued; supply is now NOS or grey market (for-sale threads on diyAudio). TI status is unverified.
- Measured noise advantage over the AD797 is small. EEVblog found the noise curves 'not hugely better', so the headline 0.88/0.9 nV figure should not be over-read.
- Its advantage is low voltage noise, so it pays off in low-impedance circuits. Douglas Self gives common-mode distortion in the LME49990 its own section, so check non-inverting/follower use.
- Costs more than the sibling LME49710. Soomal judged the LME49710 better value in the AE-9 and the OPA627 a better pick for mic/SBX use.

## Counterfeits

No documented counterfeit or remarked LME49990 reports turned up. Risk is still elevated: the part is reportedly discontinued, SOIC-only, and traded as NOS or grey-market stock (e.g. diyAudio for-sale threads), often pre-soldered on SOIC-to-DIP adapter boards such as the BrownDog boards on Yahoo! Auctions, where the marking is hard to inspect. Buy traceable stock. Check the laser marking (National or TI logo, 'LME49990' / 'MA'). Also measure quiescent current and noise; a remarked cheaper bipolar part would show clearly higher e_n than about 0.9 nV/rtHz.

## Related parts and alternatives

TI OPA1611 (active bipolar audio single, 1.1 nV/rtHz, very low THD; OPA1612 dual), Analog Devices AD797 (0.9 nV/rtHz bipolar single; the part the LME49990 was most often compared with), Analog Devices / Linear LT1115 (0.9 nV/rtHz, audio-optimised) and LT1028 (0.85 nV/rtHz), TI/National LME49710 (cheaper sibling single, still commonly rolled; Soomal's value pick), Analog Devices ADA4898-1 (bipolar about 0.9 nV/rtHz single, SOIC; not verified in audio-community sources this run)

## Open questions

- TOOLING: the WebSearch budget was exhausted again for this verification pass (200/200), so no WebSearch queries could run. Checks used GitHub code search (about 20 queries) and local scratchpad copies only. The datasheet revision chain, the TI status and all forum and review URLs still need a web pass.
- TI literature number and revision letter(s) for the LME49990 datasheet are not confirmed. Read the footer of ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/lme49990.pdf, then probe /lit/ds/<lit><rev>/ for older letters.
- National DS number and the full National revision chain are unknown. Was there a 2010 initial release before the 'August 24, 2011' version cited in the TI model header? The diyAudio thread ID 160149 suggests community discussion by about 2010 (inference).
- All national.com URLs in archive_seed_urls (/ds/LM/, /pf/LM/, /mpf/LM/) and ti.com/lit/gpn/lme49990 are INFERRED from the vendors' usual URL patterns and were not seen in any result.
- EOL/discontinuation date, PCN number and last-time-buy date are unknown. The TI product-page status (Obsolete vs NRND vs Active) is unverified. The only 'discontinued' text found in this pass is a satirical Chinese post.
- The 0.88 vs 0.9 nV/rtHz headline discrepancy: which datasheet revision or page gives 0.88?
- Datasheet specs not verified: GBW (110 MHz claimed), THD+N (0.00001% claimed) and its conditions, slew rate, current noise, quiescent current, supply range, output current, and recommended decoupling. A third-party simplified LTspice model (LME49990_ED.lib) uses GBW=100 MHz, slew 20 V/us, SVn=1 nV/rtHz and SIn=2.8 pA/rtHz. These are community model parameters, not datasheet values, and are recorded here only as leads.
- Douglas Self's measured common-mode distortion results for the LME49990 ('The Design of Active Crossovers' 2nd ed., and possibly 'Small Signal Audio Design') were not retrieved.
- The EEVblog-wiki summary's 'LM797' is a transcription error for the AD797; the transcript itself corrects to '797'.
- The 10 uF-or-more bypass requirement is Japanese forum folklore (kakaku.com/zigsow). Check whether the datasheet application section says anything similar.
- No die, process or fab change under this part number was found (none expected for a short-lived single-source part), but no TI PCN search could be done.
