# LME49990 (National Overture E-Series / TI ultra-low-noise bipolar single)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*National's Dec 2009 Overture E-Series flagship single (0.9 nV/rtHz, 0.00001% THD+N), rolled as an AD797/LT1028 rival in phono and DAC stages.*

**Technology:** Bipolar-input, voltage-feedback single op-amp from National's LME 'Overture E-Series' audio line. The datasheet title is 'Ultra-low Distortion, Ultra-low Noise Operational Amplifier'. Front-page specs: 0.9 nV/rtHz typ and 1.3 nV/rtHz max at 1 kHz, 1/f corner 43 Hz, THD+N 0.00001% (AV=1, 3 Vrms, 1 kHz, 600 ohm), GBW 110 MHz, slew rate 22 V/us, supply range +/-5 V to +/-18 V, output current +/-27 mA. The only package is SOIC-8 (LME49990MA).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A cult-favourite single among op-amp rollers and low-noise designers. In the diyAudio thread 'LME49990 audio op amp - better than AD797?', a long-time AD797 fan reportedly called it the 'winner by a mile' (per discovery notes, not re-checked). In Soomal's 2020 Creative AE-9 test, the reviewer called it the highest-end single of the series. It made the hybrid fast roll-off filter mode sound more refined, but the reviewer judged the LME49710 better value. Zigsow lists it with the AD797, THS4631 and LT1115 as a famous single and praises its resolution. Aurorasound frames it as National's rival to the LT1028A/OPA627A. Engineers are cooler. In EEVblog #541 the 'outperforming the 797' line came from a viewer's letter. Dave Jones himself called the datasheet an 'audiophile wank alert' and found the noise curves 'not hugely better' than the AD797's. TI's own E2E answer is that there is no exact replacement; the OPA1611/1612 are the closest. Douglas Self gives it sections in 'The Design of Active Crossovers' (2nd ed.), including one on its common-mode distortion.

**How people describe the sound:** very extended bass (diyAudio, per discovery notes), clear midrange, sweet top end, finer, more refined texture (Soomal), high resolution: harmonies and reverb tails fully revealed (Zigsow), harsh and thin when under-bypassed (Japanese users)

**Typical uses:** MC/MM phono preamplifiers (DIY), DAC and sound-card output/filter stages, e.g. Creative Sound Blaster AE-9 op-amp swaps, low-impedance, low-noise preamps and measurement front-ends (e.g. suggested for an HP 35670A front-end in EEVblog #541), op-amp rolling in DIP-8 sockets via SOIC-to-DIP adapter boards

**Caveats:**

- Obsolete: TI first made it NRND under a process end of life, and distributors now list it Obsolete. Supply is NOS or grey market only.
- SOIC-8 only, so DIP sockets need an adapter. The extra lead length matters for a part with 110 MHz GBW; this is a general layout concern, not a specific report.
- Japanese users say it needs 10 uF or more of local supply bypassing, and that with only 0.1 uF it sounds harsh and thin. This is forum folklore, not a datasheet statement.
- Its measured noise advantage over the AD797 is small (EEVblog #541), so do not over-read the headline 0.88/0.9 nV figure.
- It pays off in low-impedance circuits. Douglas Self treats its common-mode distortion separately, so check non-inverting and follower use.
- TI's suggested replacement, the OPA1611, has nearly as low distortion, but its THD rises more at high frequency (TI E2E, per search summary).
- Costs more than the sibling LME49710. Soomal judged the LME49710 better value in the AE-9.

### Revision chain status

Vendor documents: National Semiconductor's LME49990 datasheet (National DS number still unknown), later renumbered by TI as SNOSB16. No second sources exist; the part is single-source. Chain from oldest to newest: (1) National Rev 1.0, 12/16/2009, initial release. The date comes from a search summary of the revision-history table. No copy of this exact edition has been identified. (2) National 'Overture(R) E-Series' edition dated January 8, 2010, held by Octopart (https://datasheet.octopart.com/LME49990MA-National-Semiconductor-datasheet-8636748.pdf). Its internal revision number (probably 1.01 or similar) was not shown. (3) SNOSB16A: MISSING. Its date and any copy are unknown, and it did not appear in searches for 'SNOSB16A'. It is inferred only because B exists. (4) SNOSB16B, 'DECEMBER 2009 - REVISED AUGUST 2011': Mouser snosb16b-61898.pdf. (5) SNOSB16C, 'DECEMBER 2009 - REVISED APRIL 2013': Digi-Key and the ti.com symlink. C appears to be the final revision before the part went obsolete. Six new third-party mirror URLs were found: datasheetspdf, two more datasheet4u variants, and datasheet39, all carrying National file id 946925; an alldatasheet.net HTML view of NSC 394357; and a datasheetcatalog TI copy. None of the summaries showed a revision letter, so all are low confidence. Still missing: SNOSB16A (date, change log, copy), the National DS number and any national.com-era URL confirmed in search results, the change logs for Revs B and C, and the NRND/obsolescence PCN. Archive seeds are URLs I built from standard TI and National patterns (ti.com/lit/ds/snosb16x/snosb16x.pdf, national.com/ds/LM/, national.com/pf/LM/); none appeared in search results, so treat them only as Wayback lookup candidates. ## Counterfeits

No documented counterfeit or remarked LME49990 reports turned up. Risk is still elevated. The part is Obsolete (TI process end of life), single-source and SOIC-only, and it trades as NOS or grey-market stock, often pre-soldered on SOIC-to-DIP adapters (e.g. BrownDog boards on Yahoo! Auctions), where the marking is hard to inspect. Buy traceable stock. Check the laser marking (National or TI logo, 'LME49990'/'MA'). Also measure quiescent current (about 9 mA typ per distributor data) and noise; a remarked cheaper bipolar part would show clearly more than about 0.9 nV/rtHz at 1 kHz.

## Related parts and alternatives

TI OPA1611 (TI's suggested replacement per E2E; 1.1 nV/rtHz bipolar audio single; THD rises more at HF; OPA1612 dual), Analog Devices AD797 (0.9 nV/rtHz bipolar single; the part most often compared with the LME49990), Analog Devices / Linear LT1115 (0.9 nV/rtHz, audio-optimised) and LT1028 (0.85 nV/rtHz), TI/National LME49710 (cheaper sibling single; Soomal's value pick; also discontinued, per Gearspace EOL thread), Analog Devices ADA4898-1 (about 0.9 nV/rtHz bipolar single, SOIC; not verified in audio-community sources)
