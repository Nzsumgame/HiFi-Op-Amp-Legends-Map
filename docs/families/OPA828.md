# OPA828 / OPA2828 (45 MHz, 150 V/us SiGe JFET-input, "next-generation OPA627/OPA827")

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** JFET-input

*TI's 2018 fast JFET-input op-amp (4 nV/rtHz, 45 MHz, 150 V/us, -130 dB THD+N). DIY rollers use it on DIP adapters as a modern OPA627: extremely neutral, wide soundstage.*

**Technology:** JFET-input (BiFET). The datasheet says it is built on TI's "high-voltage, SiGe-complementary, JFET-bipolar process technology" (also called a "SiGe precision, high-speed, high-voltage, BiFET wafer process") and that "patented wafer processing techniques" reduce JFET gate-region noise. Offset voltage is laser-trimmed at two temperatures; offset drift and quiescent current are also trimmed. It has a proprietary slew-boost circuit that fires on large input differential voltage, plus an overload power limiter that turns slew-boost off after about 300 ns. There are no anti-parallel diodes across the inputs ('MUX-friendly'), so the differential input can reach the full supply, but the inputs are diode-clamped to the rails. It is specified at G=+1 (57 deg phase margin with CL=30 pF). The output is not rail-to-rail in practice (0.9 V typ / 1.2 V max from each rail at 10 kohm), even though some distributor blurbs label it 'RRO'.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded: Datasheet respec).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

DIY and op-amp-rolling hobbyists treat the OPA828 as the modern high-speed successor to the OPA627, matching TI's 'next generation OPA627 and OPA827' positioning. A long diyAudio thread, 'OPA828 / OPA2828 vs OPA627' (at least 7 pages), is web-confirmed. In it one user calls the OPA2828 extremely neutral, with a very wide soundstage, very transparent, with lots of micro-detail and no harshness, and rates it above the OPA1642. Another calls the OPA828 completely transparent and fatigue-free over a full day. A third reports no oscillation in any circuit tried, including breadboards. The thread also notes that OPA627 fans may hear the OPA2828 as 'thin'. These are individual forum opinions, not a consensus measurement. Audiophonics sells an 'OPA828 Dual OPA DIP8' adapter unit for rolling. Japanese 2026 roundups (maimai-audio), which use descriptors such as elegant, low-colour and spacious, were not re-verified. GitHub-verified uses: the open-source Q17 power amplifier switched U1 from OPA1641 to OPA828 'for JFET lovers'; a Japanese DIY board (indare/pcb_work) uses two singles on a DIP module; a 2025 instrumentation project chose it over the OPA627/637 on price (~$7 vs ~$30). The datasheet gives -130 dB THD+N and 137 dB CCIF IMD. No independent ASR or Samuel Groner measurement was located.

**How people describe the sound:** extremely neutral, very wide soundstage, very transparent, lots of micro-detail, doesn't hurt the ears / no listening fatigue, can sound 'thin' to OPA627 fans, elegant (JP blog, unverified), low colouration (JP blog, unverified), spacious (JP blog, unverified)

**Typical uses:** DAC I/V conversion and post-filter / output stages, line buffers and preamps, RIAA phono preamps, headphone amps with a separate buffer (OPA828 + LME49600), power-amplifier input/error-amp stage (Q17 amplifier), op-amp rolling on SOIC-to-DIP adapter modules (e.g. Audiophonics 'OPA828 Dual OPA DIP8' unit; two singles on a dual-DIP adapter, or OPA2828 on a PowerPAD-capable adapter)

**Caveats:**

- No DIP package exists. The single comes in SOIC-8 or HVSSOP-8 and the dual only in HVSSOP-8 PowerPAD, so socketed use needs adapter boards, which add parasitics. TI says to solder the DGN exposed pad and tie it to V- or GND.
- This is a fast part (45 MHz GBW, 150 V/us, 57 deg phase margin at CL=30 pF). TI says to isolate heavier capacitive loads with a small series resistor (e.g. 50 ohm). Layout, decoupling and adapter parasitics matter. The indare/pcb_work review puts it in its 'layout-sensitive' group, well below LT1364, and rates it relatively well-behaved. A diyAudio user reports no oscillation, even on breadboards.
- Slew-boost engages when the inputs are driven far apart. TI warns this 'can distort the signal' and advises keeping the inputs close together where low distortion is needed (relevant to clipping and overload, not normal linear closed-loop use).
- Minimum supply is +/-4 V (8 V). Common-mode range is only (V-)+2.5 V to (V+)-3.5 V, and the output needs 0.9 V typ (1.2 V max) of headroom from each rail. Despite 'RRO' in some distributor blurbs, it is not rail-to-rail and is unsuitable for 5 V or low-voltage portable gear. TI E2E notes the swing spec is a 'slam' test; the linear range is the one given with the AOL spec.
- It draws 5.5 mA per channel (about 165 mW/ch at +/-15 V), which gives a self-rise of roughly 20 C with the SOIC RthJA of 121.5 C/W. JFET bias current rises steeply with temperature (max +/-400 pA at 0-85C, +/-3 nA at 125C). A two-single adapter draws about 11 mA.
- Output current is +/-30 mA linear (50 mA short-circuit). That is fine for 600 ohm and above, but it is not a stand-alone low-impedance headphone driver.
- The inputs have no anti-parallel protection diodes. Differential input up to the supply is allowed, but the inputs are diode-clamped to the rails, so limit input current to 10 mA.
- Datasheet limits for OPA828ID/IDR changed under PCN20221117006 (Nov 2022, spec-only, same device). Do not compare Rev B (2018-2022) limits with Rev C/D limits as if the silicon had changed.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Datasheet respec: Texas Instruments, November 2022 (PCN20221117006), coinciding with SBOS671C…

'Same part number, different datasheet' hazard, not a die change. TI's PCN20221117006 announced a specification change for the SOIC OPA828ID/IDR 'to accurately reflect device characteristics', with no expected impact on fit, form, function, quality or reliability and no device change. SOIC limits in Rev C/D therefore differ from Rev B in at least one parameter, which was not identified in this pass. A 2019 TI E2E thread titled 'OPA828: Output swing specification disagrees with datasheet figures' may be related (unconfirmed).

- **When:** November 2022 (PCN20221117006), coinciding with SBOS671C (Oct 2022) / SBOS671D (Dec 2022)
- **Affected:** OPA828ID, OPA828IDR
- **How to tell old from new:** No marking or orderable change: TI states there are no changes to product identification. Parts cannot be told apart physically. Only the datasheet revision differs: SBOS671B (Dec 2018) or earlier gives the old limits; SBOS671C/D gives the revised limits.
- **Audio impact:** None expected: the silicon is unchanged, and noise, GBW, slew rate and THD+N typicals appear the same in the Rev-B-era text and Rev D. The only practical risk is a design-margin check against old Rev B limits.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - it is a spec-only PCN with the same device and markings. Re-check worst-case budgets only if a design was qualified against the Rev B limit table.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Verification:** WebSearch confirmed that DigiKey hosts PCN20221117006 covering OPA828ID/OPA828IDR. The specific revised parameter was still not identified.

## Other change notes (lifecycle, packaging, successors, lore)

### 1. Package / assembly: Texas Instruments, October 2022 (preview, SBOS671C) / December 2022…

A package extension, not a documented die change. TI added a thermally enhanced DGN package for the single and introduced the dual OPA2828 in DGN only (web-confirmed). DGN parts carry a separate, tighter DC spec set than the SOIC OPA828. TI does not say that a new die, process or fab is involved, and no DGN-specific PCN was found. The SOIC part is the same device, but its datasheet limits were revised at the same time (see PCN20221117006). Whether the DGN part uses revised silicon or only a different trim, test or package flow is unknown. AC specs (noise, GBW, slew rate, THD+N) are common to both packages.

- **When:** October 2022 (preview, SBOS671C) / December 2022 (production, SBOS671D)
- **Affected:** OPA828IDGNR, OPA828IDGNT, OPA2828IDGNR, OPA2828IDGNT
- **How to tell old from new:** Orderable suffix DGN (HVSSOP-8 PowerPAD) vs D (SOIC-8). Top marking '2RAJ' (OPA828 DGN) or '2QGJ' (OPA2828 DGN) vs 'OPA828' (SOIC). The spec rows first appear in datasheet Rev C/D.
- **Audio impact:** Negligible for audio. The deltas are DC precision and a few dB of CMRR; noise, GBW, slew rate and THD+N are specified identically. The DGN runs cooler because of the PowerPAD. The dual needs a PowerPAD-capable adapter.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - different footprints, suffixes and markings mean the parts cannot be confused. Only the OPA2828 is restricted to HVSSOP-8, with an exposed pad that should be soldered.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Verification:** Not re-searched (medium-confidence packaging entry, outside the priority set); deltas taken from the current TI datasheet via the prior pass. It is unknown whether DGN parts use different silicon or only a different trim, test or package flow.

## Counterfeits

No OPA828 or OPA2828 counterfeits were documented in the sources found. This pass's six web searches did not target counterfeits, so the question is open either way. Authenticity facts from TI's 2025 package addenda: (1) TI makes no DIP-8 version of either part, so any 'OPA828'/'OPA2828' DIP is an adapter board (e.g. Audiophonics' 'OPA828 Dual OPA DIP8' unit); a DIP-8 IC body laser-marked OPA828/OPA2828 is not a TI package. (2) The OPA2828 exists only in HVSSOP-8 (DGN), marked '2QGJ', so an SOIC-8 or DIP body marked 'OPA2828' is suspect. (3) A genuine SOIC OPA828 is marked 'OPA828' and a DGN OPA828 '2RAJ'. The OPA627 it replaces in rolling circles is heavily counterfeited. The OPA828 is much cheaper (~$7 vs ~$30 for OPA627/637 per a 2025 project note), which lowers the incentive to fake it, but adapter modules are usually bought from marketplaces. Inspect the chip marking and, where possible, check slew rate (~150 V/us) and pA-level bias current.
