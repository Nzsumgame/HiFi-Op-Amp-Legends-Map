# OPA828 / OPA2828 (45 MHz, 150 V/us SiGe JFET-input, "next-generation OPA627/OPA827")

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** JFET-input

*TI's 2018 fast JFET-input op-amp (4 nV/rtHz, 45 MHz, 150 V/us, -130 dB THD+N). DIY rollers put it on DIP adapters as a modern OPA627 and describe it as very neutral and spacious.*

**Technology:** JFET-input (BiFET). The datasheet says it is built on TI's "high-voltage, SiGe-complementary, JFET-bipolar process technology" (also called a "SiGe precision, high-speed, high-voltage, BiFET wafer process") and that "patented wafer processing techniques" reduce JFET gate-region noise. Offset voltage is laser-trimmed at two temperatures; offset drift and quiescent current are also trimmed. It has a proprietary slew-boost circuit that fires on large input differential voltage, plus an overload power limiter that turns slew-boost off after about 300 ns. There are no anti-parallel diodes across the inputs ('MUX-friendly'), so the differential input can reach the full supply, but the inputs are diode-clamped to the rails. It is specified at G=+1 (57 deg phase margin with CL=30 pF).

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Among DIY and op-amp-rolling hobbyists the OPA828 is treated as the modern high-speed successor to the OPA627, which matches TI's own 'next generation OPA627 and OPA827' positioning. Discovery searches found a diyAudio 'OPA828/OPA2828 vs OPA627' thread, threads on RIAA and OPA828+LME49600 builds, and Japanese 2026 roundups (maimai-audio) that describe it as an elegant, low-colour, spacious FET all-rounder. Those forum and blog sources were not re-verified in this pass. GitHub-verified uses: the open-source Q17 power amplifier replaced OPA1641 with OPA828 in U1 'for JFET lovers' and reports 0 mV output DC offset with OPA828 vs 1 mV with OPA1611. A Japanese DIY board (indare/pcb_work) keeps OPA828 on a two-singles DIP module and rates it easy to try as-is. A 2025 instrumentation project picked it over the OPA627/637 on price (about $7 vs about $30). On measurements, the datasheet gives -130 dB THD+N into 600 ohm and 10 kohm and 137 dB CCIF IMD. No independent Audio Science Review or Samuel Groner op-amp measurement was located.

**How people describe the sound:** extremely neutral, very wide soundstage, very transparent, lots of micro-detail, doesn't hurt the ears, elegant, low colouration, spacious

**Typical uses:** DAC I/V conversion and post-filter / output stages, line buffers and preamps, RIAA phono preamps, headphone amps with a separate buffer (OPA828 + LME49600), power-amplifier input/error-amp stage (Q17 amplifier), op-amp rolling on SOIC-to-DIP adapter modules (two singles on a dual-DIP adapter, or OPA2828 on a PowerPAD-capable adapter)

**Caveats:**

- No DIP package exists. The single comes in SOIC-8 or HVSSOP-8 and the dual only in HVSSOP-8 PowerPAD, so socketed use needs adapter boards, which add parasitics. TI says to solder the DGN exposed pad and tie it to V- or GND.
- This is a fast part (45 MHz GBW, 150 V/us, 57 deg phase margin at CL=30 pF). TI says to isolate heavier capacitive loads with a small series resistor, for example 50 ohm. Layout, decoupling and adapter parasitics matter. The indare/pcb_work datasheet review puts it in its 'layout-sensitive' group, but well below LT1364, and rates it relatively well-behaved. The review reports no oscillation.
- Slew-boost engages when the inputs are driven far apart. TI warns this 'can distort the signal' and advises keeping the inputs close together where low distortion is needed (relevant to clipping and overload, not normal linear closed-loop use).
- Minimum supply is +/-4 V (8 V). Common-mode range is only (V-)+2.5 V to (V+)-3.5 V and the output needs 0.9 V typical (1.2 V max) of headroom from each rail, so it is unsuitable for 5 V or low-voltage portable gear.
- It draws 5.5 mA per channel (about 165 mW/ch at +/-15 V). With the SOIC RthJA of 121.5 C/W that is roughly a 20 C self-rise. JFET bias current rises steeply with temperature (max +/-400 pA at 0-85C, +/-3 nA at 125C), which matters in very high-impedance stages. A two-single adapter draws about 11 mA.
- Output current is +/-30 mA linear (50 mA short-circuit): fine for 600 ohm and above, but it is not a stand-alone low-impedance headphone driver.
- The inputs have no anti-parallel protection diodes. Differential input up to the supply is allowed, but the inputs are diode-clamped to the rails, so limit input current to 10 mA.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Texas Instruments: October 2022 (preview, SBOS671C) / December 2022…

This is a package extension, not a documented die change. TI added a thermally enhanced DGN package for the single and introduced the dual OPA2828 in DGN only. The DGN parts carry a separate, tighter DC spec set than the SOIC OPA828. TI does not state that a new die, process or fab is involved, and no PCN was found. The SOIC (D) part appears to keep its original 2018 specification: TI's Rev C log lists no D-limit change, the Rev-B-era features text shows the same D typicals (50 uV, 0.45 uV/C, 1 pA), and the Rev-B-based PSpice model uses 0.45 uV/C drift. Whether the DGN part uses revised silicon or only a different trim, test or package flow is unknown. AC specs (noise, GBW, slew rate, THD+N) are common to both packages.

- **When:** October 2022 (preview, SBOS671C) / December 2022 (production, SBOS671D)
- **Affected:** OPA828IDGNR, OPA828IDGNT, OPA2828IDGNR, OPA2828IDGNT
- **How to tell old from new:** Orderable suffix DGN (HVSSOP-8 PowerPAD) vs D (SOIC-8). Top marking '2RAJ' (OPA828 DGN) or '2QGJ' (OPA2828 DGN) vs 'OPA828' (SOIC). The spec rows first appear in datasheet Rev C/D. SOIC parts have no documented change.
- **Audio impact:** Negligible for audio. The deltas are DC precision (offset, drift, bias) and a few dB of CMRR; noise, GBW, slew rate and THD+N are specified identically. The DGN runs cooler because of the PowerPAD, which also keeps JFET bias current lower. The main practical impact is packaging: the dual needs a PowerPAD-capable adapter.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - the parts use different footprints, suffixes and markings, so they cannot be confused. The SOIC D variant is unchanged per the revision history. Only the OPA2828 is restricted to HVSSOP-8 with an exposed pad that should be soldered.
- **Confidence:** medium

## Counterfeits

No OPA828 or OPA2828 counterfeits were documented in the sources available; web search was unavailable in both the research and verification passes, so this is unconfirmed either way. Authenticity facts from TI's 2025 package addenda: (1) TI makes no DIP-8 version of either part, so any 'OPA828' or 'OPA2828' DIP must be an adapter board; a DIP-8 IC body laser-marked OPA828/OPA2828 is not a TI package. (2) The OPA2828 exists only in HVSSOP-8 (DGN) with top marking '2QGJ', so an SOIC-8 or DIP body marked 'OPA2828' is suspect. (3) A genuine SOIC OPA828 is marked 'OPA828' and a DGN OPA828 '2RAJ', each with possible logo and lot-trace marks. The OPA627, which it replaces in rolling circles, is heavily counterfeited. The OPA828 is much cheaper (a 2025 project note puts it at about $7 vs about $30 for OPA627/637), which lowers the incentive to fake it, but adapter modules are usually bought from marketplaces. Inspect the chip marking on any adapter and, where possible, check slew rate (~150 V/us) and pA-level bias current.

This pass checked the record against the full SBOS671D text (two TI builds, 2025-07-26 and 2026-08-11), hyperlinks embedded in the PDF, GitHub code search and git clones (Q17 schematics and changelogs, PSpice model headers, indare review, a 2019 text copy of the Rev-B-era datasheet). Forum and blog URLs and the community sonic descriptors remain unverified.
- Original SBOS671 (Sep 2018), Rev A (Nov 2018), Rev B (Dec 2018) and Rev C (Oct 2022): no PDF copies were located. The /lit/ds/sbos671X/sbos671X.pdf URLs and /lit/ds/symlink/opa2828.pdf are inferred from TI's usual pattern and have not been seen. Wayback captures of symlink/opa828.pdf between Dec 2018 and Oct 2022 should hold Rev B.
- Did the Sep/Nov 2018 pre-production sheets list different typical values or an OPA2828 preview? Unknown. The Rev-B-era text copy shows a single SOIC OPA828 only.
- Is the DGN-package OPA828/OPA2828 revised silicon (better Vos, Ib and drift, lower CMRR) or the same die with a different trim, test or package? TI does not say. No PCN search was possible.
- The '.B' duplicate orderable rows appear across many TI addenda (OPA1611/1612, OPA1652, OPA1656, OPA1642 too), so they are not OPA828-specific. Their exact meaning (alternate site or material flow?) is still unconfirmed.
- SBOS671D labels Rev C as 'December 2018' in the C->D heading but 'October 2022' in its own entry. This appears to be a TI typo.
- No independent audio-bench measurement (ASR, Samuel Groner, etc.) of OPA828 THD or IMD was located.
