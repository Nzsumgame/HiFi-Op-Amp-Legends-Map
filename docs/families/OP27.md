# OP27 / OP37 (PMI/ADI low-noise precision bipolar; OP37 = decompensated, gain ≥5)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** Bipolar classics

*PMI's c.1980 3 nV/√Hz zener-trimmed precision bipolar op-amp, long used in phono, tape-head and mic preamps; ADI's datasheet still carries those audio circuits.*

**Technology:** Monolithic bipolar, single channel. The NPN input stage runs at high quiescent current for low e_n. It has input bias-current cancellation, back-to-back input clamp diodes without series resistors, and on-chip zener-zap offset trim at wafer test. OP27 is unity-gain stable (8 MHz, 2.8 V/µs). OP37 is the same design decompensated for closed-loop gain ≥5 (63 MHz, 17 V/µs); the datasheet schematics are identical.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (2 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

The reputation is mostly pro-audio and engineering, not 'op-amp rolling'. PMI and ADI pitched the OP27/OP37 at professional audio: the datasheet cites ±10 V into 600 Ω with low distortion and shows RIAA phono, NAB tape-head and transformerless and transformer-coupled mic preamps. A 1990s hobbyist audio FAQ (Atari ST diskmag) says 'The OP-27, OP-37, LT1028, and LT1115 are very well received for phono preamps, head amplifiers, and microphone preamplifiers' and lists the OP-27 among good audio singles, without the 'highly recommended' star. Douglas Self's 'The Design of Active Crossovers' (2nd ed.) has a section titled 'The OP27 Opamp'; its content was not read this run. The diyAudio quotes ('a classic', 'nice forgotten opamp') come from discovery notes and were not re-read. No Audio Science Review or Samuel Groner measurements were verified.

**How people describe the sound:** 'a classic' (diyAudio, via discovery notes; unverified), 'nice forgotten opamp' for phono (diyAudio, via discovery notes; unverified), 'very well received' for phono, head-amp and mic preamps (1990s Atari ST diskmag audio FAQ)

**Typical uses:** MM phono RIAA preamps (datasheet Figure 41), Microphone preamps, transformerless (OP37 at 50 dB) or transformer-coupled (OP27), Tape-head (NAB) playback preamps, Low-noise gain stages from low source impedance (<1–3 kΩ), Precision instrumentation and ADC drivers

**Caveats:**

- OP37 is decompensated and stable only at gain ≥5. ADI warns that an unplugged mic input (100% feedback) can oscillate and suggests a dummy resistor Rp.
- Bias-current cancellation raises current noise (1.7 pA/√Hz at 10 Hz) and gives poor IB matching. ADI: 'The noise advantage of the OP27 disappears when high source resistors are used.' For low-frequency work the OP07 is better above about 3 kΩ source resistance.
- OP27 slew rate is low (2.8 V/µs), and the 8 MHz GBW causes RIAA equalisation errors at gains above 40 dB (datasheet). Self's comments on the OP27 were not verified.
- The unprotected input diodes conduct when the differential input exceeds 0.7 V. Fast large pulses in unity-gain buffers pull current: use Rf ≥ 500 Ω, plus 20–50 pF across Rf when Rf > 2 kΩ.
- Best linearity when peak output current is below ±10 mA (OP37 datasheet, 'Optimizing Linearity').
- Single channel only: needs an adapter for dual-op-amp sockets. Draws about 3 mA per amplifier.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Linear Technology / Texas Instruments…: c.1989-1990s

LTC second-sourced the part and published OP-27/OP-37 data sheets in its 1990 databook. Whether LTC used its own die is unknown. The earlier inference from LTC's OP27A macromodel matching its LT1007 model is withdrawn, because both are behavioural models built from the same LTC template (written 11-21-1989) and say nothing about the die. TI's involvement rests only on MicroSim-generated macromodels in a third-party 'ti' folder. No side-by-side specs or measurements were found.

- **When:** c.1989-1990s
- **Affected:** OP-27A/C/E/G (LTC), OP-37A/C/E/G (LTC), OP-227/OP-237 (LTC), OP-27C/E/G, OP-37A (TI, unconfirmed)
- **How to tell old from new:** Vendor logo on the package. LTC used the hyphenated 'OP-27' name in its databook references. Actual package marking formats were not verified.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. No listening or measurement comparisons found.
- **Drop-in risk:** low – all are pin-compatible OP07/OP27-pinout singles specified to the same OP-27/OP-37 grade names; any differences would be in unspecified parameters.
- **Confidence:** low

### 2. Analog Devices: between Rev E (12/05) and Rev F (5/06)

Pb-free (RoHS) lead-finish versions were added to the ordering guide in Rev F (5/06). This is a package and lead-finish change, not a documented die change: Rev F has a single specification table covering both leaded and Pb-free codes. The OP37 datasheet seen (Rev B, 12/02) predates Pb-free codes.

- **When:** between Rev E (12/05) and Rev F (5/06)
- **Affected:** OP27EPZ, OP27GPZ, OP27GSZ, OP27GSZ-REEL, OP27GSZ-REEL7
- **How to tell old from new:** Trailing 'Z' after the package letter P or S in the ordering code (Rev F footnote 'Z = Pb-free part'). Do not confuse it with the CERDIP 'Z' package suffix in OP27AZ/EZ/GZ.
- **Audio impact:** None expected. No reports of audible or measured differences found.
- **Drop-in risk:** low – same die specs and pinout; only lead finish (and reflow profile) differ.
- **Confidence:** high

## Counterfeits

No OP27/OP37-specific counterfeit or remarking reports were verified (web search unavailable this run). General guidance only: genuine current parts carry ADI Pb-free codes (e.g. OP27GPZ/GSZ). Treat cheap marketplace 'OP27' parts with PMI or old LTC markings, or new-looking CERDIP/TO-99 A-grade parts, with suspicion. A quick check is the ~3 mA supply current (90 mW typ at ±15 V) and the ~3 nV/√Hz noise floor with a low source impedance; a relabelled generic op-amp will be much noisier.
