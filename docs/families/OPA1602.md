# OPA1602 / OPA1604 (TI SoundPlus dual/quad bipolar-input, 2.5 nV/√Hz)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*TI's 2011 SoundPlus bipolar dual/quad: 2.5 nV/√Hz and 0.00003% THD+N at 2.6 mA/ch. TI support steered LME49720 users to it, and OEMs use it for I/V and line stages.*

**Technology:** Bipolar-input, rail-to-rail-output, unity-gain-stable voltage-feedback op-amp (TI SoundPlus line; the datasheet carries the Burr-Brown Audio logo). Surface-mount only: SOIC-8/MSOP-8 (VSSOP, DGK) for the dual, SOIC-14/TSSOP-14 for the quad.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

An OEM and engineering favourite rather than an op-amp-rolling legend. It comes only in SMD packages, so enthusiasts rarely swap it. It pairs low noise and distortion with modest supply current (2.6 mA/ch). Per a TI E2E thread and Gearspace discussion (not re-checked this run), TI pointed LME49720 users to it after that part's EOL. Reported design wins come from mirrored reviews (Soomal, Frieve) and maker pages: the iBasso DX90 uses a pair of OPA1602 per channel as the DAC I/V stage (with OPA1611 and BUF634), and the Cayin RU3 dongle uses OPA1602 alongside SGM8262 in parallel drive. DIY projects use it as a transformerless balanced line driver, and the quad OPA1604 shows up in multichannel mixers. No op-amp-specific listening tests or ASR/Samuel Groner measurements were found.

**How people describe the sound:** neutral, restrained, relaxed, slightly soft mids: Soomal's description of the whole iBasso DX90 player, not of the op-amp alone

**Typical uses:** DAC I/V conversion (iBasso DX90, per Soomal), Portable DAC/headphone dongle output stage in parallel drive (Cayin RU3), Replacement for the LME49720/LM4562 in new designs (TI support suggestion per E2E, unconfirmed this run), Transformerless balanced line driver (DIY XPS project), Multichannel line mixers and filter banks (OPA1604)

**Caveats:**

- SMD only (SOIC/MSOP/TSSOP). A DIP-8 socket needs an adapter board.
- About ±30 mA output drive suits line level, not low-impedance headphones on its own. The designs above add BUF634s or parallel sections.
- Current noise of about 1.8 pA/√Hz (TI figure) makes noise rise with high source impedances, a general bipolar-input trade-off.
- No op-amp-specific stability or oscillation reports were found.

## Counterfeits

No counterfeit or remark reports specific to OPA1602/OPA1604 were found. As an SMD part it is often sold on marketplaces pre-mounted on DIP-8 adapter boards, where the chip's origin cannot be checked. Buy from authorized distributors (TI, DigiKey, Mouser) and check the top-side marking and lot trace code with TI's marking lookup.
