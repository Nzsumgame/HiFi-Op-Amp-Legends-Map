# LM118 / LM218 / LM318 (fast bipolar single op-amp, 15 MHz / 50 V/us)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*Fast 1970s bipolar op-amp (15 MHz, 50 V/us min) that high-slew advocates favoured in the late-70s TIM debate. Now a niche pick for fast buffers and DAC I/V stages.*

**Technology:** Bipolar with NPN differential input and an input differential clamp (per LTC SPICE model comments). Internally compensated for unity gain, and external compensation can be added: feed-forward compensation in inverting use gives >150 V/us and nearly double the bandwidth (TI datasheet). Pins 1/5/8 are compensation/balance pins. Single channel, ±5 V to ±20 V supplies.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (2 recorded: Second-source difference).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A fast 1970s bipolar op-amp (15 MHz, 50 V/us min). It is reportedly among the high-slew parts favoured in the late-1970s slew-induced-distortion/TIM debate (Walter Jung et al., Audio magazine), but the specific articles were not verified. Today it is a niche part in DIY circles for fast buffers and DAC I/V stages. Community evidence is thin: there are no published noise specs and no measured reviews were found. It is not a low-noise audio design. Some hobby write-ups wrongly call it a dedicated audio op-amp.

**Typical uses:** DAC output buffer / I-V stage (DIY), fast line buffers, active video filters and video receivers, function generators / oscillators, fast D/A output amplifiers, pulse amplifiers, class-D amplifier control loop (academic paper)

**Caveats:**

- High-speed part: bypass pins 4 and 7 with 0.1 uF capacitors placed right at the pins. It is prone to oscillation with stray capacitance on the inverting input and with long socket/adaptor leads.
- Pins 1/5/8 are compensation/balance pins, so feed-forward-compensated circuits are not drop-in with parts that use pin 8 as NC or pins 1/5 as offset null.
- Feed-forward compensation (>150 V/us) applies only to inverting configurations.
- Quiescent current is about 5 mA typ per amp (third-party figure).
- Needs at least ±5 V supplies.
- No noise specification. Not a low-noise part compared with NE5534/LME49710-class devices.
- TI sells two separate LM318 lines (LM318 / SLOS063 and LM318-N / SNOSBS8), and LTC made its own version, so behaviour may differ by maker.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Second-source difference: Texas Instruments (TI-legacy) vs…, Concurrent since 1976 (TI SLOS063 original date) and…

TI sells two separately documented LM318 product lines under the same generic number: its own second source (datasheet since June 1976) and National's original (now LM318-N). The die difference is inferred from their separate origins. A third-party audio SPICE modeller also keeps separate National and TI models with different parameters. No vendor document compares the two.

- **When:** Concurrent since 1976 (TI SLOS063 original date) and within the TI catalogue since the 2011 National acquisition
- **Affected:** LM318, LM318D, LM318DR, LM318P, LM318PSR, LM318N/NOPB, LM318M, LM318MX/NOPB, LM118H
- **How to tell old from new:** By datasheet: SLOS063 ('High-Performance Operational Amplifiers', ti.com/product/LM318, lm318.pdf) versus SNOSBS8 ('LM118-N/LM218-N/LM318-N Operational Amplifiers', ti.com/product/LM318-N, lm318-n.pdf). By package code: TI-style D/DR/P/PSR are TI-legacy; National-style N/M/MX/H/J and /NOPB are ex-National.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. Compensation, slew and stability margins may differ, so a part swap can change behaviour in fast or feed-forward-compensated circuits.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium: same pinout and headline specs (15 MHz, 50 V/us min), but separate designs and datasheets. Check which datasheet (SLOS063 or SNOSBS8) applies to the orderable you buy.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Verification:** Not web-searched; second-source differences were outside the priority kinds, and no PCN, die-change or respec claim needed checking.

## Counterfeits

No specific LM318 counterfeit reports were found. General precaution for cheap marketplace DIP-8 LM318N parts: they may be remarked slower op-amps. As a quick check, run a unity-gain square wave at ±15 V. A genuine part slews at least 50 V/us, while a remarked 741/4558-class part slews under about 1-2 V/us. This is generic advice, not a documented LM318 case. Note also that LM318M (National SOIC) is reported obsolete, so new-stock offers of it deserve extra scrutiny.
