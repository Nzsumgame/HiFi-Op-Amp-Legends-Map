# LM118 / LM218 / LM318 (fast bipolar single op-amp, 15 MHz / 50 V/us)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*1970s 15 MHz, 50-70 V/us bipolar op-amp that high-slew advocates recommended during the late-70s TIM/slew debate. Today it is a niche choice for fast buffers and DAC I/V stages.*

**Technology:** Bipolar, NPN differential input (LTC model notes 'bipolar npn input, internal comp.' plus an input differential-mode clamp). Internally compensated for unity gain. Pins 1/5/8 are external compensation/balance pins that allow feed-forward compensation. Single channel, ±5 V to ±20 V supplies.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (2 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A fast 1970s bipolar op-amp (15 MHz, 50-70 V/us). In the late-1970s slew-induced-distortion/TIM debate (Walter Jung and co-authors, Audio magazine) high-slew parts such as the LM318 were favoured for audio. Today it is a niche/cult part in DIY circles, used for fast buffers and DAC I/V stages. Community evidence is thin. It is not a low-noise audio design and has no published noise spec in any source seen. Some hobby write-ups wrongly call it a 'dedicated audio op-amp'. Audio-DIY SPICE modellers (s-audio.systems) keep separate National, TI and LTC LM318 models, a sign of interest in vendor-to-vendor differences.

**Typical uses:** DAC output buffer / I-V stage (DIY), fast line buffers, active video filters and composite-video receivers, function generators / oscillators, fast D/A output amplifiers, pulse amplifiers, class-D amplifier control loop (academic paper)

**Caveats:**

- High-speed part: needs 0.1 uF bypass capacitors right at pins 4 and 7. It is prone to oscillation with stray capacitance on the inverting input and with long socket/adaptor leads.
- Pins 1/5/8 are compensation/balance pins. Circuits that use feed-forward caps on these pins are not drop-in with parts where pin 8 is NC or pins 1/5 are offset null (and vice versa).
- Feed-forward compensation (>150 V/us) only applies to inverting configurations.
- Quiescent current about 5 mA typ (10 mA max) per amp.
- Needs at least ±5 V supplies. A hobby simulation needed >5.3 V to operate.
- No noise specification. Not a low-noise part compared with NE5534/LME49710-class devices.
- Several different dies are sold under one name (National vs TI-legacy vs LTC), so behaviour may differ by maker.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Texas Instruments vs National…: Concurrent: TI's own LM318 existed by 1989; both lines…

Two different LM318 dies appear to be sold under the same generic number: TI's own LM318 (TI macromodel dated 1989) and National's original LM318 (now LM318-N). A third-party audio SPICE modeller (Shtybel Nazar, s-audio.systems) keeps separate LM318NS and LM318TI models with different internal parameters. No vendor document comparing them was seen.

- **When:** Concurrent: TI's own LM318 existed by 1989; both lines have sat in the TI catalogue since the 2011 National acquisition
- **Affected:** LM318, LM318D, LM318DR, LM318P, LM318N, LM318M, LM118H
- **How to tell old from new:** Product page / datasheet: ti.com/product/LM318 (lm318.pdf) vs ti.com/product/LM318-N (lm318-n.pdf, 'LM118/LM218/LM318 Operational Amplifiers'). Package code: TI style D/DR/P for TI-legacy vs National style M/N/H/J (N/NOPB) for ex-National.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. Compensation, slew and stability margins may differ, so a part swap can change behaviour in fast or feed-forward-compensated circuits.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium - same pinout and nominal headline specs, but different dies and datasheets. Check which datasheet applies to the orderable you buy.
- **Confidence:** low

## Counterfeits

No specific LM318 counterfeit reports were found (web search was unavailable). General precaution for cheap marketplace DIP-8 LM318N parts: remarked slower op-amps are common in this segment. A quick check is a unity-gain square-wave test at ±15 V: a genuine part should slew at least 50 V/us and have about 15 MHz small-signal bandwidth, while a remarked 741/4558-class part slews under 1-2 V/us. This is generic advice, not a documented LM318 case.
