# LM118 / LM218 / LM318 (fast bipolar single op-amp, 15 MHz / 50 V/us)

National Semiconductor (1970s (exact intro not verified) - 2011) · second sources: Texas Instruments, Linear Technology (now Analog Devices), STMicroelectronics · Tier C (niche / baseline)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** TI sells two separately documented LM318 product lines under the same generic number: its own second source (datasheet since June 1976) and National's original (now LM318-N). (+1 more) [Details](#silicon-changes)

## Why it's rated

A fast 1970s bipolar op-amp (15 MHz, 50 V/us min). It is reportedly among the high-slew parts favoured in the late-1970s slew-induced-distortion/TIM debate (Walter Jung et al., Audio magazine), but the specific articles were not verified. Today it is a niche part in DIY circles for fast buffers and DAC I/V stages. Community evidence is thin: there are no published noise specs and no measured reviews were found. It is not a low-noise audio design. Some hobby write-ups wrongly call it a dedicated audio op-amp.

**Used in:** DAC output buffer / I-V stage (DIY), fast line buffers, active video filters and video receivers, function generators / oscillators, fast D/A output amplifiers, pulse amplifiers, class-D amplifier control loop (academic paper)

## Watch out

- High-speed part: bypass pins 4 and 7 with 0.1 uF capacitors placed right at the pins. It is prone to oscillation with stray capacitance on the inverting input and with long socket/adaptor leads.
- Pins 1/5/8 are compensation/balance pins, so feed-forward-compensated circuits are not drop-in with parts that use pin 8 as NC or pins 1/5 as offset null.
- Feed-forward compensation (>150 V/us) applies only to inverting configurations.
- Quiescent current is about 5 mA typ per amp (third-party figure).
- Needs at least ±5 V supplies.
- No noise specification. Not a low-noise part compared with NE5534/LME49710-class devices.
- TI sells two separate LM318 lines (LM318 / SLOS063 and LM318-N / SNOSBS8), and LTC made its own version, so behaviour may differ by maker.
- **Counterfeits:** No specific LM318 counterfeit reports were found. General precaution for cheap marketplace DIP-8 LM318N parts: they may be remarked slower op-amps. As a quick check, run a unity-gain square wave at ±15 V. A genuine part slews at least 50 V/us, while a remarked 741/4558-class part slews under about 1-2 V/us. This is generic advice, not a documented LM318 case. Note also that LM318M (National SOIC) is reported obsolete, so new-stock offers of it deserve extra scrutiny.

## Silicon changes

### Second-source difference: Texas Instruments (TI-legacy) vs National Semiconductor (now TI LM318-N), Concurrent since 1976 (TI SLOS063 original date) and within the TI catalogue since the 2011 National acquisition

TI sells two separately documented LM318 product lines under the same generic number: its own second source (datasheet since June 1976) and National's original (now LM318-N). The die difference is inferred from their separate origins. A third-party audio SPICE modeller also keeps separate National and TI models with different parameters. No vendor document compares the two.

- **Affected:** LM318, LM318D, LM318DR, LM318P, LM318PSR, LM318N/NOPB, LM318M, LM318MX/NOPB, LM118H
- **How to tell:** By datasheet: SLOS063 ('High-Performance Operational Amplifiers', ti.com/product/LM318, lm318.pdf) versus SNOSBS8 ('LM118-N/LM218-N/LM318-N Operational Amplifiers', ti.com/product/LM318-N, lm318-n.pdf). By package code: TI-style D/DR/P/PSR are TI-legacy; National-style N/M/MX/H/J and /NOPB are ex-National.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. Compensation, slew and stability margins may differ, so a part swap can change behaviour in fast or feed-forward-compensated circuits.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium: same pinout and headline specs (15 MHz, 50 V/us min), but separate designs and datasheets. Check which datasheet (SLOS063 or SNOSBS8) applies to the orderable you buy.
- **Confidence:** medium

### Second-source difference: Linear Technology, c. 1989-1990 (LTC 1990 Linear Databook)

LTC made its own LM318 second source plus an improved LT118A/LT318A. The SPICE models give LT318A different parameters from the National LM318. Datasheet deltas were not seen, and nothing was web-verified.

- **Affected:** LM318, LM318S8, LT118A, LT318A
- **How to tell:** LTC logo and marking. The LT prefix marks the improved grade; the S8 suffix means SO-8.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: intended as a drop-in second source, but datasheet deltas are unverified.
- **Confidence:** low
