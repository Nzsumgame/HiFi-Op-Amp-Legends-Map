# LM118 / LM218 / LM318

**Fast bipolar single op-amp, 15 MHz / 50 V/µs** · National Semiconductor (1970s–2011) → Texas Instruments (2011–present) · second sources: Texas Instruments, Linear Technology (now Analog Devices), Unisonic Technologies (UTC), STMicroelectronics · Tier C (niche / baseline)

## Why it's rated

A fast 1970s bipolar op-amp, reportedly among the high-slew parts favoured in the late-1970s TIM / slew-induced-distortion debate (Walter Jung et al., Audio magazine). Today a niche DIY pick for fast buffers and DAC I/V. Evidence is thin: no noise specs, no measured reviews. Not a low-noise audio design, despite some hobby write-ups.

**Used in:** DAC output buffer / I-V stage (DIY), Fast line buffers, Active video filters and video receivers, Function generators, pulse and fast D/A amplifiers, Class-D amplifier control loop (academic paper)

## Watch out

- Bypass pins 4 and 7 with 0.1 µF at the pins; oscillates with stray inverting-input capacitance or long socket leads.
- Pins 1/5/8 are compensation/balance pins: not drop-in where pin 8 is NC or 1/5 are offset null.
- Feed-forward compensation (>150 V/µs) works only in inverting configurations. Needs at least ±5 V; ~5 mA per amp.
- No noise specification; not low-noise compared with NE5534/LME49710-class parts.
- Two TI lines (SLOS063 and SNOSBS8) plus an LTC version: behaviour may differ by maker.
- **Counterfeits:** No LM318-specific counterfeit reports. Cheap marketplace DIP-8 'LM318N' may be remarked slow op-amps: a unity-gain ±15 V square-wave test shows ≥50 V/µs for genuine parts versus 1–2 V/µs for 741/4558 class. LM318M is obsolete, so scrutinise new stock.

## Silicon changes

### Second-source difference: Texas Instruments (TI-legacy) vs National Semiconductor (now TI LM318-N), 1976 onward; both TI-sold since 2011

TI sells two separately documented LM318 lines under one number: its own second source (SLOS063, since June 1976) and National's original (LM318-N, SNOSBS8). A die difference is inferred from separate origins; one audio SPICE modeller keeps distinct models. No vendor comparison exists.

- **Affected:** LM318, LM318D, LM318DR, LM318P, LM318PSR, LM318N/NOPB, LM318M, LM318MX/NOPB, LM118H
- **How to tell:** Datasheet: SLOS063 'High-Performance' (product LM318) vs SNOSBS8 (LM318-N). Package codes D/DR/P/PSR are TI-legacy; N/M/MX/H/J and /NOPB are ex-National.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. Compensation, slew and stability margins may differ in fast or feed-forward circuits.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium: same pinout and headline specs, separate designs; check which datasheet covers your orderable.
- **Confidence:** medium

### Second-source difference: Linear Technology, c. 1989–1990 · LTC 1990 Linear Databook

LTC made its own LM318 second source plus an improved LT118A/LT318A. SPICE models give LT318A different parameters from National's LM318. Datasheet deltas unknown.

- **Affected:** LM318, LM318S8, LT118A, LT318A
- **How to tell:** LTC logo and marking; LT prefix = improved grade; S8 suffix = SO-8.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: intended drop-in second source; datasheet deltas unverified.
- **Confidence:** low
