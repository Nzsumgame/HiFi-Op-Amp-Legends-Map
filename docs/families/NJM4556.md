# NJM4556 / NJM4556A

**Bipolar dual, high output current (70 mA)** · JRC (1980s–?) → New JRC (by 2003–2021) → Nisshinbo Micro Devices (2022–present) · Tier B (widely praised)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** Same '4556', different silicon. [Details](#silicon-changes)

*JRC's 70 mA dual that drives 150 Ω directly: the paralleled output stage of NwAvGuy's O2 and a Japanese DIY staple.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | THD+N | Supply range | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 nV/√Hz typ; curve ≈8 nV/√Hz from 20 Hz to 1 kHz | 8 MHz typ | 3 V/µs typ | ≈0.0002–0.0003% at 1 kHz, 3–4 Vrms (graph) | ±2 V to ±18 V (4–36 V single) | 9 mA typ / 12 mA max per package |

## Why it's rated

Valued for current drive, not a distinctive sound. NwAvGuy paralleled both halves per channel in the O2's output stage, reportedly saying it 'killed everything else' for current at low distortion. Japanese DIY calls it a standard (定番) chip that sounds surprisingly good for about 30 yen; one test found it more energetic but rougher than NJM4580.

**Described as:** energetic, lively, rougher than NJM4580, surprisingly good for price, clean current drive

**Used in:** O2 / ODA headphone amp output stage, Japanese DIY and kit headphone amps (aitendo), Eurorack headphone and line-out modules, Behringer UCA202 and Roland AC-90 phones outputs, Line drivers into 150–600 Ω loads

## Watch out

- DIP8 (AD) and SIP8 (AL) are NRND since March 2025; SMD AM and AV continue.
- JRC DMP8 is wider than JEDEC SOIC-8; DMP8/SSOP8 dissipation is only 300/250 mW, so derate.
- Modest speed (3 V/µs, 8 MHz); THD at 20 kHz rises steeply with output level.
- High offset (6 mV max) and bias (500 nA max) matter in DC-coupled designs like the O2.
- A vintage '4556' may be NEC's decompensated uPC4556 or a Yamaha part, not an NJM4556.
- **Counterfeits:** No reports. DIP NJM4556AD circulates on eBay as '"Original" JRC'. Vintage '4556' may be NEC uPC4556 or Yamaha YM4556. Check: about ±11 V into 150 Ω at ±15 V, about 9 mA per package, stable as a follower. Buy authorized.

## Silicon changes

### Second-source difference: NEC (vs JRC), 1980s · NEC uPC4556 (Taito boards)

Same '4556', different silicon. NEC's uPC4556 is a decompensated fast dual from its uPC4558 (20 MHz at gain > 20 dB, 5 V/µs), seen on late-1980s Taito boards. JRC's NJM4556/A is a compensated 70 mA / 150 Ω driver, used at unity gain in the O2.

- **Affected:** uPC4556, uPC4556C, '4556' markings on vintage boards
- **How to tell:** Check prefix and logo, not digits: NEC 'uPC4556' (DIP8, C suffix) differs from JRC 'NJM4556'/'NJM4556A'.
- **Audio impact:** A uPC4556 at unity gain (e.g. O2 buffer) risks oscillation; an NJM4556A in a high-gain vintage stage loses bandwidth and slew.
- **Drop-in risk:** high: decompensated part is no drop-in for unity-gain buffers; pinout same, stability needs differ.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Compensation | NJM4556A: internally compensated; used as a unity-gain buffer in the O2 | uPC4556: decompensated; GBW guaranteed only at gain >= 20 dB |
| GBW | 8 MHz typ | 20 MHz (at gain >= 20 dB) |
| Slew rate | 3 V/us typ | 5 V/us |
| Output drive | +/-70 mA into 150 ohm (+/-10.5 V min) | not seen in datasheet summaries (no 150 ohm drive claim) |
