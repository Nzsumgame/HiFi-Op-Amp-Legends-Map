# AD744 / AD746 (precision 500 ns-settling BiFET single / dual)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*ADI's c.1990 fast-settling JFET-input single/dual, pitched by ADI for audio (0.0003% THD), now best known as the die found inside fake OPA627s.*

**Technology:** BiFET (JFET-input) op-amp by Analog Devices. ADI describes the AD744 as having the DC characteristics of the AD711 BiFET family with faster settling, slew and bandwidth. The AD744 single has an external compensation/decompensation pin and offset-null pins. The AD746 dual is two AD744 amplifiers on one chip (ADI datasheet wording) and has no compensation pin. The AD744 datasheet lists plastic mini-DIP, SOIC, cerdip, TO-99 metal can and chip (bare die) form. Datasheet titles: "Precision, 500 ns Settling BiFET Op Amp" (AD744) and "Dual Precision, 500 ns Settling, BiFET Op Amp" (AD746).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche part for Hi-Fi hobbyists. ADI itself pitched the AD744 for demanding audio applications (0.0003% THD, 13 MHz GBW) and as a fast-settling buffer for 12/14-bit DACs and ADCs. Tangent's CMoy op-amp survey (read via the Soomal translation) measured the AD744: $4.52 at Digi-Key, minimum supply 6.8 V for 0.5 V into 33 ohms; no listening verdict was found. Its main notoriety is as the donor die in counterfeit OPA627s (ZeptoBars, April 2014, covered by Hackaday, Head-Fi and diyAudio). The discovery note calling it a '1990s DIY I/V favourite' has no primary community source.

**Typical uses:** Fast-settling buffers for 12/14-bit DACs and ADCs (ADI positioning), Audio stages (ADI marketing cites 0.0003% THD), DAC I/V and line stages (discovery note; not verified), Precision current drivers and instrumentation

**Caveats:**

- The AD744's compensation pin has no counterpart on the OPA627 (ZeptoBars), so it is not a clean pin-for-pin OPA627 substitute. Check the pin function before op-amp rolling.
- Noise is about 3x the OPA627's (ZeptoBars); ADI's LTspice model uses ~16 nV/rtHz.
- Offset is higher than the OPA627's (0.5 mV vs 0.1 mV, per ZeptoBars).
- Weaker headphone driver in Tangent's table (6.8 V needed for 0.5 V into 33 ohms).
- Some order codes are obsolete (AD746JRZ-REEL).

## Counterfeits

No fakes sold as AD744 were found. The hazard runs the other way: genuine AD744 dies have been remarked as OPA627. In April 2014 ZeptoBars decapped a cheap eBay DIP 'OPA627' (about $2 per Hackaday; $2.70 with shipping per the Habr text). It held an ADI AD744 die with only one laser-trimmed resistor, against several on a genuine metal-can OPA627. ZeptoBars thought a Chinese packager had bought AD744 dies or wafers; ADI's datasheet lists chip (die) form. The AD744's frequency-compensation feature was unused because the OPA627 has no matching pin. The 'AD'/'B744' die markings come from the Habr text and were not re-confirmed here. How to spot an AD744-based fake: (1) about 3x the noise and higher offset (0.5 mV vs 0.1 mV) than a genuine OPA627; (2) lower supply current, 3.5 mA for the AD744 (ADI datasheet) against about 7 mA for the OPA627 (TI figure, not re-checked here); (3) a decap. Discussed on Hackaday, Head-Fi and diyAudio ('more fake eBay semis (OPA627=AD744)'). Buy genuine AD744/AD746 through authorized distribution.

## Open questions

- Dates of AD744 Rev. D and AD746 Rev. B, and their Revision History tables, were not captured. Rev. C of the AD744 is only mentioned; which mirror holds it is unknown.
- It is unknown which revisions the alldatasheet, datasheetcatalog, manualmachine and Octopart copies hold. Check them, and check Wayback copies of the static/imported-files URLs.
- Lifecycle status per order code: only AD746JRZ-REEL is confirmed obsolete (Digi-Key). ADI PDN 10_0081 and PDN 14_0014 appeared in search results, but no link to AD744/AD746 was shown; check them.
- The datasheet values for noise (model 16 nV/rtHz), bias current and offset by grade are still model-derived.
- No ADI die revision, fab move or PCN was found. It is unknown whether the 'B744' die mark means a mask revision.
- Tangent's original English text on the AD744 was not retrieved.
- The AD744 pin 8 (compensation) function versus OPA627 pin 8 has not been confirmed from a datasheet pinout.
