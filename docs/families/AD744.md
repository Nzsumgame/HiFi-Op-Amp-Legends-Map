# AD744 / AD746 (precision 500 ns-settling BiFET single / dual)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*ADI's c.1990 fast-settling JFET-input single/dual, a niche DIY part now best known as the die found inside fake OPA627s.*

**Technology:** BiFET: a JFET-input op-amp made by Analog Devices. The AD744 single has an external compensation/decompensation pin and offset-null pins. The AD746 dual has no compensation pin; the node lists in ADI's 1991 SPICE macro-models confirm this. ADI's LTspice symbol for the AD744 points to the AD746 model (Value2 AD746), which implies the same core amplifier. Datasheet titles: "Precision, 500 ns Settling BiFET Op Amp" (AD744) and "Dual Precision, 500 ns Settling, BiFET Op Amp" (AD746).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche part for Hi-Fi hobbyists. Tangent's CMoy headphone-amp op-amp survey (read via the Soomal Chinese translation) measured the AD744: $4.52 single at Digi-Key, minimum supply 6.8 V for 0.5 V into 33 ohms and 9.2 V for 2 V into 330 ohms. It was also on his listening-test priority list, but no listening verdict was found. Bordodynov's large LTspice collection files the AD744/AD746 models under 'AudioOpamp'. Its main notoriety today is as the donor die in counterfeit OPA627s (ZeptoBars 2014). The discovery notes call it a '1990s DIY I/V favourite', but no primary community source for that was retrieved this session.

**Typical uses:** DAC I/V and line stages (discovery note; not verified this session), Fast-settling DAC output buffers and sample-and-hold, as the datasheet positions it (500 ns settling), Precision current drivers and instrumentation (for example, the ColdMatter precision current driver Eagle design block)

**Caveats:**

- The AD744's extra compensation pin has no counterpart on the OPA627 (ZeptoBars), so it is not a clean pin-for-pin OPA627 substitute. Check the pin 8 function before op-amp rolling.
- Noise is about 3x the OPA627's (ZeptoBars), and ADI's LTspice model uses about 16 nV/rtHz, which is high next to modern audio JFET parts.
- Offset is higher than the OPA627's (0.5 mV vs 0.1 mV, per ZeptoBars).
- It is a weaker headphone driver: in Tangent's table it needs 6.8 V for 0.5 V into 33 ohms, against 5.5 V for the OPA132 and 5.6 V for the NE5532.
- No verified reports were found about oscillation in rolling sockets or about heat.

## Counterfeits

No fakes sold as AD744 were found. The concern runs the other way: genuine AD744 dies have been remarked as OPA627. In the ZeptoBars 2014 teardown (Habr post 218571, also covered by Hackaday on 2014-04-10), a DIP 'OPA627' bought on eBay for $2.70 including shipping held an ADI die marked 'AD' and 'B744'. It matched the die photo in ADI's AD744 datasheet. It had only 1 laser-trimmed resistor, against at least 4 on a genuine OPA627 die (a metal-can OPA627 bought for $5 was the reference). The AD744's compensation pad was not bonded out. ZeptoBars thought the counterfeiter bought AD744 bare dies and packaged them as OPA627. How to spot an AD744-based fake: (1) about 3x higher noise and higher offset (0.5 mV vs 0.1 mV) than a genuine OPA627; (2) lower supply current. My inference is that the AD744 draws about 3.5 mA against 7-7.5 mA for the OPA627, a figure used in Head-Fi/diyAudio OPA627 guides; this is not verified. (3) A decap shows the 'AD'/'B744' marks. When buying a genuine AD744/AD746, use authorized distribution. No marketplace remarking of these parts was documented.

## Open questions

- The WebSearch budget was already used up when this task started (200/200), so no web searches succeeded and the required 15 queries could not be run. The findings come from GitHub-hosted mirrors, EDA libraries, ADI SPICE and LTspice models, and other workers' scratch material. A follow-up pass with web access is needed.
- The ADI datasheet revision letters and dates, and the Revision History tables for both the AD744 and the AD746, were not retrieved. The full revision chain is unknown. Check Wayback copies of the static/imported-files URLs and the current /media/en/ PDFs.
- Inferred URLs, not observed: analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/AD744.pdf and AD746.pdf; analog.com/UploadedFiles/Data_Sheets/AD744.pdf and AD746.pdf; and the analog.com/en/products/ad746.html product page.
- The current lifecycle status of each grade and package is not verified. The discovery notes say some grades are obsolete. RoHS Z-suffix versions exist, and AD744KNZ was stocked at Digi-Key in 2019.
- The discovery notes claim about 13 MHz GBW and 75 V/us slew rate. These, plus THD+N, i_n, output current and supply range, were not verified from a datasheet.
- The noise (16 nV/rtHz), bias-current and supply-current (about 3.5 mA) figures are derived from SPICE models and need datasheet confirmation.
- The ZeptoBars fake die is marked 'B744'. It is unknown whether 'B' is a mask or die revision letter, which would mean an AD744 die revision exists. No PCN or die change is documented.
- Tangent's original English text on the AD744 (any listening impressions) was not retrieved; only the table figures from the Soomal translation were.
- The pin 8 function on AD744 vs OPA627 was not confirmed from a datasheet pinout.
