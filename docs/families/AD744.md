# AD744 / AD746

**JFET-input (BiFET) fast-settling op-amp, 1/2 channels** · Analog Devices (c. 1990–present) · Tier C (niche / baseline)

*ADI's c. 1990 fast-settling BiFET, pitched for audio at 0.0003% THD, now best known as the die inside fake OPA627s.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | THD+N | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ~16 nV/√Hz (SPICE model; ~3× OPA627 per ZeptoBars) | 13 MHz | 75 V/µs; 500 ns settling to 0.01% (10 V step) | 0.0003% THD | AD744 3.5 mA; AD746 7 mA (both amplifiers) |

## Why it's rated

A niche hobbyist part. ADI pitched the AD744 for demanding audio (0.0003% THD, 13 MHz) and as a fast-settling buffer for 12/14-bit DACs and ADCs. Tangent's CMoy survey (via Soomal) lists it at $4.52, needing 6.8 V for 0.5 V into 33 Ω, with no listening verdict. It is best known as the donor die in fake OPA627s.

**Used in:** Fast-settling buffers for 12/14-bit DACs and ADCs, Audio stages (ADI cites 0.0003% THD), DAC I/V and line stages (reportedly; no community source), Precision current drivers and instrumentation

## Watch out

- AD744 compensation pin has no OPA627 counterpart: not a clean OPA627 substitute. Check pin function before rolling.
- Noise about 3× the OPA627's (ZeptoBars); ADI's LTspice model uses ~16 nV/√Hz.
- Offset higher than the OPA627: 0.5 mV vs 0.1 mV (ZeptoBars).
- Weak headphone driver in Tangent's table: 6.8 V needed for 0.5 V into 33 Ω.
- Some order codes are obsolete (AD746JRZ-REEL).
- **Counterfeits:** Genuine AD744 dies get remarked as OPA627: ZeptoBars (April 2014) found one, with a single laser-trimmed resistor, in a ~$2 eBay 'OPA627'. Spot them by ~3× noise, 0.5 mV offset, 3.5 mA supply current (OPA627 ~7 mA) or decap.

## Silicon changes

None documented. Compare datasheet revisions before assuming old and new parts are identical.
