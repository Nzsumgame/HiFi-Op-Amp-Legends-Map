# AD744 / AD746 (precision 500 ns-settling BiFET single / dual)

Analog Devices (by 1991 at the latest (SPICE macro-models dated 1/91 and 3/91); ADI still hosts both product pages and datasheets in 2026) · Tier C (niche / baseline)

*ADI's c.1990 fast-settling JFET-input single/dual, pitched by ADI for audio (0.0003% THD), now best known as the die found inside fake OPA627s.*

| Slew rate |
|---|
| 75 V/us |

## Why it's rated

A niche part for Hi-Fi hobbyists. ADI itself pitched the AD744 for demanding audio applications (0.0003% THD, 13 MHz GBW) and as a fast-settling buffer for 12/14-bit DACs and ADCs. Tangent's CMoy op-amp survey (read via the Soomal translation) measured the AD744: $4.52 at Digi-Key, minimum supply 6.8 V for 0.5 V into 33 ohms; no listening verdict was found. Its main notoriety is as the donor die in counterfeit OPA627s (ZeptoBars, April 2014, covered by Hackaday, Head-Fi and diyAudio). The discovery note calling it a '1990s DIY I/V favourite' has no primary community source.

**Used in:** Fast-settling buffers for 12/14-bit DACs and ADCs (ADI positioning), Audio stages (ADI marketing cites 0.0003% THD), DAC I/V and line stages (discovery note; not verified), Precision current drivers and instrumentation

## Watch out

- The AD744's compensation pin has no counterpart on the OPA627 (ZeptoBars), so it is not a clean pin-for-pin OPA627 substitute. Check the pin function before op-amp rolling.
- Noise is about 3x the OPA627's (ZeptoBars); ADI's LTspice model uses ~16 nV/rtHz.
- Offset is higher than the OPA627's (0.5 mV vs 0.1 mV, per ZeptoBars).
- Weaker headphone driver in Tangent's table (6.8 V needed for 0.5 V into 33 ohms).
- Some order codes are obsolete (AD746JRZ-REEL).
- **Counterfeits:** No fakes sold as AD744 were found. The hazard runs the other way: genuine AD744 dies have been remarked as OPA627. In April 2014 ZeptoBars decapped a cheap eBay DIP 'OPA627' (about $2 per Hackaday; $2.70 with shipping per the Habr text). It held an ADI AD744 die with only one laser-trimmed resistor, against several on a genuine metal-can OPA627. ZeptoBars thought a Chinese packager had bought AD744 dies or wafers; ADI's datasheet lists chip (die) form. The AD744's frequency-compensation feature was unused because the OPA627 has no matching pin. The 'AD'/'B744' die markings come from the Habr text and were not re-confirmed here. How to spot an AD744-based fake: (1) about 3x the noise and higher offset (0.5 mV vs 0.1 mV) than a genuine OPA627; (2) lower supply current, 3.5 mA for the AD744 (ADI datasheet) against about 7 mA for the OPA627 (TI figure, not re-checked here); (3) a decap. Discussed on Hackaday, Head-Fi and diyAudio ('more fake eBay semis (OPA627=AD744)'). Buy genuine AD744/AD746 through authorized distribution.
