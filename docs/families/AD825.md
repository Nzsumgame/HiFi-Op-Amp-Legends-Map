# AD825 (single high-speed JFET-input op amp)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*A cheap, fast single JFET op amp (41 MHz, 125 V/us). DIY op-amp rollers call it punchy with a rich timbre, and LC Audio sold it as an upgrade part.*

**Technology:** A JFET-input, high-speed, voltage-feedback single op amp from Analog Devices. ADI's own SPICE macro-model (JCH / ADI Central Apps, 1999) uses a P-channel JFET differential input pair. Only surface-mount orderables were found: SOIC-8 and wide SOIC-16. No dual or quad version exists.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche cult part from the mid-2000s op-amp-rolling era. Head-Fi users call it punchy and intimate, with a rich timbre and thick texture that suits female vocals and strings. Some rate 2x AD825 on an adapter above other options ('AD825 is the best and nothing will change it'). Others hear it as flat or unemotional in some DACs, and as more coloured and a bit closed-in than the AD797. The LME49990 was reported as smoother with a firmer low end than the AD825. LC Audio Technology sold AD825 upgrade parts. Audio Asylum and diyAudio threads name it, for its FET input and high open-loop bandwidth, as a better replacement for the AD712 that John Curl called the weak link of the Parasound HCA-2200 MK-I. No objective audio measurements (ASR, Samuel Groner) were found. All sonic claims are forum folklore.

**How people describe the sound:** punchy, intimate, rich timbre, thick texture, good for female voices and strings, flat / unemotional in some DACs (dissenting view), coloured and a bit closed-in compared with the AD797 (dissenting view), less smooth, lighter low end than the LME49990 (comparison)

**Typical uses:** Op-amp rolling in DAC and CD-player analog stages, on SOIC-to-DIP adapters (two AD825s per dual socket), Drop-in upgrade for AD711/AD712-class JFET op amps, e.g. the Parasound HCA-2200 MK-I input stage (forum discussion), Headphone amplifiers (Tangentsoft / Head-Fi), LC Audio Technology upgrade kits

**Caveats:**

- Single-channel and surface-mount only (SOIC-8 / SOIC-16). Replacing a DIP dual needs an adapter carrying two chips.
- It is a 41 MHz part, so adapter wiring and local decoupling matter. This is general high-speed practice: no AD825-specific oscillation reports were found in the sources available.
- It needs more supply headroom to swing 2 V into 330 ohm (8.6 V minimum in the Tangentsoft test) than parts such as the AD823 (6.1 V), though it drives 33 ohm well (4.7 V).
- The praise is subjective forum opinion. No published THD+N or noise measurements of AD825 samples were found.

## Counterfeits

No AD825-specific counterfeit reports were found in the sources available. Only ADI SOIC-8 (AD825AR/ARZ) and SOIC-16 (AD825ARZ-16) orderables were found, and no DIP-8 or dual version. A bare DIP-8 chip marked 'AD825', as opposed to a SOIC chip on a DIP adapter, should therefore be treated as suspect; this is an inference, not a vendor statement. The general cautions for remarked audiophile op amps sold on marketplace sites apply: buy from ADI-authorised distributors (DigiKey 505-AD825ARZ-ND, Mouser, Farnell), and check the ADI logo, the date and lot code, and consistent laser marking.
