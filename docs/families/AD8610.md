# AD8610 / AD8620 (single/dual precision low-noise JFET)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** JFET-input

*ADI's low-noise (about 6 nV/rtHz), 25 MHz trimmed JFET op-amp. Portable-amp makers and op-amp rollers use it as an OPA627-class choice for DAC filters and headphone stages.*

**Technology:** JFET-input precision op-amp from Analog Devices. The data sheet says it is fully specified from +/-5 V to +/-13 V and unconditionally stable in all gains, even with capacitive loads well over 1 nF, and lists 'high quality audio' among its applications. The ADI Op Amp Applications Handbook calls the AD8610B/AD8620B 'the very best trimmed JFET input types', with about 100 uV offset. The trim method is not confirmed (the handbook names DigiTrim only for CMOS parts). The wafer process is also unconfirmed: PCN 26_0028 (XF26/XF18/XF12/XF8 processes) reportedly lists these parts, but which XF process they use was not seen. The single AD8610 comes in 8-lead MSOP (RM) and narrow 8-lead SOIC (R), as the data sheet confirms. The dual AD8620 comes in SOIC-8 only. Neither has a DIP version. ADI's Eagle library draws the AD8610 with offset-trim pins.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (4 recorded: Unclassified).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Popular with Head-Fi and diyAudio op-amp rollers since the mid-2000s. Tangentsoft's 'Notes on Audio Op-Amps' (Warren Young; Chinese translation on Soomal, 2005) calls it the author's favourite chip, especially for battery amps. It likens the AD8610 to the AD823 without some of the aggressiveness and advises using it in laid-back rather than aggressive systems. ADI's AN-1429 (Rev. 0, 10/2016) lists the AD8620 among ADI op-amps 'reputable with music fans', alongside the AD797, OP275 and ADA4627-1. Soomal teardowns show commercial use: the Topping NX5 (2016) runs 2x AD8610 plus BUF634, the Xuelin iHiFi990 (2017) uses 2x AD8610 as its DAC LPF, and the FiiO X7 MkII AM3A (2017) uses an AD8620 preamp. Three sources were not re-checked: Head-Fi praise threads, a Leckerton blog post (AD8610 as its output op-amp, OPA627 not clearly better) and a Zigsow review ('won't lose to the 627').

**How people describe the sound:** clear/detailed (Head-Fi; not re-checked), black background (Head-Fi; not re-checked), tight, controlled bass (Zigsow; not re-checked), AD823-like without some of its aggressiveness (Tangentsoft), more detail and smoothness than AD843 (Tangentsoft via translation; wording ambiguous), somewhat aggressive compared with AD8065/OPA227 (Tangentsoft), lean (some listeners; unverified)

**Typical uses:** DAC output low-pass filter / I/V, headphone-amp gain stage, often with a BUF634 buffer (Topping NX5), battery-powered portable headphone amps, op-amp rolling on SOIC-to-DIP adapters, precision/high-impedance instrumentation and photodiode amps

**Caveats:**

- The data sheet specifies it only from +/-5 V to +/-13 V, with an absolute max of about 27 V total. It is not a drop-in for +/-15 V or higher rails.
- The single comes in SOIC-8 or MSOP-8 and the dual in SOIC-8 only, so DIP-8 sockets need an adapter board.
- Output current is about +/-30 mA (parts DB). Designs driving low-impedance headphones often add a buffer (e.g. BUF634 in the Topping NX5), although Tangentsoft rated its drive as good for a battery amp.
- Tangentsoft advises against pairing it with aggressive or bright systems.
- No independent THD+N measurements (ASR, Samuel Groner) were found.
- The Head-Fi, Leckerton, diyAudio and Zigsow reputation sources were not re-verified.

## Counterfeits

The data sheet confirms the AD8610 in 8-lead MSOP and narrow 8-lead SOIC. The AD8620 is SOIC-8 only: Eagle's ADI library has only R/SO8, no AD8620ARM orderable was found, and Tangentsoft also notes SOIC only. So any 'AD8610/AD8620' in a native DIP-8 body, or an 'AD8620' in MSOP, is suspect. Genuine SOIC parts on SOIC-to-DIP adapters are legitimate and are what rollers sell. Marketplace fakes (AliExpress/eBay DIP modules) are reported but not verified. Check the chip under the adapter for ADI marking, and buy from authorized distributors. No remarked-die folklore was found. ADI PCNs do exist: mold compound (07_0024) and a 2026 fab-site qualification (26_0028), but none reports a spec change.
