# AD8610 / AD8620 (single/dual precision low-noise JFET)

Analog Devices (early 2000s (introduction date not verified) to present) · Tier B (widely praised)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** ADI PCN 26_0028 qualifies Analog Devices International, Limerick, Ireland (ADLK) as a wafer-fab site for products on the XF26/XF18/XF12/XF8 processes. [Details](#silicon-changes)

*ADI's low-noise (about 6 nV/rtHz), 25 MHz trimmed JFET op-amp. Portable-amp makers and op-amp rollers use it as an OPA627-class choice for DAC filters and headphone stages.*

| Slew rate | Supply range |
|---|---|
| 60 V/us | +/-5 V to +/-13 V |

## Why it's rated

Popular with Head-Fi and diyAudio op-amp rollers since the mid-2000s. Tangentsoft's 'Notes on Audio Op-Amps' (Warren Young; Chinese translation on Soomal, 2005) calls it the author's favourite chip, especially for battery amps. It likens the AD8610 to the AD823 without some of the aggressiveness and advises using it in laid-back rather than aggressive systems. ADI's AN-1429 (Rev. 0, 10/2016) lists the AD8620 among ADI op-amps 'reputable with music fans', alongside the AD797, OP275 and ADA4627-1. Soomal teardowns show commercial use: the Topping NX5 (2016) runs 2x AD8610 plus BUF634, the Xuelin iHiFi990 (2017) uses 2x AD8610 as its DAC LPF, and the FiiO X7 MkII AM3A (2017) uses an AD8620 preamp. Three sources were not re-checked: Head-Fi praise threads, a Leckerton blog post (AD8610 as its output op-amp, OPA627 not clearly better) and a Zigsow review ('won't lose to the 627').

**Described as:** clear/detailed (Head-Fi; not re-checked), black background (Head-Fi; not re-checked), tight, controlled bass (Zigsow; not re-checked), AD823-like without some of its aggressiveness (Tangentsoft), more detail and smoothness than AD843 (Tangentsoft via translation; wording ambiguous), somewhat aggressive compared with AD8065/OPA227 (Tangentsoft), lean (some listeners; unverified)

**Used in:** DAC output low-pass filter / I/V, headphone-amp gain stage, often with a BUF634 buffer (Topping NX5), battery-powered portable headphone amps, op-amp rolling on SOIC-to-DIP adapters, precision/high-impedance instrumentation and photodiode amps

## Watch out

- The data sheet specifies it only from +/-5 V to +/-13 V, with an absolute max of about 27 V total. It is not a drop-in for +/-15 V or higher rails.
- The single comes in SOIC-8 or MSOP-8 and the dual in SOIC-8 only, so DIP-8 sockets need an adapter board.
- Output current is about +/-30 mA (parts DB). Designs driving low-impedance headphones often add a buffer (e.g. BUF634 in the Topping NX5), although Tangentsoft rated its drive as good for a battery amp.
- Tangentsoft advises against pairing it with aggressive or bright systems.
- No independent THD+N measurements (ASR, Samuel Groner) were found.
- The Head-Fi, Leckerton, diyAudio and Zigsow reputation sources were not re-verified.
- **Counterfeits:** The data sheet confirms the AD8610 in 8-lead MSOP and narrow 8-lead SOIC. The AD8620 is SOIC-8 only: Eagle's ADI library has only R/SO8, no AD8620ARM orderable was found, and Tangentsoft also notes SOIC only. So any 'AD8610/AD8620' in a native DIP-8 body, or an 'AD8620' in MSOP, is suspect. Genuine SOIC parts on SOIC-to-DIP adapters are legitimate and are what rollers sell. Marketplace fakes (AliExpress/eBay DIP modules) are reported but not verified. Check the chip under the adapter for ADI marking, and buy from authorized distributors. No remarked-die folklore was found. ADI PCNs do exist: mold compound (07_0024) and a 2026 fab-site qualification (26_0028), but none reports a spec change.

## Silicon changes

### Fab / process transfer: Analog Devices, 2026 (PCN 26_0028 Rev. -; publication and effective dates not reliably captured)

ADI PCN 26_0028 qualifies Analog Devices International, Limerick, Ireland (ADLK) as a wafer-fab site for products on the XF26/XF18/XF12/XF8 processes. The stated aim is 'leveraging the existing qualified process at the Analog Devices Limerick, Ireland Fab' for continuity of supply. Search summaries list AD8610 and AD8620 orderables among the affected parts. The same JFET die is made at a newly qualified or additional fab under the same part numbers. Whether this is a full move or a second source was not read.

- **Affected:** AD8610ARZ-REEL7, AD8610BRZ, AD8620ARZ, other AD8610/AD8620 orderables (full list not read)
- **How to tell:** Not identifiable from the marking. ADI says traceability is kept through standard lot records. Trace by ADI date code or lot against the PCN effective date.
- **Audio impact:** None documented. ADI states no impact to fit, form, function or reliability. No measurements comparing Limerick-fab parts with earlier parts were found.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - same part numbers and data sheet, and ADI states the parts are equivalent. The only effect is lot provenance.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Wafer fab site | previous fab for XF-process products (not named in the sources seen) | ADI Limerick, Ireland (ADLK) qualified |
