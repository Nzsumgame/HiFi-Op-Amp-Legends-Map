# AD8610 / AD8620

**Precision JFET-input op-amp, 1/2 channels** · Analog Devices (c. 2001–present) · Tier B (widely praised)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** ADI PCN 26_0028, 'Qualification of ADI Limerick Wafer Fab XF26, XF18, XF12, XF8 Processes', lists AD8610/AD8610ARZ and AD8620/AD8620ARZ with reel variants in Appendix A. [Details](#silicon-changes)

*ADI's trimmed 6 nV/√Hz, 25 MHz JFET: an OPA627-class pick for DAC filters and portable headphone amps.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | Supply range |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 nV/√Hz typ | 25 MHz typ | 60 V/µs typ, 40 V/µs min (a parts DB says 50) | ±5 V to ±13 V (abs max 27.3 V total) |

## Why it's rated

Popular with Head-Fi and diyAudio rollers since the mid-2000s. Tangentsoft (Warren Young) calls it a favourite, especially for battery amps: like the AD823 minus some aggressiveness. ADI's AN-1429 lists the AD8620 among parts 'reputable with music fans'. Used in Topping NX5 (with BUF634), Xuelin iHiFi990 (DAC LPF) and FiiO X7 MkII AM3A.

**Described as:** clear, detailed, black background, tight bass (Zigsow), AD823 minus aggressiveness, smoother than AD843 (Tangentsoft), more aggressive than AD8065/OPA227, lean (some listeners), rivals OPA627 (Leckerton, Zigsow)

**Used in:** DAC output low-pass filter / I/V, Headphone gain stage with BUF634 buffer (Topping NX5), Battery-powered portable headphone amps, Op-amp rolling on SOIC-to-DIP adapters, Precision instrumentation and photodiode amps

## Watch out

- Specified only ±5 to ±13 V (abs max 27.3 V total); not a drop-in for ±15 V rails.
- SOIC/MSOP only (dual SOIC only); DIP sockets need an adapter.
- ±30 mA output; low-impedance headphones usually need a buffer (BUF634 in the Topping NX5).
- Tangentsoft advises against aggressive or bright systems. No ASR or Samuel Groner THD+N data found.
- Battery use (Tangentsoft): 5.7 V total swings 0.5 V into 33 Ω; 7.6 V swings 2.0 V into 330 Ω.
- **Counterfeits:** AD8610 is MSOP-8/SOIC-8 only and AD8620 SOIC-8 only, so a native DIP-8 or MSOP 'AD8620' is suspect. SOIC parts on DIP adapters are legitimate. Marketplace DIP fakes are reported, unconfirmed. Check the ADI marking; buy from authorized distributors.

## Silicon changes

### Fab / process transfer: Analog Devices, 2026 · PCN 26_0028 Rev. -

ADI PCN 26_0028, 'Qualification of ADI Limerick Wafer Fab XF26, XF18, XF12, XF8 Processes', lists AD8610/AD8610ARZ and AD8620/AD8620ARZ with reel variants in Appendix A. Same die and part numbers; whether this is a move or an added fab is unknown.

- **Affected:** AD8610ARZ-REEL7, AD8610BRZ, AD8620ARZ, other AD8610/AD8620 reel variants (Appendix A)
- **How to tell:** Not visible in the marking. Trace by ADI date code or lot against the PCN effective date.
- **Audio impact:** None documented. ADI states no fit, form, function or reliability impact; no comparative measurements found.
- **Drop-in risk:** low – same part numbers and datasheet; only lot provenance changes.
- **Confidence:** high

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Wafer fab site | previous fab for XF-process products (not named in the sources seen) | ADI Limerick, Ireland (ADLK) qualified |
