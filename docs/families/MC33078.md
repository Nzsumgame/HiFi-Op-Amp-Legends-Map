# MC33078 / MC33079 (low-noise bipolar dual/quad)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** Bipolar classics

*Motorola's low-noise bipolar dual/quad (4.5 nV/√Hz, 16 MHz, 7 V/µs). Musiland used the MC33079 instead of the OP275 in its sound cards, and Soomal credited it with better treble detail.*

**Technology:** Bipolar, internally compensated, low-noise voltage-feedback op-amp. MC33078 is the dual and MC33079 the quad. The datasheets specify ±5 V to ±18 V supplies. The polarity of the input pair is unconfirmed. Motorola's SPICE library (MOTOR.LIB) tags the MC33078 model 'PNP Input', but that model's own netlist is a generic Boyle template with an N-channel JFET (NJF) input pair, so it is no evidence either way. onsemi's parametric table gives the input bias current as +300 nA. That is the same sign it gives the NPN-input NE5532, while the PNP-input LM358 is listed negative (-45 nA), which hints at an NPN input stage. This is an inference that has not been checked against the datasheet schematic.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A budget low-noise bipolar dual/quad whose best-documented enthusiast praise comes from Soomal (China). Musiland dropped its usual AD OP275 for the MC33079 in the Monitor 03 US and 03 PS (PCM1798 DAC, 2011). It then used the part in the 06 MX (a TI part, PCM1798, 2013), the Monitor 10 (MC33079DG after an AK4490, 2016) and the 06 Plus (onsemi MC33079DG as a balanced I/V stage after an ES9028Q2M, 2017). Soomal said the 03 US had 'better treble detail' (高频细节表现更好) than the OP275-based 02 US. It heard the 03 PS as a little brighter than the OP275-based 01 PS, with finer resolution and clearly better HF transients, but called the overall gain incremental (量变, not 质变). Both are whole-product judgements. Soomal noted that the 4.5 nV/√Hz figure equals the OPA627's, yet in the 06 MX review said neither the op-amp nor the headphone chip was a top-tier Hi-Fi part. One EDAboard thread weighs it as a TL074 upgrade alongside OPA22x, and a guitar-pedal op-amp swap table says the TI MC33078 has 'very nice highs'. No Audio Science Review, Samuel Groner or Douglas Self measurements were found.

**How people describe the sound:** better treble detail (Soomal, Monitor 03 US vs OP275-based 02 US; product-level), a little brighter than the OP275-based 01 PS; Soomal attributes it to a change in tonal colour, since both measure flat (03 PS; product-level), finer resolution, clearly improved HF transients, better on plucked instruments (Soomal, 03 PS; product-level), 'very nice highs' (guitar-pedal op-amp swap table, TI MC33078; folklore), clean, slightly bright (op-amp-roller folklore; no source found)

**Typical uses:** I/V stage for differential current-output DACs (PCM1798 in Musiland Monitor 03 US / 03 PS / 06 MX), balanced I/V stage after an ESS ES9028Q2M (Musiland Monitor 06 Plus, onsemi MC33079DG), post-DAC filter/gain stage (Musiland Monitor 10 with AK4490; the pricier 10 Future used an LME49740 instead), headphone-amp gain stage (Soomal 'E-brand F-type' headphone amp, MC33078, 2010), general consumer/pro audio preamps, active filters, guitar pedals, and op-amp rolling in place of TL07x/NE5532

**Caveats:**

- Needs at least ±5 V (10 V total), so it does not suit low-voltage single-supply or USB-bus designs without a boost converter.
- Bipolar input with about 300 nA typ bias current, so source impedances and DC paths matter.
- TI-made MC33078 has reportedly moved to a new die shared with the NE5532, LM833 and RC4580 (2024-25). This is not confirmed by any TI datasheet or PCN found, and older reviews may not describe current TI stock.
- Several makers (onsemi, TI, ST, UTC) sell parts under the same number, and ST specifies 15 MHz GBW against 16 MHz for the others. No ON-vs-TI-vs-ST listening or measurement comparison was found.
- All the Soomal praise is whole-product judgement of sound cards, and Soomal itself called the MC33079 not a top-tier Hi-Fi part. Brand attribution in reviews is loose: Soomal called an onsemi MC33079DG 'Motorola'.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Texas Instruments: 2024-2025 (reported; discussed 2025-2026)

A TI E2E thread titled 'NE5532, LM833, RC4580 and MC33078 all now the same die' (thread 1652528) and a Gearspace thread report that TI moved these four parts onto one new die. A search summary in the original session said a TI reply attributed this to an older fab node closing. That wording could not be re-checked in this pass. Hackaday (June 2026) covers TI's change to 'the NE5532 and others', but whether it names MC33078 was not confirmed. No MC33078-specific before/after specs were found.

- **When:** 2024-2025 (reported; discussed 2025-2026)
- **Affected:** MC33078 (TI orderables: MC33078D, MC33078DR, MC33078DGKR, MC33078DGKT, MC33078P)
- **How to tell old from new:** No reliable identifier yet. The new NE5532 die is marked by SLOS075K, but TI's MC33078 datasheet was still SLLS633C (Nov 2006) when last indexed. Look for a TI PCN or a new SLLS633 revision (D or later), and compare date codes and lot traceability. onsemi, ST, UTC and Motorola-marked parts are not affected by a TI-internal die change.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown for MC33078. The new TI NE5532 die (SLOS075K) is specified at 5 V/µs slew (was 9), ±18 V maximum supply, no input protection diodes and 1 kV HBM ESD. If the MC33078 really shares that die, TI's 7 V/µs and 4.5 nV/√Hz MC33078 figures would be expected to change, yet no revised SLLS633 has been seen. This inconsistency is unresolved.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium: vendor-forum and forum reports name the MC33078, but no TI MC33078 datasheet change or PCN was found. Re-qualify new TI lots in designs that rely on slew rate, ±18 V operation or input-protection behaviour.
- **Confidence:** low

## Counterfeits

No MC33078/MC33079-specific counterfeit reports were found. Unbranded marketplace listings (for example an Amazon 'MC33078P ... DIP8 1 Pack' from an unstated maker) are the usual risk. Alldatasheet marking pages give the TI top mark as 'M33078' (not re-confirmed); the onsemi and ST markings were not captured. Folklore prefers Motorola-marked (batwing 'M') parts, so relabelled 'Motorola' vintage parts are plausible but unconfirmed. Even reviewers mix up brands: Soomal called an onsemi MC33079DG 'Motorola'. Treat 'Motorola' parts with date codes well after ON Semiconductor's 1999 spin-off with suspicion, although the exact end of Motorola-logo marking was not established. Buy from authorized distributors, and check the logo, date code and marking against the vendor's marking table.
