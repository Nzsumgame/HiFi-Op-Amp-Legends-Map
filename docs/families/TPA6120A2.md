# TPA6120A2 (TI dual current-feedback headphone amplifier)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** Headphone drivers

*TI's 2004 stereo current-feedback headphone driver, rated at 128 dB dynamic range. Its current drive powered the Xonar Essence STX and many DAC/amps.*

**Technology:** Dual (stereo) current-feedback amplifier (CFA) headphone driver. Split supply of ±5 V to ±15 V, or a single supply up to about 30 V. PowerPAD packages: 20-pin SOIC (DWP) and 14-pin QFN (RGY). Surface-mount only, with no DIP version.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

TI's ready-made current-feedback headphone driver was widely used in the 2009-2016 Chinese and PC-audio boom. Examples: the Asus Xonar Essence STX (Soomal 2009, whose packaging touts support for 600 Ω headphones), Musiland Monitor sound cards, FiiO E9, and Sony PHA-1/PHA-2. It is still used in current DAC/amps (SMSL, Sabaj, Aiyima, WiiM, FiiO, Shanling). Enthusiasts rate it for strong current drive, very low noise and distortion figures on paper, and low cost. It is also a common output stage inside an op-amp + CFA composite loop (e.g. OPA1612 or OPA2134 driving a TPA6120A2). Soomal says it had tested at least 10 (later 16) products using the chip. Soomal notes results vary widely with the design, and that designers had become better at using it by 2016. The measured reputation is mixed: implementations with series output resistors show about 10 Ω output impedance (ASR on Sony PHA-1; Roland UA-M10). High-end composite designs measure very well, e.g. the Topping L30 II on ASR, which Frieve says uses the TPA6120A2.

**How people describe the sound:** powerful drive, including high-impedance (600 Ω) headphones (Soomal), very low noise (spec-driven reputation), thick, weighty presentation (Soomal, Sabaj A20d), coarse or grainy, lacking finesse, in a high-output-impedance implementation (Soomal, Sabaj A20d 2022), strongly implementation- and supply-dependent (Soomal)

**Typical uses:** Sound-card headphone outputs: Asus Xonar Essence STX/ST, Creative Sound Blaster ZxR / E5 / X7 / BlasterX G5, Musiland Monitor 01 PS / 03 US / 03 PS, Portable amps and DAPs: Sony PHA-1 / PHA-2, OPPO HA-2, FiiO E9 and X7 AM5 module, Topping NX3, Shanling M5 Ultra, Desktop DAC/amps: SMSL SAP-9 / M20 / DO400, Sabaj A20d, Aiyima DAC-A10, WiiM Ultra, Roland UA-M10, Rupert Neve RNHP, Current buffer inside a VFA-CFA composite loop (with OPA1612, OPA2134, NE5532), DIY headphone amp kits and boards (pavouk.org, Head-Fi TPA6120 thread)

**Caveats:**

- TI's typical application uses series output resistors (bsikar/ra8p1-ereader-kicad notes this conflict). Many products therefore have high output impedance, e.g. about 10 Ω on Sony PHA-1 (ASR) and Roland UA-M10. Soomal also criticised Sabaj A20d results for this reason.
- Very high drive can damage low-impedance headphones or IEMs if gain is not limited. Soomal warned about this on the Musiland Monitor 01 PS.
- Very wide-bandwidth CFA: sensitive to layout, feedback resistor value and capacitive loads. Composite loops need careful compensation and a scope above 300 MHz to verify stability (PWieland/SCAM).
- The PowerPAD must be soldered to a thermal via array (Zeren/Automobile-PMR layout checklist).
- Surface-mount only (PowerPAD SOIC-20 / QFN-14), so it cannot be swapped in a DIP socket.
- Needs rails up to ±15 V; Soomal reports performance improves at higher supply voltage. Portable designs need boost converters.

## Counterfeits

No documented counterfeit or remarked TPA6120A2 reports were found in this session. It is a PowerPAD surface-mount part sold mostly on pre-built AliExpress/Taobao modules and kits (KGUSS, Aiyima, Sunbuck 'TPA6120A2' boards), so the supply chain for loose chips is unverifiable. Buy from TI or authorized distributors (Mouser 595-TPA6120A2DWPR / 595-TPA6120A2RGYT). A genuine TI top mark and date code, and an intact exposed PowerPAD, are the only practical checks.
