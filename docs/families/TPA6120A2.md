# TPA6120A2

**Current-feedback stereo headphone driver, class AB** · Texas Instruments (2004–present) → Texas Instruments · Tier C (niche / baseline)

*TI's 2004 CFA headphone driver: 128 dB dynamic range, 1300 V/µs; inside the Xonar Essence STX and many DAC/amps.*

|---|---|---|---|---|

## Why it's rated

TI's ready-made current-feedback headphone driver, ubiquitous in the 2009–2016 PC-audio and Chinese DAC/amp boom and still used by SMSL, Sabaj, FiiO, Shanling (M5 Ultra) and Rupert Neve (RNHP). Prized for drive, low noise and low cost. Soomal tested 10–16 products: results depend on implementation. Series output resistors give ~10 Ω output impedance; good composite designs measure very well.

**Described as:** powerful, drives 600 Ω, very low noise, thick, weighty, coarse at high Zout, implementation-dependent

**Used in:** Sound cards: Xonar Essence STX/ST, Sound Blaster ZxR/E5/X7/G5, Musiland, Portables: Sony PHA-1/PHA-2, OPPO HA-2, FiiO E9/X7-AM5, Topping NX3, Desktop: SMSL SAP-9/M20/DO400, Sabaj A20d, Aiyima DAC-A10, WiiM Ultra, Current buffer in VFA–CFA composites (OPA1602/OPA1612, OPA2134, NE5532), DIY headphone amps (pavouk.org, Cocoacrumbs, Head-Fi, AMB)

## Watch out

- TI's reference circuit uses series output resistors: ~10 Ω output impedance on Sony PHA-1 (ASR) and Roland UA-M10.
- Huge drive can damage IEMs or low-impedance headphones if gain is not limited (Soomal, Musiland Monitor 01 PS).
- Wide-band CFA: sensitive to layout, feedback resistor and capacitive load; composites need careful compensation (TI E2E).
- SMD only (PowerPAD SOIC-20 / VQFN-14); no DIP swap; PowerPAD must be soldered to thermal vias.
- Needs ±5 V to ±15 V rails; portables need boost converters. Soomal: sounds better at higher supply.
- **Counterfeits:** No counterfeit reports found. Loose chips mostly reach buyers via AliExpress/Taobao modules with unverifiable supply chains. Buy from TI, Mouser (595-TPA6120A2DWPR / 595-TPA6120A2RGYT), DigiKey or LCSC (C70439); check the TI mark, date code and intact PowerPAD.
