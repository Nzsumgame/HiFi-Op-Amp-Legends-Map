# OPA111 / OPA2111 (Burr-Brown Difet low-noise precision, single/dual)

Burr-Brown (1980s (launch year unverified; probably in the 1986 BB IC Data Book) to 2000) → Burr-Brown / Texas Instruments (OPA627 datasheet SBOS165, Aug 2000 to rev C Jan 2025) → Texas Instruments (2000 to present (OPA2111 NRND, OPA2111KP obsolete, OPA111 no longer available)) · Tier B (widely praised)

*Burr-Brown's low-noise Difet (8 nV/√Hz max at 10 kHz, pA bias). TI's OPA627 datasheet calls it 'the popular OPA111', and Japanese rollers prize the metal-can OPA2111AM.*

## Why it's rated

A niche cult part among vintage-op-amp rollers, and a well-known pA-bias, low-noise instrumentation front end. Vendor context: TI's OPA627/637 datasheet benchmarks against 'the popular OPA111'. Japan: hobby blogs praise the metal-can OPA2111AM. One reported that a swap from OPA827 gave less bass emphasis, clearer vocals and a wider soundstage; others describe a warm, analog sound. A Zigsow reviewer found the plastic OPA2111KP harder-sounding and more fatiguing than the OPA2134. China: Soomal groups OPA111 with the BB Difet parts and says OPA627/637 resolve treble much better; its 'warm, full' description applies to Difet op-amps as a class. On Head-Fi, one poster lists OPA2111KP among favourite duals. No ASR or Samuel Groner measurements were found.

**Described as:** warm, analog (JP blogs), wider soundstage / 'veil lifted' (OPA2111AM, JP blogs), less bass emphasis, clearer vocals than OPA827 (one JP swap report), warm, full, relaxed (Soomal, said of Difet op-amps as a class), less treble resolution than OPA627/OPA637 (Soomal), harder-sounding, slightly fatiguing vs OPA2134 (OPA2111KP, one Zigsow review)

**Used in:** Op-amp rolling in headphone amps (JP/CN hobbyists), High-impedance, low-bias front ends: EEG/biopotential (the brain-duino shield uses OPA2111KP), Current-to-voltage converters for sensors (CBA 2010 paper), DC servo/integrator in low-noise physics preamps (arXiv physics/9902042), Vintage preamp/phono stages (discovery note, not verified)

## Watch out

- OPA2111 is NRND at TI, OPA2111KP is obsolete, and OPA111 is no longer available. Supply is NOS or broker stock, so remarked parts are a risk.
- Slow by modern standards (about 2 MHz GBW, 2 V/µs).
- TO-99 metal-can (M-suffix) parts need lead forming or an adapter to fit a DIP-8 socket.
- Differences between the metal-can AM and plastic KP are grade/package differences plus forum folklore. No die change is documented.
- Soomal's descriptors apply to Difet op-amps as a class.
- Quiescent current, output drive and maximum supply were not verified against the datasheet.
- **Counterfeits:** No documented counterfeit reports were found. The risk is structural: OPA2111 is NRND at TI, OPA2111KP is obsolete, OPA111 is no longer available, and supply is NOS or broker stock (a 2023 broker bought KP at €18 and resold at €35). eBay 'Audio Grade' marketing and cult prices for the metal-can AM invite remarks. Practical checks (general advice, not vendor guidance): (1) Burr-Brown logo and a plausible pre-2000 date code, with marking that matches grade and package (M = TO-99, P = plastic DIP). (2) Input bias current should be low-pA (datasheet max 1 pA for OPA111 best grade, 4 pA for OPA2111). A bipolar remark (nA) or generic JFET part (tens of pA) stands out. (3) GBW should be about 2 MHz and slew about 2 V/µs, so a faster slew suggests an NE5532/TL07x-class remark.
