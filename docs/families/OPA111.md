# OPA111 / OPA2111

**Difet JFET-input low-noise precision op-amp, 1/2 ch** · Burr-Brown (c. 1984–2000) → Texas Instruments (2000–present) · Tier B (widely praised)

*Burr-Brown's low-noise Difet with pA bias, 'the popular OPA111' to TI. Japanese rollers prize the metal-can OPA2111AM.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | Supply range |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 nV/√Hz max at 10 kHz; 30 nV/√Hz at 10 Hz (BM) | 2 MHz | 2 V/µs | ±18 V max |

## Why it's rated

A niche cult part among vintage rollers and a known pA-bias instrumentation front end. Japanese blogs praise the metal-can OPA2111AM as warm and analog; one swap from OPA827 gave clearer vocals and wider soundstage. A Zigsow reviewer found the plastic KP harder than OPA2134. Soomal says OPA627/637 resolve treble far better. No ASR or Samuel Groner measurements.

**Described as:** warm, analog, wider soundstage, clearer vocals, less bass emphasis, warm, full (Difet class), less treble resolution, harder-sounding (KP)

**Used in:** Op-amp rolling in headphone amps (JP/CN hobbyists), EEG/biopotential front ends (brain-duino uses OPA2111KP), Current-to-voltage converters for sensors, DC servo in low-noise physics preamps, Vintage preamp and phono stages (unconfirmed)

## Watch out

- Discontinued; supply is NOS or broker stock, so remarks are a risk.
- Slow by modern standards (about 2 MHz, 2 V/µs).
- TO-99 cans need lead forming or an adapter for DIP-8 sockets.
- AM vs KP sound differences are grade/package plus folklore; no die change documented.
- Soomal's descriptors are for Difet parts as a class; Iq and output drive unconfirmed.
- **Counterfeits:** No documented fakes; NOS supply and cult prices invite remarks. Check BB logo, pre-2000 date code, marking (M = TO-99, P = DIP). Bias should be low-pA, not nA; slew far above 2 V/µs suggests an NE5532/TL07x remark.

## Silicon changes

None documented. Compare datasheet revisions before assuming old and new parts are identical.
