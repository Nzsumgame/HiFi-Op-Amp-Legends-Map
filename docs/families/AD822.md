# AD820 / AD822 / AD824 (ADI single-supply rail-to-rail-output JFET-input)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*Low-power ADI JFET-input op amps with rail-to-rail outputs for single-supply use. Japanese DIY builders use the AD822 in battery CMoy amps and compare its sound to the OPA2134.*

**Technology:** JFET-input precision op amps (1/2/4 channels) with rail-to-rail output and an input range that extends below the negative rail. AD820: 5 V to 36 V single or ±2.5 V to ±18 V dual supply, 1.8 MHz, 3 V/µs, 800 µA max. AD822: 5 V to 30 V single or ±2.5 V to ±15 V dual, output within 10 mV of each rail. AD824: 3 V single up to ±15 V, 2 MHz, 2 V/µs, 500 µA per amplifier.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded: Unclassified).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

The praise is modest and comes mostly from Japanese DIY sources. A zigsow review calls the AD822 'a single-supply part that holds up well enough for audio'. nabe's spec-vs-sound notes say its sound tendency is similar to the OPA2134, with a cool FET-input character, and find the OPA2350 fuller-bodied. The very low current draw makes it a battery CMoy headphone-amp choice, but nabe notes some transient problems into 15-ohm loads. Akizuki stocks the DIP version for hobbyists.

**How people describe the sound:** cool / FET-input character (nabe), tendency similar to OPA2134 (nabe), less full-bodied than OPA2350 (nabe)

**Typical uses:** battery-powered CMoy headphone amplifiers, single-supply portable audio stages, DIY op-amp swapping (DIP-8 AD822ANZ)

**Caveats:**

- Designed as a low-power precision/instrumentation part, not an audio part
- Limited drive: transient problems reported into 15-ohm headphone loads (nabe)
- Praise is lukewarm ('good enough for audio'), not legendary status

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Unclassified: Analog Devices, 2026 (by PCN number; exact date not confirmed)

ADI PCN 26_0029 lists AD820, AD822 and AD824 variants together with the AD823 and AD8224 JFET-input amplifiers. Search results did not show what the change is (fab/process, assembly, test or package). A multi-product JFET-family PCN could mean a process or fab change, but that is unconfirmed.

- **When:** 2026 (by PCN number; exact date not confirmed)
- **Affected:** AD820, AD822, AD823, AD824, AD8224
- **How to tell old from new:** Covered by ADI PCN 26_0029 Rev. -. Check the PCN for how to tell pre- and post-change parts apart (date code or other marking).
- **Audio impact:** Unknown until the PCN text is read.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium - a 2026 PCN covers the whole JFET-RRO family, but the nature of the change is unconfirmed
- **Confidence:** low

## Counterfeits

No specific AD820/AD822/AD824 counterfeit reports were found in this check. The DIP-8 AD822ANZ circulates widely in the hobby market, so buy from authorized distributors or reputable retailers such as Akizuki.

## Related parts and alternatives

ADA4622-1 / ADA4622-2 / ADA4622-4 (ADI's stated next generation), OPA2134 (nabe compares its sound tendency to this part), OPA2350 (single-supply CMOS RRIO; nabe finds it fuller-bodied), AD823 (faster ADI JFET-input RRO dual)
