# THS4631 (single high-voltage, high-slew-rate, wideband JFET-input op amp)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*A ±15 V JFET-input op amp with 210 MHz GBW and 1000 V/µs slew, used as a cult DAC I/V and buffer part in Japan: airy and transparent once it is stable.*

**Technology:** JFET-input high-speed voltage-feedback op amp from TI (the datasheet calls the input stage JFET). Unity-gain stable: 325 MHz at G=1 and 210 MHz GBW. Single channel. Runs on ±5 to ±15 V (10–30 V single supply). SMD only: SOIC-8 (D), HSOIC-8 PowerPAD (DDA; 'SOIC-PP' in Rev B) and HVSSOP-8 PowerPAD (DGN; 'MSOP-PP' in Rev B). Standard single-op-amp pinout: 2 = IN−, 3 = IN+, 4 = V−, 6 = OUT, 7 = V+, and 1/5/8 are NC. The TI documents disagree about the thermal pad. Rev B, and the layout section of Rev C, say the PowerPAD is 'electrically isolated from all pins and the active circuitry', recommend tying it to ground, and allow any potential from VS− to VS+. The new Pin Functions table in Rev C says the pad is 'internally connected to V−' and must be soldered to V−.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche cult part in the Japanese DIY and op-amp-rolling scene. It is mostly used as the I/V stage after a current-output DAC, and in headphone amps and buffers via DIP-8 conversion boards. One Ameblo blog titles it 'the rumoured op-amp' (噂のオペアンプ). Shirokuma sells it pre-mounted on a dual 8-pin DIP conversion board, and the maimai-audio blog bought one from Bispa. Users report that when it is not oscillating it gives extended highs, air, transparency and expressiveness. A Zigsow review is titled 'tsundere honours-student character' (ツンデレ優等生キャラ): superb when tamed, difficult otherwise. A cocolog blog compares it with LF357H and OPA627SM, and a Zigsow OPA627AP review lists it with AD797, LT1115 and LME49990 as famous singles. TI's datasheet lists 'Current DAC output buffer' among its applications. No Audio Science Review, Samuel Groner or other audio-band measurements are known to this record.

**How people describe the sound:** airy, transparent, extended highs, expressive, 'tsundere honours student' (ツンデレ優等生): excellent once stable

**Typical uses:** I/V conversion after current-output DACs, DIP-8 op-amp rolling via conversion boards (e.g. Shirokuma dual board) in DACs, Headphone amp op-amp swap (e.g. Elekit TU-HP01, per the xgender-isao blog), High-impedance wideband buffer / line driver

**Caveats:**

- Oscillates easily in rolling and adapter use; Japanese users report needing an output Zobel network. TI's own datasheet warns that large feedback resistors (e.g. 5 kΩ/5 kΩ inverting) oscillate with the input capacitance and recommends a feedback capacitor, which matters for I/V stages. A non-audio GitHub hardware project (ZS-1100A) also replaced the THS4631 with OPA180/OPA197 'for better stability'.
- Runs hot: 12.5 mA typ and 14.5 mA max quiescent per channel in Rev C (11.5 typ / 13 max in Rev B). That is about 0.375 W idle at ±15 V, and a dual adapter carries two chips. Japanese users advise heat management at ±12 V and above.
- The thermal pad connection is ambiguous. Rev B says the PowerPAD is electrically isolated, recommends ground and allows VS− to VS+. Rev C's pin table says it is internally connected to V− and must be soldered to V−, yet Rev C's layout section still says isolated/ground. Tying the pad to V− is permitted by both revisions; grounding it is unsafe if Rev C's pin table is right.
- SMD only (SOIC/HSOIC/HVSSOP), so socket use needs an adapter. It is a single, so it replaces a dual only on a dual-conversion board.
- Noise is specified only above 10 kHz (7 nV/√Hz). The 1 kHz figure, in the JFET 1/f region, is not tabulated, and there is no audio-band THD+N spec.
- Parts made after the March 2025 datasheet (Rev C) may be different silicon, with higher Iq, higher output current and re-specified flatness with CF. The listening impressions (c. 2018–2020) predate this.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Texas Instruments: Published in SLOS451C (March 2025).

Rev C re-characterises the THS4631 on 'new silicon data'. Output current rises sharply (typ 98 to 180 mA; min at 25 °C 90 to 120 mA), and quiescent current rises (typ 11.5 to 12.5 mA; max 13 to 14.5 mA). The 0.1 dB flatness with an 8.2 pF feedback capacitor is re-specified from 38 to 6 MHz. The new pin table also describes the PowerPAD as tied to V− instead of electrically isolated. Headline specs are unchanged: 7 nV/√Hz, 20 fA/√Hz, 210 MHz GBW, 1000 V/µs, ±500 µV Vos max, 100 pA Ib max. The spec changes are vendor-documented. That they reflect a new die, process, fab or leadframe attach is an inference; TI does not say so. The pattern resembles other TI legacy-part refreshes of 2024–2025.

- **When:** Published in SLOS451C (March 2025). TI was still serving Rev B, with the legacy limits, in a PDF generated 2019-03-21, and TI's PSpice model dated 2020-11-02 still uses the Rev B Iq and Isc values (11.5 mA; +98/−95 mA). The re-characterisation therefore went public between 2019 and March 2025. No PCN was located, and the physical change date is unknown.
- **Affected:** THS4631DDA, THS4631DR, THS4631DGNR
- **How to tell old from new:** By datasheet only. SLOS451C (Rev C, March 2025) says 'Updated graphs with new silicon data' and carries new limits; SLOS451B (Aug 2011) describes the legacy part. The top marking is unchanged ('4631' on D/DDA, 'ADK' on DGN, the same in the 2017 and 2025 addenda), so the marking cannot tell old from new. No date-code cutoff or PCN number was found. The '.A'/'.B' orderable suffixes in the 2025 addendum are a TI-wide addendum convention (they also appear on NE5532, TL071, LF353, OPA1652 and OPA1611), so they are not evidence of a THS4631 die change.
- **Audio impact:** No listening or measurement comparison of old and new lots was found. In practice: more idle heat, already the main known problem in DIP-adapter use; much stronger static drive, useful for low-impedance and headphone loads; and a different AC response with feedback capacitance. That last point matters because the THS4631 is already oscillation-prone in I/V stages, so Zobel and CF values tuned on old parts may need re-checking. The Japanese sound impressions (c. 2018–2020) were formed on pre-Rev-C silicon.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium: pinout, package and headline AC specs are unchanged. But higher Iq and heat, a changed compensated-loop response, and the ambiguous thermal-pad potential (grounded pad vs. internal V− tie) can upset marginal stability or thermal designs, or adapters that ground the pad. Tie the pad to V−, which both revisions allow.
- **Confidence:** medium

## Counterfeits

No THS4631-specific counterfeit reports were located; no web search could run in this pass. Genuine markings are the same in TI's 2017 and 2025 package addenda: '4631' on SOIC-8 (D) and HSOIC-8 PowerPAD (DDA), and 'ADK' on HVSSOP/MSOP-8 PowerPAD (DGN). A DDA part must have an exposed pad underneath; a '4631' SOIC without a pad is the plain D package. Because the part is SMD-only, hobbyists usually buy it pre-mounted on DIP adapters from small vendors (e.g. Shirokuma), or as bare DDA parts from shops such as eleshop.jp. Prefer authorized distributors or known adapter makers over anonymous marketplace sellers. The marking cannot tell pre- and post-2025 silicon apart.
