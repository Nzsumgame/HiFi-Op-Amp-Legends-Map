# NJM4580 / JRC4580 (incl. TI RC4580) (dual audio bipolar)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** Japanese audio series (New JRC / Nisshinbo)

*Japan's cheap 'standard audio op-amp' in countless sound cards, CD players and DACs; praised for a neutral, high-resolution 'plain as water' sound, rated above NE5532 by Soomal.*

**Technology:** Bipolar dual op-amp in the standard dual pinout (pin-compatible with 4558/5532-type duals), optimised for audio: low noise, 15 MHz GBW, high output current, low THD, ±2 V minimum supply. NJM4580 input stage is PNP according to JP hobby notes and both NJR-labelled SPICE macromodels (Bee Technologies 2007 'MANUFACTURER: NEW JAPAN RADIO' model and njm4580_v2), medium confidence; the datasheet equivalent circuit was not read. TI RC4580 is a separate TI-made part whose input polarity is contested: Headphonesty says NPN inputs with a complementary output, while Rich Cabot and diyAudio posters describe the RC4580-die NE5532 as PNP-input.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (3 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

This is the default 'standard audio op-amp' in Japanese and Chinese consumer audio. Soomal found the JRC4580 in many mid- and high-end sound cards, active speakers, headphone amps and DACs. It called the part 'not top-tier but better than the so-called king of op-amps NE5532' and describes it as high-resolution and neutral or plain ('平白如水'), adaptable but characterless, unlike the warm OP275. Japanese hobby sources call it the standard audio op-amp: fast and smooth with low residual noise, mellow and easy to listen to, though some find it overly smooth or veiled. Opinion is not uniform. nabe.adiary's CMoy comparison puts NJM4580 in C rank, below NE5532 (B). English forums say it is chosen for price rather than performance, and some call it 'somewhat edgy and lacking detail'. Its ±2 V minimum supply makes it popular for USB-powered DIY; a 2012 srad.jp commenter ran the '50-yen NJM4580DD' straight from USB power.

**How people describe the sound:** neutral / plain ('平白如水', '音色中性平淡' - Soomal), high resolution ('解析力出色', '解析力高' - Soomal), not warm (contrasted with OP275 - Soomal), adaptable but characterless ('对声音的适应性强，但缺乏特点' - Soomal), fast and smooth, low residual noise (JP hobby notes), mellow and easy listening; to some, overly smooth or veiled (JP notes on NJM4580DD), relatively flat with good HF extension (JP repair-shop note), 'somewhat edgy and lacking detail' (minority English-forum view)

**Typical uses:** Sound-card analog outputs (Soomal: TerraTec DMX 6Fire LT; TempoTec Hifier Fantasia / Variations / Sonata / X-Fi Rhapsody; Musiland Moli; 傲王 SQ717; AUDIOPROBE SPARTAN CUE 110), Sound-card recording input gain stage (Musiland X-Sword II main card, per Soomal), Active speakers (Musiland C260 / C280 / N66II), Headphone amps and DACs (Musiland HP10; TempoTec Fantasia DAII Plus unbalanced output), Japanese CD players and AV gear (ONKYO / DENON / PIONEER per JP repair-shop notes), DIY USB-powered DAC and headphone-amp stages (±2 V minimum supply), Preamp, tone-control, active-filter and headphone-amp stages (datasheet target applications)

**Caveats:**

- Not a high-end part. Soomal calls it good but not top-tier and characterless, and nabe.adiary ranks it below NE5532. The 'better than NE5532' verdict comes from one reviewer.
- Soomal says it is often confused with the far cheaper, pin-compatible JRC4558.
- Nisshinbo gives no input noise current and only an RIAA-weighted noise figure (RS=2.2 kΩ, 30 kHz LPF); nV/√Hz is only a graph.
- Nisshinbo's 20250303 datasheet marks NJM4580D (DIP8) and NJM4580L (SIP8) NRND. The D-rank DIP (NJM4580DD / D-D) is not addressed separately, and future DIP rolling stock may need SMD-to-DIP adapters.
- TI RC4580 is a different part (GBW 12 vs 15 MHz). The reputation was earned by the JRC part, and TI's NE5532, LM833 and MC33078 reportedly now use the RC4580-type die.
- The datasheet claims headphone-amp suitability (±13.5 V typ into 2 kΩ at ±15 V), but low-impedance headphones benefit from a buffer (general practice, not measured here).
- The '50 yen' price comes from a 2012 comment; current prices were not checked.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Texas Instruments: Production change in 2024 (Gearspace/Hackaday).

A TI E2E thread titled 'NE5532, LM833, RC4580 and MC33078 all now the same die' (4 June 2026) is reported by Hackaday and Headphonesty as TI's confirmation. Rich Cabot (Audio Precision co-founder) said the new NE5532 is 'not a 5532 on a new process… they just relabeled a 4580', and an EEVblog user reported private TI confirmation. TI's own SLOS075K revision history gives the NE5532 deltas below. The new NE5532 figures (12 MHz, 5 V/µs, ±18 V, 6 mA) match the RC4580 headline specs, which supports the donor-die reading (inference).

- **When:** Production change in 2024 (Gearspace/Hackaday). NE5532 datasheet SLOS075K December 2025; TI E2E thread 4 June 2026.
- **Affected:** NE5532, NE5532A, LM833, MC33078, RC4580 (reported donor die)
- **How to tell old from new:** NE5532/NE5532A: SLOS075K (December 2025) documents the new silicon; SLOS075J (January 2015) and earlier describe the legacy die. RC4580 has no documented marker: its datasheet went to SLOS412E in November 2024 (change list not captured). Check TI PCN device lists and lot/date codes from 2024 onward.
- **Audio impact:** No RC4580-specific audio change is documented. A post-2024 TI NE5532, LM833 or MC33078 now behaves like an RC4580-class part (5 V/µs, ±18 V max), not like the classic 5532.
- **Drop-in risk:** low for RC4580 as far as documented (headline specs unchanged), but RC4580's own post-migration change list is unknown; high for NE5532/LM833 users designing to legacy ±18 to ±22 V rails.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| NE5532 abs-max supply (SLOS075K revision history) | ±22 V | ±18 V (RC4580 operating max: ±18 V) |
| NE5532 slew rate | 9 V/µs typ | 5 V/µs typ (RC4580: 5 V/µs) |
| NE5532 unity-gain bandwidth | 10 MHz typ | 12 MHz typ (RC4580: 12 MHz) |
| NE5532 HBM ESD | 2000 V | 1000 V |
| NE5532 supply current | 8 mA typ | 6 mA typ |
| NE5532 specified items | output-swing (p-p), small-signal gain, max output-swing bandwidth, output impedance, crosstalk, overshoot specified | removed from datasheet |

### 3. Texas Instruments vs New JRC / Nisshinbo: April 2003 (TI RC4580 introduced) - present

TI's RC4580 is a pin- and function-compatible second source. No evidence was found that it is a JRC die, and its GBW is lower. Its other headline figures match the NJM4580 (±2 to ±18 V, 0.8 µVrms, 5 V/µs). Input polarity: NJM4580 is PNP per NJR-labelled SPICE macromodels and JP notes. TI RC4580 is contested: Headphonesty says NPN, while Cabot and diyAudio describe the RC4580-die NE5532 as PNP.

- **When:** April 2003 (TI RC4580 introduced) - present
- **Affected:** RC4580, RC4580-Q1, NJM4580
- **How to tell old from new:** Tell them apart by part number and logo: TI RC4580 (orderables RC4580ID/IDR/IP/IPWR) vs JRC/NJR/Nisshinbo NJM4580 (often called JRC4580 after its marking).
- **Audio impact:** No comparative listening test or measurement of NJM4580 vs RC4580 was found. The reputation (Soomal and JP community) was earned by the JRC part.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: drop-in pinout and similar supply range, with slightly lower GBW on the TI part.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Gain bandwidth product (typ) | 15 MHz (NJM4580, f=10 kHz) | 12 MHz (TI RC4580) |

## Counterfeits

Soomal warns that JRC4580 is often confused with the far cheaper, pin-compatible JRC4558. Relabelling 4558s as 4580s is therefore the plausible main risk, but no documented teardown or measurement case was found, and GitHub searches for NJM4580 fake/remark reports returned nothing (low confidence). Buy from authorised channels (Mouser, RS, Marutsu, Akizuki, Keisei, Digi-Key). Check that the logo (JRC/NJR/Nisshinbo), laser-marking font and date codes are consistent. Distrust cheap 'NOS' DIP lots, since the DIP NJM4580D is NRND and supply will tighten. D-rank parts appear as 'NJM4580DD' in distributor listings and as 'NJM4580D-D' in the 2025 datasheet. A quick bench check: GBW about 15 MHz and slew about 5 V/µs; 4558-class parts are much slower. TI RC4580 is a legitimate but different die (12 MHz GBW).

## Related parts and alternatives

MUSES8820 (Nisshinbo): the usual first upgrade from NJM4580/NE5532 in Japan (discovery notes), NJM8801 (Nisshinbo): a zigsow reviewer places it above NJM4580 and below MUSES8820, NL8802 (Nisshinbo, 2025): described in discovery notes as the MUSES8820's successor bipolar dual, NJM2114 / NJM5532 (JRC low-noise duals), Renesas µPC4570: 4558-derived Japanese peer; C-Area wiki says its bass is tighter than NJM4580DD's, TI RC4580: pin-compatible second source (different die, 12 MHz GBW), LM4562 / LME49720: lower distortion, higher-supply modern bipolar, OPA1612 / OPA1602: modern low-noise bipolar duals
