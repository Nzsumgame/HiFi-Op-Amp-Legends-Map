# NJM4580 / JRC4580 (incl. TI RC4580) (dual audio bipolar)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** Japanese audio series (New JRC / Nisshinbo)

*Japan's cheap 'standard audio op-amp' in countless sound cards, CD players and DACs; praised for a neutral, high-resolution 'plain as water' sound, rated above NE5532 by Soomal.*

**Technology:** Bipolar dual op-amp in the standard dual pinout (pin-compatible with 4558/5532-type duals), optimised for audio ('specially designed for improving tone control'): low noise, 15 MHz GBW, high output current, low THD, ±2 V minimum supply. NJM4580 input stage is PNP according to JP hobby notes and two NJR-labelled SPICE macromodels (medium confidence; datasheet equivalent circuit not read). TI RC4580 is a separate TI-made part whose input polarity is contested: Headphonesty says the RC4580-die NE5532 went from a PNP to an NPN input stage, while Rich Cabot and diyAudio posters describe the RC4580-die NE5532 as PNP-input. The classic NE5532 is commonly documented with an NPN input pair, so Headphonesty's direction may be reversed (inference, not checked this pass).

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (3 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

The default 'standard audio op-amp' in Japanese and Chinese consumer audio. Soomal found the JRC4580 in many mid- and high-end sound cards, active speakers, headphone amps and DACs, calling it 'not top-tier but better than the so-called king of op-amps NE5532' and describing it as high-resolution and neutral or plain ('平白如水'), adaptable but characterless, unlike the warm OP275. Japanese hobby sources call it the standard audio op-amp: fast and smooth with low residual noise, mellow and easy to listen to, though some find it overly smooth or veiled. Opinion is not uniform: nabe.adiary's CMoy comparison puts NJM4580 in C rank below NE5532 (B); a Chinese PC-audio primer calls it NE5532-like and average; English forums say it is chosen for price rather than performance, and some call it 'somewhat edgy and lacking detail'. Its ±2 V minimum supply makes it popular for USB-powered DIY; a 2012 srad.jp commenter ran the '50-yen NJM4580DD' straight from USB power.

**How people describe the sound:** neutral / plain ('平白如水', '音色中性平淡' - Soomal), high resolution ('解析力出色', '解析力高' - Soomal), not warm (contrasted with OP275 - Soomal), adaptable but characterless ('对声音的适应性强，但缺乏特点' - Soomal), fast and smooth, low residual noise (JP hobby notes), mellow and easy listening; to some, overly smooth or veiled (JP notes on NJM4580DD), relatively flat with good HF extension (JP repair-shop note), similar to NE5532, average (Chinese PC-audio primer, per search summary), 'somewhat edgy and lacking detail' (minority English-forum view)

**Typical uses:** Sound-card analog outputs (Soomal: TerraTec DMX 6Fire LT; TempoTec Hifier Fantasia / Variations / Sonata / X-Fi Rhapsody; Musiland Moli; 傲王 SQ717; AUDIOPROBE SPARTAN CUE 110), Sound-card recording input gain stage (Musiland X-Sword II main card, per Soomal), Active speakers (Musiland C260 / C280 / N66II), Headphone amps and DACs (Musiland HP10; TempoTec Fantasia DAII Plus unbalanced output), Japanese CD players and AV gear (ONKYO / DENON / PIONEER per JP repair-shop notes), DIY USB-powered DAC and headphone-amp stages (±2 V minimum supply), Preamp, tone-control, active-filter and headphone-amp stages (datasheet target applications)

**Caveats:**

- Not a high-end part. Soomal calls it good but not top-tier and characterless, nabe.adiary ranks it below NE5532, and a Chinese primer calls it average. The 'better than NE5532' verdict comes from one reviewer (Soomal).
- Soomal says it is often confused with the far cheaper, pin-compatible JRC4558.
- Nisshinbo gives no input noise current and only an RIAA-weighted noise figure (RS=2.2 kΩ, 30 kHz LPF); nV/√Hz is only a graph.
- Nisshinbo's 20250303 datasheet marks NJM4580D (DIP8) and NJM4580L (SIP8) NRND. The D-rank DIP (NJM4580DD / D-D) is not addressed separately, and future DIP rolling stock may need SMD-to-DIP adapters.
- TI RC4580 is a different part (GBW 12 vs 15 MHz). The reputation was earned by the JRC part; TI's NE5532, LM833 and MC33078 now reportedly use the RC4580 die.
- The datasheet claims headphone-amp suitability (±13.5 V typ into 2 kΩ at ±15 V), but low-impedance headphones benefit from a buffer (general practice, not measured here).
- The '50 yen' price comes from a 2012 comment; current prices were not checked.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Texas Instruments: Production change 2024-2025 (RFAB PCNs);

A TI E2E thread titled 'NE5532, LM833, RC4580 and MC33078 all now the same die' confirms the consolidation. Hackaday reports Rich Cabot (Audio Precision co-founder) identifying the RC4580 die in the new NE5532 ('they just relabeled a 4580'). Headphonesty reports field failures on boards using ±22 V-class rails, removal of the NE5532's input protection diodes and halved HBM ESD; the E2E summary notes the classic NE5532 has back-to-back input diodes whereas the RC4580 does not. For RC4580 buyers this is the donor design, so little change is expected; the hazard is for NE5532/LM833/MC33078 users.

- **When:** Production change 2024-2025 (RFAB PCNs); NE5532 datasheet SLOS075K December 2025; TI E2E thread June 2026; Hackaday 3 June 2026.
- **Affected:** NE5532, NE5532A, LM833, MC33078, RC4580 (donor die)
- **How to tell old from new:** NE5532/NE5532A: SLOS075K (December 2025) documents the new silicon; SLOS075J (January 2015) and earlier describe the legacy die. RC4580 has no documented marker: its datasheet went to SLOS412E in November 2024 (change list not captured). Check TI PCN device lists and lot/date codes from 2024 onward.
- **Audio impact:** No RC4580-specific audio change is documented. A post-2024 TI NE5532, LM833 or MC33078 now behaves like an RC4580-class part (5 V/µs, ±18 V max, no input diodes), not like the classic 5532.
- **Drop-in risk:** low for RC4580 (it is the donor die; headline specs unchanged), but its own post-migration change list is unknown; high for NE5532/LM833 users on legacy ±18 to ±22 V rails or relying on input diodes.
- **Confidence:** high

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| NE5532 abs-max supply (SLOS075K revision history) | ±22 V | ±18 V (RC4580 operating max: ±18 V) |
| NE5532 slew rate | 9 V/µs typ | 5 V/µs typ (RC4580: 5 V/µs) |
| NE5532 unity-gain bandwidth | 10 MHz typ | 12 MHz typ (RC4580: 12 MHz) |
| NE5532 HBM ESD | 2000 V | 1000 V |

### 2. Texas Instruments: PCNs November 2023 - February 2025 (20231114002.1;…

PCN 20240429005.1 is titled 'Qualification of RFAB using qualified Process Technology, Die Revision, Datasheet, and additional Assembly site/BOM options for select devices'. PCN 20250129000.1 qualifies RFAB (HPA8 process, 300 mm wafers) with a die change resulting from the process change. PCN 20231114002.1 is described by a search summary and a TI E2E China thread ('RC4580: PCN change-Wire binding') as a bond-wire material change listing RC4580IDR, i.e. assembly, not die. diyAudio posters say the NE5532/LM833 family moved from SFAB to RFAB with a redesign. Whether RC4580 itself got a die revision or only served as the template for the others is not established.

- **When:** PCNs November 2023 - February 2025 (20231114002.1; 20240429005.1 dated 30 April 2024; 20250129000.1 dated 3 February 2025)
- **Affected:** RC4580IDR (PCN 20231114002.1, bond-wire change, per search summary), RC4580IP (earlier search summary), RC4580 membership in PCN 20240429005.1 / 20250129000.1 device lists not confirmed
- **How to tell old from new:** Read the PCN device lists and check lot/date codes. For RC4580 the only document-level marker found is datasheet SLOS412E (November 2024). Headline specs look unchanged, so a bench test is unlikely to tell old from new.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown: no measurements of pre- vs post-2024 RC4580 were found.
- **Drop-in risk:** low (uncertain): headline specs appear unchanged. Check abs-max and ESD in SLOS412E before relying on older margins.
- **Confidence:** low

### 3. Texas Instruments vs New JRC / Nisshinbo: April 2003 (TI RC4580 introduced) - present

TI's RC4580 is a pin- and function-compatible second source. No evidence was found that it is a JRC die, and its GBW is lower. Its other headline figures match the NJM4580 (±2 to ±18 V, 0.8 µVrms, 5 V/µs, 0.0005% THD). Input polarity: NJM4580 is PNP per NJR-labelled SPICE macromodels and JP notes; TI RC4580 is contested (Headphonesty NPN vs Cabot/diyAudio PNP).

- **When:** April 2003 (TI RC4580 introduced) - present
- **Affected:** RC4580, RC4580-Q1, NJM4580
- **How to tell old from new:** Tell them apart by part number and logo: TI RC4580 (orderables RC4580ID/IDR/IP/IPWR) vs JRC/NJR/Nisshinbo NJM4580 (often called JRC4580 after its marking).
- **Audio impact:** No comparative listening test or measurement of NJM4580 vs RC4580 was found. The reputation (Soomal and JP community) was earned by the JRC part.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: drop-in pinout and similar supply range, with slightly lower GBW on the TI part.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Gain bandwidth product (typ) | 15 MHz (NJM4580, f=10 kHz) | 12 MHz (TI RC4580) |

## Counterfeits

Soomal warns that JRC4580 is often confused with the far cheaper, pin-compatible JRC4558. Relabelling 4558s as 4580s is therefore the plausible main risk, but no documented teardown or measurement case was found, and the discovery claim that 'JRC4580' clones/remarks are common was not confirmed (low confidence). Buy from authorised channels (Mouser, RS, Marutsu, Akizuki, Keisei, Digi-Key). Check that the logo (JRC/NJR/Nisshinbo), laser-marking font and date codes are consistent. Distrust cheap 'NOS' DIP lots, since the DIP NJM4580D is NRND and supply will tighten. D-rank parts appear as 'NJM4580DD' in distributor listings and 'NJM4580D-D' in the 2025 datasheet. Quick bench check: GBW about 15 MHz and slew about 5 V/µs; 4558-class parts are much slower. TI RC4580 is a legitimate but different die (12 MHz GBW).
