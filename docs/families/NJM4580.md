# NJM4580 / JRC4580 (incl. TI RC4580) (dual audio bipolar)

New Japan Radio (JRC / NJR) (before 2003 (earliest datasheet located: Ver.2003-03-13; exact introduction unknown) - 2021) → Nisshinbo Micro Devices (2022 - present) · second sources: Texas Instruments · Tier A (legend / revision hazard)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** TI E2E thread 'NE5532, LM833, RC4580 and MC33078 all now the same die' confirms the consolidation. (+2 more) [Details](#silicon-changes)

*Japan's cheap 'standard audio op-amp' in countless sound cards, CD players and DACs; praised for a neutral, high-resolution 'plain as water' sound, rated above NE5532 by Soomal.*

## Why it's rated

The default 'standard audio op-amp' in Japanese and Chinese consumer audio. Soomal found the JRC4580 in many mid- and high-end sound cards, active speakers, headphone amps and DACs, calling it 'not top-tier but better than the so-called king of op-amps NE5532' and describing it as high-resolution and neutral or plain ('平白如水'), adaptable but characterless, unlike the warm OP275. Japanese hobby sources call it the standard audio op-amp: fast and smooth with low residual noise, mellow and easy to listen to, though some find it overly smooth or veiled. Opinion is not uniform: nabe.adiary's CMoy comparison puts NJM4580 in C rank below NE5532 (B); a Chinese PC-audio primer calls it NE5532-like and average; English forums say it is chosen for price rather than performance, and some call it 'somewhat edgy and lacking detail'. Its ±2 V minimum supply makes it popular for USB-powered DIY; a 2012 srad.jp commenter ran the '50-yen NJM4580DD' straight from USB power.

**Described as:** neutral / plain ('平白如水', '音色中性平淡' - Soomal), high resolution ('解析力出色', '解析力高' - Soomal), not warm (contrasted with OP275 - Soomal), adaptable but characterless ('对声音的适应性强，但缺乏特点' - Soomal), fast and smooth, low residual noise (JP hobby notes), mellow and easy listening; to some, overly smooth or veiled (JP notes on NJM4580DD), relatively flat with good HF extension (JP repair-shop note), similar to NE5532, average (Chinese PC-audio primer, per search summary), 'somewhat edgy and lacking detail' (minority English-forum view)

**Used in:** Sound-card analog outputs (Soomal: TerraTec DMX 6Fire LT; TempoTec Hifier Fantasia / Variations / Sonata / X-Fi Rhapsody; Musiland Moli; 傲王 SQ717; AUDIOPROBE SPARTAN CUE 110), Sound-card recording input gain stage (Musiland X-Sword II main card, per Soomal), Active speakers (Musiland C260 / C280 / N66II), Headphone amps and DACs (Musiland HP10; TempoTec Fantasia DAII Plus unbalanced output), Japanese CD players and AV gear (ONKYO / DENON / PIONEER per JP repair-shop notes), DIY USB-powered DAC and headphone-amp stages (±2 V minimum supply), Preamp, tone-control, active-filter and headphone-amp stages (datasheet target applications)

## Watch out

- Not a high-end part. Soomal calls it good but not top-tier and characterless, nabe.adiary ranks it below NE5532, and a Chinese primer calls it average. The 'better than NE5532' verdict comes from one reviewer (Soomal).
- Soomal says it is often confused with the far cheaper, pin-compatible JRC4558.
- Nisshinbo gives no input noise current and only an RIAA-weighted noise figure (RS=2.2 kΩ, 30 kHz LPF); nV/√Hz is only a graph.
- Nisshinbo's 20250303 datasheet marks NJM4580D (DIP8) and NJM4580L (SIP8) NRND. The D-rank DIP (NJM4580DD / D-D) is not addressed separately, and future DIP rolling stock may need SMD-to-DIP adapters.
- TI RC4580 is a different part (GBW 12 vs 15 MHz). The reputation was earned by the JRC part; TI's NE5532, LM833 and MC33078 now reportedly use the RC4580 die.
- The datasheet claims headphone-amp suitability (±13.5 V typ into 2 kΩ at ±15 V), but low-impedance headphones benefit from a buffer (general practice, not measured here).
- The '50 yen' price comes from a 2012 comment; current prices were not checked.
- **Counterfeits:** Soomal warns that JRC4580 is often confused with the far cheaper, pin-compatible JRC4558. Relabelling 4558s as 4580s is therefore the plausible main risk, but no documented teardown or measurement case was found, and the discovery claim that 'JRC4580' clones/remarks are common was not confirmed (low confidence). Buy from authorised channels (Mouser, RS, Marutsu, Akizuki, Keisei, Digi-Key). Check that the logo (JRC/NJR/Nisshinbo), laser-marking font and date codes are consistent. Distrust cheap 'NOS' DIP lots, since the DIP NJM4580D is NRND and supply will tighten. D-rank parts appear as 'NJM4580DD' in distributor listings and 'NJM4580D-D' in the 2025 datasheet. Quick bench check: GBW about 15 MHz and slew about 5 V/µs; 4558-class parts are much slower. TI RC4580 is a legitimate but different die (12 MHz GBW).

## Silicon changes

### Die redesign: Texas Instruments, Process-migration PCN 20231114002.1 (15 Nov 2023) for NE5532DR/LM833DR/MC33078DR; production change 2024-2025 (SFAB to RFAB). NE5532 datasheet SLOS075K Dec 2025. TI E2E confirmation 2026; Hackaday 3 Jun 2026.

TI E2E thread 'NE5532, LM833, RC4580 and MC33078 all now the same die' confirms the consolidation. Hackaday reports that Rich Cabot (Audio Precision co-founder) identified the RC4580 die in the new NE5532 ('they just relabeled a 4580'). Headphonesty reports field failures on boards with ±22 V-class rails, removal of the NE5532's input protection diodes, and halved HBM ESD. The E2E summary notes the classic NE5532 has back-to-back input diodes and the RC4580 does not. For RC4580 buyers this is the donor design, so little change is expected. The hazard is for TI NE5532, LM833 and MC33078 users, whose parts now behave like an RC4580-class device. JRC/Nisshinbo NJM4580 is not involved.

- **Affected:** RC4580 (donor die; TI RC4580ID/IDR/IP/IPWR), NE5532 / NE5532A / SA5532 / SA5532A (TI), LM833 (TI), MC33078 (TI)
- **How to tell:** NE5532/NE5532A: SLOS075K (Dec 2025) documents the new silicon; SLOS075J (Jan 2015) and earlier describe the legacy die. RC4580 has no documented marker: its datasheet went to SLOS412E in Nov 2024 (change list not captured). No PCN was found saying that the RC4580 itself was re-fabricated or redesigned.
- **Audio impact:** No RC4580-specific audio change is documented. A post-2024 TI NE5532, LM833 or MC33078 behaves like an RC4580-class part (5 V/µs, ±18 V max, no input diodes), not like the classic 5532.
- **Drop-in risk:** low for RC4580 - it is the donor die and its headline specs are unchanged, though its own post-migration change list is unknown. High for TI NE5532/LM833 users on legacy ±18 to ±22 V rails or relying on the input diodes.
- **Confidence:** high

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| NE5532 abs-max supply (SLOS075K) | ±22 V | ±18 V (RC4580 operating max: ±18 V) |
| NE5532 slew rate | 9 V/µs typ | 5 V/µs typ (RC4580: 5 V/µs) |
| NE5532 unity-gain bandwidth | 10 MHz typ | 12 MHz typ (RC4580: 12 MHz) |
| NE5532 HBM ESD | 2000 V | 1000 V |
| NE5532 supply current | 8 mA typ | 6 mA typ |
| NE5532 input differential protection diodes | back-to-back diodes across inputs | removed (RC4580 die has none) - Headphonesty / E2E summary |

### Datasheet respec: Texas Instruments, Datasheet SLOS412D (Nov 2014) to SLOS412E (Nov 2024). Related RFAB PCNs 20240429005.1 (30 Apr 2024) and 20250129000.1 (3 Feb 2025) exist, but RC4580 membership is unconfirmed.

TI revised the RC4580 datasheet to Rev E in November 2024, in the same window as the SFAB-to-RFAB migration and the NE5532/LM833/MC33078 consolidation onto the RC4580 die. The Rev E change list was not captured. The datasheet feature bullet says 'Operating voltage ±2 V to ±18 V', while TI's product page uses 'Dual, 32-V' / '±2 V to ±16 V' wording; which of these changed, and when, is unconfirmed. - **Audio impact:** Unknown: no measurements of pre- versus post-2024 RC4580 were found.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - headline specs appear unchanged. Check abs-max and ESD in SLOS412E before relying on older margins.
- **Confidence:** low

### Second-source difference: Texas Instruments vs New JRC / Nisshinbo, April 2003 (TI RC4580 introduced, SLOS412) to present

TI's RC4580 is a pin- and function-compatible second source. No evidence was found that it is a JRC die, and its GBW is lower. Its other headline figures match the NJM4580 (±2 to ±18 V, 0.8 µVrms, 5 V/µs, 0.0005% THD). Input polarity: the NJM4580 is PNP per NJR-labelled SPICE macromodels and Japanese notes. For TI's RC4580 it is disputed (Headphonesty says NPN; Cabot/diyAudio say PNP). Since the 2024-2026 consolidation, the TI RC4580 die also ships as TI NE5532/LM833/MC33078.

- **Affected:** RC4580, RC4580-Q1, NJM4580
- **How to tell:** Tell them apart by part number and logo: TI RC4580 (orderables RC4580ID/IDR/IP/IPWR) versus JRC/NJR/Nisshinbo NJM4580, often called JRC4580 after its marking.
- **Audio impact:** No comparative listening test or measurement of NJM4580 versus RC4580 was found. The reputation (Soomal, Japanese community) was earned by the JRC part.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - drop-in pinout and similar supply range, with slightly lower GBW on the TI part.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Gain bandwidth product (typ) | 15 MHz (NJM4580, f = 10 kHz) | 12 MHz (TI RC4580) |
