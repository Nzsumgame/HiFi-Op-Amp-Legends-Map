# NJM4580 / JRC4580 / RC4580

**Bipolar dual audio op-amp (TI RC4580 second source)** · New JRC (before 2003–2021) → Nisshinbo Micro Devices (2022–present) · second sources: Texas Instruments · Tier A (legend / revision hazard)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** TI moved NE5532, LM833 and MC33078 onto the RC4580 die (E2E thread; Rich Cabot via Hackaday: 'they just relabeled a 4580'). (+2 more) [Details](#silicon-changes)

*Japan's cheap standard audio op-amp, found in countless sound cards and DACs; Soomal: neutral, above the NE5532.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | THD+N | Supply range | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.8 µVrms RIAA typ (D-rank ≤1.4); RC4580 6.5 nV/√Hz | 15 MHz (NJM4580); 12 MHz (RC4580) | 5 V/µs typ (both) | 0.0005% THD typ (both) | ±2 V to ±18 V; RC4580 page: ±2 to ±16 V (32 V) | 6 mA typ, 9 mA max (NJM4580) |

## Why it's rated

Asia's default 'standard audio op-amp'. Soomal finds it in many sound cards, speakers and DACs: neutral and plain ('平白如水'), high-resolution but characterless, and better than the NE5532. Japanese hobbyists call it fast, smooth and easy to listen to. Others rank it below the NE5532 or call it average. Its ±2 V minimum suits USB-powered DIY.

**Described as:** neutral, plain (平白如水), high resolution (解析力高), not warm (vs OP275), adaptable but characterless, fast, smooth, low noise, mellow; to some, veiled, flat, good HF extension, 'somewhat edgy' (minority view)

**Used in:** Sound-card outputs and inputs (TerraTec, TempoTec, Musiland), Active speakers, headphone amps and DACs (Musiland, TempoTec), Japanese CD players and AV gear (ONKYO, DENON, PIONEER), USB-powered DIY DACs and headphone amps (±2 V minimum), Preamp, tone-control and active-filter stages (datasheet)

## Watch out

- Not high-end: nabe ranks it below the NE5532; 'better than NE5532' is Soomal's view alone.
- Often confused with the far cheaper, pin-compatible JRC4558 (Soomal).
- No current-noise spec; noise is only RIAA-weighted (RS 2.2 kΩ), with nV/√Hz shown as a graph.
- DIP NJM4580D and SIP NJM4580L are NRND; future DIP rolling may need SMD-to-DIP adapters.
- TI RC4580 is a different part (12 vs 15 MHz). Swings ±13.5 V into 2 kΩ; buffer low-impedance headphones.
- **Counterfeits:** No documented fakes; the plausible risk is 4558s relabelled as 4580s (Soomal notes the confusion). Buy authorised; check logo, font and date codes; distrust cheap NOS DIP lots. Bench check: ~15 MHz GBW, ~5 V/µs slew; 4558-class parts are slower.

## Silicon changes

### Die redesign: Texas Instruments, 2023-11 – 2026 · PCN 20231114002.1

TI moved NE5532, LM833 and MC33078 onto the RC4580 die (E2E thread; Rich Cabot via Hackaday: 'they just relabeled a 4580'). Headphonesty reports failures on ±22 V-class rails, removed input diodes and halved HBM ESD. RC4580 is the donor, so little changes for it; JRC NJM4580 is unaffected.

- **Affected:** RC4580 (donor die; TI RC4580ID/IDR/IP/IPWR), NE5532 / NE5532A / SA5532 / SA5532A (TI), LM833 (TI), MC33078 (TI)
- **How to tell:** NE5532: SLOS075K (Dec 2025) is new silicon, SLOS075J (Jan 2015) legacy. RC4580: no marker; SLOS412E (Nov 2024) changes unknown. SFAB → RFAB, 2024–2025.
- **Audio impact:** None documented for RC4580. Post-2024 TI NE5532, LM833 and MC33078 behave like RC4580-class parts: 5 V/µs, ±18 V max, no input diodes.
- **Drop-in risk:** low for RC4580 (donor die); high for TI NE5532/LM833 users on ±18–22 V rails or needing input diodes.
- **Confidence:** high

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| NE5532 abs-max supply (SLOS075K) | ±22 V | ±18 V (RC4580 operating max: ±18 V) |
| NE5532 slew rate | 9 V/µs typ | 5 V/µs typ (RC4580: 5 V/µs) |
| NE5532 unity-gain bandwidth | 10 MHz typ | 12 MHz typ (RC4580: 12 MHz) |
| NE5532 HBM ESD | 2000 V | 1000 V |
| NE5532 supply current | 8 mA typ | 6 mA typ |
| NE5532 input differential protection diodes | back-to-back diodes across inputs | removed (RC4580 die has none) - Headphonesty / E2E summary |

### Datasheet respec: Texas Instruments, 2024-11 · SLOS412D → SLOS412E

Rev E came during the SFAB-to-RFAB move and die consolidation; its change list is unknown. RFAB PCNs 20240429005.1 (30 Apr 2024, die revision) and 20250129000.1 (3 Feb 2025, HPA8, 300 mm) exist, but RC4580 membership is unconfirmed. Datasheet says ±18 V; product page ±16 V.

- **Affected:** RC4580ID, RC4580IDR, RC4580IP, RC4580IPWR
- **How to tell:** Datasheet revision letter (SLOS412E). Headline specs look unchanged, so a bench test is unlikely to separate old from new.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown: no measurements of pre- versus post-2024 RC4580 found.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: headline specs appear unchanged; check SLOS412E abs-max and ESD before relying on older margins.
- **Confidence:** low

### Second-source difference: Texas Instruments vs New JRC / Nisshinbo, 2003-04–present · SLOS412

TI's RC4580 is a pin- and function-compatible second source, not shown to be a JRC die. GBW is lower (12 vs 15 MHz); other headline figures match. NJM4580 input is PNP per NJR SPICE models; RC4580's is disputed (Headphonesty NPN, Cabot/diyAudio PNP). Its die now also ships as NE5532/LM833/MC33078.

- **Affected:** RC4580, RC4580-Q1, NJM4580
- **How to tell:** Part number and logo: TI RC4580 (ID/IDR/IP/IPWR) versus JRC/NJR/Nisshinbo NJM4580, often called JRC4580 after its marking.
- **Audio impact:** No comparative listening test or measurement found; the reputation was earned by the JRC part.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: drop-in pinout and similar supply range; slightly lower GBW on the TI part.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Gain bandwidth product (typ) | 15 MHz (NJM4580, f = 10 kHz) | 12 MHz (TI RC4580) |
