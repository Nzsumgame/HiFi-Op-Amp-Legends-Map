# OPA827 (single low-noise precision JFET-input)

Burr-Brown (later TI) (c. 1989 onward (date not re-verified)) → Texas Instruments (Burr-Brown product line) (November 2006 (datasheet original date) to present) → Texas Instruments (2018 onward (dual OPA2828 since Dec 2022)) · Tier B (widely praised)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** At Rev G the datasheet moved from Mixed Status to Production Data. [Details](#silicon-changes)

*TI's 2006 single JFET op-amp (4 nV/√Hz, 22 MHz) that DIYers treat as the modern OPA627: a DAC I/V favourite, called 'successor to the legendary stone' in a Japanese review.*

| Slew rate | THD+N |
|---|---|
| 28 V/us typ, 20 V/us min | 0.00004 % (-128 dB) |

## Why it's rated

Hi-Fi DIYers treat it as the cheaper modern successor to the OPA627. They use it mostly as a DAC I/V converter and in socket-rolled line and headphone stages on SOIC-to-DIP adapters. In Xonar Essence ST/STX I/V discussions it is named with LT1361, LT1028 and OPA627 as the best I/V choices. Impressions conflict. English-forum posts gathered by an earlier sweep (not re-read) call it almost neutral, clean and more dynamic and less fickle than the OPA627. A diyAudio 'Best Sounding DUAL opamps' thread describes it as bass-centric, dark, warm and laid-back, with an impressive soundstage. In Japan two Zigsow reviews carry the titles 'The strongest lineage' and 'Successor to the legendary stone', the stone being the OPA627. TI's datasheet supports audio use (-128 dB THD+N at 3 Vrms/600 Ω; 'professional audio applications'). No ASR or Samuel Groner measurement was found.

**Described as:** almost neutral (lens note), very clean, clear and fluid (lens note), more dynamic and less fickle than OPA627 (lens note), bass-centric; deep, dark, warm, laid-back (diyAudio dual-opamps thread), impressive soundstage, transparent, orderly low-mids (Zigsow, lens note), more assertive low end than OPA2111 (one Japanese swap report)

**Used in:** DAC current-to-voltage (I/V) stage, e.g. socket rolling in Asus Xonar Essence ST/STX I/V positions, Portable DAC/headphone-amp op-amp swaps on DIP-8 adapters (e.g. a 2015 Fostex HP-A4 OPA827 swap blog), Line-level buffers and preamps, Engineering (datasheet application list): 16- to 18-bit ADC drivers, DAC output buffers, transimpedance amps, filters, precision ±10 V front ends, professional audio

## Watch out

- SMD only (SOIC-8 / VSSOP-8), so socket use needs a DIP-8 adapter. It is single channel, so a dual socket needs two parts on a 2-in-1 adapter.
- Not rail-to-rail: input CM range and output swing reach only to within 3 V of each rail. On ±5 V rails usable swing is only about ±2 V.
- 4.8 mA typ (5.2 mA max) quiescent current per channel, and it needs at least ±4 V (8 V total).
- 22 MHz GBW on adapter boards: keep supply decoupling at the chip (general practice, not a documented failure report).
- Listener impressions conflict (neutral/clean vs dark/warm/bass-centric). Several descriptors come from lens summaries, not first-hand re-reads.
- DC precision: a PhD thesis (PatrickBaus) saw non-symmetric cubic INL with OPA827 as an ADC buffer, and a 2019 blog shows higher 1/f noise than the OPA227 near DC.
- JFET bias current rises steeply with temperature (±5 nA max at 125 °C vs ±10 pA at 25 °C).
- Costs much more than common audio duals.
- **Counterfeits:** No OPA827-specific counterfeit or remarking report surfaced, but no search this run targeted counterfeits. Genuine TI markings per the SBOS376H package addendum (Oct 2013): SOIC-8 (D) reads 'OPA / 827 / A' plus logo and lot/date code; VSSOP-8 (DGK) reads 'NSP'. DGK lead finish may be NiPdAu or NiPdAuAg. TI has only ever listed the D and DGK packages, so a DIP-8 'OPA827' is not a genuine TI package. The OPA2827 dual never went beyond Product Preview, so any 'OPA2827' offered for sale is suspect. The part is often sold pre-soldered on adapters by marketplace sellers, and its benchmark OPA627 is among the most-faked audio op-amps. Buy from TI or authorised distributors, and check Iq (4.8 mA typ) and slew rate (at least 20 V/us).

## Silicon changes

### Datasheet respec: Texas Instruments, SBOS376G, 2012 (the prior pass gives February 2012; the Rev G document is copyright 2006-2012). Rev F was March 2009.

At Rev G the datasheet moved from Mixed Status to Production Data. TI changed the input bias current and input offset drift Features bullets and the drift/bias text in the Description, and deleted the planned high-grade option (and its footnote) from the ordering table. Offset, drift and bias limits were set, and SR and short-circuit current minimums were added. This was a datasheet change, most likely final characterisation. No PCN or die change was found, so it is a spec hazard, not a confirmed silicon change.

- **Affected:** OPA827AID, OPA827AIDR, OPA827AIDGKR, OPA827AIDGKT
- **How to tell:** None known on the part. Parts sold before 2012 were sold against the Rev F (Mixed Status) and earlier specs.
- **Audio impact:** Negligible for audio. It matters only for DC-precision designs that relied on preview limits or the dropped high grade.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - same pinout and packages, and no known die change
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Product status | Mixed Status (preview grade included) | Production Data |
| High-grade ordering option | listed in Package/Ordering table (Rev F) | deleted (Rev G) |
| Input offset voltage / drift / Ib limits | Rev F values (not retrieved); Features bullets differed | Rev G+: 150 uV max, 2.0 uV/°C max, ±10 pA max at 25 °C |
| Slew rate minimum | not specified | 20 V/us |
| Short-circuit current minimum | not specified | ±55 mA |
