# OPA827 (single low-noise precision JFET-input)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** JFET-input

*TI's 2006 single JFET op-amp (4 nV/√Hz, 22 MHz) that DIYers treat as the modern OPA627: a DAC I/V favourite, called 'successor to the legendary stone' in a Japanese review.*

**Technology:** JFET-input precision voltage-feedback op-amp, single channel only (the datasheet says 'OPA827 series', but only a single exists). Supply ±4 V to ±18 V (8 to 36 V total), abs max 40 V. Specified from -40 °C to +125 °C. Packages: SOIC-8 (D) and VSSOP/MSOP-8 (DGK). Input common-mode range and output swing are not rail-to-rail: both reach only to within 3 V of each rail. Before Rev G (Feb 2012) the datasheet also listed a high grade, OPA827I. There is no dual or quad OPA827. TI's later dual in this line is OPA2828, in production since Dec 2022.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Hi-Fi DIYers treat it as the cheaper modern successor to the OPA627. They use it mostly as a DAC I/V converter and in socket-rolled line and headphone stages on SOIC-to-DIP adapters. In Xonar Essence ST/STX I/V discussions it is named with LT1361, LT1028 and OPA627 as the best I/V choices. Impressions conflict. English-forum posts gathered by the discovery lens (not re-read) call it almost neutral, clean and fluid, and more dynamic and less fickle than the OPA627. A diyAudio 'Best Sounding DUAL opamps' thread describes it as bass-centric, deep, dark, warm and laid-back, with an impressive soundstage. In Japan two Zigsow reviews carry the titles 'The strongest lineage' (最強の系譜) and 'Successor to the legendary stone' (伝説の石の後継品). The 'legendary stone' is the OPA627. A Japanese swap report says going from OPA827 to OPA2111 reduced the low-end emphasis and widened the soundstage. TI's datasheet supports audio use (-128 dB THD+N at 3 Vrms/600 Ω; 'professional audio applications'). No ASR or Samuel Groner measurement was found.

**How people describe the sound:** almost neutral (lens note), very clean, clear and fluid (lens note), more dynamic and less fickle than OPA627 (lens note), bass-centric; deep, dark, warm, laid-back (diyAudio dual-opamps thread), impressive soundstage, transparent, orderly low-mids (Zigsow, lens note), more assertive low end than OPA2111 (one Japanese swap report)

**Typical uses:** DAC current-to-voltage (I/V) stage, e.g. socket rolling in Asus Xonar Essence ST/STX I/V positions, Portable DAC/headphone-amp op-amp swaps on DIP-8 adapters (e.g. the PON備忘録 blog's Fostex HP-A4 OPA827 swap, 2015), Line-level buffers and preamps, Engineering (datasheet application list): ADC drivers, DAC output buffers, transimpedance amps, PLL filters, integrators, active filters, seismic and test equipment

**Caveats:**

- SMD only (SOIC-8 / VSSOP-8), so socket use needs a DIP-8 adapter. It is single channel, so a dual socket needs two parts on a 2-in-1 adapter.
- Not rail-to-rail: input CM range and output swing reach only to within 3 V of each rail. On ±5 V rails usable swing is only about ±2 V, so it is a poor choice for low-voltage portable gear.
- 4.8 mA typ (5.2 mA max) quiescent current per channel, and it needs at least ±4 V (8 V total).
- 22 MHz GBW on adapter boards: keep supply decoupling at the chip. This is general practice, not a documented failure report.
- Listener impressions conflict (neutral/clean vs dark/warm/bass-centric), the usual op-amp-rolling subjectivity caveat. Several descriptors come from discovery-lens summaries, not first-hand re-reads.
- DC precision: a PhD thesis (PatrickBaus) found that OPA827 and ADA4625-1 as ADC buffers gave a non-symmetric cubic INL, likely due to lower JFET open-loop gain. A 2019 blog post (Furrah) shows higher 1/f noise than the bipolar OPA227 near DC.
- JFET bias current rises steeply with temperature (±5 nA max at 125 °C vs ±10 pA at 25 °C).
- Costs much more than common audio duals.

## Counterfeits

No OPA827-specific counterfeit or remarking report was found. Web search was unavailable this run, so this is absence of evidence. Genuine TI markings per the SBOS376H package addendum (Oct 2013): SOIC-8 (D) reads 'OPA / 827 / A' plus logo and lot/date code; VSSOP-8 (DGK) reads 'NSP'. DGK lead finish is listed as NiPdAu or NiPdAuAg, so lead colour can legitimately differ between lots. TI has only ever listed the D and DGK packages, so a DIP-8 chip marked OPA827 is not a genuine TI package. Genuine DIP-socket use is always an SOIC or MSOP on an adapter. Risk context: the part is often sold pre-soldered on adapters by marketplace sellers, and its benchmark OPA627 is among the most-faked audio op-amps. Buy from TI or authorised distributors. Check that markings are consistent across a batch and that Iq (4.8 mA typ, 5.2 mA max) and slew rate (at least 20 V/us, 28 typ) are plausible.

## Related parts and alternatives

TI OPA828 (single) / OPA2828 (dual, DGN only): TI's declared 'next generation OPA627 and OPA827' (SBOS671), 4 nV/√Hz, 45 MHz, 150 V/us, TI OPA627 (Burr-Brown Difet): the predecessor benchmark, TI OPA1641 / OPA1642 / OPA1644: lower-cost TI audio JFET-input single/dual/quad, TI OPA140 / OPA2140 / OPA4140: lower-power precision JFET-input family with dual/quad options (specs not re-verified here), ADI ADA4627-1 / ADA4625-1: JFET-input precision alternatives; the thesis tested ADA4625-1 alongside OPA827
