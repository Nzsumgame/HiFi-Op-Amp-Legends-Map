# OPA827 (single low-noise precision JFET-input)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** JFET-input

*TI's 2006 single JFET op-amp (4 nV/√Hz, 22 MHz) that DIYers treat as the modern OPA627: a DAC I/V favourite, called 'successor to the legendary stone' in a Japanese review.*

**Technology:** JFET-input precision voltage-feedback op-amp. Supply ±4 V to ±18 V (8 to 36 V total), abs max 40 V. Specified from -40 °C to +125 °C. Packages: SOIC-8 (D) and VSSOP/MSOP-8 (DGK). Input common-mode range and output swing are not rail-to-rail: both reach only to within 3 V of each rail. Only the single ever reached production. Early SBOS376 revisions (2006 to 2008) were titled 'OPA827, OPA2827' and announced a dual OPA2827 as Product Preview. That is why the datasheet says 'OPA827 series' and carried 'Mixed Status'. The 2013 orderables list has no OPA2827, and no evidence was found that it ever shipped. Before Rev G (Feb 2012) the datasheet also listed a high grade, OPA827I. TI's later dual in this line is OPA2828, in production since Dec 2022.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (2 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Hi-Fi DIYers treat it as the cheaper modern successor to the OPA627. They use it mostly as a DAC I/V converter and in socket-rolled line and headphone stages on SOIC-to-DIP adapters. In Xonar Essence ST/STX I/V discussions it is named with LT1361, LT1028 and OPA627 as the best I/V choices. Impressions conflict. English-forum posts gathered by an earlier sweep (not re-read) call it almost neutral, clean and more dynamic and less fickle than the OPA627. A diyAudio 'Best Sounding DUAL opamps' thread describes it as bass-centric, dark, warm and laid-back, with an impressive soundstage. In Japan two Zigsow reviews carry the titles 'The strongest lineage' and 'Successor to the legendary stone', the stone being the OPA627. TI's datasheet supports audio use (-128 dB THD+N at 3 Vrms/600 Ω; 'professional audio applications'). No ASR or Samuel Groner measurement was found.

**How people describe the sound:** almost neutral (lens note), very clean, clear and fluid (lens note), more dynamic and less fickle than OPA627 (lens note), bass-centric; deep, dark, warm, laid-back (diyAudio dual-opamps thread), impressive soundstage, transparent, orderly low-mids (Zigsow, lens note), more assertive low end than OPA2111 (one Japanese swap report)

**Typical uses:** DAC current-to-voltage (I/V) stage, e.g. socket rolling in Asus Xonar Essence ST/STX I/V positions, Portable DAC/headphone-amp op-amp swaps on DIP-8 adapters (e.g. a 2015 Fostex HP-A4 OPA827 swap blog), Line-level buffers and preamps, Engineering (datasheet application list): 16- to 18-bit ADC drivers, DAC output buffers, transimpedance amps, filters, precision ±10 V front ends, professional audio

**Caveats:**

- SMD only (SOIC-8 / VSSOP-8), so socket use needs a DIP-8 adapter. It is single channel, so a dual socket needs two parts on a 2-in-1 adapter.
- Not rail-to-rail: input CM range and output swing reach only to within 3 V of each rail. On ±5 V rails usable swing is only about ±2 V.
- 4.8 mA typ (5.2 mA max) quiescent current per channel, and it needs at least ±4 V (8 V total).
- 22 MHz GBW on adapter boards: keep supply decoupling at the chip (general practice, not a documented failure report).
- Listener impressions conflict (neutral/clean vs dark/warm/bass-centric). Several descriptors come from lens summaries, not first-hand re-reads.
- DC precision: a PhD thesis (PatrickBaus) saw non-symmetric cubic INL with OPA827 as an ADC buffer, and a 2019 blog shows higher 1/f noise than the OPA227 near DC.
- JFET bias current rises steeply with temperature (±5 nA max at 125 °C vs ±10 pA at 25 °C).
- Costs much more than common audio duals.

## Counterfeits

No OPA827-specific counterfeit or remarking report surfaced, but no search this run targeted counterfeits. Genuine TI markings per the SBOS376H package addendum (Oct 2013): SOIC-8 (D) reads 'OPA / 827 / A' plus logo and lot/date code; VSSOP-8 (DGK) reads 'NSP'. DGK lead finish may be NiPdAu or NiPdAuAg. TI has only ever listed the D and DGK packages, so a DIP-8 'OPA827' is not a genuine TI package. The OPA2827 dual never went beyond Product Preview, so any 'OPA2827' offered for sale is suspect. The part is often sold pre-soldered on adapters by marketplace sellers, and its benchmark OPA627 is among the most-faked audio op-amps. Buy from TI or authorised distributors, and check Iq (4.8 mA typ) and slew rate (at least 20 V/us).
