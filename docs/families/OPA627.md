# OPA627 / OPA637 (Burr-Brown Difet precision JFET single; OPA637 decompensated, G>=5)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** JFET-input

*Burr-Brown's Difet JFET single, long the 'king' of op-amp rolling, prized for detail and a warm, smooth sound, with pA bias current and 4.5 nV/rtHz noise.*

**Technology:** JFET-input single op-amp on Burr-Brown's Difet (dielectrically isolated) process. The OPA627 is unity-gain stable. The OPA637 is decompensated and stable at gains of 5 or more; a Burr-Brown part catalogue describes it as 'Stable in gain >=5'. Burr-Brown SPICE macromodels are dated October 1990. According to the discovery run, TI's SBOS165 Rev B (2024) and later datasheets drop the 'Difet' wording and retitle the part 'Precision, High-Speed JFET'. This fact-check pass did not re-verify that.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (2 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Widely treated as the benchmark audiophile JFET single that later JFET parts are compared against. It is called the 'king' on English-language forums and 'legendary' (伝説の石) in Japanese reviews; neither label was re-verified in this pass. Soomal (China) groups the OPA627 and OPA637 with Burr-Brown's DiFET parts as warm-leaning, with solid, full mids, strong dynamics and an open, spacious sound, and says they resolve high frequencies better than the older OPA111/OPA2107. Soomal rates the OPA637 slightly above the OPA627. Critics note that blind tests, such as Leckerton's (not re-verified), do not show it clearly beating cheaper parts like the AD8610.

**How people describe the sound:** detailed / high resolution (better HF resolution than OPA111/OPA2107 per Soomal), warm-leaning, solid full mids, strong dynamics, spacious (偏暖风格，中频扎实饱满，整体动态出色，声音宽松大气; Soomal 2016), warm, easy to listen to (声音温暖耐听; Soomal AE-9 op-amp swap, 2020), smooth, dark, mellow 'Burr-Brown sound' (forum folklore), 柔美 (soft, graceful; DiyEden, not re-verified), transparent, dynamic, long decay (JP blog, not re-verified)

**Typical uses:** DAC post-filter / I/V stages. Soomal reports that the HiFiMAN HM-901 uses an OPA627 with an OPA2107 in the low-pass filter after the ES9018., Headphone amp cards: HiFiMAN IEM card (2x OPA627AU + BUF634AU), balanced card (4x OPA627AU + BUF634AU), Minibox card (P-suffix OPA627 + BUF634), and 'gold Minibox' (2x TO-99 OPA637BM + 8x BUF634), all per Soomal., line buffers and preamps, phono and high-impedance inputs (pA bias), op-amp rolling in sound cards and DACs (for example the Creative AE-9, per Soomal 2020) via single-to-dual adapters, transimpedance / photodiode and instrumentation front ends (the OPA637 appears in Art of Electronics 3rd ed. TIA comparisons)

**Caveats:**

- It is a single op-amp only, so dual sockets need an adapter board, and adapter-mounted AUs are common.
- The OPA637 is unstable below a noise gain of about 5. Do not drop it into unity-gain buffer sockets.
- Quiescent current is up to 7.5 mA per amp, which means heat and battery drain in portables.
- It is one of the most counterfeited audiophile op-amps, so assume marketplace parts are fake.
- The DIP (AP/BP) and TO-99 (AM/BM/SM) versions are reported discontinued or NRND, leaving only the SOIC AU/BU from TI (per discovery notes).
- Per diyAudio 2024 (not re-verified), the current TI datasheet shows offset trim only on the TO-99, and molded packages list pins 1/5 as NC.
- Soomal notes that some listeners hear the OPA627 as 'both ends tilted up' (两头翘, bright at the frequency extremes), and attributes this to circuit design or to country of origin.
- Soomal folklore holds that country of origin matters: US-made is best, Taiwan next, and SE Asia is less balanced with weaker dynamics. No measurements support this.
- A/B-grade differences are reportedly inaudible (DiyEden), and blind tests reportedly show it is not clearly better than the AD8610 (Leckerton). Neither was re-verified.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Texas Instruments: 2024-2025 datasheet revisions (SBOS165B Apr 2024, SBOS165C…

Per the discovery run, TI's 2024 datasheet rewrite deleted the 'Difet' process wording, added a new B-grade SOIC (OPA627BU) and changed the internal schematic figure. A diyAudio thread reports that molded parts lost the offset-trim pin function. The thread title groups the OPA132 and OPA627; TI reportedly explained the OPA132 case as a more precisely trimmed die, which was not verified here. These are documentation-level changes. They may indicate a new die or process under the same part number, but TI has not confirmed a silicon change, no PCN was found, and none of this was re-verified in this pass.

- **When:** 2024-2025 datasheet revisions (SBOS165B Apr 2024, SBOS165C Jan 2025, per discovery run); after June 2022, when TI models still cited Rev A
- **Affected:** OPA627AU, OPA627BU, OPA637AU
- **How to tell old from new:** The datasheet is SBOS165B/C (titled 'JFET', not 'Difet'), and the OPA627BU orderable exists only from 2024. Pins 1/5 are reportedly marked NC on molded packages, while the TO-99 keeps trim. No date-code cut-over or PCN was found.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. No before/after measurements were found. Circuits that null offset through pins 1/5 lose that adjustment. Whether noise, distortion or sound differ from Burr-Brown-era parts is untested.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium - Offset-trim circuits may not work on new molded parts. The electrical spec deltas between Rev A and Rev C were not captured.
- **Confidence:** low

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Pins 1/5 (SOIC/DIP) | Offset Trim (SBOS165A) | NC on molded packages (Rev B/C, per diyAudio 2024; not verified) |
| Process description in datasheet | Precision High-Speed Difet | Precision, High-Speed JFET (Difet references deleted; per discovery run) |
| SOIC grades | AU only | AU and BU (BU preview in Rev B, production in Rev C; per discovery run) |

### 2. Burr-Brown -> Texas Instruments: after 2000 (TI acquisition); exact transfer date unknown

Soomal (2016) states that Taiwan-made OPA637/OPA627 are considered second only to US-made, and more balanced with better dynamics than SE-Asian-made parts. Japanese sellers reportedly claim that Burr-Brown-era parts use a different process rule and thicker bond wires (maimai-audio, not re-verified). No vendor document, PCN or measurement supports a die change. This is folklore.

- **When:** after 2000 (TI acquisition); exact transfer date unknown
- **Affected:** OPA627AP, OPA627BP, OPA627AU, OPA627AM, OPA627BM, OPA637AP, OPA637BP, OPA637BM
- **How to tell old from new:** Collector folklore: a Burr-Brown logo, a pre-2000 date code, and US or Taiwan country of origin rather than SE Asian assembly marks.
- **Audio impact:** Claimed only. Soomal suggests that the 'both ends tilted up' (两头翘) sound some hear may be a design issue or an origin issue. Unverified.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - Same pinout and datasheet specs. The practical risk is that 'vintage BB' listings attract remarked fakes.
- **Confidence:** low

## Counterfeits

One of the most counterfeited audiophile op-amps. In April 2014, ZeptoBars decapped a $2.7 'OPA627' bought from China/eBay (habr 218571; Hackaday 2014-04-10 says about $2, against at least $15 for genuine parts). It contained an Analog Devices AD744 die marked 'AD' and 'B744'. The genuine die shows at least 4 laser-trimmed resistors and the fake shows 1. The AD744 settles in a similar 500 ns to 0.01%, but has about 3x the noise and 0.5 mV offset against 0.1 mV. OPA602 and AD711-class donor dies are also reported, but this pass did not verify them. Community checks from Head-Fi and diyAudio (not re-verified): measure the pin 1-5 (trim) resistance and the quiescent current, which should be about 7-7.5 mA; fakes also fail square-wave and slew tests. Maimai-audio, a single blog source, lists OPA627AU lot codes A3L, AEB, AKG, D5L, K4D, LAE, LDE, LPS, MDE and T3K as almost all fake. Soomal (2016) makes several points. A genuine Taiwan- or US-made TO-99 OPA637BM costs ¥250 or more, so ones selling for tens of yuan are fake. Every 'custom metal-can' version is fake. SM-suffix parts are very rare. Some fakes put an ordinary PDIP chip inside a metal shell, and metal cans have even been invented for parts never made that way (for example the AD797). A metal can that looks new, or whose origin marking has changed, should be treated as suspect. Soomal (2021) adds that genuine OPA637BM now come only from second-hand, pulled-part channels. Because the DIP and TO-99 are reportedly discontinued, any 'new' OPA627AP/BP or AM/BM/SM from a marketplace is suspect. Buy the SOIC AU/BU from TI or an authorized distributor. A genuine OPA627AU on a DIP adapter is the normal DIP substitute.

## Related parts and alternatives

## Open questions

- This fact-check pass had no web search: the shared budget was exhausted (200/200) before its first query. All checking used GitHub code search (about 30 queries). Most datasheet mirror URLs, forum threads, E2E threads and all Rev B/C details therefore remain unverified.
- The SBOS165A date conflict is resolved in favour of October 2015, per TI PSpice model headers for both parts (June 2022). The 'Revision A (April 2015)' reading from the discovery run's summary of the Rev C history table is probably wrong, but the Rev C table itself should be checked.
- Does the SBOS165C header show 'August 2000' as the first-issue date? Rev A shows September 2000.
- Confirm in the Rev B/C pin-function table that the D (SOIC) package pins 1/5 are NC, and check the P (DIP) and TO-99 packages.
- Did TI move the OPA627/637 to a new die or process around 2024? Look for a TI PCN or an E2E statement.
- Capture the electrical-spec deltas between SBOS165A and SBOS165C: e_n at 1 kHz, Vos, Ib, Iq typ/max, GBW, slew, output current and THD+N.
- The OPA637 GBW (80 MHz) is still unverified. The OPA637 slew (135 V/us) is corroborated only by Burr-Brown-era Electronics Workbench model parameters.
- Third-party SPICE catalogue headers give 5.4 nV/rtHz (OPA627) and 5.2 nV/rtHz (OPA637) without a stated frequency. Check whether these are the 1 kHz datasheet typicals.
- Is there an OPA637BU? Is the OPA637AU still active? What did E2E thread 1386960 conclude about whether the OPA627AU is end of life?
- Exact TI discontinuation dates and notices for the OPA627AP/BP, AM/BM/SM and the OPA637 DIP/TO-99.
- Burr-Brown PDS number and the original launch-year datasheet: the macromodels prove the part existed by October 1990, and the c.1989 launch is unverified.
- No Audio Science Review or Samuel Groner distortion measurements of the OPA627 were captured.
- Archive seeds of the form ti.com/lit/ds/sbos165{,a,b,c}/..., www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/opa637.pdf and ti.com/product/OPA637 are INFERRED patterns. focus.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/opa627.pdf is now confirmed from a 2004 MIT OCW page.
- The revision assignments of the distributor and third-party copies are inferred from title wording or header features, not from a printed revision code.
