# OPA627 / OPA637 (Burr-Brown Difet precision JFET single; OPA637 decompensated, G>=5)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** JFET-input

*Burr-Brown's Difet JFET single, long called the 'king' of op-amp rolling and prized for detail and a smooth sound, with pA bias and 4.5 nV/rtHz noise.*

**Technology:** JFET-input single op-amp. The Burr-Brown datasheet describes a high-speed, dielectrically isolated complementary NPN/PNP process with laser-trimmed Difet input circuitry. The OPA627 is unity-gain stable. The OPA637 is decompensated and stable at gains of 5 or more. TI's current SBOS165C (Jan 2025) retitles the family 'OPA6x7 Precision, High-Speed JFET Operational Amplifiers', and its revision history records that the Difet references were deleted.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (2 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Widely treated as the benchmark audiophile JFET single that later JFET parts are compared against. It is called the 'king' on English-language forums and 'legendary' in Japanese reviews. Soomal (China) groups the OPA627/637 with Burr-Brown's DiFET parts as warm-leaning, with full mids, strong dynamics and a spacious sound, and says they resolve HF better than the OPA111/OPA2107. Soomal also rates the OPA637 slightly above the OPA627. Critics such as Leckerton note that in blind listening it does not clearly beat cheaper parts like the AD8610. None of these characterizations were re-verified in this pass.

**How people describe the sound:** detailed / high resolution (Soomal), warm-leaning, full mids, dynamic, spacious (Soomal 2016), warm, easy to listen to (Soomal AE-9 swap, 2020), smooth, dark 'Burr-Brown sound' (forum folklore)

**Typical uses:** DAC post-filter / I/V stages (HiFiMAN HM-901 uses an OPA627 with an OPA2107 in the LPF, per Soomal), Headphone amp cards: HiFiMAN IEM, balanced and Minibox cards pair it with the BUF634, per Soomal, line buffers and preamps, phono and high-impedance inputs (pA bias), op-amp rolling in sound cards and DACs via single-to-dual adapters, transimpedance / photodiode and instrumentation front ends

**Caveats:**

- It is a single op-amp only, so dual sockets need an adapter board.
- The OPA637 is unstable below a noise gain of about 5. Do not drop it into unity-gain buffer sockets.
- Quiescent current is up to about 7.5 mA per amp, which means heat and battery drain.
- It is one of the most counterfeited audiophile op-amps.
- The DIP versions are obsolete, and in 2024 TI said the SOIC AU might not be packaged for a while (E2E 1386960).
- The current TI datasheet shows pins 1/5 as NC on the molded packages, and offset trim applies to the TO-99 only.
- Soomal folklore that country of origin matters (US > Taiwan > SE Asia) is not backed by any measurements.
- A/B-grade differences are reportedly inaudible, and blind tests reportedly show no clear win over the AD8610. Neither claim was re-verified.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Texas Instruments: SBOS165B (April 2024) / SBOS165C (January 2025); after…

TI's 2024-2025 rewrite deleted the 'Difet' wording, added a new B-grade SOIC (OPA627BU), and dropped offset-trim pins on the molded packages. For the sister OPA132, which also has trim pins changed to NC, TI E2E 1517005 attributes the change to higher-precision manufacturing that makes trim unnecessary. diyAudio users cite 'newer, more efficient manufacturing processes'. In 2024, TI E2E 1386960 said the AU was temporarily not being packaged while BU and TO-can stock was coming. That points to a new die or trim process under the same part numbers, but TI has not confirmed a silicon change for the OPA627 and no PCN was found.

- **When:** SBOS165B (April 2024) / SBOS165C (January 2025); after June 2022, when TI models still cited Rev A
- **Affected:** OPA627AU, OPA627BU, OPA637AU
- **How to tell old from new:** New documentation is SBOS165B/C, titled 'OPA6x7 ... JFET'. The OPA627BU orderable exists only from 2024-2025. In the current pin table, molded-package pins 1/5 are NC, and the datasheet says the external offset pot applies to TO-99 only. No date-code cut-over or PCN was found.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. No before/after measurements were found. Circuits that null offset through pins 1/5 lose that adjustment on new molded parts.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium - Offset-trim circuits may not work on new SOIC parts, and the electrical spec deltas between Rev A and Rev C were not captured.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Pins 1/5 (SOIC/DIP) | Offset Trim (SBOS165A) | NC; trim documented for TO-99 only (current SBOS165) |
| Process description | Precision High-Speed Difet | Precision, High-Speed JFET (Difet references deleted) |
| SOIC grades | AU only | AU and BU (BU production in Rev C, Jan 2025) |

### 2. Burr-Brown -> Texas Instruments: after 2000 (TI acquisition); exact transfer date unknown

Soomal (2016) ranks Taiwan-made parts second only to US-made, and ahead of SE-Asian-made. Japanese sellers claim Burr-Brown-era parts use a different process rule and thicker bond wires. No vendor document, PCN or measurement supports this. It is folklore.

- **When:** after 2000 (TI acquisition); exact transfer date unknown
- **Affected:** OPA627AP, OPA627BP, OPA627AU, OPA627AM, OPA627BM, OPA637AP, OPA637BP, OPA637BM
- **How to tell old from new:** Collector folklore: a Burr-Brown logo, a pre-2000 date code, and US or Taiwan origin.
- **Audio impact:** Claimed only. Unverified.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - Same pinout and specs. The practical risk is that 'vintage BB' listings attract remarked fakes.
- **Confidence:** low

## Counterfeits

One of the most counterfeited audiophile op-amps. In 2014, ZeptoBars decapped a $2.7 'OPA627' from China/eBay and found an Analog Devices AD744 die (marked 'AD'/'B744'). The genuine die shows at least 4 laser-trimmed resistors and the fake shows 1; the AD744 has about 3x the noise and 0.5 mV offset against 0.1 mV. OPA602 and AD711-class donor dies are also reported but were not verified. Community checks (Head-Fi, diyAudio, not re-verified) are quiescent current of about 7-7.5 mA plus square-wave and slew tests. The pin 1-5 trim-resistance check is unreliable on new TI molded parts, whose pins 1/5 are NC. Maimai-audio (a single blog) lists OPA627AU lot codes A3L, AEB, AKG, D5L, K4D, LAE, LDE, LPS, MDE and T3K as almost all fake. Soomal makes several points: TO-99 OPA637BM selling for tens of yuan are fake; every 'custom metal-can' version is fake; some fakes put an ordinary PDIP chip inside a metal shell; and SM parts are very rare. The DIP versions are obsolete, so any 'new' OPA627AP/BP is suspect. Buy the SOIC AU/BU from TI or an authorized distributor.
