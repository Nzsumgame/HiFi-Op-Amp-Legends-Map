# OPA627 / OPA637 (Burr-Brown Difet precision JFET single; OPA637 decompensated, G>=5)

Burr-Brown (by 1990 (launch c.1989 unverified) - 2000) → Texas Instruments (2000 - present) → Rochester Electronics (post-discontinuation) · Tier A (legend / revision hazard)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** TI's 2024-2025 rewrite deleted all 'Difet' references, added a B-grade SOIC (OPA627BU), and turned the molded packages' offset-trim pins into NC. [Details](#silicon-changes)

*Burr-Brown's Difet JFET single, long called the 'king' of op-amp rolling and prized for detail and a smooth sound, with pA bias and 4.5 nV/rtHz noise.*

| Slew rate | Supply range |
|---|---|
| 55 V/us (OPA627); 135 V/us (OPA637) | +/-4.5 V to +/-18 V |

## Why it's rated

Widely treated as the benchmark audiophile JFET single that later JFET parts are compared against. It is called the 'king' on English-language forums and 'legendary' in Japanese reviews. Soomal (China) groups the OPA627/637 with Burr-Brown's DiFET parts as warm-leaning, with full mids, strong dynamics and a spacious sound, and says they resolve HF better than the OPA111/OPA2107. Soomal also rates the OPA637 slightly above the OPA627. Critics such as Leckerton note that in blind listening it does not clearly beat cheaper parts like the AD8610. None of these characterizations were re-verified in this pass.

**Described as:** detailed / high resolution (Soomal), warm-leaning, full mids, dynamic, spacious (Soomal 2016), warm, easy to listen to (Soomal AE-9 swap, 2020), smooth, dark 'Burr-Brown sound' (forum folklore)

**Used in:** DAC post-filter / I/V stages (HiFiMAN HM-901 uses an OPA627 with an OPA2107 in the LPF, per Soomal), Headphone amp cards: HiFiMAN IEM, balanced and Minibox cards pair it with the BUF634, per Soomal, line buffers and preamps, phono and high-impedance inputs (pA bias), op-amp rolling in sound cards and DACs via single-to-dual adapters, transimpedance / photodiode and instrumentation front ends

## Watch out

- It is a single op-amp only, so dual sockets need an adapter board.
- The OPA637 is unstable below a noise gain of about 5. Do not drop it into unity-gain buffer sockets.
- Quiescent current is up to about 7.5 mA per amp, which means heat and battery drain.
- It is one of the most counterfeited audiophile op-amps.
- The DIP versions are obsolete, and in 2024 TI said the SOIC AU might not be packaged for a while (E2E 1386960).
- The current TI datasheet shows pins 1/5 as NC on the molded packages, and offset trim applies to the TO-99 only.
- Soomal folklore that country of origin matters (US > Taiwan > SE Asia) is not backed by any measurements.
- A/B-grade differences are reportedly inaudible, and blind tests reportedly show no clear win over the AD8610. Neither claim was re-verified.
- **Counterfeits:** One of the most counterfeited audiophile op-amps. In 2014, ZeptoBars decapped a $2.7 'OPA627' from China/eBay and found an Analog Devices AD744 die (marked 'AD'/'B744'). The genuine die shows at least 4 laser-trimmed resistors and the fake shows 1; the AD744 has about 3x the noise and 0.5 mV offset against 0.1 mV. OPA602 and AD711-class donor dies are also reported but were not verified. Community checks (Head-Fi, diyAudio, not re-verified) are quiescent current of about 7-7.5 mA plus square-wave and slew tests. The pin 1-5 trim-resistance check is unreliable on new TI molded parts, whose pins 1/5 are NC. Maimai-audio (a single blog) lists OPA627AU lot codes A3L, AEB, AKG, D5L, K4D, LAE, LDE, LPS, MDE and T3K as almost all fake. Soomal makes several points: TO-99 OPA637BM selling for tens of yuan are fake; every 'custom metal-can' version is fake; some fakes put an ordinary PDIP chip inside a metal shell; and SM parts are very rare. The DIP versions are obsolete, so any 'new' OPA627AP/BP is suspect. Buy the SOIC AU/BU from TI or an authorized distributor.

## Silicon changes

### Die redesign: Texas Instruments, SBOS165B (April 2024) / SBOS165C (January 2025, OPA627BU moved from preview to production); after June 2022, when TI models still cited Rev A

TI's 2024-2025 rewrite deleted all 'Difet' references, added a B-grade SOIC (OPA627BU), and turned the molded packages' offset-trim pins into NC. For the sister OPA132, TI E2E 1517005 attributes the same change to a 'new FAB change and design'; the diyAudio thread says trim is no longer functional from Rev C because the parts are made with higher precision (one poster speculates the pads are simply no longer bonded). This points to a new die or process under the same part numbers, but TI has not confirmed a silicon change for the OPA627 specifically and no PCN was found.

- **Affected:** OPA627AU, OPA627BU, OPA637AU
- **How to tell:** New documentation is SBOS165B/C, titled 'OPA6x7 Precision, High-Speed JFET Operational Amplifiers'. In the current pin table, molded-package pins 1/5 are NC, and external offset trim is documented for TO-99 only. The OPA627BU orderable exists only from 2024-2025. No PCN or date-code cut-over was found; BB-logo and pre-2024 TI parts keep functional trim pins.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. No before/after measurements were found. Circuits that null offset through pins 1/5 lose that adjustment on new molded parts. Impressions formed on Burr-Brown-era parts may not carry over.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium - offset-trim circuits do not work on new molded parts, and the electrical spec deltas between Rev A and Rev C were not captured
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Pins 1/5 (SOIC/DIP) | Offset Trim (SBOS165A) | NC; trim documented for TO-99 only (SBOS165B/C) |
| Process description | Precision High-Speed Difet | Precision, High-Speed JFET (Difet references deleted) |
| SOIC grades | AU only | AU and BU (BU production data in Rev C, Jan 2025) |

**Confirmed:**

- SBOS165C is current, with the header 'AUGUST 2000 - REVISED JANUARY 2025'. The Rev C history records the OPA627BU changing from preview to production and gives the Rev B date as April 2024 (https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/opa627.pdf).
