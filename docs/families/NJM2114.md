# NJM2114 / JRC2114 (dual low-noise bipolar, improved 5532-type)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** Japanese audio series (New JRC / Nisshinbo)

*JRC's drop-in NJM5532 replacement with faster slew, wider bandwidth and lower offset. Stock socketed op-amp on Creative X-Fi Titanium HD/ZxR/X7/AE-9 and ASUS Essence STX.*

**Technology:** Bipolar dual op-amp with NPN input. It is internally compensated and uses the standard 8-pin dual pinout with no compensation pins. The NJR datasheet says it 'could be replaced in application with NJM5532' and has superior slew rate, bandwidth and offset voltage compared with NJM5532. Mirror title: 'High Performance Low-Noise Dual Operational Amplifier'.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded: Unclassified).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A mid-tier Japanese audio dual, marketed by JRC as an improved NJM5532 and perceived that way. JP DIY reviews of NJM2114DD are mostly positive: a Zigsow review is titled 'same price class as NE5532 but better sound', and reviewers describe clear, firm bass and more clarity than NJM2068. Opinion is not unanimous: one JP comparison ranks NJM2114 (with NJM4580) C-rank, below NE5532, LM4562 and OPA2134 (B-rank). Internationally it is best known as the stock JRC2114(D) in the Creative X-Fi Titanium HD, Zx, ZxR, X7 and AE-9, the ASUS Xonar Essence STX, the Gigabyte Z170X-Gaming G1 and the TempoTec DAII Plus. Soomal considers it a neutral buffer/I-V part. No independent measurements were found.

**How people describe the sound:** 'Same price class as NE5532 but better sound' (Zigsow NJM2114DD review title), clear, firm bass with relatively subdued highs (JP NJM2114DD reviews, via search summary), not bass-heavy like NJM5532; drier than OPA2134; slightly more coloured than LME49860 (JP reviews, via search summary), 'higher-performance 2068: wins on information and clarity' (JP comparison, via search summary), 'JRC2114对声音影响较小': has relatively little effect on the sound (Soomal AE-9 review, 2020)

**Typical uses:** Creative Sound Blaster X-Fi Titanium HD (2010): socketed JRC2114D, presumed filter and I/V, with LME49710 singles (Soomal), Creative Sound Blaster ZxR (2013): socketed NJM2114D + LME49710 on front channels, SMD JRC2114 on others. Creative spec: 'Headphone Op-Amps (Swappable) NJM2114D', TPA6120A2 driver., Creative Sound Blaster Zx (2013) and Sound Blaster X7 (2015, JRC2114D + LME49710) (Soomal), Creative Sound Blaster AE-9 (2020): socketed JRC2114 buffer with NE5534 singles; SMD JRC2114 + L49720 on sub-channels (Soomal), ASUS Xonar Essence STX (2009): socketed 2114D used as the I/V converter (Soomal), E-MU 0202 USB (Soomal teardown, 2010); Gigabyte Z170X-Gaming G1 onboard audio (2015); TempoTec Fantasia DAII Plus DAC, two 2114D as I/V (2017), I/V stage in some Philips / Marantz CD players (head-fi opamp thread p.113; models unverified, low confidence), Drop-in NJM5532 / NJM2068 / NJM4580 swap in Japanese headphone amps, USB-DAC filters and cassette decks

**Caveats:**

- Soomal's AE-9 review (2020-04-15) says op-amps on Creative's EARLIER cards were already good, so swapping brought almost no improvement. It calls the AE-9's stock NE5534 singles puzzling, and advises that users wanting a different sound should upgrade the two singles (LME49710 for continuity) rather than the JRC2114.
- Mixed JP rankings: one comparison surfaced by search ranks it C-rank, below NE5532. The page was not pinned; the candidate is nabe.adiary.jp/opamp-compare.
- Availability: a 2023 JP blog reports that Nisshinbo made it a maintenance (pre-EOL, not for new designs) product. Marutsu has ended sales of NJM2114D and Mouser shows NJM2114L as obsolete. Expect shrinking authorised stock and more grey-market parts.
- No objective measurements (ASR, Groner, jh4vaj) were found. Treat it as a 5532-class bipolar NPN-input part: input bias current and source impedance matter, and with 13 MHz GBW and 15 V/µs it needs sensible decoupling and layout.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Unclassified: Nisshinbo Micro Devices (ex-New JRC), Reported 7 April 2023 (foxtango101 blog)

A Japanese audio blog reports that Nisshinbo designated several ex-JRC op-amps as maintenance products (保守品, i.e. planned for discontinuation), naming NJM2114 among them together with NJM4556A, NJM2082DD, NJM4560, NJM2041 and NJM2043. This is a lifecycle change, not a silicon change. One search summary also claimed NJM2068 and NJM4580 were affected, but the more specific summary of the same blog did not list them, so that claim is unconfirmed.

- **When:** Reported 7 April 2023 (foxtango101 blog)
- **Affected:** NJM2114 (package variants not captured)
- **How to tell old from new:** Nisshinbo's maintenance-product (保守品) list, searched with 'NJM' or the op-amp category, per the blog. Distributors (DigiKey, Mouser) still list NJM2114D and NJM2114M, and a later search found no explicit 'discontinued' flag on them.
- **Audio impact:** None. This is a supply and lifecycle warning only.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - no silicon change; future availability risk only, and which packages are affected was not captured.
- **Confidence:** low

## Counterfeits

No NJM2114-specific counterfeit or remark reports were found. 'JRC clones are common' remains an unsourced generic remark. 'JRC2114D' is the normal name derived from the genuine part's JRC-logo + '2114D' marking and does not by itself mean a clone. Grey-market risk is rising: Nisshinbo has put the part on its maintenance list, Marutsu has ended NJM2114D sales, and Mouser shows NJM2114L as obsolete. Marketplace listings exist, such as AliExpress item 3256808639958981, which is titled with every alias (NJM2114DD/NJM2114D/JRC2114DD/JRC2114D/2114DD/2114D) at once, and a Reverb 'JRC NJM2114D ... Improved NE5532, Lot of 6'. Prefer authorised channels (Mouser 513-NJM2114D / 513-NJM2114M, DigiKey, Akizuki, Keisei) and check that the marking and date code match the claimed DD grade.
