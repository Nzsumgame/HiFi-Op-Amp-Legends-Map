# LM4562 / LME49720 / LME49710 / LME49740 (National ultra-low-distortion bipolar audio op-amps)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*National's 2006-07 audio op-amp with 2.7 nV/rtHz noise and 0.00003% THD+N; the standard NE5532 'upgrade', with the metal-can HA versions as collector items.*

**Technology:** Bipolar input, voltage feedback, unity-gain stable; 55 MHz GBW, ±2.5 V to ±17 V supply. LM4562 (dual) and LME49720 (dual) are one die sold under two part numbers (per TI E2E). LME49710 is the single and LME49740 the quad of the same series. Inputs have differential protection diodes. Packages: SOIC (MA), PDIP (NA), TO-99 metal can (HA; dual and single only). Under TI PCN 20180308002 (March 2018), LM4562/LME49720 wafer production moved from a 150 mm line to TI's RFAB (Richardson, TX), on a newer process with a die-shrink redesign. Part numbers did not change.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded: Fab / process transfer).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Hi-Fi op-amp rollers and DIY builders treat this as the default 'better than NE5532' part. Its distortion measures near the limits of test equipment. NwAvGuy's 2011 op-amp measurements are often cited in its favour; the discovery sweep paraphrases him as saying it matches 'any elite part' (wording not re-checked). It is reported as a stock or socketed part in Asus Xonar and Creative Sound Blaster cards (exact part per card not re-checked), and it is widely used in Chinese DACs. Listeners call it neutral, clear, transparent and detailed, and sometimes 'clinical'. Japanese folklore holds that the LME49720 marking sounds brighter and the LM4562 bassier, although TI says the silicon is the same. Forum posters single out the TO-99 HA versions. All of these are subjective, unblinded reports.

**How people describe the sound:** neutral, clear / transparent, detailed, clean, tight bass, clinical / analytical (some listeners), 'too clear' on violins (diyAudio, comparing it with LME49723), LME49720: brighter, glossier; LM4562: bassier, denser (Japanese folklore, same die), cleaner treble than a discrete stage (Soomal)

**Typical uses:** NE5532/4580 replacement in CD players, DACs and sound cards, DAC I/V and output stages, headphone amplifier gain stages, phono and mic preamps (LME49710HA prized), pro-audio line stages (±17 V max), low-noise RF/SDR audio and IQ stages (seen in GitHub SDR projects)

**Caveats:**

- 55 MHz GBW: can oscillate in some sockets, adapters and layouts; needs good local decoupling
- Maximum supply is ±17 V (36 V abs max); for ±18 V to ±22 V rails use LME49860
- Differential input protection diodes limit use with large differential inputs (TI E2E 337068)
- Low-frequency (1/f / popcorn) noise questions raised on TI E2E 415907
- Start-up behaviour of LM4562/LME49710/LME49720 is discussed on the Pro Audio Design Forum (topic 641; details not re-checked)
- Sensitivity to air-coupled EM/RF fields reported for the LM4562/LME497x0 family (ASR thread 10687)
- Since 2018 (PCN 20180308002), LM4562/LME49720 use a die-shrink die from RFAB under the same part number. Old-vs-new sound or measurement claims are unverified.
- DIP and TO-99 stock on marketplaces is often counterfeit
- Pricing has often differed between the LM4562 and LME49720 despite identical silicon
- Sonic descriptors are forum impressions, not controlled listening tests

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Fab / process transfer: Texas Instruments, PCN 20180308002 dated 2018-03-09 (proposed first ship…

PCN 20180308002 is titled 'Transfer of select VIP3 devices from GFAB to DFAB (DL-LIN) Wafer Fab site'. It moved LM4562/LME49720 wafer fabrication from TI Greenock, Scotland (closing) to TI Dallas DL-LIN, keeping the same VIP3 bipolar process family. The move brought the parts back to Active after c.2015-2017 EOL and lifebuy scares. The diyAudio thread on it is titled 'LM4562 (aka LME49720) Fab moved from GB to US'. Forum posters also report a sharp price drop in early 2019. The PCN text found describes a fab-site transfer only. No die redesign and no datasheet change is documented. An earlier-pass claim of an SFAB-to-RFAB die-shrink redesign was a conflation with the NE5532-family PCN and has been removed (see dropped).

- **When:** PCN 20180308002 dated 2018-03-09 (proposed first ship 2018-06-09). Reissued as PCN 20180308002-001 dated 2018-03-26 (proposed first ship 2018-09-26). Stock from the new fab reached distribution around early 2019.
- **Affected:** LM4562 (SOIC MA/MAX confirmed; other orderables are on the PCN list, which was not seen in full), LME49720 (SOIC MA/MAX confirmed; other orderables are on the PCN list, which was not seen in full)
- **How to tell old from new:** The part number and marking are unchanged, so only the lot or date code identifies the fab. diyAudio posters say that date codes from 2019 onward come from the new Dallas fab. They also say lots starting 'JR' were fabbed in Greenock and assembled in Malaysia, which would mark pre-transfer stock (forum claim only). No die-revision letter or marking change was found. The PCN lists the affected orderables and first-ship dates, but the full list was not seen.
- **Audio impact:** No published spec change. No old-vs-new measurement comparison was found. One diyAudio report says 2019+ date-code parts from the new fab have not shown the popcorn (burst) noise seen in some earlier LM4562 lots. This is anecdotal, not systematically tested, and is plausibly a fab-cleanliness effect. Claims that LM4562 and LME49720 sound different are folklore: they are the same die under two part numbers (Hackaday 2026), and many such claims predate the transfer.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: same part number, pinout, process family and datasheet limits. A fab move can still shift unspecified behaviour such as 1/f or popcorn noise, RF susceptibility and overload recovery. No old-vs-new data exists.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Verification:** Two WebSearches confirmed the PCN 20180308002 title ('Transfer of select VIP3 devices from GFAB to DFAB (DL-LIN)') and the -001 first ship date of 2018-09-26. The wafer-size change is unconfirmed because the sources conflict. Four searches for an LM4562-specific SFAB-to-RFAB or 2024-2025 PCN found nothing, so the pass-1 and completeness-sweep entries were merged into this one.

## Other change notes (lifecycle, packaging, successors, lore)

### 1. Lifecycle: Texas Instruments, c.2015-2016: EOL/lifebuy notices (LM4562/LME49720 TO-99…

TI discontinued select devices sourced from GFAB because the Greenock site was closing (PDN 20170721000C). The list includes LME49710HA/NOPB, LME49710MAX/NOPB and LME49710NA/NOPB, with OPA1611AID/OPA1611AIDR as recommended replacements. This superseded an earlier TI E2E statement that LME49710 'remains without changes' and was Active in all three packages. The LM4562 and LME49720 TO-99 packages were in lifebuy. Around January 2025, diyAudio posters reported LM4562/LME49720 out of stock at TI with steep price rises (one post: LME49720 at 1k quantity from GBP 1.48 to GBP 3.78). No new PDN or PCN was found for that episode. No LME49740 lifecycle notice was found.

- **When:** c.2015-2016: EOL/lifebuy notices (LM4562/LME49720 TO-99 lifebuy per TI E2E). PDN 20170721000C (2017): last order 2018-08-07, last ship 2019-02-07. Around January 2025: supply tightness and price rises, with no notice found.
- **Affected:** LME49710HA/NOPB, LME49710MAX/NOPB, LME49710NA/NOPB, LM4562HA (TO-99 metal can), LME49720HA (TO-99 metal can; the TI E2E reply spells it 'LME49270')
- **How to tell old from new:** Discontinuance, not a silicon change. After the PDN, all LME49710 packages (SOIC, PDIP, TO-99) went EOL. The LM4562/LME49720 TO-99 went lifebuy/EOL. LM4562/LME49720 SOIC and PDIP were briefly reported as EOL and then returned to Active through the GFAB-to-DFAB transfer (PCN 20180308002). Any LME49710 or TO-99 LM4562/LME49720 sold today is old stock or a possible counterfeit.
- **Audio impact:** None to the silicon. Buyers of LME49710 or TO-99 parts after EOL are buying remaining stock, or possibly remarked or counterfeit parts.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: discontinuance only. The suggested substitute, OPA1611, has a different input structure and different noise and bias trade-offs, so it is not a drop-in sonic or electrical equivalent. Check before substituting.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Verification:** WebSearch confirmed PDN 20170721000C: GFAB closure, LME49710HA/MAX/NA with OPA1611 replacement, last order 2018-08-07, last ship 2019-02-07. A search confirmed the TI E2E reply (LME49710 then Active; LM4562/'LME49270' TO-99 lifebuy). No 2025 PDN was found.

### Revision chain status

LM4562 (National DS201572 / TI SNAS326). The datasheet's own National revision table gives rev 1.0 (08/16/06, initial) -> 1.1 (08/22/06, short-circuit current spec) -> 1.2 (09/12/06, CMRR curves) -> 1.3 (09/26/06, application schematic capacitor values) -> 1.4 (05/03/07, EC-table general note; May 2007 edition at radio-hobby.org) -> 1.5 (10/17/07, all PSRR curves replaced; Oct 2007 edition, BU copy). The chain then jumps to SNAS326I (Jan 2010). Search summaries tie SNAS326I to the qobuz copy (already listed, not re-added); raffia.ch has the same doubled-title TI-rewrap pattern. TI revisions follow: J (Apr 2013) -> K (Dec 2013). Still missing: how TI letters A-H map onto National revs 1.0-1.5 (no summary showed a SNAS326A-H letter); any 2008-2009 National edition (rev 1.6+); a confirmed copy of the Aug/Sept 2006 editions (datasheet4u/datasheetspdf id 559726 may be one, per an 'August 2006' summary, unconfirmed). LME49720 (SNAS393). Rev 1.0 03/30/07 (Mar 2007 original; elitesecurity copy) -> 1.1 05/03/07 (probably SNAS393A; no copy found) -> 1.2 10/22/07 (PSRR curves; = SNAS393B Oct 2007: Mouser snas393b, op316, electrokit, and the kyohritsu Japanese copy, whose title reads '2007年10月') -> C Apr 2013 -> D Nov 2016. Still missing: a copy of the May 2007 edition and a confirmed National DS number. LME49710 (SNAS376). Rev 1.0 (table prints 11/16/07, surely Nov 2006) -> 1.1 12/12/06 -> 1.2 01/15/07 -> 1.3 03/09/07 (= SNAS376B Mar 2007) -> C Apr 2013 (new copies: Mouser snas376c, rcscomponents, Octopart 102491025). Still missing: copies of the Nov 2006, Dec 2006 and Jan 2007 editions, and the letter for SNAS376A. The National DS number is probably 202104, inferred from graphic IDs 20210489/90. LME49740 (SNAS377). Rev 1.0 02/28/07 (initial web release) -> 1.01 02/08/08 (SO package caption fix; = SNAS377A, now corroborated; Octopart 15941898 National copy) -> B Apr 2013 (new copies: DigiKey, audioXpress). Still missing: a confirmed copy of the Feb 2007 original (alldatasheet 190918 NSC may be it) and the National DS number. The archive seeds follow the standard national.com/ds/LM/ and ti.com symlink patterns; no search result confirmed them. No SNAS393A, SNAS376A or SNAS326A-H letter appeared in any search result.

## Counterfeits

The evidence comes from blogs and forums and was not re-verified on the web this pass. Marketplace DIP (NA) and TO-99 (HA) parts are frequently fake. Documented cases: a fake LME49710 (tinytransistors.net, 2022-09-24); a fake 'LME49720NA' (diyAudio thread 386852); a suspected fake LM4562NA (diyAudio thread 266368); and a Chinese 'LM4562NA' that JE6LVE measured at about 0.001% THD, against about 0.0006% for a genuine Akizuki-sourced part (je6lve.tom-system.com/archives/453). How to spot fakes: buy SOIC from authorized distributors; be suspicious of cheap TO-99 cans and National-logo NA parts; compare THD and quiescent current (12 mA max for the dual) with a known-good part; check that the marking and logo match the package era (National logo before 2011, TI after). Lot codes starting 'JR' indicate genuine Greenock-fab, Malaysia-assembled production (per diyAudio). A same-part-number die change (PCN 20180308002, 2018) is not counterfeiting, so a genuine new-production part may differ internally from vintage stock. The LM4562 vs LME49720 'voicing' claims are a separate matter: per TI E2E, both are the same silicon.
