# LM4562 / LME49720 / LME49710 / LME49740 (National ultra-low-distortion bipolar audio op-amps)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*National's 2006-07 audio op-amp with 2.7 nV/rtHz noise and 0.00003% THD+N; the standard NE5532 'upgrade', with the metal-can HA versions as collector items.*

**Technology:** Bipolar input, voltage feedback, unity-gain stable; 55 MHz GBW, ±2.5 V to ±17 V supply. LM4562 (dual) and LME49720 (dual) are one die sold under two part numbers (per TI E2E). LME49710 is the single and LME49740 the quad of the same series. Inputs have differential protection diodes. Packages: SOIC (MA), PDIP (NA), TO-99 metal can (HA; dual and single only). Under TI PCN 20180308002 (March 2018), LM4562/LME49720 wafer production moved from a 150 mm line to TI's RFAB (Richardson, TX), on a newer process with a die-shrink redesign. Part numbers did not change.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Hi-Fi op-amp rollers and DIY builders treat this as the default 'better than NE5532' part. Its distortion measures near the limits of test equipment. NwAvGuy's 2011 op-amp measurements are often cited in its favour; the discovery sweep paraphrases him as saying it matches 'any elite part' (wording not re-checked). It is reported as a stock or socketed part in Asus Xonar and Creative Sound Blaster cards (exact part per card not re-checked), and it is widely used in Chinese DACs. Listeners call it neutral, clear, transparent and detailed, and sometimes 'clinical'. Japanese folklore holds that the LME49720 marking sounds brighter and the LM4562 bassier, although TI says the silicon is the same. Forum posters single out the TO-99 HA versions. All of these are subjective, unblinded reports.

**How people describe the sound:** neutral, clear / transparent, detailed, clean, tight bass, clinical / analytical (some listeners), 'too clear' on violins (diyAudio, comparing it with LME49723), LME49720: brighter, glossier; LM4562: bassier, denser (Japanese folklore, same die), cleaner treble than a discrete stage (Soomal)

**Typical uses:** NE5532/4580 replacement in CD players, DACs and sound cards, DAC I/V and output stages, headphone amplifier gain stages, phono and mic preamps (LME49710HA prized), pro-audio line stages (±17 V max), low-noise RF/SDR audio and IQ stages (seen in GitHub SDR projects)

**Caveats:**

- 55 MHz GBW: can oscillate in some sockets, adapters and layouts; needs good local decoupling
- Maximum supply is ±17 V (36 V abs max); for ±18 V to ±22 V rails use LME49860
- Differential input protection diodes limit use with large differential inputs (TI E2E 337068)
- Low-frequency (1/f / popcorn) noise questions raised on TI E2E 415907
- Start-up behaviour of LM4562/LME49710/LME49720 is discussed on the Pro Audio Design Forum (topic 641; details not re-checked)
- Sensitivity to air-coupled EM/RF fields reported for the LM4562/LME497x0 family (ASR thread 10687)
- Since 2018 (PCN 20180308002), LM4562/LME49720 use a die-shrink die from RFAB under the same part number. Old-vs-new sound or measurement claims are unverified.
- DIP and TO-99 stock on marketplaces is often counterfeit
- Pricing has often differed between the LM4562 and LME49720 despite identical silicon
- Sonic descriptors are forum impressions, not controlled listening tests

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Texas Instruments: PCN 20180308002 issued March 2018 (Mouser copy 20180309,…

TI moved LM4562/LME49720 wafer fabrication from a 150 mm (6-inch) line to RFAB in Richardson, TX, on a newer process with a die-shrink redesign. The move revived the parts after the c.2015 'process-node end of life' notice. The TI E2E answer names the source fab as SFAB (Sherman, TX); a diyAudio thread is titled 'Fab moved from GB to US'. The PCN also covered other op-amps. Per TI E2E, LME49710 'remains without changes'.

- **When:** PCN 20180308002 issued March 2018 (Mouser copy 20180309, revision -001 dated 20180326); new-die supply from c.2018-2019
- **Affected:** LM4562 (MA/MAX; other packages not confirmed), LME49720 (MA/MAX; other packages not confirmed)
- **How to tell old from new:** The part number and marking are unchanged. Use the lot or date code. Per diyAudio, lots starting 'JR' were fabbed in Greenock (UK) and assembled in Malaysia, which marks pre-move stock. Date codes from about mid-2018 onward are likely the RFAB die. The PCN lists the affected orderables and the first ship date, but they were not seen. Forum posts note a sharp LM4562 price drop in early 2019 when new-die stock arrived.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. No published spec change and no old-vs-new measurement comparison was found. Claims of batch-to-batch sound differences are unverified forum folklore, and some predate the move.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: same pinout, part number and datasheet limits. However, a die-shrink redesign could change unpublished behaviour (1/f or popcorn noise, RF susceptibility, start-up, overload recovery), and no old-vs-new data was found.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Die / process | original National-design die on 150 mm wafers | die-shrink redesign on a newer TI process at RFAB |
| Wafer size | 150 mm | 200 mm (per search summaries of the PCN and forum; not 300 mm as a discovery hint said) |
| Datasheet electrical limits | SNAS326K (Dec 2013) / SNAS393D (Nov 2016) | no datasheet revision found after the PCN, so no published spec delta |

## Counterfeits

The evidence comes from blogs and forums and was not re-verified on the web this pass. Marketplace DIP (NA) and TO-99 (HA) parts are frequently fake. Documented cases: a fake LME49710 (tinytransistors.net, 2022-09-24); a fake 'LME49720NA' (diyAudio thread 386852); a suspected fake LM4562NA (diyAudio thread 266368); and a Chinese 'LM4562NA' that JE6LVE measured at about 0.001% THD, against about 0.0006% for a genuine Akizuki-sourced part (je6lve.tom-system.com/archives/453). How to spot fakes: buy SOIC from authorized distributors; be suspicious of cheap TO-99 cans and National-logo NA parts; compare THD and quiescent current (12 mA max for the dual) with a known-good part; check that the marking and logo match the package era (National logo before 2011, TI after). Lot codes starting 'JR' indicate genuine Greenock-fab, Malaysia-assembled production (per diyAudio). A same-part-number die change (PCN 20180308002, 2018) is not counterfeiting, so a genuine new-production part may differ internally from vintage stock. The LM4562 vs LME49720 'voicing' claims are a separate matter: per TI E2E, both are the same silicon.
