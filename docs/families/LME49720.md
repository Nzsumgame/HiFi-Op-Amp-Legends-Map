# LM4562 / LME49720 / LME49710 / LME49740 (National ultra-low-distortion bipolar audio op-amps)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*National's 2006-07 audio op-amp with 2.7 nV/rtHz noise and 0.00003% THD+N; the standard NE5532 'upgrade', with the metal-can HA versions as collector items.*

**Technology:** Bipolar input, voltage feedback, unity-gain stable; 55 MHz GBW, ±2.5 V to ±17 V supply. LM4562 (dual) and LME49720 (dual) are one die sold under two part numbers (per TI E2E). LME49710 is the single and LME49740 the quad of the same series. Inputs have differential protection diodes. Packages: SOIC (MA), PDIP (NA), TO-99 metal can (HA; dual and single only).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Hi-Fi rollers and DIY builders treat this as the default 'better than NE5532' part. Its distortion measures near the limits of test equipment. NwAvGuy's 2011 op-amp measurements are often cited in its favour (exact wording not re-checked). It is reported as a stock or socketed part in Asus Xonar and Creative Sound Blaster cards (Titanium HD, ZxR, X7; exact part per card not re-checked), and it is widely used in Chinese DACs. Listeners call it neutral, clear, transparent and detailed, and sometimes 'clinical'. Japanese folklore holds that the LME49720 marking sounds brighter and the LM4562 bassier, although the silicon is the same. Forum posters single out the TO-99 HA versions as quieter and more transparent. These are subjective, unblinded reports.

**How people describe the sound:** neutral, clear / transparent, detailed, clean, tight bass, clinical / analytical (some listeners), 'too clear' on violins (diyAudio, comparing it with LME49723), LME49720: brighter, glossier; LM4562: bassier, denser (Japanese folklore, same die), cleaner treble than a discrete stage (Soomal)

**Typical uses:** NE5532/4580 replacement in CD players, DACs and sound cards, DAC I/V and output stages, headphone amplifier gain stages, phono and mic preamps (LME49710HA prized), pro-audio line stages (±17 V max), low-noise RF/SDR audio and IQ stages (seen in GitHub SDR projects)

**Caveats:**

- 55 MHz GBW: can oscillate in some sockets, adapters and layouts; needs good local decoupling
- Maximum supply is ±17 V (36 V abs max); for ±18 V to ±22 V rails use LME49860
- Differential input protection diodes limit use with large differential inputs (TI E2E 337068)
- Low-frequency (1/f / popcorn) noise questions raised on TI E2E 415907
- Sensitivity to air-coupled EM/RF fields reported for the LM4562/LME497x0 family (ASR thread 10687)
- DIP and TO-99 stock on marketplaces is often counterfeit
- Pricing has often differed between the LM4562 and LME49720 despite identical silicon
- Sonic descriptors are forum impressions, not controlled listening tests

## Counterfeits

This pass did not re-verify the counterfeit evidence; it comes from blogs and forums. DIP (NA) and TO-99 (HA) parts from marketplaces are frequently fake. Documented cases: a fake LME49710 (tinytransistors.net, 2022-09-24); a fake 'LME49720NA' (diyAudio thread 386852); and a Chinese 'LM4562NA' that JE6LVE measured at about 0.001% THD, against about 0.0006% for a genuine Akizuki-sourced part (je6lve.tom-system.com/archives/453). How to spot fakes: buy SOIC from authorized distributors; treat cheap TO-99 cans and National-logo NA parts with suspicion; compare THD and quiescent current (the dual draws 12 mA max per the datasheet) with a known-good part; check the marking and logo against the package era (National logo before 2011, TI after). The LM4562 vs LME49720 'voicing' claims are a separate matter from counterfeiting: per TI E2E, both are the same silicon.
