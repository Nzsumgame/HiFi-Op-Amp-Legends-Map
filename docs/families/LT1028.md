# LT1028 / LT1128 (single ultralow-noise bipolar, 0.85 nV/√Hz)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*A 0.85 nV/√Hz bipolar single from Linear Tech. It has long been the benchmark for MC, tape-head and mic head-amps, and it is a cult pick for op-amp rolling (e.g., Xonar I/V stages).*

**Technology:** Bipolar single op amp. The input stage runs at nearly 1 mA collector current, so voltage noise is very low but current noise is high. Bias-current cancellation holds IB to about 25 nA typ; the cancellation injects correlated current noise, which is rejected only when the source resistances are matched. The LT1028 is decompensated: stable at gain >= +2 or -1, 75 MHz GBW. The LT1128 is the unity-gain-stable version, 20 MHz GBW. Pin 5 is an over-compensation terminal and pins 1/8 are VOS trim. The inputs have back-to-back protection diodes with no series resistors.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Hi-Fi enthusiasts and engineers treat the LT1028 as the reference ultralow-noise bipolar op amp for very low source impedances: MC phono head amps, tape heads and dynamic mics. Its own datasheet shows MAG phono and tape-head preamps. In diyAudio's 'The best sounding audio integrated opamps' thread, one poster calls it 'the only real L.T. star' (web-confirmed). Several diyAudio threads build MC/MM RIAA stages around it, and an Audio Asylum thread weighs it against the AD797 for MC phono. It is sold as a single DIP-8 for op-amp rolling (e.g., Audiophonics) and is a popular I/V pick for Asus Xonar Essence ST/STX rolling. Japanese Dr.DAC users call it monitor-like and not fussy about sources, and Aurorasound presented NS's LME49990 as a challenger to the LT1028A (both not re-verified). The 1993 rec.audio Audio FAQ already called it 'very well received' for phono, head-amp and mic preamps. Engineers note its high current noise and a noise bump around 400 kHz. The sonic descriptors are community opinion and were not re-verified.

**How people describe the sound:** very expressive, transparent, smooth and refined, better bass dynamics and fullness, monitor-like, no weak genres, little source dependence

**Typical uses:** MC phono head amp / pre-preamp, MM phono and tape-head preamps (datasheet application circuits; diyAudio RIAA builds), low-impedance microphone preamps, DAC I/V stage (e.g., Asus Xonar Essence ST/STX op-amp rolling), op-amp rolling in Dr.DAC-type DACs and headphone amps, low-noise measurement preamps, TIAs, laser current drivers

**Caveats:**

- The LT1028 is NOT unity-gain stable (gain >= +2 or -1). It can oscillate as a follower, unity buffer or I/V stage with a large feedback cap. Use the LT1128 or follow the datasheet RC/RS2 rules. With 75 MHz GBW it is sensitive to socket and adapter layout.
- High input bias current (+/-25 nA) and current noise (1 pA/rtHz at 1 kHz, 4.7 at 10 Hz). With source impedances well above about 1 kohm (about 400 ohm at 10 Hz per the datasheet table), current noise dominates and another part is better. Bias-current cancellation adds noise when the source resistances at the two inputs are unmatched.
- High supply current: 7.4 mA typ, 9.5-10.5 mA max at 25 C, and up to 12.5 mA max (non-A, -40 to 85 C). It runs warm and loads small supplies. It is specified at +/-15 V, and PSRR is specified from +/-4 V.
- Single only, with no dual version. Dual sockets need adapters, which adds oscillation risk.
- Pin 5 is an over-compensation pin and pins 1/8 are VOS trim. Check that the host board leaves pin 5 open or uses it correctly. The SW16 package has a different pinout and is NRND.
- Input diodes have no series resistors. Large differential transients (for example slew overload in unity-gain use) need input current limiting.
- There is a noise bump around 400 kHz in the HF noise curve. It is inaudible but raises integrated wideband noise (Libbrecht & Hall 1993 and Seck et al. 2016, as cited in P. Baus's PhD thesis).

### Revision chain status

## Counterfeits

No specific counterfeit reports were found; a web search found none. Marketplace listings exist, such as eBay lots of '1PCS LT1028ACN8 DIP-8 ... NEW' and '50 Stück ... LT1028CN8 ... NEU'. Their provenance is unknown, and a bulk lot of a part that sells for several dollars is a red flag. Identification per Rev D: - SO-8 parts are top-marked '1028' / '1128' (plus date and lot code). - PDIP and SW16 parts carry the full part number (e.g., 'LT1028ACN8'). - The temperature grade appears on the shipping-container label. - #PBF / #TRPBF / #TRMPBF are orderable suffixes, not package markings. - AM/M grades and the H (TO-5) and J8 (CERDIP) packages are obsolete, so 'new' metal-can or ceramic parts are old stock or fakes. Quick bench checks at +/-15 V: - A genuine part draws about 7.4 mA (9.5 mA max A grade, 10.5 mA non-A, at 25 C). - Its 1 kHz noise should be at or below 1.1-1.2 nV/rtHz. - A remarked generic bipolar op amp would typically show about 3 nV/rtHz or more. - An LT1028 sold as a unity-gain buffer that proves stable at unity gain may be a relabelled LT1128 or another part.

## Related parts and alternatives

AD797 (ADI): about 0.9 nV/rtHz bipolar single with high output drive and a very low-distortion reputation. It is the usual head-to-head rival for MC phono (e.g., Audio Asylum 'LT1028 or AD797 for MC phono stage'). Seck et al. (via Baus) found lower integrated wideband noise than the LT1028, which has a 400 kHz bump., LT1115 (Linear/ADI): 0.9 nV/rtHz audio-optimised sibling, marketed by LT as an ultralow-noise, low-distortion audio op amp., LT1128 (same datasheet): use it where unity-gain stability is needed (buffers, some I/V stages)., OPA1611 / OPA211 (TI): about 1.1 nV/rtHz bipolar, unity-gain stable, modern supply range. OPA1612 is the dual., LME49990 (National/TI, discontinued): 0.9 nV/rtHz, positioned by Aurorasound as a challenger to the LT1028A., ADA4898-1/-2 (ADI): 0.9 nV/rtHz, unity-gain stable, dual available. Much lower open-loop gain (about 0.14 V/uV) than the LT1028.
