# LT1028 / LT1128 (single ultralow-noise bipolar, 0.85 nV/√Hz)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*A 0.85 nV/√Hz bipolar single from Linear Tech. It has long been the benchmark for MC, tape-head and mic head-amps, and it is a cult pick for op-amp rolling (e.g., Xonar I/V stages).*

**Technology:** Bipolar single op amp. The input stage runs at nearly 1 mA collector current, so voltage noise is very low but current noise is high. Bias-current cancellation holds IB to about 25 nA typ; the cancellation injects correlated current noise, which is rejected only when the source resistances are matched. The LT1028 is decompensated: stable at gain >= +2 or -1, 75 MHz GBW. The LT1128 is the unity-gain-stable version, 20 MHz GBW. Pin 5 is an over-compensation terminal and pins 1/8 are VOS trim. The inputs have back-to-back protection diodes with no series resistors.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Hi-Fi enthusiasts and engineers treat the LT1028 as the reference ultralow-noise bipolar op amp for very low source impedances: MC phono head amps, tape heads and dynamic mics. Its own datasheet shows MAG phono and tape-head preamps. One diyAudio poster calls it 'the only real Linear Technology star' among audio op amps, and the same thread lists it among the best VFB op amps. It is a popular I/V pick when rolling Asus Xonar Essence ST/STX op amps. Japanese Dr.DAC users call it monitor-like and not fussy about sources. Aurorasound presented NS's LME49990 as a challenger to the LT1028A. The 1993 rec.audio Audio FAQ already called it 'very well received' for phono, head-amp and mic preamps. Engineers note its high current noise and a noise bump around 400 kHz. The community sonic claims come from earlier discovery searches and were not re-verified in this run.

**How people describe the sound:** very expressive, transparent, smooth and refined, better bass dynamics and fullness, monitor-like, no weak genres, little source dependence

**Typical uses:** MC phono head amp / pre-preamp, MM phono and tape-head preamps (datasheet application circuits), low-impedance microphone preamps, DAC I/V stage (e.g., Asus Xonar Essence ST/STX op-amp rolling), op-amp rolling in Dr.DAC-type DACs and headphone amps, low-noise measurement preamps, TIAs, laser current drivers

**Caveats:**

- The LT1028 is NOT unity-gain stable (gain >= +2 or -1). It can oscillate as a follower, unity buffer or I/V stage with a large feedback cap. Use the LT1128 or follow the datasheet RC/RS2 rules. With 75 MHz GBW it is sensitive to socket and adapter layout.
- High input bias current (+/-25 nA) and current noise (1 pA/rtHz at 1 kHz, 4.7 at 10 Hz). With source impedances well above about 1 kohm (about 400 ohm at 10 Hz per the datasheet table), current noise dominates and another part is better. Bias-current cancellation adds noise when the source resistances at the two inputs are unmatched.
- High supply current: 7.4 mA typ, 9.5-10.5 mA max at 25 C, and up to 12.5 mA max (non-A, -40 to 85 C). It runs warm and loads small supplies. It is specified at +/-15 V, and PSRR is specified from +/-4 V.
- Single only, with no dual version. Dual sockets need adapters, which adds oscillation risk.
- Pin 5 is an over-compensation pin and pins 1/8 are VOS trim. Check that the host board leaves pin 5 open or uses it correctly. The SW16 package has a different pinout.
- Input diodes have no series resistors. Large differential transients (for example slew overload in unity-gain use) need input current limiting.
- There is a noise bump around 400 kHz in the HF noise curve. It is inaudible but raises integrated wideband noise (Libbrecht & Hall 1993 and Seck et al. 2016, as cited in P. Baus's PhD thesis).

## Counterfeits

No specific counterfeit reports were located. Web search was unavailable, and a GitHub code search for LT1028 counterfeit/fake/remarked returned nothing. The discovery notes flag cheap marketplace listings as of doubtful origin (low confidence). Identification per Rev D: - SO-8 parts are top-marked '1028' / '1128' (plus date and lot code). - PDIP and SW16 parts carry the full part number (e.g., 'LT1028ACN8'). - The temperature grade appears on the shipping-container label. - #PBF / #TRPBF / #TRMPBF are orderable suffixes, not package markings. - AM/M grades and the H (TO-5) and J8 (CERDIP) packages are obsolete, so 'new' metal-can or ceramic parts are old stock or fakes. Quick bench checks at +/-15 V: - A genuine part draws about 7.4 mA (9.5 mA max A grade, 10.5 mA non-A, at 25 C). - Its 1 kHz noise should be at or below 1.1-1.2 nV/rtHz. - A remarked generic bipolar op amp would typically show about 3 nV/rtHz or more.

## Related parts and alternatives

AD797 (ADI): about 0.9 nV/rtHz bipolar single with high output drive and a very low-distortion reputation. It is the usual head-to-head rival. Seck et al. (via Baus) found lower integrated wideband noise than the LT1028, which has a 400 kHz bump., LT1115 (Linear/ADI): 0.9 nV/rtHz audio-optimised sibling, marketed by LT as an ultralow-noise, low-distortion audio op amp., LT1128 (same datasheet): use it where unity-gain stability is needed (buffers, some I/V stages)., OPA1611 / OPA211 (TI): about 1.1 nV/rtHz bipolar, unity-gain stable, modern supply range. OPA1612 is the dual., LME49990 (National/TI, discontinued): 0.9 nV/rtHz, positioned by Aurorasound as a challenger to the LT1028A., ADA4898-1/-2 (ADI): 0.9 nV/rtHz, unity-gain stable, dual available. Much lower open-loop gain (about 0.14 V/uV) than the LT1028.
