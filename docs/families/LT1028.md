# LT1028 / LT1128

**Ultralow-noise bipolar single (LT1028 decompensated)** · Linear Technology (by 1989–2017) → Analog Devices (2017–present) · second sources: Texas Instruments · Tier B (widely praised)

*Linear Tech's 0.85 nV/√Hz single: the benchmark for MC, tape-head and mic preamps, and a cult rolling pick.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | THD+N | Supply range | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.85 nV/√Hz typ, 1.1 max (A); 0.9 typ, 1.2 max (C) | 75 MHz typ, 50 min (LT1028); 20 MHz typ, 13 min (LT1128) | 15 V/µs typ, 11 min (LT1028); 6 typ, 5.0 min (LT1128) | Graphs only; <0.0018% in sine-oscillator circuit | PSRR spec ±4 V to ±18 V; abs max ±22 V (±16 V >105 °C) | 7.4 mA typ, 9.5 max (A); 7.6 typ, 10.5 max (C) |

## Why it's rated

The reference ultralow-noise op-amp for MC phono, tape heads and dynamic mics. A diyAudio poster calls it 'the only real L.T. star'; Audio Asylum weighs it against the AD797. Popular for Xonar ST/STX I/V rolling; Japanese Dr.DAC users call it monitor-like. The 1993 rec.audio FAQ: 'very well received'. Engineers note high current noise and a 400 kHz noise bump.

**Described as:** very expressive, transparent, smooth and refined, better bass dynamics, fullness, monitor-like, no weak genres, little source dependence

**Used in:** MC phono head amp / pre-preamp, MM phono and tape-head preamps, Low-impedance microphone preamps, DAC I/V and op-amp rolling (Xonar ST/STX, Dr.DAC), Low-noise measurement preamps, TIAs, laser drivers

## Watch out

- LT1028 needs gain ≥+2 or −1; may oscillate as a follower or with a large I/V feedback cap. Use LT1128.
- High bias (±25 nA) and current noise: above ~1 kΩ (~400 Ω at 10 Hz) pick another part; match sources.
- Draws 7.4 mA typ (to 12.5 mA max); runs warm. Specified at ±15 V.
- Single only; pin 5 is over-compensation, pins 1/8 VOS trim; SW16 has a different pinout.
- Input diodes lack series resistors; limit current to 25 mA. A 400 kHz noise bump raises wideband noise.
- **Counterfeits:** No counterfeit reports; bulk eBay lots are a red flag. SO-8 marks '1028'/'1128'; PDIP/SW16 show the full number. AM/M, TO-5, CERDIP are obsolete. Check ~7.4 mA supply current and ≤1.2 nV/√Hz noise; a fake usually shows ~3 nV/√Hz.
