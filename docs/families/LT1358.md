# LT1357 / LT1358 / LT1359

**Fast bipolar C-Load op-amp, 1/2/4 channels** · Linear Technology (1994–2017) → Analog Devices (2017–present) · Tier C (niche / baseline)

*Low-power (2 mA/amp) sibling of the LT1364: 25 MHz, 600 V/µs. A niche sound-card buffer pick called warm and forward.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | Supply range | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 nV/√Hz typ | 25 MHz typ | 600 V/µs typ | ±2.5 V to ±15 V | 2.5 mA max per amp (about 2 mA typ) |

## Why it's rated

A niche pick from the sound-card op-amp-rolling era (Asus Xonar, Audiotrak Prodigy HD2). Forum posts call the LT1358 "warm, bright, tonal rich and sounds forward"; some prefer it to the faster LT1364 in buffer duty, and it is named alongside the LT1364 and LT1361. Subjective impressions only; no ASR or Samuel Groner measurements exist.

**Described as:** warm, bright, tonally rich, forward

**Used in:** Sound-card swaps (Xonar, Prodigy HD2 buffer/output), DIY DAC buffer and I/V stages, Wideband amps, active filters, video/cable drivers

## Watch out

- Praise rests on a few subjective forum posts.
- 25 MHz / 600 V/µs: keep layout tight and decouple locally; C-Load won't fix long socket leads.
- Low power (2.5 mA max/amp); output current unverified, so check before driving headphones.
- Bipolar inputs with up to 500 nA bias: watch offset with high source impedances.
- Supply limit is ±15 V; ±18 V rails are out of spec.
- **Counterfeits:** No specific reports. Buy from authorized ADI distributors; distrust marketplace DIP-8s, especially leaded non-#PBF parts sold new. Remarked slow parts miss 600 V/µs or 25 MHz; supply should be ≤ 2.5 mA/amp. Compare marking and date code.

## Silicon changes

None documented. Compare datasheet revisions before assuming old and new parts are identical.
