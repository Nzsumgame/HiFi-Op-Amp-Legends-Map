# AD826 (ADI high-speed, low-power dual video op-amp)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*ADI's 50 MHz, 350 V/µs video dual. CD-player tweakers found it cleaner and more open than the AD827, and 'almost as good' as an OPA637.*

**Technology:** Bipolar high-speed voltage-feedback dual op-amp. ADI's macro-model uses an NPN input pair. It is unity-gain stable and rated by ADI to drive unlimited capacitive load. It runs from a single +5 V to +36 V supply or split ±2.5 V to ±18 V. ADI's specs match the single-channel AD817.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

This is a niche op-amp for rolling in CD players, not a mainstream audio legend. In the Audio Asylum 'tweaks' forum and on diyAudio, users who swapped it into CD-player output and I/V stages called the sound 'much cleaner, more open, and more detailed'. They also reported more precise instrument placement than the AD827, and in one Audio Asylum comparison it was 'almost as good' as the OPA637. Its appeal is video-class speed (50 MHz, 350 V/µs) combined with unconditional stability into capacitive loads. No independent measurements (Audio Science Review, Samuel Groner) were found. A 2005 translation of Tangent's op-amp notes (DiyEden/Soomal) put the AD826 on its list to test, but no review followed.

**How people describe the sound:** cleaner, more open, more detailed, precise instrument placement, close to OPA637 (per one listener)

**Typical uses:** CD-player analog output stage op-amp swap, DAC current-to-voltage (I/V) converter, Video and line drivers (the designed use), Buffers driving cables or capacitive loads

**Caveats:**

- The evidence is thin: a few forum posts from the discovery notes, which this pass did not re-read. There are no published measurements.
- This is a 50 MHz video part. Tight decoupling and short leads are sensible when socket-rolling, although ADI rates it stable into any capacitive load. This is engineering inference, not a reported failure.
- Input bias current is high (3.3 µA typ, 6.6 µA max), which can cause DC offset with large feedback or source resistances.
- Voltage noise is moderate at about 15 nV/√Hz, well above NE5532- or AD797-class parts.
- It draws about 6.8–7.5 mA per amplifier (about 14–15 mA per package), so it runs warmer and drains batteries faster than audio duals.
- The Tangent/DiyEden headphone-amp notes argued that ultra-high-speed op-amps are generally a poor fit for simple headphone amps.

## Counterfeits

No reports of fake or remarked AD826 parts were found. Web search was unavailable, so forums and AliExpress reviews were not checked. Loose 'AD826ARZ/AD826AR/AD826 SOP-8, 10 pcs/lot' marketplace listings exist (cited by a 2021 report). As with other older ADI DIP and SOIC parts, buy from authorised distributors (Digi-Key AD826ANZ-ND). Check the ADI logo, the lot/date-code format and the Z (RoHS) suffix. Be wary of very cheap non-Z 'AD826AN' stock.

## Related parts and alternatives

AD817 (ADI single-channel sibling with identical headline specs in ADI's AAE-25 table), AD827 (older ADI 50 MHz, unlimited-CL dual; 9–36 V supply; also audio-praised), AD828 (later ADI low-power dual video op-amp; audio suitability not verified here), LM6172 (TI/National dual high-speed low-power voltage-feedback amp; one KiCad BOM puts the AD826 on an LM6172 symbol, so similar class and pinout), AD8065/AD8066 (ADI FastFET high-speed op-amps with pA bias current, more widely praised for audio)

- THD, differential gain/phase and 0.1 dB-flatness figures were not captured. The student report's flatness and full-power bandwidth rows were garbled.
- No PCN was found for the lead-free (AN→ANZ, AR→ARZ) conversion or for any fab or die change. Whether the non-Z AN and AR parts are obsolete is unverified.
