# AD826 (ADI high-speed, low-power dual video op-amp)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*ADI's 50 MHz, 350 V/µs video dual. CD-player tweakers found it cleaner and more open than the AD827, and 'almost as good' as an OPA637.*

**Technology:** Bipolar high-speed voltage-feedback dual op-amp. ADI's macro-model uses an NPN input pair. It is unity-gain stable and ADI rates it to drive unlimited capacitive load. It runs from a single +5 V to +36 V supply or split ±2.5 V to ±18 V, drawing 15 mA max for both amplifiers. ADI's headline specs match the single-channel AD817.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

This is a niche op-amp for rolling in CD players, not a mainstream audio legend. ADI designed it for video, CATV, copiers, LCDs, scanners and fax machines. In the Audio Asylum 'tweaks' forum and on diyAudio, a few users who swapped it into CD-player output and I/V stages called the sound 'much cleaner, more open, and more detailed'. They also reported more precise instrument placement than the AD827, and one Audio Asylum poster found it 'almost as good' as the OPA637. Its appeal is video-class speed (50 MHz, 350 V/µs) combined with stability into capacitive loads. No independent measurements (Audio Science Review, Samuel Groner) were found. A 2005 translation of Tangent's op-amp notes (DiyEden/Soomal) put the AD826 on its list to test, but no review followed.

**How people describe the sound:** cleaner, more open, more detailed, precise instrument placement, close to OPA637 (per one listener)

**Typical uses:** CD-player analog output stage op-amp swap, DAC current-to-voltage (I/V) converter, Video and line drivers (the designed use), Buffers driving cables or capacitive loads

**Caveats:**

- The evidence is thin: a few forum posts from the discovery notes, which were not re-read in this pass. There are no published measurements.
- This is a 50 MHz video part. Tight decoupling and short leads are sensible when socket-rolling, although ADI rates it stable into any capacitive load. This is engineering inference, not a reported failure.
- Input bias current is high (3.3 µA typ, 6.6 µA max), which can cause DC offset with large feedback or source resistances.
- Voltage noise is moderate at 15 nV/√Hz, well above NE5532- or AD797-class parts.
- It draws up to 15 mA per package (7.5 mA max per amplifier), so it runs warmer and drains batteries faster than audio duals.
- The DIP versions (AD826AN/ANZ) are reportedly discontinued, so DIP socket-rollers depend on old stock or SOIC-to-DIP adapters.
- The Tangent/DiyEden headphone-amp notes argued that ultra-high-speed op-amps are generally a poor fit for simple headphone amps.

## Counterfeits

No reports of fake or remarked AD826 parts were found. The leaded DIP (AD826AN) and reportedly the AD826ANZ are discontinued per ADI PDN 10_0081 Rev. B (search summary). Any 'new' DIP stock is therefore old inventory or suspect. Loose 'AD826ARZ/AD826AR SOP-8, 10 pcs/lot' marketplace listings exist. Buy from authorised distributors (Mouser, Digi-Key, Newark) and check the ADI logo, the lot/date-code format and the Z (RoHS) suffix.

## Related parts and alternatives

AD817 (ADI single-channel sibling with identical headline specs in ADI's AAE-25 table), AD827 (older ADI 50 MHz, unlimited-CL dual; also audio-praised), AD828 (later ADI low-power dual video op-amp; audio suitability not verified here), LM6172 (TI/National dual high-speed low-power voltage-feedback amp; similar class and pinout), AD8065/AD8066 (ADI FastFET high-speed op-amps with pA bias current, more widely praised for audio)

- THD, differential gain/phase and 0.1 dB-flatness figures were not captured.
- No PCN was found for any fab or die change. No silicon-change hazard is known.
