# AD826

**Bipolar high-speed low-power dual video op-amp** · Analog Devices (c. 1992–present) · Tier C (niche / baseline)

*ADI's 50 MHz, 350 V/µs video dual; CD-player tweakers found it cleaner than the AD827, 'almost as good' as an OPA637.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | Supply range | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 nV/√Hz typ | 50 MHz (±15 V); 35 MHz (±5 V); 29 MHz (+5 V) | 350 V/µs (±15 V); 250 (±5 V); 200 (+5 V) typ | ±2.5 V to ±18 V; +5 V to +36 V single | 15 mA max both amps (7.5 mA max each); 6.8 mA/amp |

## Why it's rated

A niche CD-player rolling part, not a mainstream legend; ADI designed it for video, CATV, copiers, scanners and fax. A few Audio Asylum and diyAudio users heard it as cleaner, more open and more detailed than the AD827, one 'almost as good' as an OPA637. No independent measurements exist.

**Described as:** cleaner, more open, more detailed, precise instrument placement, close to OPA637

**Used in:** CD-player analog output stage swap, DAC current-to-voltage (I/V) converter, Video and line drivers (designed use), Buffers driving cables or capacitive loads

## Watch out

- Thin evidence: a few forum posts, no published measurements. Soomal/DiyEden listed it for testing in 2005; no review followed.
- High bias current (3.3 µA typ) causes DC offset with large resistances; noise 15 nV/√Hz.
- Draws up to 15 mA per package; runs warmer than audio duals.
- DIP versions reportedly discontinued; rollers need old stock or SOIC adapters.
- 50 MHz part: decouple tightly. Tangent's notes call ultra-fast op-amps a poor fit for simple headphone amps.
- **Counterfeits:** No AD826 counterfeit reports. DIP versions are discontinued (reportedly PDN 10_0081 Rev. B), so 'new' DIP stock is old or suspect. Buy from Mouser, Digi-Key or Newark, not loose SOP-8 marketplace lots; check ADI logo, date code, Z suffix.

## Silicon changes

None documented. Compare datasheet revisions before assuming old and new parts are identical.
