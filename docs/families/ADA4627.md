# ADA4627-1 / ADA4637-1

**JFET-input single op-amp; ADA4637-1 decompensated** · Analog Devices (2009–present) → Analog Devices (2010–present) · Tier B (widely praised)

*ADI's 2009 JFET single (6.1 nV/√Hz, 19 MHz), often tipped as an OPA627 alternative 'reputable with music fans'.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | Supply range |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 nV/√Hz | 19 MHz (ADA4627-1) / ~80 MHz (ADA4637-1) | 60 V/µs (ADA4627-1) / 170 V/µs (ADA4637-1) | ±5 V to ±15 V; titled 36 V (early) / 30 V (Rev. F) |

## Why it's rated

ADI's AN-1429 lists the ADA4627-1 with the AD797, OP275 and AD8620 as op-amps 'reputable with music fans' (vendor marketing). A diyAudio thread, 'ADA4627 outperforms Burr-Brown's OPA627', calls it refined and dynamic; a Japanese Dr.DAC3 comparison (Shirokuma) heard less treble harshness than the OPA627. The ADA4637-1 is liked for DAC I/V. No independent measurements found.

**Described as:** refined, dynamic, 'everything sounds BIGGER', less harsh than OPA627, clean

**Used in:** OPA627 alternative in single sockets (SOIC-to-DIP adapter), DAC I/V (ADA4637-1 only at noise gain ≥ 5), microphone preamplifiers, op-amp rolling in DACs and headphone gear (Dr.DAC3), low-bias transimpedance stages (STM tunnelling amps)

## Watch out

- ADA4637-1 needs noise gain ≥ 5; not for unity-gain buffers or low-gain stages.
- Single only, SOIC-8 or LFCSP-8, no DIP; dual sockets need an adapter.
- Slew advantage over OPA627 (55 V/µs) is marginal for ADA4627-1, clear for ADA4637-1.
- Sonic claims are subjective forum and blog listening; AN-1429 praise is marketing.
- No independent measurement data found.
- **Counterfeits:** No counterfeit reports found. Claims that fans prefer it because, unlike the OPA627, it is not faked are low-confidence folklore. Buy from ADI or authorised distributors; avoid marketplace 'ADA4627 on DIP adapter' modules of unknown provenance.
