# NJM8830 (Nisshinbo ultra-low-distortion, ultra-low-noise full-swing audio dual)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** Japanese audio series (New JRC / Nisshinbo)

*Nisshinbo's 2022 bipolar audio dual: 0.000012% THD, 2.5 nV/√Hz, 100 mA rail-to-rail output. A low-voltage (±5.25 V max) specialist for headphone and DAC stages.*

**Technology:** Dual audio op amp with bipolar input and rail-to-rail (full-swing) output. Vendor typicals: THD+N 0.000012% at 1 kHz, 2.5 nV/√Hz at 1 kHz, GBW 90 MHz, slew rate 30 V/µs, output current ±100 mA. It runs only from ±2.0 V to ±5.25 V (4 to 10.5 V total). It comes only in SMD packages, HSOP8-M1 and DFN8-W1 (ESON8-W1); there is no DIP version.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Nisshinbo launched it in June 2022, with coverage in PHILE WEB, Nikkan Kogyo and PR Times, and Akizuki sells it to hobbyists with a DIP8 conversion kit. Japanese hobbyists who swapped it in report positive results. A Threads user who fitted it in an FX-Audio amp calls the sound 'メッチャ高音質' (extremely high sound quality), and another swap in place of a MUSES8920E reportedly gave stronger bass and punch. A diyAudio thread ('NJM8830 – A New Challenger in the Hi-Fi Op-Amp Arena') posts bench tests: in its comparison the NJM8830 beats the OPA1642A but does not surpass the OPA1612A, with less overshoot and faster settling in a transient test. One diyAudio poster calls it a strong IEM or low-impedance headphone driver rather than a general-purpose op amp.

**How people describe the sound:** 'extremely high sound quality' (Threads user, FX-Audio swap), stronger bass and punch vs MUSES8920E (Japanese swap report), low overshoot, fast settling on square-wave tests (diyAudio bench posts)

**Typical uses:** IEM / low-impedance headphone driver, preamplifier and active-filter stages on low-voltage rails (vendor positioning), DAC output / buffer stages in 5 V-class audio gear, op-amp rolling via the HSOP8-to-DIP8 adapter (Akizuki kit)

**Caveats:**

- Maximum operating supply is only ±5.25 V (10.5 V total). Do not fit it in sockets fed from ±12 V or ±15 V rails.
- SMD only (HSOP8-M1 / DFN8-W1), so socket swaps need an adapter board.
- Supply current of about 6 to 6.5 mA; a diyAudio poster calls it unsuitable for portable gear.
- The 90 MHz GBW means adapter and socket installs need short, well-decoupled layouts to avoid instability (general engineering caution).
- In the diyAudio comparison it measured below the OPA1612A.
- Praise is mostly Japanese hobbyist anecdotes plus one diyAudio measurement thread; there are no controlled listening tests.

## Counterfeits

No counterfeit reports found. It is a recent, single-source part sold through authorized channels (Mouser, LCSC, Akizuki). It comes only in HSOP8-M1 and DFN8-W1 packages, so any 'DIP-8 NJM8830' is either an adapter board (such as Akizuki's GM1 DIP conversion kit) or a mislabeled or fake part.
