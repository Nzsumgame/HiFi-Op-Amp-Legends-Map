# ADA4898-1 / ADA4898-2 (single/dual 0.9 nV/√Hz high-voltage bipolar)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*A 0.9 nV/√Hz, 55 V/µs bipolar op amp that runs on ±5 V to ±16 V; some diyAudio DAC builders favour it for I/V stages.*

**Technology:** Bipolar-input, voltage-feedback, unity-gain-stable high-speed op amp for ±5 V to ±16 V supplies. The ADI datasheet title is 'High Voltage, Low Noise, Low Distortion, Unity-Gain Stable, High Speed Op Amp'. The only package is an 8-lead SOIC with an exposed pad (EPAD), which may be tied to −VS or left floating. The Rev. F datasheet changed the single's (ADA4898-1) outline code from RD-8-1 to RD-8-4. Search summaries give RD-8-2 for the dual (ADA4898-2).

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded: Fab / process transfer).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche part in DIY DAC circles. On diyAudio it comes up as a current-to-voltage (I/V) choice after current-output DACs, including in 'best dual I/V op amp these days' threads. Builders cite its very low voltage noise, bandwidth and ±16 V headroom. ADI pitches it for 16-bit and 18-bit systems. It is also used in instrumentation LNAs and photodetectors, such as Gerhard Hoffmann's 'lono' parallel-op-amp LNA. One audio review site calls the ADA4898-1 a cheap 0.9 nV/√Hz equivalent of the LT1115. The praise quotes come from the discovery notes and were not re-checked by web search.

**How people describe the sound:** deep, tight and impactful bass (diyAudio, I/V use; per discovery notes, not re-checked), excellent in DAC I/V stages (diyAudio; per discovery notes)

**Typical uses:** I/V converter after current-output audio DACs, DAC output buffer / reconstruction filter, Low-noise preamp / LNA front ends (parallel arrays, as in Hoffmann 'lono' and aewallin ADA4898_LNA), Photodiode transimpedance and servo loops, 16-bit/18-bit ADC drivers (ADI's stated target)

**Caveats:**

- Comes only in SOIC-8 with an exposed pad. There is no DIP version, so op-amp rolling needs an adapter. The pad may be tied to −VS or left floating, but never to ground or +VS.
- Draws about 7.9 mA per amplifier, so a dual dissipates about 0.47 W at ±15 V. It runs warm on DIP adapters that have no copper under the pad.
- Needs at least ±5 V, so it does not suit low-voltage portable designs.
- Current noise is fairly high (2.4 pA/√Hz). The noise-optimal source impedance is about 375 Ω, so it works best with low-impedance sources.
- General high-speed caution (engineering inference): with 50 to 65 MHz of bandwidth, layout, decoupling and capacitive loading matter. Socket or adapter wiring can cause peaking or oscillation.
