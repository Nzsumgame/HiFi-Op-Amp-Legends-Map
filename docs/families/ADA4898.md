# ADA4898-1 / ADA4898-2 (single/dual 0.9 nV/√Hz high-voltage bipolar)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*A 0.9 nV/√Hz, 65 MHz bipolar op amp that runs on up to ±16 V; diyAudio DAC builders use it in I/V stages and praise its 'deep, tight and impactful bass'.*

**Technology:** Bipolar-input, voltage-feedback, unity-gain-stable high-speed op amp for ±5 V to ±16 V supplies. ADI's datasheet title reads 'High Voltage, Low Noise, Low Distortion, Unity-Gain Stable, High Speed Op Amp'. The only package seen is an 8-lead SOIC with exposed pad (ADI RD-8-2), for both the single and the dual.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

This is a niche-cult part in DIY DAC circles. On diyAudio it is a favourite for current-to-voltage (I/V) conversion after current-output DACs, and it is recommended in 'best dual I/V op amp these days' threads. Measurement-minded builders like its very low voltage noise, its bandwidth, and headroom up to ±16 V. It is also a common choice for instrumentation LNAs and photodetectors, including Gerhard Hoffmann's 'lono' parallel-op-amp LNA. A Japanese/English audio review site names the ADA4898-1 as the cheap 0.9 nV/√Hz equivalent of the LT1115. The praise quotes come from the discovery notes and were not re-checked this run.

**How people describe the sound:** deep, tight and impactful bass (diyAudio, I/V use), excellent in DAC I/V stages (diyAudio)

**Typical uses:** I/V converter after current-output audio DACs, DAC output buffer / reconstruction filter, Low-noise preamp / LNA front ends (parallel arrays, as in Hoffmann 'lono' and aewallin ADA4898_LNA), Photodiode transimpedance and servo loops (TU Darmstadt APQ photodetector/PDH/MTS modules)

**Caveats:**

- Comes only in SOIC-8 with an exposed pad (RD-8-2). There is no DIP version, so op-amp rolling needs an adapter board, and the exposed pad must be connected as the datasheet says (rail not verified here).
- Draws about 8 mA per amplifier, so a dual dissipates about 0.48 W at ±15 V. It runs warm on DIP adapters that have no copper under the pad.
- Needs at least ±5 V, so it does not suit low-voltage portable designs.
- Current noise is fairly high (2.4 pA/√Hz). The noise-optimal source impedance is about 375 Ω (0.9 nV divided by 2.4 pA), so it works best with low-impedance sources such as I/V stages and low-R feedback networks.
- General high-speed caution (engineering inference, not a specific report): with 65 MHz of bandwidth, layout, decoupling and capacitive loading matter. Socket or adapter wiring can cause peaking or oscillation.

## Counterfeits

No counterfeit or remarking reports were found. Web search was unavailable, so forums were not checked. The part is cheap (about US$2.70 to $5 per third-party 2026 notes) and comes only in SOIC-8 with an exposed pad, so fakes have little appeal. Pre-built DIP-8 adapter modules sold for op-amp rolling have not been checked. Buy from ADI-authorised distributors and check that the part has a real exposed pad and ADI top marking.
