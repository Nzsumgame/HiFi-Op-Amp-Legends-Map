# OPA604 / OPA2604

**FET-input low-distortion audio op-amp, 1/2 channels** · Burr-Brown (1991–2000) → Texas Instruments (2000–present) · Tier A (legend / revision hazard)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** Unannounced same-part-number derating: a TI non-conformance form said the OPA2604 must be held to ±20 V; at ±24 V parts showed long-term offset drift and higher Iq, likely thermal. [Details](#silicon-changes)

*Burr-Brown's early-90s ±24 V FET audio op-amp: the warm, tube-like (胆味) classic of CD players, sound cards, phones.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | THD+N | Supply range | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 nV/√Hz (1 kHz); 10/15/25 at 10 kHz/100 Hz/10 Hz | 20 MHz typ | 25 V/µs typ (15 V/µs min) | 0.0003% typ at ±15 V | ±4.5 to ±24 V (±25 V abs max); late OPA2604 ±20 V | ±10.5 mA typ, ±12 mA max (OPA2604, both amps) |

## Why it's rated

A defining 'Burr-Brown sound' part: warm, laid back, easy to listen to. Japanese bloggers call it 'the legendary op-amp' (伝説のオペアンプ); Soomal notes a strong valve flavour (胆味) and fame near the NE5532's, and found it in phones, DAPs and DACs including TempoTec Serenade. Its ±24 V rating suited high rails. 2025 Japanese tests reportedly rank it below MUSES8820/8920.

**Described as:** warm (偏暖), tube-like (胆味), laid back, rich midrange, gloss (艶), spacious, fat, bouncy bass, soft, smooth highs

**Used in:** CD player and DAC I/V and output stages, DAC low-pass filters (Sony PHA-3, Shanling H3A, SoundAware), Sound-card, USB DAC and headphone-amp rolling (reportedly Maverick D2), Phone Hi-Fi paths (vivo Xplay/Xplay 3S, Smartisan T1), Pro line stages on ±18 to ±24 V rails

## Watch out

- Fast (20 MHz): one OPA2604AP oscillated above ~18 V in a Hansen-type amp; harder to use than OPA132/134.
- Needs proper rails: Soomal found high distortion at ~±9.5 V (Teclast T51) and in the Smartisan T1.
- Driving low-impedance headphones directly sounds thin and harsh (Soomal); Mineden reportedly finds the mids thin.
- OPA2604 has been EOL since c. 2016; most market stock is fake.
- Keep late or unknown OPA2604s at or below ±20 V: offset drift and rising Iq reported at ±24 V.
- **Counterfeits:** Rampant: a 2019 survey found 70 of 75 Yahoo Auctions OPA2604s fake. Remarked TL072s show ~3 MHz, 13 V/µs (genuine: 20 MHz, 25 V/µs, ~10.5 mA); markings may wipe off. BB and TI logos are both genuine. Safest: two OPA604s.

## Silicon changes

### Datasheet respec: Texas Instruments, c. 2015–2016 (diyAudio, 2016-08-24)

Unannounced same-part-number derating: a TI non-conformance form said the OPA2604 must be held to ±20 V; at ±24 V parts showed long-term offset drift and higher Iq, likely thermal. TI E2E gives heat near ±24 V as the EOL reason. Datasheet SBOS006A (Dec 2015) still says ±24 V.

- **Affected:** OPA2604AP, OPA2604AU, OPA2604AU/2K5, OPA2604APG4, OPA2604AUE4, OPA2604AUG4, OPA2604AU/2K5E4
- **How to tell:** No marking, datasheet or PCN change; communicated by non-conformance form. Date codes unknown; late TI-logo lots suspect. OPA604 single reportedly unaffected.
- **Audio impact:** Only ±20 to ±24 V designs risk offset drift and heat. Normal ±15 V rolling, distortion and noise are unaffected.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium: designs relying on ±24 V may drift or run hot; ±15 V use unaffected.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum operating supply (OPA2604) | ±24 V (PDS-1069E, SBOS006, SBOS006A) | ±20 V (non-conformance constraint; never printed in a datasheet) |
| Behaviour at ±24 V | Fully specified (PSRR spec covers ±5 to ±24 V) | Reported long-term large offset drift and increased Iq |
