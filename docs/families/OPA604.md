# OPA604 / OPA2604 (Burr-Brown FET-input, low-distortion audio op-amp)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** JFET-input

*Burr-Brown's early-90s FET-input audio op-amp with on-chip distortion rejection and ±24 V rails; the warm, "tube-like" (胆味) classic of CD-player, sound-card and phone Hi-Fi.*

**Technology:** FET-input voltage-feedback op-amp. The block diagram shows 'Distortion Rejection Circuitry' and an 'Output Stage', both marked 'Patents Granted: #5053718, 5019789'. The datasheet cites new circuit techniques and special laser trimming of dynamic circuit performance for low harmonic distortion, plus a laser-trimmed offset. A copper leadframe improves heat dissipation. Supply is ±4.5 V to ±24 V (±25 V absolute maximum). It is unity-gain stable and drives 600 Ω. 'Difet' is a community label (Soomal 2016 groups OPA2604 with 'DiFET-structure' parts); the OPA2604 datasheet does not use the word.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

One of the defining 'Burr-Brown sound' op-amps. English forums call it warm, laid back and easy to listen to. Japanese PC-audio and headphone modders call it 'the legendary op-amp' (伝説のオペアンプ), with gloss (艶), spaciousness, fat bouncy bass and soft, smooth highs; nabe ranks it B+ (Japanese sources not re-verified in this pass). Soomal (2010) calls it an op-amp with a strong '胆味' (valve flavour), and Soomal (2013) says its fame among enthusiasts is 'not much lower than NE5532'. Soomal teardowns confirm it in the vivo Xplay and Xplay 3S (OPA2604AU), Smartisan T1, Sony PHA-3 (two as LPF after ES9018S), Shanling H3A (I/V and LPF), SoundAware M1 and A280III, TempoTec Serenade USB and Teclast T51. Its ±24 V rating made it one of the few audio op-amps usable on high rails. In a 2016 review Soomal rated warm DiFET-type parts (including OPA2604) above OPA1612-type parts for audio on average. Newer Japanese listening tests (new_western_elec 2025, not re-verified) rank it below MUSES8820/8920 and NL8802/NL8902.

**How people describe the sound:** warm, tube-like / 胆味 (valve flavour), laid back, easy to listen to, musical, rich midrange, gloss / 艶, spacious, fat, bouncy bass, soft, smooth highs, 偏暖的音色 (warm-leaning tone)

**Typical uses:** CD player and DAC output / I-V stages (PCM DAC I/V converter is a datasheet application), DAC low-pass filter stages (Sony PHA-3, Shanling H3A, SoundAware A280III/M1), PC sound-card and USB DAC op-amp rolling, Headphone amplifiers, Smartphone Hi-Fi audio paths (vivo Xplay/Xplay 3S, Smartisan T1), Maverick D2 DAC stock part; Fosi rolling kit (not re-verified), Professional audio line stages on high (±18 to ±24 V) rails

**Caveats:**

- Fast (20 MHz GBW): a translated English headphone-amp op-amp survey (translated by 杜毅 for DiyEden, reposted by Soomal 2005) reports that an OPA2604AP oscillated above about 18 V in one Hansen-type amp but ran at 34 VDC in other amps. The author called it much harder to use than OPA132/134 and heard it as nearly identical to them.
- Needs adequate rails: the 0.0003% THD is specified at ±15 V. Soomal measured the Teclast T51 running it at about ±9.5 V and called the claim unachievable. Soomal said the Smartisan T1's OPA2604 showed high distortion, and that phones need a boost converter for it (T2 switched to OPA1612).
- Soomal (2010, T51 context): driving low-impedance headphones directly makes it sound thin and harsh, with more bass distortion.
- Some Japanese reviewers (Mineden) find the midrange thin (not re-verified).
- Colored 'warm' voicing: 2025 Japanese tests prefer newer parts for resolution (not re-verified). Meizu's 2014 marketing showed a CMRR chart favouring OPA1612 over OPA2604.
- OPA2604AP DIP is reported discontinued, so most market stock is fake (see counterfeit notes).
- The reported TI EOL rationale (package dissipation near ±24 V in DIP) is unconfirmed. SBOS006A gives PDIP the better RθJA (46.7 vs 107.9 °C/W for SOIC).

## Counterfeits

Fakes are reported to be extremely common for the OPA2604AP (DIP). In 2019 new_western_elec (nw-electric blog) reportedly found 70 of 75 OPA2604s bought on Yahoo Auctions were fake (93%; not re-verified). Reported fakes include remarked TL072-class dies, markings that wipe off with ethanol, and sanded or re-coated tops without moulded rounded edges on thinner bodies. Kokoro-navi (2011) reportedly documented fake OPA2134PA/OPA2604AP, and a fake was reported inside an Aune mini USB DAC (both not re-verified). TI E2E has an 'original or fake OPA2134/OPA2604' thread. Soomal (2010) compared a genuine part from a TI channel, a market fake and the chip pulled from a Teclast T51. The fake was obviously fake; the T51 chip differed from the genuine one only in marking clarity. Noting that most pin-compatible op-amps cannot run above ±18 V, Soomal stress-tested the T51 chip at ±23 V. It burned out, which proved nothing, and Soomal provisionally treated it as genuine. Tests: marking durability under solvent, package edge and mould finish, and parameters against the datasheet. A genuine OPA2604 draws about 10.5 mA total (both amplifiers, max 12 mA at ±15 V) with 20 MHz GBW and 25 V/µs, versus about 3 MHz and 13 V/µs for a TL072 remark. Genuine SOIC parts are marked 'OPA 2604AU' (TI addendum). BB-logo and TI-logo lots are both genuine, and no die change is documented: the electrical table is identical from 1997 to 2015. Safest route: buy the reportedly still-active OPA604AP/AU from authorised distributors and use two on a dual adapter.

## Related parts and alternatives

OPA1656 (TI CMOS; named in a TI E2E FAQ as the 'next-generation OPA2134 and OPA2604 replacement'; supply ±2.25 V to ±18 V per SBOS901C, so not a ±24 V drop-in), OPA1688 (TI; reportedly suggested by TI as an OPA2604 replacement; check its supply limit before high-rail use), OPA604AP/AU singles on a dual DIP-8 adapter (single-channel sibling, reportedly still active), OPA2134 (sibling Burr-Brown FET audio op-amp; ±18 V max), MUSES8920 (Japanese community successor; 'keeps OPA2604 softness with wider stage'), OPA1642 (TI JFET-input audio op-amp; ±18 V max, so not a high-rail drop-in)
