# OPA604 / OPA2604 (Burr-Brown FET-input, low-distortion audio op-amp)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** JFET-input

*Burr-Brown's early-90s FET-input audio op-amp with on-chip distortion rejection and ±24 V rails; the warm, "tube-like" (胆味) classic of CD-player, sound-card and phone Hi-Fi.*

**Technology:** FET-input voltage-feedback op-amp. The block diagram shows 'Distortion Rejection Circuitry' and an 'Output Stage', both marked 'Patents Granted: #5053718, 5019789'. The datasheet cites new circuit techniques and special laser trimming of dynamic circuit performance for low harmonic distortion, plus a laser-trimmed offset. A copper leadframe improves heat dissipation. Supply is ±4.5 V to ±24 V (±25 V absolute maximum) per the datasheets, but a 2016 report says late OPA2604 production had to be held to ±20 V (see silicon_changes). It is unity-gain stable and drives 600 Ω. 'Difet' is a community label (Soomal 2016 groups OPA2604 with 'DiFET-structure' parts); the OPA2604 datasheet does not use the word.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (2 recorded: Unclassified).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

One of the defining 'Burr-Brown sound' op-amps. English forums call it warm, laid back and easy to listen to. Japanese PC-audio and headphone bloggers call it 'the legendary op-amp' (伝説のオペアンプ, e.g. southenst.com), with gloss (艶), spaciousness, fat bouncy bass and soft, smooth highs; nabe ranks it B+ (not re-verified). Soomal (2010) calls it an op-amp with a strong '胆味' (valve flavour), and Soomal (2013) says its fame among enthusiasts is 'not much lower than NE5532'. Soomal teardowns confirm it in the vivo Xplay and Xplay 3S (OPA2604AU), Smartisan T1, Sony PHA-3 (two as LPF after ES9018S), Shanling H3A (I/V and LPF), SoundAware M1 and A280III, TempoTec Serenade USB and Teclast T51. Its ±24 V rating made it one of the few audio op-amps usable on high rails. In a 2016 review Soomal rated warm DiFET-type parts (including OPA2604) above OPA1612-type parts for audio on average. Newer Japanese listening tests (new_western_elec 2025, not re-verified) rank it below MUSES8820/8920 and NL8802/NL8902.

**How people describe the sound:** warm, tube-like / 胆味 (valve flavour), laid back, easy to listen to, musical, rich midrange, gloss / 艶, spacious, fat, bouncy bass, soft, smooth highs, 偏暖的音色 (warm-leaning tone)

**Typical uses:** CD player and DAC output / I-V stages (PCM DAC I/V converter is a datasheet application), DAC low-pass filter stages (Sony PHA-3, Shanling H3A, SoundAware A280III/M1), PC sound-card and USB DAC op-amp rolling, Headphone amplifiers, Smartphone Hi-Fi audio paths (vivo Xplay/Xplay 3S, Smartisan T1), Maverick D2 DAC stock part; Fosi rolling kit (not re-verified), Professional audio line stages on high (±18 to ±24 V) rails

**Caveats:**

- Fast (20 MHz GBW): a translated English headphone-amp op-amp survey (translated by 杜毅 for DiyEden, reposted by Soomal 2005) reports that an OPA2604AP oscillated above about 18 V in one Hansen-type amp but ran at 34 VDC in other amps. The author called it much harder to use than OPA132/134 and heard it as nearly identical to them.
- Needs adequate rails: the 0.0003% THD is specified at ±15 V. Soomal measured the Teclast T51 running it at about ±9.5 V and called the claim unachievable. Soomal said the Smartisan T1's OPA2604 showed high distortion, and that phones need a boost converter for it (T2 switched to OPA1612).
- Soomal (2010, T51 context): driving low-impedance headphones directly makes it sound thin and harsh, with more bass distortion.
- Some Japanese reviewers (Mineden) find the midrange thin (not re-verified).
- Colored 'warm' voicing: 2025 Japanese tests prefer newer parts for resolution (not re-verified). Meizu's 2014 marketing showed a CMRR chart favouring OPA1612 over OPA2604.
- OPA2604 (all packages) is EOL since c. 2016, so most market stock is fake (see counterfeit notes).
- High-rail use is the weak point: TI E2E says OPA2604 performance could not be guaranteed near its ±24 V limit because of heat/package dissipation, and a 2016 diyAudio report says late parts had to be held to ±20 V (offset drift and rising Iq at ±24 V). Keep late-lot or unknown-origin OPA2604s at or below ±20 V.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Unclassified: Texas Instruments, c. 2015-2016 (reported in diyAudio 'OPA2604 is dead', 24…

Unannounced supply derating of the same part number: a document quoted on diyAudio (2016) says the OPA2604 no longer supports ±24 V and must be constrained to ±20 V, with long-term large offset drift and increased Iq at ±24 V (thought to be thermal). A TI E2E answer gives the same reason for the discontinuation (performance could not be guaranteed near the ±24 V limit because of heat/package dissipation). Whether a die or process change caused it is not known.

- **When:** c. 2015-2016 (reported in diyAudio 'OPA2604 is dead', 24 Aug 2016, alongside TI's lifetime-buy / EOL)
- **Affected:** OPA2604AP, OPA2604AU, OPA2604AU/2K5, OPA2604APG4, OPA2604AUE4, OPA2604AUG4, OPA2604AU/2K5E4
- **How to tell old from new:** No marking, datasheet or PCN change is documented; SBOS006A (Dec 2015) still specifies ±4.5 to ±24 V. Affected date codes unknown. Late TI-logo lots are the suspect population; whether OPA604 singles are affected is unknown.
- **Audio impact:** Only high-rail designs (±20 to ±24 V pro-audio line stages, some DIY amps) are at risk: offset drift and extra heat. Typical ±15 V op-amp rolling is unaffected.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium - designs that rely on the OPA2604's unique ±24 V rating may drift or run hot with late lots; ±15 V use unaffected
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum operating supply | ±24 V (PDS-1069E, SBOS006, SBOS006A) | ±20 V (reported constraint; never printed in a datasheet) |
| Behaviour at ±24 V | Fully specified (PSRR spec covers ±5 to ±24 V) | Reported long-term large offset drift and increased Iq |

### 2. Unclassified: Texas Instruments, About 2016 (supply derating);

OPA2604 is no longer supported at ±24 V and must be limited to ±20 V. At ±24 V, symptoms included large long-term offset drift and increased Iq, which points to a thermal cause. In E2E thread 953479 ('Status of the OPA2604AU/2K5, PCN available?') a TI engineer said OPA2604 has EOL status and suggested using two OPA604 singles, which remain Active. The asker noted that TI had published no PCN or report. Distributors list OPA2604AU/2K5 as obsolete, while older TI datasheet order tables still show ACTIVE, so status for other OPA2604 part numbers (for example, OPA2604AP) is unclear. No die or fab PCN beyond the derating was found.

- **When:** About 2016 (supply derating); E2E thread 953479, about 2020 (OPA2604AU/2K5 status)
- **Affected:** OPA2604AU/2K5, OPA2604AU, OPA2604 (family, status disputed)
- **How to tell old from new:** Per the diyAudio thread 'OPA2604 is dead', the supply derating was communicated through non-conformance documentation, not a datasheet revision. OPA2604AU/2K5 is listed as Obsolete by distributors. No PCN or PDN was issued, according to the E2E asker.
- **Audio impact:** Rail-voltage headroom is reduced by 4 V per rail. Designs running ±22-24 V rails risk offset drift and higher quiescent current. Distortion and noise are unaffected at compliant supplies.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium - drop-in only for designs at ±20 V or below. Supply of the dual is at risk; the OPA604 single needs a two-package substitution.
- **Confidence:** medium

|---|---|---|
| Max supply voltage (OPA2604) | ±24 V | ±20 V |
| OPA2604AU/2K5 lifecycle | Active | Obsolete (distributor listing; TI E2E says EOL) |

## Counterfeits

Fakes are extremely common for the OPA2604, and all remaining stock is post-EOL. A survey by the 'fakecomponents' account (19 Sep 2019) found 70 of 75 OPA2604s bought individually on Yahoo Auctions were fake (93%); the new_western_elec blog (Oct 2019) reported it and advised against buying there. The nabe blog has a 'fake-looking TI op-amp' post, and a Yahoo Chiebukuro Q&A discusses fake OPA2604s ordered from abroad. Reported fakes include remarked TL072-class dies, markings that wipe off with ethanol, and sanded or re-coated tops without moulded rounded edges on thinner bodies (not re-verified). Kokoro-navi (2011) reportedly documented fake OPA2134PA/OPA2604AP, and a fake was reported inside an Aune mini USB DAC (both not re-verified). TI E2E has an 'original or fake OPA2134/OPA2604' thread. Soomal (2010) compared a genuine part from a TI channel, a market fake and the chip pulled from a Teclast T51: the fake was obviously fake; the T51 chip differed from the genuine one only in marking clarity. Soomal stress-tested the T51 chip at ±23 V; it burned out, which proved nothing (and late genuine parts are reportedly not rated for ±24 V anyway). Tests: marking durability under solvent, package edge and mould finish, and parameters against the datasheet. A genuine OPA2604 draws about 10.5 mA total (max 12 mA at ±15 V) with 20 MHz GBW and 25 V/µs, versus about 3 MHz and 13 V/µs for a TL072 remark. Genuine SOIC parts are marked 'OPA 2604AU' (TI addendum). BB-logo and TI-logo lots are both genuine; no die change is documented, but late lots reportedly need rails held to ±20 V. Safest route: buy the still-active OPA604AP/AU from authorised distributors and use two on a dual adapter.

## Related parts and alternatives

OPA1656 (TI CMOS; named in a TI E2E FAQ as the 'next-generation OPA2134 and OPA2604 replacement'; ±2.25 V to ±18 V per SBOS901C, so not a ±24 V drop-in), OPA1642 (TI JFET-input; suggested on TI E2E as a lower-noise, low-distortion OPA2604 alternative when 20 MHz is not needed; ±18 V max), OPA2156 (TI CMOS; suggested on TI E2E when more bandwidth is needed; check supply limit before high-rail use), OPA1688 (TI; reportedly suggested as an OPA2604 replacement, source not pinned down; check its supply limit), OPA604AP/AU singles on a dual DIP-8 adapter (single-channel sibling, ACTIVE on ti.com), OPA2134 (sibling Burr-Brown FET audio op-amp, still in PDIP but lower bandwidth; ±18 V max), MUSES8920 (Japanese community successor; 'keeps OPA2604 softness with wider stage')
