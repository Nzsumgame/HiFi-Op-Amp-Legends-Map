# OPA604 / OPA2604 (Burr-Brown FET-input, low-distortion audio op-amp)

Burr-Brown (c. 1991-2000 (OPA2604 datasheet ©1991; OPA2604E PSpice macromodel 'revised 3/21/92'; OPA604 datasheet ©1992)) → Texas Instruments (2000-present (OPA604); 2000 to c. 2016 (OPA2604)) · Tier A (legend / revision hazard)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** Unannounced supply derating under the same part number. [Details](#silicon-changes)

*Burr-Brown's early-90s FET-input audio op-amp with on-chip distortion rejection and ±24 V rails; the warm, "tube-like" (胆味) classic of CD-player, sound-card and phone Hi-Fi.*

| Slew rate | THD+N | Supply range |
|---|---|---|
| 25 V/µs typ (15 V/µs min) | 0.0003% typ | ±4.5 V to ±24 V operating; ±25 V absolute max (datasheet). Late OPA2604 production reportedly constrained to ±20 V (not in any datasheet) |

## Why it's rated

One of the defining 'Burr-Brown sound' op-amps. English forums call it warm, laid back and easy to listen to. Japanese PC-audio and headphone bloggers call it 'the legendary op-amp' (伝説のオペアンプ, e.g. southenst.com), with gloss (艶), spaciousness, fat bouncy bass and soft, smooth highs; nabe ranks it B+ (not re-verified). Soomal (2010) calls it an op-amp with a strong '胆味' (valve flavour), and Soomal (2013) says its fame among enthusiasts is 'not much lower than NE5532'. Soomal teardowns confirm it in the vivo Xplay and Xplay 3S (OPA2604AU), Smartisan T1, Sony PHA-3 (two as LPF after ES9018S), Shanling H3A (I/V and LPF), SoundAware M1 and A280III, TempoTec Serenade USB and Teclast T51. Its ±24 V rating made it one of the few audio op-amps usable on high rails. In a 2016 review Soomal rated warm DiFET-type parts (including OPA2604) above OPA1612-type parts for audio on average. Newer Japanese listening tests (new_western_elec 2025, not re-verified) rank it below MUSES8820/8920 and NL8802/NL8902.

**Described as:** warm, tube-like / 胆味 (valve flavour), laid back, easy to listen to, musical, rich midrange, gloss / 艶, spacious, fat, bouncy bass, soft, smooth highs, 偏暖的音色 (warm-leaning tone)

**Used in:** CD player and DAC output / I-V stages (PCM DAC I/V converter is a datasheet application), DAC low-pass filter stages (Sony PHA-3, Shanling H3A, SoundAware A280III/M1), PC sound-card and USB DAC op-amp rolling, Headphone amplifiers, Smartphone Hi-Fi audio paths (vivo Xplay/Xplay 3S, Smartisan T1), Maverick D2 DAC stock part; Fosi rolling kit (not re-verified), Professional audio line stages on high (±18 to ±24 V) rails

## Watch out

- Fast (20 MHz GBW): a translated English headphone-amp op-amp survey (translated by 杜毅 for DiyEden, reposted by Soomal 2005) reports that an OPA2604AP oscillated above about 18 V in one Hansen-type amp but ran at 34 VDC in other amps. The author called it much harder to use than OPA132/134 and heard it as nearly identical to them.
- Needs adequate rails: the 0.0003% THD is specified at ±15 V. Soomal measured the Teclast T51 running it at about ±9.5 V and called the claim unachievable. Soomal said the Smartisan T1's OPA2604 showed high distortion, and that phones need a boost converter for it (T2 switched to OPA1612).
- Soomal (2010, T51 context): driving low-impedance headphones directly makes it sound thin and harsh, with more bass distortion.
- Some Japanese reviewers (Mineden) find the midrange thin (not re-verified).
- Colored 'warm' voicing: 2025 Japanese tests prefer newer parts for resolution (not re-verified). Meizu's 2014 marketing showed a CMRR chart favouring OPA1612 over OPA2604.
- OPA2604 (all packages) is EOL since c. 2016, so most market stock is fake (see counterfeit notes).
- High-rail use is the weak point: TI E2E says OPA2604 performance could not be guaranteed near its ±24 V limit because of heat/package dissipation, and a 2016 diyAudio report says late parts had to be held to ±20 V (offset drift and rising Iq at ±24 V). Keep late-lot or unknown-origin OPA2604s at or below ±20 V.
- **Counterfeits:** Fakes are extremely common for the OPA2604, and all remaining stock is post-EOL. A survey by the 'fakecomponents' account (19 Sep 2019) found 70 of 75 OPA2604s bought individually on Yahoo Auctions were fake (93%); the new_western_elec blog (Oct 2019) reported it and advised against buying there. The nabe blog has a 'fake-looking TI op-amp' post, and a Yahoo Chiebukuro Q&A discusses fake OPA2604s ordered from abroad. Reported fakes include remarked TL072-class dies, markings that wipe off with ethanol, and sanded or re-coated tops without moulded rounded edges on thinner bodies (not re-verified). Kokoro-navi (2011) reportedly documented fake OPA2134PA/OPA2604AP, and a fake was reported inside an Aune mini USB DAC (both not re-verified). TI E2E has an 'original or fake OPA2134/OPA2604' thread. Soomal (2010) compared a genuine part from a TI channel, a market fake and the chip pulled from a Teclast T51: the fake was obviously fake; the T51 chip differed from the genuine one only in marking clarity. Soomal stress-tested the T51 chip at ±23 V; it burned out, which proved nothing (and late genuine parts are reportedly not rated for ±24 V anyway). Tests: marking durability under solvent, package edge and mould finish, and parameters against the datasheet. A genuine OPA2604 draws about 10.5 mA total (max 12 mA at ±15 V) with 20 MHz GBW and 25 V/µs, versus about 3 MHz and 13 V/µs for a TL072 remark. Genuine SOIC parts are marked 'OPA 2604AU' (TI addendum). BB-logo and TI-logo lots are both genuine; no die change is documented, but late lots reportedly need rails held to ±20 V. Safest route: buy the still-active OPA604AP/AU from authorised distributors and use two on a dual adapter.

## Silicon changes

### Datasheet respec: Texas Instruments, c. 2015-2016 (TI 'Non-conforming' form quoted in the diyAudio thread 'OPA2604 is dead', 24 Aug 2016). Datasheet SBOS006A (Dec 2015) still specifies ±4.5 to ±24 V.

Unannounced supply derating under the same part number. TI told customers that the OPA2604 no longer supports ±24 V and must be constrained to ±20 V: at ±24 V, parts showed large long-term offset drift and increased supply current, thought to be thermal (the dual runs hot). A TI E2E answer gives the same reason for the later discontinuation: performance could not be guaranteed near the published ±24 V limit because of heat and package dissipation. Whether a die or process change caused it is not known.

- **Affected:** OPA2604AP, OPA2604AU, OPA2604AU/2K5, OPA2604APG4, OPA2604AUE4, OPA2604AUG4, OPA2604AU/2K5E4
- **How to tell:** No marking, datasheet or PCN change. The derating was communicated through a TI non-conformance form, not a datasheet revision. Affected date codes are unknown; late TI-logo lots are the suspect population. Per diyAudio, the OPA604 single does not have the thermal problem.
- **Audio impact:** Only high-rail designs (±20 to ±24 V pro-audio line stages, some DIY amps) are at risk: offset drift and extra heat. Typical ±15 V op-amp rolling is unaffected; distortion and noise at compliant supplies are unaffected.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium - designs that rely on the OPA2604's ±24 V rating may drift or run hot; drop-in only at ±20 V or below; ±15 V use unaffected
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum operating supply (OPA2604) | ±24 V (PDS-1069E, SBOS006, SBOS006A) | ±20 V (non-conformance constraint; never printed in a datasheet) |
| Behaviour at ±24 V | Fully specified (PSRR spec covers ±5 to ±24 V) | Reported long-term large offset drift and increased Iq |
