# LME49880

**JFET-input audio op-amp, dual (Overture E-Series)** · National Semiconductor (2009-12–2011) → Texas Instruments (2011–c. 2016–17) · Tier C (niche / baseline)

*National's late Overture JFET dual (7 nV/√Hz, 0.00003% THD+N): PSOP-only, obsolete, rolled on DIP adapters in Japan.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | THD+N | Supply range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 nV/√Hz typ | 25 MHz typ | ±18 V/µs typ | 0.00003% typ | ±5 V to ±17 V |

## Why it's rated

A niche part, discussed mainly by Japanese op-amp rollers and in an early diyAudio thread. Japanese users reportedly call it tight, clean and high-resolution, with a 'rich NatSemi tone'. It is PSOP-only, so shops (diyinhk; reportedly Kyoritsu/eleshop, Digit, Bispa) sell it on DIP-8 adapters. No independent measurements found; the datasheet figures support its low-distortion reputation.

**Described as:** tight, clean, high-resolution, rich 'NatSemi tone', clean mids and highs

**Used in:** DAC I/V and output/LPF stages via DIP-8 adapter, Headphone amps and preamps with DIP-8 sockets, Hi-fi preamp and filter stages (datasheet positioning)

## Watch out

- PSOP-8 with exposed pad only, no DIP; adapters vary in layout, decoupling and pad connection (rail unconfirmed).
- Obsolete at TI; supply is old, broker or adapter stock, with rising price and fake risk.
- Praise is subjective, from a small mostly Japanese community; no independent measurements.
- **Counterfeits:** No LME49880 fake reports found. Since TI obsoleted it (c. 2016–17), 'new' stock is old, broker or adapter-mounted. Genuine parts are PSOP-8 with an exposed underside pad; a padless SOIC-8 is a red flag. Check marking and date-code era.

## Silicon changes

None documented. Compare datasheet revisions before assuming old and new parts are identical.
