# LME49880 (National / TI Overture E-Series dual JFET-input audio op-amp)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*National's late Overture E-Series JFET dual (7 nV/√Hz, 0.00003% THD+N). It came only in PSOP, is now obsolete, and Japanese op-amp rollers run it on DIP adapters.*

**Technology:** Dual JFET-input audio op-amp from National's LME 'Overture E-Series' (siblings: LME49720, LME49860, LME49990). The datasheet says the JFET process was used to lower flicker noise and the noise corner frequency. Supply range is ±5 V to ±17 V. It is unity-gain stable, drives capacitive loads up to 100 pF, and has typical input bias of 5 pA. The only package is an 8-lead PSOP with an exposed pad (MR suffix; TI's package addendum calls it 'SO PowerPAD').

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche part, discussed mostly in the Japanese op-amp-rolling community and in an early diyAudio thread. Japanese users reportedly call it tight, clean and high-resolution, with a 'rich NatSemi tone'. It exists only in PSOP-8, so shops sell it pre-mounted on DIP-8 adapter boards: diyinhk's 'LME49880 DIP Version' is confirmed, and Kyoritsu/eleshop, Digit and Bispa reportedly do the same. The Japanese review and Q&A pages were not re-read, so the sonic claims are unverified folklore. No independent measurements (ASR, Samuel Groner) were found. The datasheet figures (7 nV/√Hz, 0.00003% THD+N) back its reputation as a low-distortion JFET part.

**How people describe the sound:** tight, clean, high-resolution, rich 'NatSemi tone', clean mids and highs

**Typical uses:** Op-amp swapping in DACs (I/V and output/LPF stages) via a DIP-8 adapter board, Op-amp swapping in headphone amps and preamps with DIP-8 sockets, Designed for high-fidelity audio preamp/filter stages (datasheet positioning)

**Caveats:**

- PSOP-8 with exposed pad only. Socketed use needs a third-party DIP adapter, whose layout, decoupling and exposed-pad connection can affect results. Which supply rail the pad must connect to is not yet confirmed.
- The part is obsolete at TI (process-technology end-of-life). Supply is now old stock, broker stock or adapter boards, and prices and counterfeit risk rise accordingly.
- The praise is subjective and comes from a small, mostly Japanese community whose pages were not re-read. No independent measurements were found.

## Counterfeits

No LME49880-specific counterfeit reports were found. TI has obsoleted the part (process-technology end-of-life, about 2016-2017), so any 'new' stock is old inventory, broker stock or pre-mounted DIP adapter boards. Genuine parts come in an 8-lead PSOP with an exposed thermal pad on the underside. A plain SOIC-8 body with no pad, marked LME49880, is a red flag, although the pad is hidden once the chip is soldered to an adapter. Check that the top marking and date code fit the National (2009-2011) or TI era, and buy from reputable shops.
