# AD797 (ultralow-noise, ultralow-distortion bipolar single)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*ADI's 0.9 nV/√Hz, −120 dB THD single op amp. It is a reference part for moving-coil phono and mic preamps, and a long-standing rival to the OPA627 in op-amp-rolling debates.*

**Technology:** Bipolar, single-channel, voltage-feedback. Ultralow-noise bipolar input stage, 110 MHz gain-bandwidth (specified at G = 1000) and 20 V/µs slew. ADI makes no dual or quad version, so dual sockets need an adapter. ADI PCN 26_0029 (2026) places AD797ARZ/BRZ among its Complementary Bipolar (CB) process products; this comes from a search summary of the PCN.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (1 recorded: Unclassified).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

The benchmark ultralow-noise bipolar single. Builders use it for MC phono head amps (audioXpress MC100, Wyn Palmer MC preamp), mic preamps and DAC I/V stages, and it has long been set against the OPA627 in Head-Fi and diyAudio debates. Japanese reviewers reportedly call its sound 'satin-finish', robust and crisper than the OPA627 (not re-verified). Soomal (China) groups it with the AD847 as famous for big dynamics, a grand sound and powerful bass. Soomal's Creative AE-9 review says the AD797 (and OPA1611) had better dynamics and resolution than the NE5534 but sounded too hard to be pleasing there. Engineering sources use it as a reference low-noise part. Its place in Samuel Groner's distortion survey is unverified.

**How people describe the sound:** sweet, sublime clarity (Head-Fi; not re-verified), satin-finish, high-quality sound (Zigsow JP; not re-verified), robust, full-bodied; crisper and more 'digital' than OPA627 (JP reviews; not re-verified), huge dynamics, grand sound, powerful bass (Soomal: 以动态大，声音大气，低频气势十足闻名), ADI-style emphasised bass and treble with powerful but dry bass; Soomal says AD847/AD797 share the trend, better dynamics and resolution than NE5534 but too hard, not pleasing (Soomal AE-9 review: 偏硬，不够讨喜)

**Typical uses:** moving-coil phono head amps and RIAA preamps, microphone and mixing-console preamps, DAC I/V converters, test and measurement low-noise front ends (e.g. precision current sources, sonar LNAs), single-channel op-amp rolling, usually on a dual adapter

**Caveats:**

- Sensitive to layout, decoupling and capacitive load, so it is not a safe blind drop-in for rolling. The datasheet has a 'Driving Capacitive Loads' section (radiolocman Rev. K p.13).
- Single only. Dual sockets need an adapter board, which adds lead length and oscillation risk.
- High current noise (about 2 pA/√Hz) makes it a poor fit for high source or feedback impedances. One builder's datasheet review found it runs at gain 2, correcting an earlier 'needs gain ≥10' assumption, but loses its e_n advantage in a 20 kΩ network through i_n×R.
- Draws about 8 mA per amplifier, more than typical audio duals.
- Heavily counterfeited, especially DIP parts (including ANZ) and any metal-can 'AD797'.
- Sonic descriptors are subjective and mostly single-source forum or review impressions.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Unclassified: Analog Devices, 2026 (PCN 26_0029 Rev. -; exact publication and first-ship…

ADI PCN 26_0029, 'Qualification of ADI Camas Wafer Fab CB Processes', qualifies the Camas, WA wafer fab for Complementary Bipolar (CB) products, and a search summary lists AD797ARZ and AD797BRZ among them. This is a fab-site move of the same process, not a stated die redesign. The source fab is not confirmed: a related PCN (25_0087) moved CBCMOS1 products from Wilmington, MA to Camas.

- **When:** 2026 (PCN 26_0029 Rev. -; exact publication and first-ship dates not captured)
- **Affected:** AD797ARZ, AD797BRZ
- **How to tell old from new:** None visible. Per the PCN, traceability is kept through standard ADI lot traceability, so no top-mark change is stated. Old and new fab can be told apart only by lot or date code through ADI or the distributor, and no cut-over date code was captured.
- **Audio impact:** None expected. A fab-site qualification normally keeps the data sheet unchanged, but this was not confirmed from the PCN text, and no measurements comparing old- and new-fab parts are known.
- **Drop-in risk:** low — same process qualified at a second fab, no stated data-sheet or marking change; the full PCN text was not read
- **Confidence:** medium

## Counterfeits

Counterfeiting is heavy. A 2021 diyAudio thread ('*Thousands Sold*, Great Reviews... Received Fake AD797 Like LM741. Did You Buy These?') reports thousands of fakes sold on eBay (over 2,500 in five-packs alone; seller AdeleParts2010). They behaved like LM741s: 33.8 nV/√Hz noise against the genuine 0.9 nV/√Hz, about 1 mA supply current against 8.2 mA, and 0.3 V to 0.9 V offset against 80 µV max. Pin 8 was open circuit, and the offset-null network looked like an LM741's. Negative eBay feedback later disappeared. A Head-Fi thread ('Cheapish AD797 and OPA627 on eBay, fakes?') and a Polish audiostereo.pl thread ('AD797ANZ fake or not') report the same problem; neither was re-checked. Soomal (China) says ADI never made a metal-can AD797, so every gold metal-can 'AD797' is fake (Soomal's statement, not an ADI document). Screening: (1) measure quiescent current, about 8 mA per part; (2) measure offset (≤80 µV) and noise; (3) check pin 8 for a diode junction; (4) reject metal cans and unusually cheap DIP AN/BN/ANZ. Buy only from authorized distributors.
