# AD797 (ultralow-noise, ultralow-distortion bipolar single)

**Tier** A: legend / hazard · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*ADI's 0.9 nV/√Hz, −120 dB THD single op amp. It is a reference part for moving-coil phono and mic preamps, and a long-standing rival to the OPA627 in op-amp-rolling debates.*

**Technology:** Bipolar, single-channel, voltage-feedback. Ultralow-noise bipolar input stage, 110 MHz gain-bandwidth (a high-gain figure) and 20 V/µs slew. ADI makes no dual or quad version, so dual sockets need an adapter.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

The benchmark ultralow-noise bipolar single. Builders use it for MC phono head amps (audioXpress MC100, Wyn Palmer MC preamp), mic preamps and DAC I/V stages, and it has long been set against the OPA627 in Head-Fi and diyAudio debates. Japanese reviewers reportedly call its sound 'satin-finish', robust and crisper than the OPA627 (not re-verified). Soomal (China) groups it with the AD847 as famous for big dynamics, a grand sound and powerful bass. Soomal's Creative AE-9 review says the AD797 (and OPA1611) had better dynamics and resolution than the NE5534 but sounded too hard to be pleasing there. Engineering sources use it as a reference low-noise part. Its place in Samuel Groner's distortion survey is unverified.

**How people describe the sound:** sweet, sublime clarity (Head-Fi; not re-verified), satin-finish, high-quality sound (Zigsow JP; not re-verified), robust, full-bodied; crisper and more 'digital' than OPA627 (JP reviews; not re-verified), huge dynamics, grand sound, powerful bass (Soomal: 以动态大，声音大气，低频气势十足闻名), ADI-style emphasised bass and treble with powerful but dry bass; Soomal says AD847/AD797 share the trend, better dynamics and resolution than NE5534 but too hard, not pleasing (Soomal AE-9 review: 偏硬，不够讨喜)

**Typical uses:** moving-coil phono head amps and RIAA preamps, microphone and mixing-console preamps, DAC I/V converters, test and measurement low-noise front ends (e.g. precision current sources, sonar LNAs), single-channel op-amp rolling, usually on a dual adapter

**Caveats:**

- Sensitive to layout, decoupling and capacitive load, so it is not a safe blind drop-in for rolling. The datasheet has a 'Driving Capacitive Loads' section.
- Single only. Dual sockets need an adapter board, which adds lead length and oscillation risk.
- High current noise (about 2 pA/√Hz) makes it a poor fit for high source or feedback impedances. One builder's datasheet review found it runs at gain 2, correcting an earlier 'needs gain ≥10' assumption, but loses its e_n advantage in a 20 kΩ network through i_n×R.
- Draws about 8 mA per amplifier, more than typical audio duals.
- Heavily counterfeited, especially DIP parts (including ANZ) and any metal-can 'AD797'.
- Sonic descriptors are subjective and mostly single-source forum or review impressions.

## Counterfeits

Counterfeiting is heavy. A 2021 diyAudio thread ('Thousands sold, great reviews received: fake AD797 like LM741, did you buy these?') reports one eBay seller's fakes measuring about 33.8 nV/√Hz noise and about 1 mA supply current, against the genuine 0.9 nV/√Hz and 8.2 mA typ. These figures come from discovery notes and were not re-verified. A Head-Fi thread ('Cheapish AD797 and OPA627 on eBay, fakes?') covers the same problem. A Polish audiostereo.pl thread titled 'AD797ANZ fake or not' shows that Pb-free DIPs are also suspected. Soomal (China) says the best-known invented gold metal-can op amp is the 'AD797', because ADI never made an AD797 metal can (verified Soomal text; Soomal's statement, not an ADI document). Screening: (1) measure quiescent current, about 8 mA per part; (2) check noise and bandwidth; (3) reject any metal-can part and any unusually cheap DIP AN/BN/ANZ. Buy only from authorized distributors.
