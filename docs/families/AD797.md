# AD797 (AD797AN / AD797BN / AD797AR / AD797BR)

**Bipolar single, ultralow-noise, ultralow-distortion** · Analog Devices (1992–present) · Tier A (legend / revision hazard)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** 'Qualification of ADI Camas Wafer Fab CB Processes' qualifies Camas, WA for Complementary Bipolar products using an existing process; AD797ARZ/BRZ reportedly listed. [Details](#silicon-changes)

*ADI's 0.9 nV/√Hz, −120 dB THD single: the reference for MC phono and mic preamps, and the OPA627's old rival.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | THD+N | Supply range | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.9 nV/√Hz typ, 1.2 max; 50 nV p-p 0.1–10 Hz | 110 MHz | 20 V/µs typ; settles 800 ns to 16 bits | −120 dB THD | ±5 V to ±15 V specified (10–36 V total, unconfirmed) | 8.2 mA typ (about 8 mA); fakes draw about 1 mA |

## Why it's rated

The benchmark low-noise bipolar single, used in MC head amps (audioXpress MC100, Wyn Palmer), mic preamps and DAC I/V, and long set against the OPA627 on Head-Fi and diyAudio. Japanese reviewers reportedly hear a 'satin-finish', crisper sound. Soomal praises big dynamics and bass but found it too hard in the Creative AE-9.

**Described as:** sweet, sublime clarity, satin-finish (Zigsow), robust, crisper than OPA627, huge dynamics, powerful bass, dry, emphasised bass/treble, too hard (偏硬)

**Used in:** Moving-coil phono head amps and RIAA preamps, Microphone and console preamps, DAC I/V converters, Low-noise test and measurement front ends, Single-channel rolling on a dual adapter

## Watch out

- Sensitive to layout, decoupling and capacitive load; not a safe blind drop-in (datasheet 'Driving Capacitive Loads').
- Single only; dual adapters add lead length and oscillation risk.
- About 2 pA/√Hz current noise: loses its edge with high source or feedback impedances. Works at gain 2.
- Draws about 8 mA per amplifier, more than typical audio duals.
- Heavily counterfeited, especially DIP parts (including ANZ) and any metal-can 'AD797'.
- **Counterfeits:** A 2021 diyAudio thread found thousands of eBay fakes behaving like LM741s: 33.8 nV/√Hz, about 1 mA, 0.3–0.9 V offset, pin 8 open. Soomal says no metal-can AD797 exists. Check Iq (about 8 mA), offset (≤80 µV), noise; buy authorized.

## Silicon changes

### Fab / process transfer: Analog Devices, 2026 · PCN 26_0029 Rev. -

'Qualification of ADI Camas Wafer Fab CB Processes' qualifies Camas, WA for Complementary Bipolar products using an existing process; AD797ARZ/BRZ reportedly listed. Same process at another fab, no stated die redesign; source fab unnamed. Related PCN 25_0087 moved CBCMOS1 from Wilmington to Camas.

- **Affected:** AD797ARZ, AD797BRZ
- **How to tell:** Nothing visible; no top-mark change. Old- and new-fab parts are distinguishable only by lot or date code via ADI or distributor. Cut-over date code unknown.
- **Audio impact:** None expected; ADI states no fit, form, function or reliability impact and unchanged limits. No old-vs-new comparisons known.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: same process at another fab; no stated data-sheet or marking change. Full parts list unread.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Wafer fab site | previous ADI fab (not named in the sources seen) | ADI Camas, WA (existing qualified CB process) |
