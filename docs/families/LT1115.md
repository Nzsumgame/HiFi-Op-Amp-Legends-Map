# LT1115

**Bipolar ultralow-noise audio single** · Linear Technology (1989–2017) → Analog Devices (2017–present) · Tier C (niche / baseline)

*Linear Tech's 1989 0.9 nV/√Hz audio single, a cult pick for MC/MM phono and mic preamps with monitor-like resolution.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | THD+N | Supply range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.9 nV/√Hz typ; 1.2 nV/√Hz max | 40 MHz min (70 MHz typ unconfirmed) | 10 V/µs min; >15 V/µs typ | 0.002% THD (AV −10); 0.002% CCIF IMD (AV +10) | ±22 V (probably abs. max); ±18 V unconfirmed |

## Why it's rated

Billed in 1989 as 'the lowest noise audio operational amplifier available'. The 1990s rec.audio FAQ praised the LT1028 and LT1115 for phono, head amps and mic preamps. diyAudio calls it 'very interesting sounding'; Japanese hobbyists call it the most monitor-like LT part. A srad.jp commenter notes AD797-level noise at half the price. No ASR or Samuel Groner measurements found.

**Described as:** very interesting sounding, monitor-like, exceptional resolution, clean and quiet

**Used in:** MC/MM phono RIAA equaliser / preamp, Moving-coil head amp, Microphone preamplifier, Low-noise measurement preamp with low source impedance, Single-op-amp socket rolling in DIY gear (Audiokarma, diyAudio)

## Watch out

- Single only; dual sockets need an adapter.
- Only SMD is the 16-lead wide SOIC with odd pinout; no SO-8; tin-lead LT1115CSW obsolete.
- Expensive: about 20 USD (Frieve) versus about 2.7 USD for the similar-noise ADA4898-1.
- Optimised for low source impedance; with high-impedance sources the 1.2 pA/√Hz current noise erases the benefit.
- rec.audio FAQ says 'not unity gain stable'; macromodel says internally compensated. 40 MHz GBW needs careful layout.
- **Counterfeits:** No documented counterfeits. Bulk '50pcs LT1115CN8' lots of a ~20 USD part suggest remarks or pulls. Buy authorised; check logo, top mark, date code and noise. Any 'LT1115 SO-8' is fake; new LT1115CSW stock is old or recovered.

## Silicon changes

None documented. Compare datasheet revisions before assuming old and new parts are identical.
