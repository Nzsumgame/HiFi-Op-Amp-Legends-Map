# LT1115 (single ultralow-noise, low-distortion bipolar audio op amp)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*Linear Tech's 1989 audio-optimised 0.9 nV/rtHz bipolar single, a cult pick for MC/MM phono and mic preamps, prized for monitor-like resolution.*

**Technology:** Bipolar, NPN input, internally compensated single op amp (the LTC macromodel header says 'extended phase compensation' and an input differential-mode clamp). Audio-optimised ultralow noise, 0.9 nV/rtHz @ 1 kHz typ. Designed and made by Linear Technology; first data sheet Dec 1989.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Linear Technology's dedicated ultralow-noise audio op amp. It is a long-standing enthusiast favourite for low-source-impedance stages: MC/MM phono (RIAA) preamps, MC head amps and microphone preamps. The 1990s rec.audio FAQ put it among the 'highly recommended' singles and said the LT1028 and LT1115 'are very well received for phono preamps, head amplifiers, and microphone preamplifiers'. diyAudio members call it 'very interesting sounding' and group it with the LT1028 and LT1122 as the LT parts worth trying. Japanese hobbyists call it the most monitor-like LT part, with exceptional resolution; it appears in Japanese phono-EQ builds and on Zigsow alongside the AD797, THS4631 and LME49990 as a famous single. A srad.jp commenter noted its datasheet noise equals the AD797's at about half the Mouser price. It is still used in new DIY designs, e.g. an LT1115 + LT1010 MC/MM phono preamp (2021-2025). No Audio Science Review or Samuel Groner measurement was located this run.

**How people describe the sound:** very interesting sounding (diyAudio), monitor-like (JP community), exceptional resolution (JP community), clean, quiet with low-impedance sources

**Typical uses:** MC/MM phono RIAA equaliser / preamp, moving-coil head amp, microphone preamplifier, low-noise line / measurement preamp with low source impedance, single-op-amp socket rolling in DIY gear

**Caveats:**

- Single only (no dual or quad), so dual sockets need an adapter.
- The only corroborated SMD version is a 16-lead wide SOIC (SW16) with a non-standard single pinout; no SO-8 was found.
- Expensive: about 20 USD per Frieve, versus about 2.7 USD for the ADA4898-1 with similar noise.
- Bipolar input with noise optimised for low source impedance, so it offers little benefit with high-impedance sources.
- The high-GBW (tens of MHz) part can oscillate in careless layouts or sockets. The rec.audio FAQ claimed the LT1115 is 'not unity gain stable'. This is unverified: the LTC macromodel describes it as internally compensated. Check the datasheet for the minimum stable gain before using it as a follower.

## Counterfeits

No documented counterfeit analysis was found, but web search was unavailable this run. Marketplace spam pages advertise 'free shipping 50pcs LT1115CN8' lots. Cheap bulk lots of a part that sells for about $20 each through ADI channels (per Frieve) are a strong red flag for remarked parts or pulls. Buy from ADI or authorised distributors. Check that the Linear/ADI logo, top mark and date code match genuine parts, and that measured noise and bias current match the datasheet. No SO-8 LT1115 was found in any library or distributor listing (SMD is SW16 only), so treat an 'LT1115 SO-8' offer with suspicion. Confidence: low.

## Related parts and alternatives

LT1028 / LT1028A (Linear/ADI sibling; ultralow noise, single, optimised for very low source impedance), ADA4898-1 / ADA4898-2 (ADI; 0.9 nV/rtHz per Frieve's comparison, much cheaper, dual available), AD797 (ADI single; datasheet noise described as equal to the LT1115's by a srad.jp commenter), OPA1611 / OPA1612 (TI low-noise bipolar audio), LME49990 (National/TI 0.9 nV/rtHz single; discontinued)

- The session's WebSearch budget was used up before this family started (0 of the requested 15+ searches ran). Everything here comes from the discovery notes, local scratchpad copies and GitHub code search. A follow-up search pass is needed.
- The Rev A (1115fa) print date and LT/TP footer code are unknown. Confirm them from the PDF header/footer or ADI page, and check whether any 1115fb or later revision exists.
- No online copy of the original Dec 1989 data sheet was found. Look in Wayback snapshots of linear.com and in LTC databooks (1990 Linear Databook and later).
- Inferred URL patterns, listed only in archive_seed_urls: http://www.linear.com/product/LT1115, http://cds.linear.com/docs/en/datasheet/1115fa.pdf, http://cds.linear.com/docs/Datasheet/1115fa.pdf, http://www.linear.com/docs/Datasheet/1115fa.pdf, http://www.linear.com/pdf/1115fa.pdf, http://www.linear.com/pdf/1115.pdf. None were seen in any result.
- LT1115CS8 (from the discovery notes) is not corroborated. The libraries show only LT1115CN8 and LT1115CSW (SW16). Confirm against the datasheet order table; also check whether industrial (I) or A grades ever existed.
- ADI lifecycle status (active / NRND / last-time-buy) is not verified.
- The rec.audio FAQ says the LT1115 is not unity-gain stable, while the LTC macromodel says 'internal comp.'. Confirm the minimum stable gain in the datasheet.
- GBW figures conflict between sources (70 MHz, 40 MHz, and 15 MHz in one JSON). i_n, Iq, output current, supply range and bias current were not verified; read them from the lt1115fa.pdf tables.
- No PCN (fab transfer, die change, assembly-site change) was found for the Linear or ADI eras, and no web search was possible. Check ADI's PCN database for LT1115.
