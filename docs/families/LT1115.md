# LT1115 (single ultralow-noise, low-distortion bipolar audio op amp)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*Linear Tech's 1989 audio-optimised 0.9 nV/rtHz bipolar single, a cult pick for MC/MM phono and mic preamps, prized for monitor-like resolution.*

**Technology:** Bipolar single op amp with NPN inputs and internal compensation. The LTC macromodel header says 'extended phase compensation' and notes an input differential-mode clamp. Voltage noise is 0.9 nV/rtHz typ and 1.2 nV/rtHz max at 1 kHz, and both voltage and current noise are 100% tested. GBW is 40 MHz min. Linear Technology designed and made it, and the first data sheet is dated Dec 1989. Analog Devices has owned it since 2017.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Linear Technology's dedicated ultralow-noise audio op amp. Its datasheet called it 'the lowest noise audio operational amplifier available', which is a vendor claim from 1989. Enthusiasts have long used it in low-source-impedance stages: MC/MM phono (RIAA) preamps, MC head amps and microphone preamps. The 1990s rec.audio FAQ said the LT1028 and LT1115 'are very well received for phono preamps, head amplifiers, and microphone preamplifiers'. diyAudio members call it 'very interesting sounding' and group it with the LT1028 and LT1122. Japanese hobbyists call it the most monitor-like LT part and use it in phono-EQ builds. A srad.jp commenter said its datasheet noise equals the AD797's at about half the Mouser price. Audiokarma has a thread on LT1115 op amps. No Audio Science Review or Samuel Groner measurement was located.

**How people describe the sound:** very interesting sounding (diyAudio), monitor-like (JP community), exceptional resolution (JP community), clean and quiet with low-impedance sources

**Typical uses:** MC/MM phono RIAA equaliser / preamp, moving-coil head amp, microphone preamplifier, low-noise instrumentation / measurement preamp with low source impedance, single-op-amp socket rolling in DIY gear

**Caveats:**

- Single only (no dual or quad), so dual sockets need an adapter.
- The only SMD version is the 16-lead wide SOIC (SW16), which has a non-standard single pinout. No SO-8 exists, and the tin-lead LT1115CSW is obsolete at Mouser.
- Expensive: about 20 USD according to Frieve, against about 2.7 USD for the ADA4898-1 with similar noise.
- The noise is optimised for low source impedance. With high-impedance sources, current noise (about 1.2 pA/rtHz) cancels the benefit.
- The rec.audio FAQ claimed it is 'not unity gain stable', but the LTC macromodel says it is internally compensated. The minimum stable gain was not confirmed this run. With 40 MHz+ GBW, careful layout and decoupling are needed.

## Counterfeits

No documented counterfeit analysis was found. Marketplace pages advertise bulk 'free shipping 50pcs LT1115CN8' lots. Cheap bulk lots of a part that sells for about 20 USD each through ADI channels are a red flag for remarked parts or pulls. Buy from ADI or authorised distributors. Check the Linear/ADI logo, top mark and date code, and check that measured noise (0.9 nV/rtHz typ, 1.2 max at 1 kHz) and bias current match the datasheet. No SO-8 LT1115 exists (SMD is SW16 only), so treat any 'LT1115 SO-8' offer as fake. The tin-lead LT1115CSW is obsolete at Mouser, so new-stock offers of it are probably old or recovered stock. Confidence: low.

## Related parts and alternatives

LT1028 / LT1028A (Linear/ADI sibling; ultralow noise, single, optimised for very low source impedance), ADA4898-1 / ADA4898-2 (ADI; about 0.9 nV/rtHz per Frieve's comparison, much cheaper, dual available), AD797 (ADI single; a srad.jp commenter said its datasheet noise equals the LT1115's), OPA1611 / OPA1612 (TI low-noise bipolar audio), LME49990 (National/TI 0.9 nV/rtHz single; availability not verified)

- No online copy of the original Dec 1989 data sheet was confirmed. Check whether the older mirrors (datasheetcatalog, alldatasheet 70309, radiolocman di=338779, digchip) hold a pre-Rev A copy, and look in LTC databooks.

- Minimum stable gain is unconfirmed: the rec.audio FAQ says 'not unity gain stable', while the macromodel says internally compensated.
- Typical GBW (70 MHz per Wickerlib), whether 1.2 pA/rtHz is typ or max, the supply spec condition (+/-18 V vs +/-22 V abs max), Iq, output current and bias current are not verified from the tables.
