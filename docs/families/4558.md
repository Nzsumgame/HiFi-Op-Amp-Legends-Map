# NJM4558 / JRC4558D / RC4558 (dual general-purpose bipolar "dual 741")

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** General-purpose baselines

*Raytheon's 1971 "dual 741". It is the Tube Screamer's JRC4558D and the cheap baseline in countless consumer audio boards that people upgrade to a 5532 or 4580.*

**Technology:** Bipolar, internally compensated dual op-amp in the 741 class (the Raytheon schematic shows a PNP input pair, a JFET/zener bias and a class-AB output). About 3 MHz GBW, 0.8–1.7 V/µs, ±18 V maximum supply. The dies from Raytheon, TI and JRC are not the same; their datasheets specify different figures. Nisshinbo's separate NJM4558C is specified faster again (1.5 V/µs, 3.5 MHz).

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (3 recorded: Datasheet respec, Die redesign, Second-source difference).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Not a hi-fi favourite. Its fame comes from guitar pedals: the JRC4558D in the Ibanez TS-808 Tube Screamer (1979) and the BOSS SD-1. There, its modest slew rate and bandwidth are credited with a warm, mid-forward overdrive. In hi-fi it is the cheap default in 1980s–90s cassette/mini-system and budget audio boards. Chinese community sources such as Soomal rank it below the NE5532 and NJM4580 ('4558 < 5532/4580'), and swapping it out is a classic upgrade. Japanese pedal folklore prizes glossy JRC4558D parts (said to be from JRC's Saga plant) and Malaysian Raytheon 4558P parts. In Jazzcaster's TS test rig, glossy and matte parts produced nearly identical harmonics above 440 Hz, but the glossy part was clearly quieter, especially at high frequencies. The author judged that only 2–3 in 100 listeners could tell them apart.

**How people describe the sound:** warm, smooth / creamy mid-range (pedal context), mid-hump (Tube Screamer circuit), less clean than an NE5532 in the same socket (opinion in the Celine source), plain budget baseline (Soomal: cheap JRC4558 vs better JRC4580)

**Typical uses:** Tube Screamer / TS9 / SD-1 overdrive pedals and clones, budget consumer audio: cassette decks, mini-systems, sound-card and USB-interface output stages, general-purpose filters and buffers on ±15 V or 9 V single supply, synth / Eurorack and retro-console audio stages (widely used as KiCad RC4558/NJM4558 symbol)

**Caveats:**

- Slow (0.8–1.7 V/µs). Full-power bandwidth for 20 Vpp is only about 13–27 kHz (derived from datasheet slew).
- Phase reversal when the common-mode input approaches V− (within about 1 V), per the TI datasheet and Chen Shuo.
- Output specified only down to 2 kΩ (±10 V min). Not a headphone driver.
- Bipolar input bias of 25–150 nA typ gives DC offset with high source impedances.
- Supply: TI recommends ±5 to ±15 V; JRC operates from ±4 V.
- Vendor dies differ (Raytheon, TI, JRC, and the NJM4558C), so 'a 4558' is not one sound or one spec.
- Vintage-JRC NOS premiums attract fakes and relabels.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Die redesign: Texas Instruments (RC4558), Fab/process PCN 20240723003 (SFAB JI1 150 mm to RFAB TIB…

TI moved the RC4558 to RFAB under PCN 20240723003 and then rewrote its datasheet (Rev H, Oct 2024; datasheet PCN 20241230001.1, Jan 2025) to reflect changed device characteristics. The new figures are 4 MHz GBW, 6.5 nV/√Hz, 2.2 V/µs, 0.0001% THD+N and a 10-30 V supply range, against 3 MHz, 8 nV/√Hz and 1.7 V/µs in Rev G. Specified shifts this large mean the die was revised for the new process, although no TI statement explicitly says 'new design'. Rev H's 4 MHz and 2.2 V/µs do not match the RC4580-class die shared by NE5532/LM833/MC33078/RC4580 (12 MHz, 5 V/µs), so this appears to be a separate process port rather than part of that consolidation (inference).

- **When:** Fab/process PCN 20240723003 (SFAB JI1 150 mm to RFAB TIB 300 mm, first ship 21 Oct 2024, per the earlier pass). Datasheet SLOS073G (Oct 2014) was replaced by SLOS073H (Oct 2024). Datasheet PCN 20241230001.1, dated 2 Jan 2025 and titled 'Datasheet for RC4559 and RC4558', gives a proposed first ship of 2 Apr 2025.
- **Affected:** RC4558P, RC4558DR, RC4558PWR, RC4558DGKR, RC4558IDR, RC4558IP, RC4558IPWR, RC4558IDGKR, RC4558D, RC4558PW, RC4558ID, RC4559P / RC4559DR (sibling part in the same PCNs)
- **How to tell old from new:** The part number is unchanged. The datasheet letter moved from G to H. Datasheet PCN 20241230001.1 says the update is driven by the changes announced in PCN 20240723003 and is meant to 'accurately reflect device characteristics'. Its device list includes RC4558P/DR/PWR/DGKR/IDR/IP/IPWR/IDGKR and RC4559P/DR. Lots shipped after about Oct 2024 (RFAB first ship) are probably new silicon (inference). No top-mark rule is known. Some TI and distributor listings still carry the old '30-V, 3-MHz, 8-nV/√Hz' headline.
- **Audio impact:** On paper the new part is quieter (about 1.8 dB lower voltage noise) and faster, with a specified THD+N. As a revised die, its distortion character, clipping and overload recovery, and input stage may differ from the classic 741-like 4558 valued in overdrive pedals (TS-type) and older consoles. The 10 V minimum supply is above the 9 V at which 4558s commonly run in guitar pedals, so that use is now outside the datasheet spec.
- **Drop-in risk:** medium - same pinout and part number. The 33% higher GBW can reduce phase margin in circuits that relied on the slower part (reactive loads, unusual compensation), and 9 V single-supply pedals sit below the new 10 V minimum. Input-stage, output-swing and phase-reversal behaviour are not documented as unchanged.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Verification:** Merged two completeness-sweep entries. Searches confirmed Rev H headline specs (4 MHz, 6.5 nV/√Hz at 10 kHz, 2.2 V/µs, 0.0001% THD+N, 10-30 V). They also confirmed that datasheet PCN 20241230001.1 (2 Jan 2025) names PCN 20240723003 as the driving change, with RC4558/RC4559 devices listed. The title of PCN 20240723003 could not be retrieved.

|---|---|---|
| Gain-bandwidth (typ) | 3 MHz (SLOS073G, Oct 2014) | 4 MHz (SLOS073H, Oct 2024) |
| Input voltage noise (typ) | 8 nV/√Hz (Rev G / old product-page headline) | 6.5 nV/√Hz at 10 kHz (Rev H) |
| Slew rate (typ) | 1.7 V/µs | 2.2 V/µs |

### 2. Second-source difference: Raytheon vs Texas Instruments vs JRC…, Raytheon 1971; TI's own document from March 1976;

The same generic part number comes from different vendor designs. The three datasheets specify materially different typical figures, and the Raytheon schematic differs from JRC's equivalent-circuit drawing, which points to independent dies rather than one die made under licence. TI's own RC4558 was later revised again (see the TI die-redesign entry).

- **When:** Raytheon 1971; TI's own document from March 1976; JRC by the late 1970s
- **Affected:** RC4558, RC4558P, NJM4558D, JRC4558D
- **How to tell old from new:** Maker logo and prefix: Raytheon RC4558N/M (Raytheon logo); TI RC4558P/DR (TI logo, marked 'RC4558P' or 'R4558'); JRC/NJR NJM4558D marked 'JRC4558D' (JRC or NJR logo).
- **Audio impact:** Small. All are 3 MHz, 741-class parts, and TI's faster slew raises full-power bandwidth. Pedal users report tonal differences between TI RC4558P and JRC4558D in TS9-type circuits (folklore). In hi-fi use all are outclassed by the 5532 and 4580.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - same pinout, supply range and compensation; differences are within general-purpose tolerance.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Verification:** Not re-searched (second-source kind). Kept as reported, with a cross-reference to the TI 2024 revision.

### 3. Datasheet respec: New JRC / Nisshinbo (NJM4558), Old JRC databook, then datasheet Ver.2013-11-05…

- **When:** Old JRC databook, then datasheet Ver.2013-11-05 (NJM4558/4559), then Ver.2019-02-21 (NJM4558)
- **Affected:** NJM4558, NJM4558D, NJM4558M, JRC4558D
- **How to tell old from new:** Datasheet version string (Ver.2013-11-05 or later versus the old databook). No marking or suffix change.
- **Audio impact:** None expected; paper re-characterisation.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - same part, wider rated temperature range.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Verification:** Split out of the glossy/matte entry. Search confirmed the NJM4558/4559 Ver.2013-11-05 datasheet and the -40 to +85 °C range. The 25 nA typical bias figure was not seen in the search snippet.

- **Drop-in risk:** low - same part number, pinout and ratings.
- **Confidence:** low
- **Verification:** Not re-searched (folklore kind). The datasheet re-characterisation was moved to its own datasheet-respec entry.

## Counterfeits

Premium-priced 'NOS vintage JRC4558D' listings, including glossy ('艶あり') parts on Japanese marketplaces, are often fakes or relabels (discovery notes). The NJM4558D DIP is reported discontinued, so expect more grey-market and remarked stock. Cheap JRC4558 parts are also confused with, or remarked as, the pin-compatible but much better JRC4580 (Soomal). A quick slew-rate or small-signal bandwidth check exposes a 4558 sold as a 4580: 4558 slew is about 1 V/µs and GBW about 3 MHz, far below a real 4580. Check that logo, font, finish, country of origin and date code are consistent (JRC logo on older parts, NJR later). Current TI parts carry the documented markings 'RC4558P', 'R4558' or 'R4558I', or the DGK codes YRP/YRS/YRU. Buy current production from authorized distributors.
