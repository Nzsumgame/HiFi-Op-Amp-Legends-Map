# NJM4558 / JRC4558D / RC4558 (dual general-purpose bipolar "dual 741")

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** General-purpose baselines

*Raytheon's 1971 "dual 741". It is the Tube Screamer's JRC4558D and the cheap baseline in countless consumer audio boards that people upgrade to a 5532 or 4580.*

**Technology:** Bipolar, internally compensated dual op-amp in the 741 class (the Raytheon schematic shows a PNP input pair, a JFET/zener bias and a class-AB output). About 3 MHz GBW, 0.8–1.7 V/µs, ±18 V maximum supply. The dies from Raytheon, TI and JRC are not the same; their datasheets specify different figures.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (2 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Not a hi-fi favourite. Its fame comes from guitar pedals: the JRC4558D in the Ibanez TS-808 Tube Screamer (1979) and the BOSS SD-1. There, its modest slew rate and bandwidth are credited with a warm, mid-forward overdrive. In hi-fi it is the cheap default in 1980s–90s cassette/mini-system and budget audio boards. Communities rank it below the NE5532 and NJM4580 ('4558 < 5532/4580'), and swapping it out is a classic upgrade. Japanese pedal folklore prizes 1980s glossy JRC4558D and Malaysian Raytheon 4558P parts. A jazzcaster spectrum test found almost no difference between glossy and matte JRC parts (discovery notes; not re-verified this session).

**How people describe the sound:** warm, smooth / creamy mid-range (pedal context), mid-hump (Tube Screamer circuit), less clean than an NE5532 in the same socket (opinion in the Celine source), plain budget baseline (Soomal: cheap JRC4558 vs better JRC4580)

**Typical uses:** Tube Screamer / TS9 / SD-1 overdrive pedals and clones, budget consumer audio: cassette decks, mini-systems, sound-card and USB-interface output stages, general-purpose filters and buffers on ±15 V or 9 V single supply, synth / Eurorack and retro-console audio stages (widely used as KiCad RC4558/NJM4558 symbol)

**Caveats:**

- Slow (0.8–1.7 V/µs). Full-power bandwidth for 20 Vpp is only about 13–27 kHz (derived from datasheet slew).
- Phase reversal when the common-mode input approaches V− (within about 1 V), per the TI datasheet and Chen Shuo.
- Output specified only down to 2 kΩ (±10 V min). Not a headphone driver.
- Bipolar input bias of 25–150 nA typ gives DC offset with high source impedances.
- Supply: TI recommends ±5 to ±15 V; JRC operates from ±4 V.
- Vendor dies differ (Raytheon, TI, JRC), so 'a 4558' is not one sound or one spec.
- Vintage-JRC NOS premiums attract fakes and relabels.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Raytheon vs Texas Instruments vs JRC…: Raytheon 1971; TI own document from March 1976;

The same generic part number comes from different vendor designs. The three datasheets specify materially different typical figures, and the Raytheon schematic differs from the JRC equivalent-circuit drawing. That points to independent dies, not one die made under licence. No vendor states a die change within any single vendor's part.

- **When:** Raytheon 1971; TI own document from March 1976; JRC by the late 1970s
- **Affected:** RC4558, RC4558P, NJM4558D, JRC4558D
- **How to tell old from new:** Maker logo and prefix: Raytheon RC4558N/M (Raytheon logo); TI RC4558P/DR (TI logo; the marking is 'RC4558P' or 'R4558'); JRC/NJR NJM4558D marked 'JRC4558D' (JRC or NJR logo).
- **Audio impact:** Small. All are 3 MHz, 741-class parts. TI's faster slew raises full-power bandwidth, and pedal users report tonal differences between TI RC4558P and JRC4558D in TS9-type circuits (folklore). In hi-fi use all are outclassed by the 5532 and 4580.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - same pinout, supply range and compensation. Differences are within a general-purpose tolerance.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Slew rate typ | Raytheon 0.8 V/µs | TI 1.7 V/µs; JRC 1 V/µs |
| Input bias current typ | Raytheon 40 nA | TI 150 nA; JRC 50 nA (old) / 25 nA (2013) |
| Input resistance typ | Raytheon 1.0 MΩ | TI / JRC 5 MΩ |
| Vos typ | Raytheon RC 2.0 mV | TI / JRC 0.5 mV |
| Overshoot (20 mV step, 100 pF) | Raytheon 35 % | TI 5 % |

### 2. JRC / NJR (old 'glossy' vs current…: 1980s parts vs later production (no vendor date)

Japanese pedal folklore claims old glossy JRC4558D parts sound clearer and tighter. No vendor document shows a die, fab or process change. The only datasheet changes (Ver.2013 vs the old databook) are re-characterisation: wider Topr, lower typical IB and an added GBW row. Jazzcaster spectrum and frequency-response tests found almost no difference, so the claim is treated as unconfirmed and likely refuted.

- **When:** 1980s parts vs later production (no vendor date)
- **Affected:** JRC4558D, NJM4558D
- **How to tell old from new:** Package finish (glossy vs matte), JRC vs NJR logo, country of assembly and date/lot code. There is no PCN or orderable-suffix change.
- **Audio impact:** No measurable difference reported (jazzcaster; discovery notes, not re-verified this session).
- **Drop-in risk:** low - same part, pinout and ratings.
- **Confidence:** low

| Input bias current typ | 50 nA | 25 nA |

## Counterfeits

Premium-priced 'NOS vintage JRC4558D' listings are frequently fakes or relabels (discovery notes). Cheap JRC4558 parts are also confused with, or remarked as, the pin-compatible but much better JRC4580 (Soomal: '经常看到有人将JRC4580和廉价的JRC4558混淆'). A quick slew-rate or small-signal bandwidth check exposes a 4558 sold as a 4580: 4558 slew is about 1 V/µs and GBW about 3 MHz, far below a real 4580. Check that logo, font and date code are consistent (JRC logo on older parts, NJR later). Current TI parts carry the documented markings 'RC4558P', 'R4558' or 'R4558I', or the DGK codes YRP/YRS/YRU. Buy current production from authorized distributors.
