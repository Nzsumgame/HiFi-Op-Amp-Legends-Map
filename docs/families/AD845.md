# AD845 (ADI 16 MHz CBFET JFET-input single, J/K/A/B/S grades)

Analog Devices (by 12/1990 to present (Z-suffix orderables still listed by distributors); non-Z leaded versions discontinued around 2010 per PDN 10_0081 (reported)) · Tier C (niche / baseline)

*ADI's 16 MHz, 100 V/us JFET-input CBFET single, around since at least 1990. One headphone-amp survey called it smooth and slightly valve-like while keeping ADI's punch.*

| Slew rate | Supply range |
|---|---|
| 100 V/us | +/-4.75 V to +/-18 V |

## Why it's rated

A niche ADI JFET single with thin enthusiast coverage. The only located listening report is a Chinese translation (DiyEden, on Soomal, 2005) of a headphone-amp op-amp survey. That one reviewer calls the AD845 smooth, with less detail than the AD843 and a slightly valve-like feel while keeping ADI's punch. It sounds looser, softer and 'slower' than Burr-Brown parts. The reviewer ranks its resolution above OPA132/134 and AD823 and below AD843/OPA627, and recommends it for mains-powered amps. A claimed 'cult following' for DAC I/V stages remains unsourced.

**Described as:** smooth, slightly valve/tube-like, keeps ADI punch/impact, looser, softer, 'slower' than Burr-Brown, resolution above OPA134/AD823, below AD843/OPA627 (one reviewer), less detail than AD843

**Used in:** headphone amplifier gain stage (mains-powered), line stage / buffer, DAC I/V stage (claimed, unverified)

## Watch out

- All listening impressions come from one reviewer's survey (via translation).
- Single channel only: dual-socket rolling needs an adapter.
- The SOIC version is a 16-lead wide-body package (AD845JR-16), not SOIC-8.
- High quiescent current (about 10 mA, per the reviewer): poor for battery amps.
- Minimum supply is +/-4.75 V (9.5 V total), which the reviewer also noted.
- Fast part (100 V/us, 16 MHz): normal high-speed layout and decoupling care applies.
- **Counterfeits:** No AD845-specific counterfeit reports were found. General checks: (1) ADI logo, full part number with grade letter, and a plausible date code. (2) The SOIC version is a 16-lead wide body (JR-16/JRZ-16), so an 'AD845' marked on an SOIC-8 body is suspect (inferred). (3) Supply current should be about 10 mA (reviewer figure); a relabelled low-power JFET part such as a TL071 draws far less (inferred). (4) Check for slew near 100 V/us and unity-gain bandwidth of at least 12.8 MHz (datasheet). (5) Non-Z JN/KN parts reportedly went out of production around 2010, so they should carry older date codes.

## Silicon changes

None documented. Compare datasheet revisions before assuming old and new parts are identical.
