# AD845 (ADI precision JFET-input single, J/K/A/B/S grades)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*A fast ADI JFET-input single from at least 1990. One headphone-amp op-amp survey called it smooth and slightly valve-like while it keeps ADI's punch.*

**Technology:** JFET-input (BiFET) single op-amp. ADI's own SPICE macro-model (12/90, Rev. A) models the input as an N-channel JFET differential pair. The 'CBFET' process name often attached to it was not verified this run.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche ADI JFET single with thin enthusiast coverage. The only located source is a Chinese translation (DiyEden, published on Soomal 2005) of an English headphone-amp op-amp survey. That single reviewer calls the AD845 'a very interesting chip': smooth, with less detail than the AD843, and a slightly valve-like feel (有点电子管的感觉) while keeping ADI's punch. It sounds looser, softer and 'slower' than Burr-Brown parts. The reviewer rated its resolution above OPA132/134 and AD823 but below AD843/OPA627. The reviewer recommends it for mains-powered systems and smooth-sound lovers, or as a cheaper stand-in before moving to the AD843. Discovery notes also claim a cult following for DAC I/V and line stages. This has not been verified and should not be treated as established.

**How people describe the sound:** smooth, slightly valve/tube-like, keeps ADI punch/impact, looser, softer, 'slower' than Burr-Brown, resolution above OPA134/AD823, below AD843/OPA627 (one reviewer), less detail than AD843

**Typical uses:** headphone amplifier gain stage (mains-powered), line stage / buffer, DAC I/V stage (claimed in discovery notes, unverified)

**Caveats:**

- All listening impressions come from one reviewer's survey (via translation). No corroborating independent listening reports were found.
- Single channel only: dual-socket rolling needs an adapter.
- The SOIC version is a 16-lead wide-body package (AD845JR-16), not SOIC-8, so SOIC-8 adapters and footprints do not fit.
- High quiescent current (about 10 mA each, per the reviewer). The reviewer warns against battery amps, especially Hansen/CHA47-style designs that need 4 chips.
- Needs relatively high supply voltage (about 9.5 V total or more, per the reviewer).
- Wide-bandwidth part: normal high-speed layout and decoupling care applies. No specific oscillation reports were found.

## Counterfeits

No AD845-specific counterfeit reports were found; web search was unavailable in both runs. The following is general guidance, partly inferred. (1) Check for the ADI logo, the full part number with grade letter and package suffix, and a plausible date code. (2) The SOIC version is a 16-lead wide-body package (AD845JR-16/JRZ-16). An 'AD845' marked on an SOIC-8 body should therefore be treated as suspect; this is inferred from the orderable lists, not vendor-confirmed. (3) Measure supply current. The reviewer reports about 10 mA for a genuine part, and a relabelled low-power JFET part such as a TL071 would draw far less; this check is an unverified inference. (4) Slew or bandwidth checks against the 100 V/us and 16 MHz headline figures are useful, but those figures are themselves not datasheet-verified.

GitHub code search was used instead until it was rate-limited (HTTP 429) after about 7 queries. No vendor datasheet header, revision letter, date, PCN or status was checked. A re-run with search budget is needed.
- Datasheet revision chain unknown: the current ADI revision letter/date, whether it has a Revision History table, and older revisions (the 12/90 SPICE models imply a datasheet existed by 1990). The datasheet title, possibly 'Precision, 16 MHz CBFET Op Amp', is unverified.
- Status unknown (production, NRND or obsolete / last-time-buy). The Z-suffix orderables prove only that production continued into the Pb-free era.
- When did the Pb-free change (JN->JNZ, KN->KNZ, JR-16->JRZ-16) happen, and under which PCN? This is a package/finish change, not a die change. It was not added to silicon_changes because no PCN was seen.
- Grade temperature ranges (J/K commercial? A/B industrial? S military?) and the AD845BQ package (CERDIP by convention vs the PDIP footprint in the EasyEDA list) are not vendor-verified. Were AD845AQ, AD845JQ or KQ, and SMD 5962 numbers, ever offered?
- Datasheet specs not verified: GBW 16 MHz and slew 100 V/us (discovery notes only), e_n at 1 kHz, i_n, THD+N, output current, supply range and quiescent current.
- All analog.com datasheet URLs in archive_seed_urls except the product page are INFERRED from ADI URL patterns and were never seen for AD845. The product page URL itself is unconfirmed.
- tangentsoft.net/audio/opamps.html is INFERRED to be the English original of the DiyEden/soomal translation, based on the matching test format (Vmin into 33/330 ohm, single/dual cost). The translation itself gives no attribution.
- The 'cult following for DAC I/V and line stages' and 'vivid and dynamic' claims from discovery notes remain unverified. Removed from descriptors pending a source.
- No PCN, die-change or fab-transfer information was found. The empty silicon_changes list reflects missing evidence, not confirmed absence.
