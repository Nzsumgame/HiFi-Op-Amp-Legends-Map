# AD843 (34 MHz CBFET JFET-input single op-amp)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*ADI's 34 MHz, 250 V/us CBFET single op-amp. A DiyEden/Soomal headphone-amp study called it the only rival to the OPA627, and smoother; Head-Fi opinion is split.*

**Technology:** Fast-settling JFET-input single op-amp on ADI's CBFET process. The ADI data sheet is titled '34 MHz, CBFET Fast Settling Op Amp': 34 MHz bandwidth, 250 V/us slew rate, 135 ns settling to 0.01% for a 10 V step, 0.6 nA input bias current. ADI's SPICE macro-model (Rev. A, 1/92) uses a p-channel JFET input pair, with the dominant pole at about 1.23 kHz and about 10.7 mA typical supply current. Single channel only; ADI never made a dual AD843. Packages: 8-lead plastic DIP (N-8), 16-pin SOIC (R-16), cerdip (Q) and metal can (H).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche cult part with split opinion. DiyEden published a Chinese translation of an English headphone-amp op-amp study on Soomal (2005). It quotes an article called 'The best op-amp I've heard' naming the AD843 the only op-amp that can rival the OPA627. The author found it smoother than the OPA627, trading some resolution for smoothness, with 'boldness, charm, vitality and passion'. A pair cost about half as much as a pair of OPA627s. The same study rates the AD845 as less detailed and says to upgrade to the AD843, and rates the AD8610 as having more detail and smoothness than the AD843. Head-Fi opinion (from an earlier search summary, not re-checked) is largely negative: 'harsh and unpleasant', 'no competition for OPA637', 'stay away from this - bad offset, oscillation, etc.'. One user found 2x AD843JN on an adapter 'really good' but warm, because of the high quiescent current. It also appears in Maverick TubeMagic D1 op-amp rolling lists. No objective measurements (Audio Science Review, Samuel Groner) were found.

**How people describe the sound:** smooth (smoother than OPA627, per DiyEden), bold / energetic ('气魄、神韵、活力和热情'), slightly less resolving than OPA627, harsh and unpleasant (Head-Fi dissent)

**Typical uses:** headphone amplifier op-amp rolling (CMoy-style, single-channel sockets), DAC output stage rolling, used in pairs on dual adapters, replacement for OPA627 in single sockets

**Caveats:**

- High quiescent current: 13 mA max per the data sheet, about 10.7 mA typical in ADI's model. It runs warm and is poor for battery amps.
- Needs a high supply voltage: hobbyist-measured minimums of 8.2 V and 12.2 V. The sound degrades quickly as the supply voltage drops.
- Head-Fi reports of bad offset and oscillation. It is a 34 MHz, 250 V/us part, so it is sensitive to layout and decoupling.
- Single only: dual-socket use needs two chips on an adapter.
- The surface-mount version is a 16-pin SOIC (R-16), not an SO-8, so it does not fit standard SO-8 adapters.

## Counterfeits

No reports of fake AD843s were found, but this was not searched specifically. Generic caution: DIP AD843JN/KN parts sold on marketplaces are likely relabelled or pulled parts. Check the ADI logo style and date code. Measure quiescent current: roughly 10-13 mA is expected (13 mA data-sheet max, about 10.7 mA model typical). A much lower Iq points to a substituted die such as a TL071-class part. Also check the offset against the grade limits: 2 mV for J, 1 mV for K. A genuine SOIC part is a 16-pin R-16 package; an 8-pin SOIC marked AD843 is suspect.

## Related parts and alternatives

OPA627 / OPA827 (TI JFET-input; the reference it is compared with in the DiyEden study), AD8610 / AD8620 (ADI precision JFET; DiyEden rates it smoother, with more detail than the AD843, and much lower supply current), AD845 (ADI JFET-input sibling; cheaper, smoother, less detailed per DiyEden), ADA4627-1 (modern ADI OPA627-class JFET single; suggested successor, not compared by listeners), OPA828 (modern TI high-speed JFET single; suggested successor)
