# AD843 (34 MHz CBFET JFET-input single op-amp)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*ADI's 34 MHz CBFET single op-amp. A DiyEden/Soomal headphone-amp study called it the only rival to the OPA627, and smoother; Head-Fi opinion is split.*

**Technology:** High-speed JFET-input single op-amp on ADI's CBFET process. ADI's own model library labels it '34Mhz CBFET'. ADI's SPICE macro-model (Rev. A, 1/92) uses a p-channel JFET input pair, with the dominant pole at about 1.23 kHz and about 10.7 mA typical supply current. Single channel only; ADI never made a dual AD843.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche cult part with split opinion. DiyEden published a Chinese translation of an English headphone-amp op-amp study on Soomal (2005). It quotes an article called 'The best op-amp I've heard' naming the AD843 the only op-amp that can rival the OPA627. The author found it smoother than the OPA627, trading some resolution for smoothness, with 'boldness, charm, vitality and passion'. A pair cost about half as much as a pair of OPA627s. The same study rates the AD845 as less detailed and says to upgrade to the AD843, and rates the AD8610 as having more detail and smoothness than the AD843. Head-Fi opinion (via an earlier search summary) is largely negative: 'harsh and unpleasant', 'no competition for OPA637', 'stay away from this - bad offset, oscillation, etc.', 'a nasty chip'. One user found 2x AD843JN on an adapter 'really good' but warm, because of the high quiescent current. It also appears in Maverick TubeMagic D1 op-amp rolling lists. No objective measurements (Audio Science Review, Samuel Groner) were found.

**How people describe the sound:** smooth (smoother than OPA627), bold / energetic ('气魄、神韵、活力和热情'), slightly less resolving than OPA627, ADI 'lively/punchy' character, harsh and unpleasant (Head-Fi dissent)

**Typical uses:** headphone amplifier op-amp rolling (CMoy-style, single-channel sockets), DAC output stage rolling, used in pairs on dual adapters, replacement for OPA627 in single sockets

**Caveats:**

- High quiescent current: about 10.7 mA typical in ADI's model. It runs warm and is poor for battery amps.
- Needs a high supply voltage: measured minimums of 8.2 V and 12.2 V. The sound degrades quickly as the supply voltage drops.
- Head-Fi reports of bad offset and oscillation. It is a 34 MHz part, so it is sensitive to layout and decoupling.
- Single only: dual-socket use needs two chips on an adapter.
- The surface-mount version appears to be a 16-lead SOIC ('-16' suffix), not a standard SO-8. This is inferred and unverified.

## Counterfeits

No reports of fake AD843s were found, but this was not searched (the web-search budget was used up). Generic caution: DIP AD843JN/KN parts sold on marketplaces are likely relabelled or pulled parts. Check the ADI logo style and date code. Measure quiescent current (about 10-12 mA expected from ADI's model); a much lower Iq points to a substituted die such as a TL071-class part. Also check the offset against the grade limits: 2 mV for J, 1 mV for K.

## Related parts and alternatives

OPA627 / OPA827 (TI JFET-input; the reference it is compared with in the DiyEden study), AD8610 / AD8620 (ADI precision JFET; DiyEden rates it smoother, with more detail than the AD843, and much lower supply current), AD845 (ADI JFET-input sibling; cheaper, smoother, less detailed per DiyEden), ADA4627-1 (modern ADI OPA627-class JFET single; suggested successor, not compared by listeners), OPA828 (modern TI high-speed JFET single; suggested successor)
