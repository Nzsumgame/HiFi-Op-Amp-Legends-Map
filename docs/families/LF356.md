# LF355 / LF356 / LF357 (LF155 / LF156 / LF157)

**JFET-input (BI-FET) single, fast settling** · National Semiconductor (1975–2011) → Texas Instruments (2011–present) · second sources: Precision Monolithics (PMI), Linear Technology, Motorola, STMicroelectronics, Fairchild · Tier C (niche / baseline)

## Why it's rated

Historically important more than currently praised. Walt Jung's 1978 survey called the 355/356/357 'second-generation FET' op-amps with better drift and offset. National stressed low noise, fast settling and capacitive-load stability. Tangent likens the LF355 to the TL071 and finds it unsuitable for low-impedance headphones. It survives in synth, sample-and-hold, I/V and lab circuits.

**Used in:** DAC I/V converters and fast D/A or A/D buffers, High-impedance buffers, photodiode and electrometer front ends, Integrators, sample-and-hold, log amplifiers, Active filters and vintage synth VCO/VCF circuits, 1970s–80s Jung-era audio line stages

## Watch out

- Input past the negative CM limit (−12 V typ, −10 V guaranteed) reverses phase; below V− can destroy it.
- Reversed insertion or supply can fuse internal conductors, a real risk when rolling.
- LF357/LF157/LF257 is decompensated (AV≥5) and oscillates as a follower; not a drop-in for LF356.
- 5 mA typ draw for a FET single; self-heating raises bias. Poor at driving low impedances.
- Single pinout; offset pot wiper to V+ on pins 1/5. 1975 LF156 needs pin 8 tied to 7.
- **Counterfeits:** No documented fakes. Main risk: remarked LF351/TL081-class parts. Genuine LF356 draws about 5 mA (LF351 about 1.8 mA) and has about 5 MHz GBW. TI marks 'LF 356N' or 'LF356 M'. Distrust post-2012 'National' date codes and unity-gain-stable 'LF357H'.

- **Audio impact:** None documented; datasheet limits unchanged and no pre- versus post-transfer measurements known.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: same datasheet, pinout and limits; no published parametric change.
- **Confidence:** medium

### Die redesign: National Semiconductor, 1975 → by 1980 (earliest production)

The 1975 LF156 pinout made pin 8 a bias reference, with typical 15 V/µs and 1.4 µs versus 12 V/µs and 1.5 µs from 1980. Possibly an early mask or metal change; bonding-only change or preliminary specs would also explain it.

- **Affected:** LF156, LF156H
- **How to tell:** 1975 sheet labels pin 8 'current source reference', tie to pin 7 if offset unused; 1980 on shows NC. Metal-can LF156s, c. 1975–76 date codes.
- **Audio impact:** None known; matters only for very early metal-can LF156s with pin 8 left open.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: only rare 1975-era LF156 cans; tie pin 8 to V+ when not trimming.
- **Confidence:** low

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Pin 8 function | Current source reference; tie to pin 7 if offset adjust unused | NC (no connection) |
| Slew rate (LF156, typ) | 15 V/us | 12 V/us |
| Settling to 0.01% (typ) | 1.4 us | 1.5 us |

### Datasheet respec: National Semiconductor, 1980 → 2000-05 (DS005646)

The LF157/LF357 schematic note changed from 2 pF to 3 pF and the feature-list load claim from 10,000 pF to 5,000 pF (the hint still says 0.01 µF). Could be a compensation change or a documentation fix. GBW (20 MHz) and slew (50 V/µs) unchanged.

- **Affected:** LF157, LF257, LF357
- **How to tell:** 'C = 2 pF on LF157' (1980) versus '*C = 3pF in LF357 series' (2000/2001 and TI Rev D). No date code or PCN boundary known.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown; guaranteed specs unchanged.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: guaranteed specs identical; change may be purely editorial.
- **Confidence:** low

| Parameter | Before | After |
