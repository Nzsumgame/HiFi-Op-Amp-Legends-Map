## Why it's rated

Historically important more than currently praised. The LF156/LF356 was National's first BI-FET op amp (1975) and, in National's and TI's own words, the first monolithic JFET-input op amp. Walt Jung's 1978 Electronic Design survey groups the 355/356/357 as 'second-generation FET' op amps with better drift and offset than general-purpose parts, and notes the OP-15/16/17 were 'patterned after' them. National's 1978 Electronics article stresses its low noise (12 nV/√Hz), fast settling and capacitive-load stability. Current Hi-Fi/DIY praise is thin: Tangent's headphone op-amp notes (2005 Chinese translation) liken the LF355 to the TL071 and find it unsuitable for low-impedance headphones. It survives in vintage synth, sample-and-hold, I/V and lab front-end circuits. No modern distortion measurements (ASR, Groner) were found; forum reputation was not re-searched this run.

**Used in:** DAC current-to-voltage converters and fast D/A or A/D buffers (fast settling), High-impedance buffers, photodiode and electrometer front ends, Integrators, sample-and-hold, log amplifiers, Active filters and vintage analog synth VCO/VCF circuits, 1970s–80s 'Jung-era' audio/line stages as a low-noise FET single

## Watch out

- If an input goes past the negative common-mode limit (−12 V typ, only −10 V guaranteed for LF35x at ±15 V) the output is forced high, so the phase reverses. An input taken below V− can destroy the part (datasheet Application Hints).
- Fitting it backwards in a socket, or reversing the supply, can fuse internal conductors (datasheet warning). This matters when op-amp rolling.
- Supply current is high for a FET single: 5 mA typ, 10 mA max for the LF356. Self-heating raises input bias current, which doubles every 10 °C.
- The LF357/LF157/LF257 is decompensated and stable only at AV≥5. It will oscillate as a follower or unity-gain stage, so it is not a drop-in for an LF356 socket.
- Offset trim uses a 25 kΩ pot with the wiper to V+, not V−; pins 1 and 5 are BALANCE.
- Poor at driving low impedances: Tangent found it unsuitable for low-impedance headphones.
- It is a single (DIP-8 single pinout), so rolling it into dual-op-amp sockets needs adapters.
- The earliest LF156 (1975 New Products page) labelled pin 8 'current source reference' and required pin 8 to be tied to pin 7 when offset adjust was unused. Later datasheets show pin 8 as NC.
- Specs are guaranteed at ±15 V for LF35x. There is no low-voltage specification.
- Post-2018 TI parts may come from a different wafer fab (PCN 20180920003.1, FFAB BI-FET qualification); datasheet limits are unchanged.
- **Counterfeits:** No documented counterfeit reports found; forums were not searched in this run. Generic screening heuristics, not reported cases: remarked LF351/TL081-class parts are the obvious risk. A genuine LF356 draws about 5 mA typ (10 max) at ±15 V; an LF351 about 1.8 mA. Genuine GBW is about 5 MHz, and offset nulls with a 25 kΩ pot on pins 1/5 with the wiper to V+. Current TI marking is 'LF 356N' (PDIP) or 'LF356 M' (SOIC) per the SNOSBH0D addendum. Treat marketplace 'NS LF357H' metal cans and 'National' parts with post-2012 date codes as suspect. A metal-can LF357 stable as a unity-gain follower is also suspect (the real part needs AV≥5).

## Silicon changes

### Fab / process transfer: Texas Instruments, PCN 20180920003.1; FFAB BI-FET qualification approved 19 Sep 2018 (implementation date not seen)

TI issued a PCN with a qualification report for 'FFAB BIFET Technology Qualification' that lists LF356 wafer, metal-can, SOIC and PDIP devices. LF356s built after the change may use die from a different wafer fab than National-era parts. The PCN as seen does not state the previous fab or any mask or design change; a move from a National-heritage fab is inferred, not confirmed.

- **Affected:** LF356 MWC (wafer), LF356H, LF356M/NOPB, LF356N/NOPB
- **How to tell:** Not visible in the marking; datasheet SNOSBH0D is unchanged. Only TI lot trace or date codes after PCN implementation would indicate FFAB wafers. Check the PCN's affected-device list and implementation date with TI or a distributor.
- **Audio impact:** None documented. Datasheet limits are unchanged, and no measurements comparing pre- and post-transfer lots are known.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: same datasheet, pinout and limits; a fab transfer with no published parametric change.
- **Confidence:** medium

### Die redesign: National Semiconductor, 1975 to before 1980 (earliest production)

The earliest documented LF156 pinout differs (pin 8 is a bias reference), and so do the typical AC figures (15 V/us and 1.4 us, against 12 V/us and 1.5 us from 1980). This suggests an early mask or metal change before volume production. It is inferred from an advance product sheet versus later datasheets and is not confirmed by National; a bonding-only change or preliminary specs would also explain it.

- **Affected:** LF156, LF156H
- **How to tell:** The 1975 'New Products' sheet labels pin 8 'current source reference' and says to tie it to pin 7 when offset adjust is unused; datasheets from 1980 on show pin 8 as NC. Affected parts would be metal-can LF156s with c.1975-76 date codes. No PCN known.
- **Audio impact:** None known. Relevant only to very early metal-can LF156s fitted where pin 8 is left open.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: affects only rare 1975-era LF156 metal cans; tie pin 8 to V+ when not trimming offset.
- **Confidence:** low

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Pin 8 function | Current source reference; tie to pin 7 if offset adjust unused | NC (no connection) |
| Slew rate (LF156, typ) | 15 V/us | 12 V/us |
| Settling to 0.01% (typ) | 1.4 us | 1.5 us |

This may be a change to the compensation capacitor in the decompensated LF157/LF357, or only a documentation correction. The GBW (20 MHz) and slew (50 V/us) specs did not change.

- **Audio impact:** Unknown; guaranteed specs unchanged.
- **Drop-in risk:** low: guaranteed specs are the same and the change may be purely editorial.
