# LF355 / LF356 / LF357 (LF155 series)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** General-purpose baselines

*National's 1975 BI-FET original: the first monolithic JFET-input op amp, with 12 nV/√Hz, 12 V/µs and fast settling. A Jung-era classic that TI still makes as the LF356.*

**Technology:** National BI-FET: an ion-implanted p-channel JFET input pair with JFET current-source loads, plus bipolar gain and output stages on one die. It is an internally compensated single; the LF357 is decompensated and needs AV≥5. Offset null is on pins 1/5, with the 25 k pot wiper to V+. LF155/LF255/LF355 is the low-current 2.5 MHz version, LF156/LF256/LF356 the 5 MHz version, and LF157/LF257/LF357 the 20 MHz version. TI qualified BI-FET production at its FFAB wafer fab in 2018 (PCN 20180920003.1).

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (4 recorded: Unclassified).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Historically important more than currently praised. The LF156/LF356 was National's first BI-FET op amp (1975) and, in National's and TI's own words, the first monolithic JFET-input op amp. Walt Jung's 1978 Electronic Design survey groups the 355/356/357 as 'second-generation FET' op amps with better drift and offset than general-purpose parts, and notes the OP-15/16/17 were 'patterned after' them. National's 1978 Electronics article stresses its low noise (12 nV/√Hz), fast settling and capacitive-load stability. Current Hi-Fi/DIY praise is thin: Tangent's headphone op-amp notes (2005 Chinese translation) liken the LF355 to the TL071 and find it unsuitable for low-impedance headphones. It survives in vintage synth, sample-and-hold, I/V and lab front-end circuits. No modern distortion measurements (ASR, Groner) were found; forum reputation was not re-searched this run.

**Typical uses:** DAC current-to-voltage converters and fast D/A or A/D buffers (fast settling), High-impedance buffers, photodiode and electrometer front ends, Integrators, sample-and-hold, log amplifiers, Active filters and vintage analog synth VCO/VCF circuits, 1970s–80s 'Jung-era' audio/line stages as a low-noise FET single

**Caveats:**

- If an input goes past the negative common-mode limit (−12 V typ, only −10 V guaranteed for LF35x at ±15 V) the output is forced high, so the phase reverses. An input taken below V− can destroy the part (datasheet Application Hints).
- Fitting it backwards in a socket, or reversing the supply, can fuse internal conductors (datasheet warning). This matters when op-amp rolling.
- Supply current is high for a FET single: 5 mA typ, 10 mA max for the LF356. Self-heating raises input bias current, which doubles every 10 °C.
- The LF357/LF157/LF257 is decompensated and stable only at AV≥5. It will oscillate as a follower or unity-gain stage, so it is not a drop-in for an LF356 socket.
- Offset trim uses a 25 kΩ pot with the wiper to V+, not V−; pins 1 and 5 are BALANCE.
- Poor at driving low impedances: Tangent found it unsuitable for low-impedance headphones.
- It is a single (DIP-8 single pinout), so rolling it into dual-op-amp sockets needs adapters.
- The earliest LF156 (1975 New Products page) labelled pin 8 'current source reference' and required pin 8 to be tied to pin 7 when offset adjust was unused. Later datasheets show pin 8 as NC.
- Specs are guaranteed at ±15 V for LF35x. There is no low-voltage specification.
- Post-2018 TI parts may come from a different wafer fab (PCN 20180920003.1, FFAB BI-FET qualification); datasheet limits are unchanged.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. Unclassified: Texas Instruments, PCN 20180920003.1; FFAB BI-FET qualification approved…

TI issued a PCN with a qualification report for 'FFAB BIFET Technology Qualification' that lists LF356 wafer, metal-can, SOIC and PDIP devices. LF356s built after the change may use die from a different wafer fab than National-era parts. The previous fab and any mask or design changes are not stated in what was seen (the move from National's heritage fab is inferred, not confirmed).

- **When:** PCN 20180920003.1; FFAB BI-FET qualification approved 19-Sep-2018 (implementation date not seen)
- **Affected:** LF356 MWC (wafer), LF356H, LF356M/NOPB, LF356N/NOPB
- **How to tell old from new:** Not visible in the marking; datasheet SNOSBH0D is unchanged. Only TI lot trace / date codes after PCN implementation would indicate FFAB wafers. Check the PCN affected-device list and implementation date with TI or a distributor.
- **Audio impact:** None documented. Datasheet limits unchanged; no measurements comparing pre- and post-transfer lots are known.
- **Drop-in risk:** low – same datasheet, pinout and limits; a fab transfer with no published parametric change.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Wafer fab | previous (National-heritage) fab, not named in the search result | FFAB |

### 2. Unclassified: National Semiconductor, 1975 to before 1980 (earliest production)

The earliest documented LF156 pinout differs (pin 8 bias reference) and typical AC figures differed (15 V/µs, 1.4 µs vs 12 V/µs, 1.5 µs from 1980). This suggests an early mask or metal change before volume production; inferred from datasheet wording, not confirmed by National.

- **When:** 1975 to before 1980 (earliest production)
- **Affected:** LF156, LF156H
- **How to tell old from new:** The 1975 'New Products' sheet labels pin 8 'current source reference' and says to tie it to pin 7 when offset adjust is unused; 1980 and later datasheets show pin 8 as NC. Affected parts would be metal-can LF156s with c.1975–76 date codes. No PCN known.
- **Audio impact:** None known. Relevant only to very early metal-can LF156s fitted where pin 8 is left open.
- **Drop-in risk:** low – affects only rare 1975-era LF156 metal cans; tie pin 8 to V+ when not trimming offset.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Pin 8 function | Current source reference; tie to pin 7 if offset adjust unused | NC (no connection) |
| Slew rate (LF156, typ) | 15 V/µs | 12 V/µs |
| Settling to 0.01% (typ) | 1.4 µs | 1.5 µs |

### 3. Unclassified: National Semiconductor, between the 1980 databook and the May 2000 DS005646 edition

- **When:** between the 1980 databook and the May 2000 DS005646 edition

- **Audio impact:** Unknown; guaranteed specs unchanged.
- **Drop-in risk:** low – guaranteed specs are the same and it may be purely editorial.
- **Confidence:** low

## Counterfeits

No documented counterfeit reports found; forums were not searched in this run. Generic screening heuristics, not reported cases: remarked LF351/TL081-class parts are the obvious risk. A genuine LF356 draws about 5 mA typ (10 max) at ±15 V; an LF351 about 1.8 mA. Genuine GBW is about 5 MHz, and offset nulls with a 25 kΩ pot on pins 1/5 with the wiper to V+. Current TI marking is 'LF 356N' (PDIP) or 'LF356 M' (SOIC) per the SNOSBH0D addendum. Treat marketplace 'NS LF357H' metal cans and 'National' parts with post-2012 date codes as suspect. A metal-can LF357 stable as a unity-gain follower is also suspect (the real part needs AV≥5).

## Related parts and alternatives

TI LF356M/MX/N /NOPB or LF356-MIL: the same family, still in production, ADI (PMI) OP-15/OP-16/OP-17: improved successors 'patterned after the 355/356/357'; obsolete but found as NOS, TI OPA1641 / OPA827: modern low-noise JFET-input audio singles, ADI AD8610 / ADA4627-1: fast-settling precision JFET singles, ADI (LT) LT1122: fast-settling JFET single for I/V and sample-and-hold duty, TI TL071H: cheap modern JFET single with similar speed (Tangent likens the LF355 to the TL071), TI OPA140 / OPA1655: modern low-noise precision singles
