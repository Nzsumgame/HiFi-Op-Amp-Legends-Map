# LF355 / LF356 / LF357 (LF155 series)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** General-purpose baselines

*National's 1975 BI-FET original: the first monolithic JFET-input op amp, with 12 nV/√Hz, 12 V/µs and fast settling. A Jung-era classic that TI still makes as the LF356.*

**Technology:** National BI-FET: an ion-implanted p-channel JFET input pair with JFET current-source loads, plus bipolar gain and output stages on one die. It is an internally compensated single; the LF357 is decompensated and needs AV≥5. Offset null is on pins 1/5, with the 25 k pot wiper to V+. LF155/LF255/LF355 is the low-current 2.5 MHz version, LF156/LF256/LF356 the 5 MHz version, and LF157/LF257/LF357 the 20 MHz version.

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon.** See [silicon changes](#silicon-changes-under-the-same-part-number) (3 recorded).

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Historically important more than currently praised. The LF156/LF356 was National's first BI-FET op amp (1975) and the first monolithic JFET-input op amp. Walt Jung's 1978 Electronic Design survey groups the 355/356/357 as 'second-generation FET' op amps with clearly better drift and offset than general-purpose parts, and notes the OP-15/16/17 were 'patterned after' them. National's own 1978 Electronics article stresses its low noise (12 nV/√Hz), fast settling and stability with capacitive loads up to 10,000 pF. In current Hi-Fi/DIY communities evidence of praise is thin. Tangent's headphone op-amp notes (read here in a 2005 Chinese translation) call the LF355 a 'Walkman' op amp with specs very like the TL071 and unsuitable for low-impedance headphones: it needed 14.9 V to put 0.5 V into 33 Ω. It survives in vintage synth VCO/VCF designs, sample-and-hold and I/V circuits, and lab front ends. No modern distortion measurements (ASR, Groner) were found this run.

**Typical uses:** DAC current-to-voltage converters and fast D/A or A/D buffers (fast settling), High-impedance buffers, photodiode and electrometer front ends, Integrators, sample-and-hold, log amplifiers, Active filters and vintage analog synth VCO/VCF circuits, 1970s–80s 'Jung-era' audio/line stages as a low-noise FET single

**Caveats:**

- If an input goes past the negative common-mode limit (−12 V typ, only −10 V guaranteed for LF35x at ±15 V) the output is forced high, so the phase reverses. An input taken below V− can destroy the part (datasheet Application Hints).
- Fitting it backwards in a socket, or reversing the supply, can fuse internal conductors (datasheet warning). This matters when op-amp rolling.
- Supply current is high for a FET single: 5 mA typ, 10 mA max for the LF356. Self-heating raises input bias current, which doubles every 10 °C; the datasheet recommends a heat sink when minimum IB matters.
- The LF357/LF157/LF257 is decompensated and stable only at AV≥5. It will oscillate as a follower or unity-gain stage, so it is not a drop-in for an LF356 socket.
- Offset trim uses a 25 kΩ pot with the wiper to V+, not V−; pins 1 and 5 are BALANCE. Some third-party summaries get this wrong.
- Poor at driving low impedances: Tangent found it unsuitable for low-impedance headphones.
- It is a single (DIP-8 single pinout), so rolling it into dual-op-amp sockets needs adapters.
- The earliest LF156 (1975 New Products page) labelled pin 8 'current source reference' and required pin 8 to be tied to pin 7 when offset adjust was unused. Later datasheets show pin 8 as NC.
- Specs are guaranteed at ±15 V for LF35x. There is no low-voltage specification.

## Silicon changes under the same part number

### 1. National Semiconductor: 1975 to before 1980 (earliest production)

The earliest documented LF156 pinout differs: pin 8 had a bias-reference function, and the typical AC figures differed (15 V/µs, 1.4 µs settling, versus 12 V/µs and 1.5 µs from 1980). This suggests an early mask or metal change before volume production. That is inferred from datasheet wording, not confirmed by National.

- **When:** 1975 to before 1980 (earliest production)
- **Affected:** LF156, LF156H
- **How to tell old from new:** The 1975 'New Products' sheet labels pin 8 'current source reference' and says it must be tied to pin 7 when offset adjust is unused; the 1980 and later datasheets show pin 8 as NC. Any affected parts would be metal-can LF156s with c.1975–76 date codes. No PCN is known.
- **Audio impact:** None known. Relevant only to collectors fitting very early metal-can LF156s into modern boards where pin 8 is left open.
- **Drop-in risk:** low – affects only rare 1975-era LF156 metal cans. If you use one, tie pin 8 to V+ when not trimming offset.
- **Confidence:** medium

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Pin 8 function | Current source reference; tie to pin 7 if offset adjust unused | NC (no connection) |
| Slew rate (LF156, typ) | 15 V/µs | 12 V/µs |
| Settling to 0.01% (typ) | 1.4 µs | 1.5 µs |

### 2. National Semiconductor: between the 1980 databook and the May 2000 DS005646 edition

A possible compensation-capacitor change in the decompensated LF157/LF357, or just a documentation correction. The spec tables for GBW (20 MHz) and slew rate (50 V/µs) did not change.

- **When:** between the 1980 databook and the May 2000 DS005646 edition
- **Affected:** LF157, LF257, LF357
- **How to tell old from new:** The datasheet schematic note changes from 'C = 2 pF on LF157' (1980) to '3 pF in LF357 series' (2000, 2001, and TI Rev D). The capacitive-load claim in the feature list changes from 10,000 pF to 5,000 pF, although the application hint still says CL(max) 0.01 µF. No date code or PCN boundary is known.
- **Audio impact:** Unknown. If real, a slightly lower-bandwidth, more stable LF357 in later lots; the guaranteed specs are unchanged.
- **Drop-in risk:** low – the guaranteed specs are the same and it may be purely editorial.
- **Confidence:** low

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Compensation capacitor note (LF157/LF357) | 2 pF | 3 pF |
| Feature-list capacitive-load claim | 10,000 pF | 5,000 pF |

### 3. Linear Technology / PMI (second sources): c.1978 (PMI); c.1990 (LT models)

Second-source dies were not necessarily National's. Electronics (June 1978) says PMI was second-sourcing 'a better-performing LF356'. LT's 1990 macromodels tag all its LF155/156/355/356 (and A) parts 'Low IOS 10pA max', against National's 20 pA (LF15x) and 50 pA (LF35x) maximums. No LT or PMI datasheet was found to confirm the full spec set.

- **When:** c.1978 (PMI); c.1990 (LT models)
- **Affected:** LF155, LF155A, LF156, LF156A, LF355, LF355A, LF356, LF356A
- **How to tell old from new:** Look for the manufacturer logo: an LT logo, or a PMI logo on a 1970s part. National and TI parts carry the NS or TI logo.
- **Audio impact:** Negligible for audio. The differences are in DC precision.
- **Drop-in risk:** low – same pinout and function; the second sources claim tighter DC specs.
- **Confidence:** low

| Parameter | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Input offset current (max, 25 °C) | National: 20 pA (LF15x/25x/356B), 50 pA (LF35x) | LT second source: 10 pA (per macromodel header) |

## Counterfeits

This run found no documented counterfeit reports; web search was unavailable, so forums were not checked. The following are generic screening heuristics, not reported cases. Remarked LF351/TL081-class parts are the obvious risk. A genuine LF356 draws about 5 mA typ (10 max) at ±15 V; an LF351 draws about 1.8 mA (National JFET macromodel header). A genuine LF356 has a GBW of about 5 MHz, and offset nulls with a 25 kΩ pot on pins 1/5 with the wiper to V+. Current genuine TI marking is 'LF 356N' (PDIP) or 'LF356 M' (SOIC), per the SNOSBH0D addendum. Treat marketplace 'NS LF357H' metal cans and 'National' parts with post-2012 date codes as suspect. A metal-can LF357 that is stable as a unity-gain follower is also suspect, because the real part is decompensated and needs AV≥5.

## Related parts and alternatives

TI LF356M/MX/N /NOPB: the same family, still in production (Active in the Jan-2026 addendum), ADI (PMI) OP-15/OP-16/OP-17: improved successors 'patterned after the 355/356/357'; obsolete but found as NOS, TI OPA1641 / OPA827: modern low-noise JFET-input audio singles, ADI AD8610 / ADA4627-1: fast-settling precision JFET singles, ADI (LT) LT1122: fast-settling JFET single, a like-for-like upgrade for I/V and sample-and-hold duty, TI TL071H: cheap modern JFET single with similar speed (Tangent likens the LF355 to the TL071), TI OPA140 / OPA1655: modern low-noise precision singles
