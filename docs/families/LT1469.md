# LT1468 / LT1469 (single/dual 90 MHz, 22 V/µs, 16-bit-accurate bipolar precision op-amp)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*Linear Tech's 16-bit-settling precision op-amp built for DAC I/V: 5 nV/√Hz, 90 MHz, ±15 V, very low distortion. A measurement-minded I/V and filter choice.*

**Technology:** Bipolar, single-stage precision high-speed op-amp. The datasheet says it is 'a single stage amplifier with an optimal thermal layout'. It uses input bias-current cancellation, and only the inverting-input bias current is trimmed (at zero common-mode voltage), so the part is tailored for inverting (I/V) use. Each input has a 100 Ω series resistor, and back-to-back diodes sit across the input-device bases. LT1468 and LT1469 are unity-gain stable. The -2 versions (LT1468-2, LT1469-2) are separate datasheets/parts, stable only at AV ≥ 2, with 200 MHz GBW and 30 V/µs (LT1469-2 datasheet title 'Dual 200MHz, 30V/µs 16-Bit Accurate AV ≥ 2 Op Amp').

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Linear's datasheets call the LT1468/LT1469 'optimum' for high-accuracy uses such as DAC current-to-voltage conversion and ADC buffers, and show an 'Ultralow Distortion Balanced Audio Line Driver' at 0.00025% THD+N (with an LT1364 supplying output current). On diyAudio, members describe it as as clean as the OPA627 but quieter and more neutral, and a better I/V choice than the OPA627; one poster, citing Samuel Groner's op-amp measurement shootout, concluded the LT1469 'should be the best of the bunch overall'. Views are not unanimous: it has been called 'rather thin, very clean', one user heard 'metallic' treble (possibly a bypassing problem), and some say it lacks output current and 'character' for a final output stage. These are forum opinions, not measurements. The specific 'very low common-mode distortion' reading of Groner's survey was not confirmed.

**How people describe the sound:** clean, quiet, neutral, thin (one listener)

**Typical uses:** I/V conversion for current-output DACs (datasheet front-page LTC1597 example; diyAudio I/V threads), DAC post-filter / active reconstruction filters, ADC input buffer / driver (datasheet LTC1604 example), balanced line driver (datasheet parallel-composite circuit with LT1364), op-amp rolling in DAC output stages (community)

**Caveats:**

- The -2 variants (LT1468-2 / LT1469-2) are stable only at gain ≥ 2 and may oscillate in unity-gain buffer sockets. Check the suffix.
- Phase reversal (datasheet): if an input comes within 0.5 V of the positive supply, the output reverses phase.
- Layout-sensitive. The datasheet asks for a ground plane, short leads, RF-quality 0.01-0.1 µF bypass capacitors in parallel with 1-10 µF low-ESR capacitors, and guard rings for best DC. Socket adapters and long leads likely raise the oscillation risk (general inference). A diyAudio report of 'metallic' treble was attributed to inadequate bypassing.
- Input-capacitance pole: for RF > 2k use CF > RG·CIN/RF. When DC noise gain is one with a large RF, CF should be ≥ CIN, and a DAC can add 'many tens of pF'. The datasheet's LTC1597 I/V uses 15 pF across 12k for optimum nulling.
- Capacitive load is limited to 100 pF at unity gain and 300 pF at gain -1. Beyond that, use a series output resistor plus a feedback capacitor (datasheet Fig. 3).
- Because of bias-current cancellation, the datasheet says balanced source resistances are NOT recommended: they degrade DC accuracy and raise noise. The noninverting-input IB is untrimmed (±40 nA max).
- Modest output current (±22 mA typ; diyAudio posters note ~20 mA as a limit for final output use). The datasheet line driver adds an LT1364 to drive 600 Ω.
- Draws about 4.1 mA per amplifier at ±15 V (up to 6.5 mA over temperature), so it runs warmer than low-power audio duals.
- Needs a proper split supply: specified at ±5 V and ±15 V, with ±4.5 V the guaranteed minimum.
- Simulation: older LTspice IV symbols (mirrored on GitHub) describe LT1468-2 and LT1469-2 as '90MHz, 22V/µs', and the LT1469 and LT1469-2 symbols reference the single-channel LT1468 and LT1468-2 models. ADI fixed the LT1468-2 label on 01/23/21.

## Counterfeits

No counterfeit or remark reports specific to the LT1468/LT1469 were found; they were not searched for specifically. Genuine markings per datasheet Rev B: SO-8 is top-marked '1469' (C grade) or '1469I' (I grade); PDIP is 'LT1469CN8' / 'LT1469IN8'; DFN is '1469', with the temperature grade only on the shipping-container label. This is a relatively expensive LT/ADI precision part, so buy from authorized distributors and treat cheap marketplace 'LT1469CN8' DIPs with suspicion (general caution, not a documented case). Also check that you did not receive an LT1469-2 (AV ≥ 2 only) when you need unity-gain stability.
