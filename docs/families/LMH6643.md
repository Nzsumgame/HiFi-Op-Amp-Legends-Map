# LMH6642 / LMH6643 / LMH6644 (National/TI 130 MHz high-output-current rail-to-rail-output op-amp)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** High-speed / video crossovers

*Low-power 130 MHz, ±75 mA rail-to-rail-output op-amp that FiiO and iBasso used as a headphone buffer; Head-Fi op-amp rollers call it warm and relaxed.*

**Technology:** Low-power voltage-feedback op-amp (bipolar, National LMH high-speed line). Rail-to-rail output within about 40 mV of the rails. Input common-mode range runs from 0.5 V below V- to 1 V below V+, so the input is not rail-to-rail. -3 dB bandwidth is 130 MHz (Av=+1), linear output current is ±75 mA and supply current is 2.7 mA per amplifier. Single-supply range is 2.7 V to 12.8 V, so it does not survive ±15 V rails.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

Its standing comes from OEM portable gear and Head-Fi op-amp rolling, not from classic hi-fi. FiiO used it as the headphone driver or buffer in the E10K, replacing the AD8397 with a claimed improvement in transient response and drive, and forum reports place it in the X3-II, X5 and E18. It was a stock buffer in iBasso portable amps; one account says some units shipped with the THS4032 instead. In the iBasso D1/D2/D10 rolling threads, users recommended ADA4841-2 (L/R) with LMH6643 buffers and described the result as 'clean, open' with good tonal balance and soundstage. Individual users called the LMH6643 warm and relaxed. Some users rated it, with the AD8397, as 'hard to beat for buffers' into low-impedance headphones, and said it works well as a virtual-ground op-amp. Other users said it loses 'a bit of impact and boldness' as a buffer, and some preferred the LME49721 or OPA2727. All of these are subjective forum impressions.

**How people describe the sound:** warm, relaxed, intimate upper-register detail (user report), clean, open (in ADA4841-2 + LMH6643 combination), slightly less impact/boldness as buffer (user report)

**Typical uses:** headphone output driver/buffer in portable amps and DAPs (FiiO E10K, X3-II, X5, E18; iBasso D-series), virtual-ground (rail-splitter) op-amp in portable amps, buffer inside or after a DAC I/V or gain stage at low supply voltages

**Caveats:**

- Maximum supply is 12.8 V (about ±6.4 V). Do not roll it into ±15 V op-amp sockets.
- At 17 nV/√Hz it is noisy compared with audio op-amps such as the NE5532 or LME49720. It suits buffer and driver duty, not low-level gain stages.
- It is a 130 MHz part. Layout and decoupling matter, and capacitive loads such as long cables may need an isolation resistor.
- The sound descriptions are subjective reports from Head-Fi rolling threads.

## Counterfeits

No counterfeit or remarking reports specific to the LMH664x turned up. The parts are cheap and available from authorized distributors. Leaded orderables without /NOPB (for example LMH6642MF) are NRND older production with a different lead finish, not a sign of fake parts. Treat marketplace 'LMH6643' SOIC or VSSOP parts with the usual caution.

## Related parts and alternatives

AD8397 (the part FiiO replaced with the LMH6643 in the E10K; similar high-current rail-to-rail-output buffer role), THS4032 (alternative stock buffer in iBasso units), LME49721 (rail-to-rail audio op-amp that some users preferred), OPA2727 (preferred by some users in rolling threads), ADA4841-2 (partner L/R op-amp in the iBasso rolling combination)
