# LMH6642 / LMH6643 / LMH6644

**130 MHz bipolar rail-to-rail-output op-amp, 1/2/4 ch** · National Semiconductor (c. 2001–2011) → Texas Instruments (2011–present) · Tier C (niche / baseline)

*Low-power 130 MHz, ±75 mA rail-to-rail-output buffer in FiiO and iBasso gear; Head-Fi rollers call it warm and relaxed.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | Supply range | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 nV/√Hz | 130 MHz (−3 dB bandwidth) | 130 V/µs | 2.7 V to 12.8 V total | 2.7 mA per amplifier |

## Why it's rated

Known from portable OEM gear and Head-Fi rolling, not classic hi-fi. FiiO used it as the E10K headphone buffer (replacing the AD8397); iBasso used it stock. Users call it warm and relaxed, 'hard to beat for buffers' into low impedances; others say it lacks some impact. All subjective forum impressions.

**Described as:** warm, relaxed, intimate upper-register detail, clean, open (with ADA4841-2), slightly less impact

**Used in:** Headphone buffer in FiiO E10K, X3-II, X5, E18; iBasso D-series, Virtual-ground (rail-splitter) op-amp in portable amps, Buffer after DAC I/V or gain stage at low supplies

## Watch out

- 12.8 V maximum supply (about ±6.4 V): never roll into ±15 V sockets.
- 17 nV/√Hz is noisy versus NE5532 or LME49720; suits buffers, not low-level gain stages.
- 130 MHz part: layout and decoupling matter; capacitive loads may need an isolation resistor.
- Some iBasso units reportedly shipped with THS4032 instead; FiiO X3-II/X5/E18 use is forum-reported.
- Users preferring LME49721 or OPA2727 report less 'impact and boldness' as a buffer.
- **Counterfeits:** No LMH664x-specific counterfeit or remarking reports. Parts are cheap and widely stocked. Leaded non-NOPB codes (e.g. LMH6642MF) are older NRND production with a different lead finish, not fakes. Treat marketplace SOIC/VSSOP parts with usual caution.

## Silicon changes

None documented. Compare datasheet revisions before assuming old and new parts are identical.
