# LT1122 (fast-settling JFET-input single op-amp, grades A/B/C/D)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*Linear Technology's 14 MHz, 80 V/us fast-settling JFET single; a niche rollers' pick for DAC output/filter stages, named alongside the LT1361, LT1364 and LT1469.*

**Technology:** Single-channel, voltage-feedback, internally compensated (unity-gain stable) op-amp with JFET inputs. Per the ADI/LTC datasheet (1122fb): 80 V/us slew, 340 ns settling, 14 MHz bandwidth at 7 mA supply current, 120 uV offset, voltage gain 500,000, aimed at fast-settling 12-bit data conversion and acquisition. LTC's SPICE macromodel uses a P-channel JFET input pair with a 2.25 mA tail current.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche, cult-level pick with no mainstream following. Head-Fi op-amp rollers name the LT1122 as a candidate alongside the LT1361, LT1364 and LT1469 in a thread on DAC output stages. diyAudio members group it with the LT1028 and LT1115 as Linear Technology chips worth trying and call the LT1122ACN8 'a good chip' (second-hand, unverified wording). Tangent's op-amp listening notes (Chinese translation on Soomal, 2005) list it among chips to test later, with no verdict. No Audio Science Review or Samuel Groner measurements are known. Outside audio, a KiCad reproduction of the HP 3458A A3 board uses an LT1122 as unity-gain buffer U404.

**How people describe the sound:** 'a good chip' (diyAudio, general praise; no specific sonic descriptors verified)

**Typical uses:** DAC output and reconstruction-filter stages (Head-Fi recommendations), Single op-amp rolling in DIP-8 sockets (LT1122ACN8 / CCN8), Fast-settling buffers for 12-bit data conversion and acquisition (datasheet design intent), Precision instrument buffers: HP 3458A A3 board U404 (KiCad reproduction)

**Caveats:**

- Single channel only: dual-op-amp sockets need an adapter carrying two of them.
- About 7 mA quiescent current per amplifier (datasheet), so it runs warmer and drains batteries faster than TL07x-class parts.
- Fast part (14 MHz, 80 V/us): treat like other fast op-amps (good decoupling, short leads); stability in a given socket not verified.
- Grades A-D probably differ in DC or settling guarantees (not verified); for audio the grade is unlikely to matter.
- Voltage-noise density and THD were not confirmed; the datasheet markets speed and settling, not low noise, so do not assume it is a low-noise audio part.

## Counterfeits

No LT1122 counterfeit or remarking reports are known (not specifically searched in this pass). Low-volume, single-source LTC/ADI part. Buy from authorized distributors; be wary of cheap marketplace 'LT1122ACN8' lots; check the LTC logo, grade letter (A/B/C/D) and date-code plausibility; confirm about 7 mA quiescent current at +/-15 V and roughly 14 MHz / 80 V/us behaviour. A re-marked slow JFET part such as a TL071 would draw far less current and be far slower.
