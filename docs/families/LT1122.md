# LT1122 (fast-settling JFET-input single op-amp, grades A/B/C/D)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** JFET-input

*Linear Technology's fast-settling JFET-input single op-amp; a niche op-amp rollers' pick for DAC output and filter stages, named alongside the LT1361, LT1364 and LT1469.*

**Technology:** Single-channel, voltage-feedback op-amp with JFET inputs. LTC's own SPICE macromodel uses a P-channel JFET input pair with a 2.25 mA tail current. LTC describes it as "Fast Settling, JFET Input Operational Amplifier", with settling guaranteed to 0.01%. Distributor data gives about 14 MHz GBW and 7 to 7.8 mA supply current.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche, cult-level pick with no mainstream following. Head-Fi op-amp rollers name the LT1122 as a candidate alongside the LT1361, LT1364 and LT1469 in threads on DAC output stages; one thread also prefers the LME49720 over the LM4562. diyAudio members group it with the LT1028 and LT1115 as the Linear Technology chips worth trying, and call the LT1122ACN8 'a good chip'. Tangent's widely translated op-amp listening notes (Chinese translation on Soomal, 2005) list the LT1122 among chips he had on hand to test later, but give no verdict. No Audio Science Review or Samuel Groner measurements were found; web search was unavailable in this pass. Outside audio, a KiCad reproduction of the HP 3458A 8.5-digit DMM A3 analog board uses an LT1122 as unity-gain buffer U404.

**How people describe the sound:** 'a good chip' (diyAudio, general praise; no specific sonic descriptors were verified)

**Typical uses:** DAC output and reconstruction-filter stages (Head-Fi recommendations), Single op-amp rolling in DIP-8 sockets (LT1122ACN8 / CCN8), Fast-settling buffers for DACs and data conversion (the design intent in its datasheet title), Precision instrument buffers: HP 3458A A3 board, U404 unity-gain buffer (KiCad reproduction)

**Caveats:**

- Single channel only: dual-op-amp sockets need an adapter carrying two of them.
- Relatively high quiescent current, about 7-7.8 mA per amplifier (distributor and datasheet-derived data), so it runs warmer and drains batteries faster than TL07x-class parts.
- A fast JFET part (about 14 MHz GBW). Stability in any given socket or layout was not verified; treat it like other fast op-amps (decoupling, short leads).
- Grades A-D probably differ in DC or settling guarantees (not verified). For audio the grade is unlikely to matter, but the A grade costs more.
- Its noise density and distortion specs were not verified in this pass, so do not assume it is a low-noise audio part.

## Counterfeits

No LT1122 counterfeit or remarking reports were found in this pass, but web search was unavailable, so this is not a clean bill of health. It is a low-volume, single-source LTC/ADI part in PDIP-8. Standard precautions apply: buy from authorized distributors (ADI, Mouser, Digi-Key); be wary of cheap marketplace 'LT1122ACN8' lots; check the LTC logo, the grade letter in the marking (A/B/C/D) and date-code plausibility; and confirm that quiescent current is about 7-8 mA at ±15 V and GBW is about 14 MHz. A re-marked slow JFET part such as a TL071 would draw far less current and have much lower bandwidth.

## Open questions

- The session's WebSearch budget (200/200) was spent before this task started, so no vendor or distributor search was possible. The following are all unverified and need a re-run with search: the LTC datasheet revision chain (footer codes such as 'LT1122 1122fa/fb' and 'LT/TP' print codes), the ADI 'Rev.' letters, revision-history tables, and any PCNs.
- Three archive seed URLs are INFERRED from common LTC/ADI URL patterns and were not seen in any source: http://www.linear.com/product/LT1122, https://www.analog.com/en/products/lt1122.html and https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/lt1122.pdf. Only http://cds.linear.com/docs/Datasheet/lt1122.pdf was actually observed (in the gEDA spicelib index).
- Current ADI lifecycle status (Production / NRND / Obsolete) of the LT1122 A/B/C/D grades is unknown. It is also unknown whether SO-8 (S8) or CERDIP (J8/MJ8, military) versions ever existed.
- Datasheet values not verified: e_n at 1 kHz and 10 kHz, i_n, slew rate, settling time to 0.01% for a 10 V step, THD, supply range, output current, offset and bias per grade.
- The LT1122's introduction year is not established; the LTC SPICE library carrying its model is dated 1990 (license) and 5/31/99 (release 3.6).
- The Head-Fi and diyAudio praise comes second-hand via search summaries (discovery notes); the exact posts and wording are unverified.
- The HP 3458A usage comes from a reverse-engineered KiCad reproduction (EleDes 3458A A3 66533 2021). Whether HP's original A3 board used the LT1122 in U404 should be confirmed from HP service documentation.
- No silicon, process or fab change under the LT1122 part number was found, but the lack of search access means one is not ruled out.
