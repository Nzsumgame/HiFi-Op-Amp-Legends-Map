# LT1122 (grades A/B/C/D)

**Fast-settling JFET-input single op-amp** · Linear Technology (c. 1990–2017) → Analog Devices (2017–present) · Tier C (niche / baseline)

*Linear Tech's 14 MHz, 80 V/µs, 340 ns-settling JFET single; a niche roller's pick for DAC output and filter stages.*

| GBW | Slew rate | Supply current |
|---|---|---|
| 14 MHz | 80 V/µs | 7 mA (Mouser: 7.5 mA typ; 7.8 mA also seen) |

## Why it's rated

A niche pick. Head-Fi rollers list it with the LT1361, LT1364 and LT1469 for DAC output stages. diyAudio members group it with the LT1028 and LT1115 as LTC chips worth trying. Tangent's notes (Soomal, 2005) list it to test later. No ASR or Samuel Groner measurements. A KiCad HP 3458A A3 reproduction uses it as buffer U404.

**Described as:** 'a good chip' (diyAudio)

**Used in:** DAC output and reconstruction-filter stages, Single op-amp rolling in DIP-8 sockets, Fast-settling buffers for 12-bit data conversion, Precision instrument buffers, e.g. HP 3458A A3 U404

## Watch out

- Single channel only: dual sockets need an adapter carrying two.
- About 7 mA quiescent per amplifier; runs warmer and drains batteries faster than TL07x parts.
- Fast part (14 MHz, 80 V/µs): decouple well and keep leads short.
- Grades A–D probably differ in DC or settling guarantees; unlikely to matter for audio.
- Marketed for speed, not low noise; voltage noise and THD are unconfirmed.
- **Counterfeits:** No LT1122 fakes reported. Buy from authorized distributors; distrust cheap 'LT1122ACN8' lots. Check logo, grade letter and date code, and confirm about 7 mA at ±15 V. A remarked TL071 would draw far less current and be much slower.

## Silicon changes

None documented. Compare datasheet revisions before assuming old and new parts are identical.
