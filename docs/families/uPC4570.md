# µPC4570 (NEC / Renesas ultra-low-noise dual bipolar)

NEC (NEC Corporation, then NEC Electronics Corporation) (introduction date not confirmed, until 31 Mar 2010) → Renesas Electronics (1 Apr 2010 to present) · Tier C (niche / baseline)

> ⚠ **Same part number, different silicon:** Renesas replaced the NEC-era uPC4570 datasheet with a new Renesas document, R03DS0135EJ. [Details](#silicon-changes)

*NEC's ultra-low-noise dual (4.5 nV/√Hz, 15 MHz, 7 V/µs), pitched as a 4558 upgrade. Japanese modders credit it with tighter bass than the NJM4580DD.*

## Why it's rated

A cult part among Japanese modders rather than a Western audiophile legend. The C-Area wiki describes it as NEC's audio op-amp developed from the 4558, like the JRC NJM4580. Comparing it with the NJM4580DD, the wiki says it has tighter bass, similar treble and possibly slightly lower resolution, and calls this a matter of taste. It appears widely in Japanese CD players, AV receivers, mini-systems and Tascam multitracks. The Tascam 424 MKI channel sound is partly credited to its UPC4570 + NJM4565 op-amps (JHS 424 Gain Stage). No independent measurements were found.

**Described as:** tight / firm bass (compared with NJM4580DD, per C-Area wiki), high-frequency extension comparable to NJM4580, possibly slightly less resolution than NJM4580 (subjective)

**Used in:** CD player output and filter stages (Japanese domestic players), AV receiver audio paths (Yamaha AV-S70, µPC4570G2), mini-system audio (Sony HCD-GRX80, UPC4570C-1), cassette multitrack channel preamps (Tascam Portastudio 424 MKI; Tascam 238S uses µPC4570HA), preamps and tone-control kits (e.g. AIYIMA boards sold with UPC4570C), op-amp rolling in Japanese DIY as an NJM4580 alternative

## Watch out

- The DIP-8 µPC4570C is reportedly a maintenance (保守) product, and aftermarket supply goes through channels such as Rochester Electronics.
- Minimum supply of about ±4 V (not re-confirmed) makes it unsuitable for low-voltage single-rail designs.
- The SIP-9 (HA) and SOP-8 (G2) versions are not drop-in replacements for DIP-8 sockets.
- The sound claims are Japanese-community folklore, and no independent distortion or noise measurements were found.
- **Counterfeits:** No reports of counterfeit µPC4570 parts were found. The DIP µPC4570C is reportedly a maintenance product and is sold through surplus and hobby channels, such as Suzusho and AliExpress tone-control kits advertised with 'UPC4570C'. DigiKey Marketplace lists UPC4570C-A through Rochester Electronics, an authorized aftermarket source. Remarked vintage Japanese op-amps are a general risk from hobby channels. The LCSC/EasyEDA 'NEC4570G2' part has an unverified origin. The actual top-mark formats were not seen.
