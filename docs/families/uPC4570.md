# µPC4570 (NEC / Renesas ultra-low-noise dual bipolar)

**Tier** C: niche / baseline · **Category** Japanese audio series (New JRC / Nisshinbo)

*NEC's lesser-known, 4558-derived counterpart to the NJM4580 (4.5 nV/√Hz, 15 MHz, 7 V/µs). Japanese modders credit it with tighter bass than the NJM4580DD.*

**Technology:** Bipolar dual op-amp with a PNP input pair (derived from the 4558), ultra-low noise and wide bandwidth, running from ±4 to ±16 V split supplies. The PNP input comes from the OrCAD JOPAMP macromodel and the 4558 lineage comes from the C-Area wiki; the NEC datasheet schematic was not seen.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A cult part among Japanese modders rather than a Western audiophile legend. It is described as NEC's audio op-amp developed from the 4558, much like the JRC NJM4580, with excellent specifications but little publicity. The C-Area wiki listening comparison against the common NJM4580DD says the µPC4570C has tighter bass, similar treble extension and possibly slightly lower resolution, adding that this is a matter of taste. It shows up widely in Japanese consumer and pro-sumer gear such as CD players, AV receivers, mini-systems and Tascam multitracks. The Tascam Portastudio 424 MKI channel sound is credited partly to its UPC4570 + NJM4565 op-amps, and the JHS 424 Gain Stage pedal reuses those original parts. No Audio Science Review, Samuel Groner or similar measurement data was found in this pass.

**How people describe the sound:** tight / firm bass (compared with NJM4580DD), high-frequency extension comparable to NJM4580, possibly slightly less resolution than NJM4580 (subjective)

**Typical uses:** CD player output and filter stages (Japanese domestic players), AV receiver audio paths (Yamaha AV-S70, µPC4570G2), mini-system audio (Sony HCD-GRX80, UPC4570C-1), cassette multitrack channel preamps (Tascam Portastudio 424 MKI; Tascam 238S uses µPC4570HA), preamps, equalizers and tone controls (e.g. AIYIMA tone-control board kits sold with UPC4570C), op-amp rolling in Japanese DIY as an NJM4580 alternative

**Caveats:**

- The DIP-8 µPC4570C is a maintenance (保守) product, so DIP supply for op-amp rolling is limited.
- Minimum supply of ±4 V makes it unsuitable for low-voltage single-rail (e.g. 5 V USB) designs.
- The SIP-9 (HA) and SOP-8 (G2) versions are not drop-in replacements for DIP-8 sockets.
- Evidence is almost entirely Japanese-community folklore, and no independent distortion or noise measurements were found.

## Counterfeits

No reports of counterfeit µPC4570 parts were found in this pass; coverage was limited because the web-search budget ran out. The DIP µPC4570C is a maintenance product now sold mostly through surplus and hobby channels, including Suzusho and AliExpress audio kits such as the AIYIMA tone-control boards advertised with 'UPC4570C'. Remarked vintage Japanese op-amps are a general risk from these channels. The LCSC/EasyEDA library lists a part named 'NEC4570G2', whose origin (genuine Renesas or third-party) is unverified. Genuine parts should carry NEC or Renesas logo markings with µPC4570 or C4570 top marks; the actual marking formats were not seen.

## Open questions

- This record rests on very little evidence. The web-search budget for the session (200 queries) was exhausted before any new query ran, so it was built from one cached WebSearch result from the discovery phase plus GitHub code search. A follow-up pass with fresh searches is needed.
- Hint 'NEC- and Renesas-era datasheets exist': partly confirmed. NEC Japanese datasheet copies (G10528JJ8V0DS00, Suzusho, Zaiko) and a live Renesas product page exist. It is not confirmed whether Renesas issued a new datasheet number or only re-covered the NEC G10528 document.
- Hint 'DIP µPC4570C is a maintenance product': confirmed by a search summary of Japanese sources. No Renesas lifecycle page or PCN number was seen.
- The full NEC edition chain (G10528JJ1V0… to JJ8V0 and any English G10528EJxVxDS00 editions) and the printed dates are unknown.
- The URLs https://www.renesas.com/en/document/dst/upc4570-data-sheet and https://www.renesas.com/ja/products/upc4570 in archive_seed_urls are inferred from Renesas URL patterns and were not seen.
- No evidence of any die, process or fab change under the µPC4570 name was found, and no silicon changes are recorded. This is unverified rather than proven.
- The OrCAD 1998 macromodel implies a unity-gain crossover of about 7 MHz (ga/2πC2 = 1.319 mS / (2π·30 pF)) against the 15 MHz GBW quoted from the datasheet. Check whether the NEC datasheet gives separate unity-gain frequency and GBW figures.
- Not captured: i_n, THD+N, quiescent current per channel, output current, Vos, and absolute maximum ratings.
- The meanings of the -1 (Sony UPC4570C-1) and -A (µPC4570C-A) suffixes are unknown, as is the current status of µPC4570G2 and µPC4570HA.
- The introduction year of the µPC4570 is unknown.
- It is unknown whether 'NEC4570G2' parts sold through LCSC are genuine Renesas stock or a third-party (e.g. Chinese) part with the same function.
