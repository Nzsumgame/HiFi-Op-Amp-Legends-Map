# AD8065 / AD8066 (single / dual FastFET JFET-input, 145 MHz)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** JFET-input

*Cheap, fast ADI JFET-input op-amp that CMoy/PIMETA battery-amp builders liked; Tangent placed its sound between the 'aggressive' AD8610 and the 'laid-back' OPA227/Burr-Brown sound.*

**Technology:** Voltage-feedback op amp with a laser-trimmed JFET input stage, built on ADI's proprietary XFCB (eXtra Fast Complementary Bipolar) process. The datasheet footnote says the AD8065/AD8066 is 'Protected by U.S. Patent No. 6,262,633', and the Theory of Operation calls the rail-to-rail output stage 'patented'; neither ties the patent to the process. The input is a cascoded N-channel JFET pair. When the common-mode voltage comes within about 3 V of +Vs, an auxiliary NPN pair takes over; this prevents phase reversal but raises Ib to tens of uA. The output is rail-to-rail, reaching within 0.5 V of either rail at up to 30 mA. Back-to-back input diodes limit the differential input to 1.8 V (absolute max). Unity-gain stable; the datasheet's model uses an open-loop crossover of 65 MHz.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

A niche Head-Fi and CMoy favourite from the mid-2000s, not a top-tier audiophile legend. Tangent's op-amp notes, read via the DiyEden Chinese translation reposted on Soomal (dated 2005-02-17), give these figures and verdicts: - Price: $4.21 (AD8065) and $5.38 (AD8066) at Digi-Key. - Minimum supply: 4.0 V for 0.5 V into 33 Ohm, and 5.7 V for 2 V into 330 Ohm. - Battery use: another good choice besides the AD823 and AD8610. It draws more current but needs less voltage, so the same battery setup can run longer. - Sound: 'not aggressive, but not laid-back'. His summary recommends it if you find the AD8610 too aggressive and the OPA227 too laid-back. He adds: 'It's a bit veiled, which is expected given the chip's price.' - Practical notes: SOIC only, so it needs an adapter, and it is rated only to 24 V (26.4 V max). Head-Fi users (search snippets, Sept 2026) called it 'a strange beast... very delicate, yet refined, and some good bass... clear, but still smooth highs', 'clear, focused, cool, fast, fairly detailed' with a 'really wide' soundstage, and 'definitely not harsh or tiring like the majority of other AD opamps'; another user found it 'too mellow and laid back, but sweet, with good space'. It was also compared with the LM4562 on diyAudio and discussed on Audio Asylum. A 2024 GitHub CMoy build guide (spbkaizo/baudio) says: 'the AD823, AD8066, AD8620 are some of my faves'. Still unverified: the OPA2228-preferred Cmoy shoot-out verdict and the Japanese blogger nabe's reported B+ rating. No audio-band THD+N measurements (ASR, Groner) were found.

**How people describe the sound:** neither aggressive nor laid-back; between the AD8610 and OPA227/Burr-Brown (Tangent), a bit veiled (Tangent), very delicate yet refined, some good bass (Head-Fi user), clear but still smooth highs (Head-Fi user), clear, focused, cool, fast, fairly detailed; really wide soundstage (Head-Fi user), not harsh or tiring like most other AD op-amps (Head-Fi user), too mellow and laid back, but sweet, with good space (dissenting Head-Fi user)

**Typical uses:** CMoy / PIMETA battery headphone amplifiers (low minimum supply voltage), Op-amp rolling on SOIC-to-DIP adapters in headphone amps and DACs at supplies of +/-12 V or less, Line and headphone gain stages and buffers, Non-audio: photodiode preamps, ADC drivers, video buffers, instrument front ends

**Caveats:**

- The maximum operating supply is 24 V total, with 26.4 V absolute max. Never fit it in +/-15 V or +/-18 V sockets: 30-36 V exceeds the absolute maximum.
- It comes only in SOIC, MSOP and SOT-23, so audio use needs a DIP adapter. A 145 MHz part on an adapter can oscillate; decouple close to the pins.
- It tolerates little capacitive load (20-25 pF for 30% overshoot at G=+1). Isolate cable or headphone loads with a series resistor.
- The JFET input range ends about 2.6-3.3 V below +Vs. Beyond that the NPN stage takes over, Ib rises to tens of uA and the offset shifts. This can matter in unity-gain non-inverting stages at low supplies.
- Back-to-back input diodes limit differential input to 1.8 V.
- The 1/f noise corner is high (about 1.3-1.5 kHz): about 11 nV/rtHz at 1 kHz and about 33 nV/rtHz at 100 Hz (Fig. 21). That is worse in the audio band than the 7 nV/rtHz headline figure.
- It draws 6.4 mA per amplifier. The datasheet warns that shorting the output 'will likely cause catastrophic failure'.
- Ib grows about 1.7x per 10 C and about 5x from 5 V to 24 V supplies, which matters with high-impedance inputs at +/-12 V.
- No audio-band THD+N specification; distortion is specified only as SFDR at 1 MHz and above.
- Sonic reputation rests on 2003-2010 forum listening impressions, which disagree (delicate/refined vs mellow/laid-back), not measurements.

## Counterfeits

One direct report, low confidence (single user). In the Chinese '电子元件网店红黑榜' (component-shop red/black list), contributor BH2VGM blacklisted Taobao seller 联科达电子 (Shenzhen) on 2024-03-27: 'AD8066、AD8032等使用劣质运放重打标，性能极差' (AD8066, AD8032 etc. are remarked inferior op-amps; performance extremely poor). Source: GitHub wt01111/DigitialPancake server/seed/shop-seed.json.  How to spot fakes: (1) No DIP-8 version exists. The Rev. D and Rev. L ordering guides list only SOIC-8, MSOP-8 and SOT-23-5 (also confirmed by ADI's product description), and Tangent (2005) noted the part was SOIC-only. A bare DIP-8 'AD8066' is fake, unless it is a SOIC chip on a visible adapter. (2) Marking codes from the ordering guides: - AD8066ARM (MSOP, leaded): H1B - AD8066ARMZ: H7C - AD8065ART (SOT-23): HRA - AD8065ARTZ: HRA# (# = RoHS; top or bottom marked) - AD8065WARTZ: H2F# SOIC parts have no marking code in the guide; ADI normally prints the part number. (3) Electrical checks from datasheet values: about 6.4 mA supply current per amplifier (about 12.8 mA per dual at +/-5 V), pA-level input bias current, about 180 V/us slew rate and 100+ MHz unity-gain bandwidth. A remarked generic bipolar op-amp would show nA-to-uA bias current, much lower slew and bandwidth, and a different Iq. (4) The absolute maximum supply is 26.4 V. A part that 'survives' +/-15 V proves nothing and has been overstressed.

- This pass ran 6 WebSearch queries (PCNs, current revision, older revisions/mirrors, lifecycle, reputation, JP datasheet). Mirrors for Rev. I (DigiKey), J (Mouser), K (Farnell) and L were found by title; none were opened.
- No copies of Rev. 0, A, B, C, E, F, G or H were located by URL. Alldatasheet page 83610 (AD8066) / 83617 (AD8066AR) are early uploads that may hold a pre-RoHS revision; worth checking. A Rev. E partial-copy lead ('AD8065_8066_RevE_p1-8.pdf' in an unreachable GitHub repo) remains unresolved.
- What exactly Rev. B (2/2003) changed in the Absolute Maximum Ratings is unknown.
- Three first-appearance questions are open: which revision first listed the Z (RoHS) part numbers (probably F or G), which added the 125 pA Ib/Ios max over temperature, and which changed the +5 V CMRR test condition (probably J; the Mouser Rev. J copy vs DigiKey Rev. I copy would settle it).
- The legacy URL http://www.analog.com/UploadedFiles/Data_Sheets/AD8065_8066.pdf is inferred and doubtful (ADI's UploadedFiles scheme used numeric-prefixed names).
- The Japanese data-sheet URL (AD8065_8066_JP.pdf) and its revision remain unconfirmed; a Japanese-language search returned only English datasheets.
- PCN search returned only generic ADI PCN/PDN forms (e.g. PCN 07_0024, 14_0065, 16_0033, 21_0035, 25_0065, PDN 10_0081) with no confirmed link to AD8065/AD8066. No silicon change is documented; whether any of these list AD8065/AD8066 is unchecked.
- Lifecycle: AD8066ARZ and AD8066ARMZ reported as Production via distributor/aggregator listings; AD8065 orderables and the automotive AD8065WARTZ-R7 not checked. Mouser still lists leaded AD8065AR/AD8066AR/AD8066ARM pages and an 'AD8065ARTZ' with no suffix (not in the Rev. L guide); status unknown.
- No audio-band THD+N measurements (Audio Science Review, Samuel Groner) were found.
- The OPA2228-vs-AD8066 Cmoy verdict and nabe's B+ rating come from discovery notes and were not confirmed by search.
- The English original of Tangent's page (tangentsoft.com/audio/opamps.html) was not surfaced by search; quotes come from the 2005 Chinese translation, which keeps the English sentence 'It's a bit veiled, which is expected given the chip's price.'
