# AD8065 / AD8066

**145 MHz FastFET JFET-input op-amp, single/dual** · Analog Devices (2002–present) → Analog Devices (2010–present) · Tier B (widely praised)

*Cheap, fast ADI JFET op-amp loved by CMoy builders; Tangent heard it between the aggressive AD8610 and laid-back OPA227.*

| Input noise (1 kHz) | GBW | Slew rate | THD+N | Supply range | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≈10.7 nV/√Hz (plot); 7 nV/√Hz typ at 10 kHz | 145 MHz (AD8065), 120 MHz (AD8066) at G = +1 | 180 V/µs typ, 130 min (±5/±12 V); 160 V/µs at +5 V | No audio-band spec; SFDR −88 dBc @ 1 MHz (±5 V) | 5–24 V total (±2.5 to ±12 V); 26.4 V absolute max | 6.4 mA typ / 7.2 mA max per amp (±5 V) |

## Why it's rated

A mid-2000s Head-Fi and CMoy favourite, not a top-tier legend. Tangent found it 'not aggressive, but not laid-back', 'a bit veiled', and a good battery pick alongside AD823 and AD8610. Head-Fi users heard it as delicate, refined and clear with smooth highs; one found it too mellow. No audio-band THD+N measurements (ASR, Groner) exist.

**Described as:** between AD8610 and OPA227, a bit veiled, delicate yet refined, clear, smooth highs, clear, focused, cool, fast, really wide soundstage, not harsh or tiring, too mellow (dissenting)

**Used in:** CMoy / PIMETA battery headphone amps (low minimum supply), Rolling on SOIC-to-DIP adapters at ±12 V or less, Line and headphone gain stages and buffers, Photodiode preamps, ADC drivers, video buffers, instrument front ends

## Watch out

- 24 V total maximum (26.4 V absolute): never fit in ±15 V or ±18 V sockets.
- SMD only; a 145 MHz part on an adapter can oscillate. Decouple at the pins; isolate loads over 20–25 pF.
- JFET range ends 2.6–3.3 V below +Vs, then Ib jumps to tens of µA. Differential input limited to 1.8 V.
- High 1/f corner: about 11 nV/√Hz at 1 kHz, 33 at 100 Hz, worse than the 7 nV headline.
- 6.4 mA per amp; output shorts 'will likely cause catastrophic failure'. Sonic reports (2003–2010) disagree.
- **Counterfeits:** A 2024 Chinese shop blacklist (one user) reports remarked AD8066s from a Taobao seller. No DIP-8 version exists, so a bare DIP 'AD8066' is fake. Check marking codes, ≈6.4 mA per amp, pA bias current and ≈180 V/µs slew.
