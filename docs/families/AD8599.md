## Why it's rated

On Head-Fi, the AD8599 is best known from the 'AD8599 is bloody good' thread, where op-amp rollers call it dynamic, vivacious, sparkling and 'alive'. One comparison thread calls it warmer than other Analog Devices op amps, with an accurate soundstage. Both come from the discovery notes, and neither thread was re-read in this pass. Engineers use it as an ultralow-noise, ultralow-distortion bipolar dual: 1.1 nV/rtHz and -120 dB THD at 1 kHz per ADI. Its claimed role as a low-noise alternative to the OPA1612 and LME49720 comes only from discovery notes (unverified). Uses confirmed in GitHub-hosted designs: the output amp of a DAC8812 MDAC volume attenuator (FutureSharks preamp-two); the RX I/Q or AF preamp in Elecraft KX3 and KX2 transceivers, next to the OPA2209; the I/Q baseband amp in the T41/Phoenix SDR; and precision low-noise instrumentation front ends. No ASR or Samuel Groner measurements were retrieved.

**Described as:** dynamic, vivacious, sparkling, alive, warmer than other Analog Devices op amps, accurate soundstage

**Used in:** Op-amp rolling in DAC and headphone-amp sockets (via SOIC-to-DIP adapter), Output amplifier for multiplying-DAC (MDAC) volume attenuators in DIY preamps, Low-noise line and headphone preamplifier stages with low source impedance, SDR I/Q baseband and RX AF preamp stages (Elecraft KX3/KX2, T41/Phoenix SDR), Precision low-noise instrumentation and ADC driver front ends

## Watch out

- The dual is SOIC-8 only (no DIP), so socket rolling needs an adapter. Adapter layout and decoupling can affect stability.
- It needs split rails of at least +/-4.5 V and must stay within the +/-18 V absolute maximum, so it does not suit low-voltage single-supply portable designs.
- Low voltage noise helps only at low source impedance. With a bipolar input, current noise and bias current matter with high-impedance sources (no AD8599 i_n or I_B figures verified).
- The input clamp diodes in ADI's model suggest a limited differential input voltage. Check the data sheet's absolute maximum ratings before using it in circuits with large input steps.
- Community sound descriptors are subjective forum impressions (low confidence), and the forum threads were not re-read.
- **Counterfeits:** No AD8597/AD8599-specific counterfeit or remarking reports were found. No search was spent on this in this pass. The AD8599 comes only in SOIC-8, and the AD8597 in SOIC-8 or LFCSP, sold mainly through authorised ADI distribution. By inference, treat any DIP-8 'AD8599' as a relabel or an adapter assembly. Buy from authorised distributors. To check a suspect part on the bench, look for e_n of about 1.1 nV/rtHz and very low THD. A relabelled 5532- or 4580-class die would measure clearly noisier and more distorted.

## Silicon changes

### Fab / process transfer: Analog Devices, PCN 25_0091 Rev. -: published 6 Jun 2025, effective 8 Sep 2025. A Rev. A form and a qualification report were issued later.

PCN 25_0091, 'Qualification of ADI Camas Wafer Fab Bipolar Process', qualifies ADI's Camas, WA fab to build ADI bipolar-process products. A search returned this PCN for an AD8597/AD8599 query, but no result confirms that either part is on the affected list. The confirmed listings are AD711/AD712/AD713, and the qualification report mentions AD712, AD624 and AD8221.

- **Affected:** AD8597 (unconfirmed), AD8599 (unconfirmed)
- **How to tell:** Not established. Check the affected-parts list in PCN 25_0091 and its Rev. A. If these parts are covered, stock with date codes after Sep 2025 may come from Camas.
- **Audio impact:** ADI states that parts use ADI-specified flows, materials and process controls 'ensuring no degradation of quality and reliability performance'. No measurements of Camas-built parts are known.
- **Drop-in risk:** low - same process transferred, and the data sheet is unchanged per ADI. Whether this applies to AD8597/AD8599 at all is unconfirmed.
- **Confidence:** low

|---|---|---|
| Wafer fab site | existing ADI bipolar fab (site not confirmed) | ADI Camas, WA qualified |
