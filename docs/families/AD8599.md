# AD8597 / AD8599 (single/dual ultralow-noise, ultralow-distortion bipolar)

**Tier** B: widely praised · **Category** Modern low-noise / ultra-low-distortion bipolar

*ADI's bipolar single and dual, rated about 1.1 nV/rtHz and about -120 dB THD. Head-Fi op-amp rollers called the AD8599 'bloody good': dynamic, sparkling and alive.*

**Technology:** Bipolar-input voltage-feedback op amp made only by Analog Devices. The ADI AD8597 macro-model's generic description reads '9/30V, BIP, OP, Low THD, Low Noise, 1X'. The ADI behavioural model has input clamp diodes: two anti-parallel strings of two series diodes (D1-D2 and D3-D4) across the inputs. The exact process and fab are not verified.

## Why enthusiasts rate it

On Head-Fi, AD8599 is best known from the 'AD8599 is bloody good' thread, where op-amp rollers call it dynamic, vivacious, sparkling and 'alive'. One comparison thread calls it warmer than other Analog Devices op amps, with an accurate soundstage. Both come from discovery notes and were not re-read this run. Engineers use it as an ultralow-noise, ultralow-distortion bipolar dual. The discovery notes also say it is recommended as a low-noise alternative to the OPA1612 and LME49720, but that source is unverified. Confirmed uses in GitHub-hosted designs: the output amp of an MDAC (DAC8812) volume attenuator in a DIY preamp (FutureSharks preamp-two); the RX I/Q or AF preamp in Elecraft KX3 and KX2 SDR transceivers, next to the OPA2209; the I/Q baseband amp (U106) in the T41/Phoenix SDR; and precision low-noise instrumentation front ends. No Audio Science Review or Samuel Groner measurements, and no Japanese or Chinese community impressions, could be retrieved.

**How people describe the sound:** dynamic, vivacious, sparkling, alive, warmer than other Analog Devices op amps, accurate soundstage

**Typical uses:** Op-amp rolling in DAC and headphone-amp sockets (via SOIC-to-DIP adapter), Output amplifier for multiplying-DAC (MDAC) volume attenuators in DIY preamps, Low-noise line and headphone preamplifier stages with low source impedance, SDR I/Q baseband and RX AF preamp stages (Elecraft KX3/KX2, T41/Phoenix SDR), Precision low-noise instrumentation and ADC driver front ends

**Caveats:**

- The dual is SOIC-8 only (no DIP), so socket rolling needs an adapter. Adapter layout and decoupling can affect stability.
- Needs split rails of at least about +/-4.5 V (9 V total, per the ADI model header), so it does not suit low-voltage portable single-supply designs.
- Low voltage noise helps only at low source impedance. As a bipolar-input part, its current noise and bias current matter with high-impedance sources (general bipolar trait; no AD8599 i_n or I_B figures verified).
- The input clamp diodes in ADI's model suggest a limited differential input voltage. Check the datasheet's absolute maximum ratings before using it as a comparator or in circuits with large input steps.
- Community sound descriptors are subjective forum impressions (low confidence), and the forum threads were not re-read this run.

## Counterfeits

No AD8597/AD8599-specific counterfeit or remarking reports were found. WebSearch was unavailable, so forums could not be checked. The AD8599 is offered only in SOIC-8 (and the AD8597 in SOIC-8 or LFCSP), and it is sold mainly through authorised ADI distribution. By inference, treat any DIP-8 'AD8599' as a relabel or an adapter assembly. Buy from authorised distributors. When in doubt, check on the bench that e_n is about 1 nV/rtHz and THD is very low. A relabelled 5532- or 4580-class die would measure clearly noisier and more distorted.
